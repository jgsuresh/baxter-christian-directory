# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 2 of 13)

*This section begins partway through Grand Direction 9 ("Spend all your days in a skillful, vigilant...") and breaks off before its end; the next file continues it. It opens with Baxter's outline of the "holy war" and then takes up the tempter's ends, the grounds he works on, and his helpers.*

*In the margin: The method.*

In opening to you this holy war, I shall, First, show you what we must do on the offensive part. Secondly, What on the defensive part. And here I shall show you, I. What it is that the tempter aims at as his end. II. What matter or ground he works upon. III. What are his succors and assistance. IV. What kind of officers and instruments he uses. V. What are his methods and actual temptations, 1. To actual sin, 2. Against our duty to God.

First, Our offensive arms are to be used, 1. Against the power of sin within us; and all its advantages and helps: for while Satan rules and possesses us within, we shall never well oppose him without. 2. Against sin in others, as far as we have opportunity. 3. Against the credit and honor of sin in the world: as the devil's servants would bring light and holiness into disgrace, so Christ's servants must cast disgrace and shame upon sin and darkness. 4. Against all the reasonings of sinners, and their subtle fallacies, by which they would deceive. 5. Against the passions and violent lusts which are the causes of men's other sins. 6. Against the strongholds and helps of sin, such as false teachers, profane revilers, ignorance, and deceit. Only be careful that on this pretext we do not step out of our ranks and places, to pull down the powers of the world by rebellions: "For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal" (2 Corinthians 10:4).

Secondly, As to our defense, I. The ends of the tempter which must be perceived are these: In general, his aim is at our utter ruin and damnation, and to draw us here to dishonor God as much as he can. But, especially, his aim is to strengthen the great heart sins, which are most mortal, and are the root, and life, and spawners of the rest: especially these: 1. Ignorance, which is the friend and cloak to all the rest. 2. Error, which will justify them. 3. Unbelief, which keeps off all that should oppose them. 4. Atheism, profaneness, unholiness, which are the defiance of God and all his armies. 5. Presumption, which emboldens them, and hides the danger. 6. Hardness of heart, which fortifies them against all the batteries of grace. 7. Hypocrisy, which makes them serve him as spies and informants in the army of Christ. 8. Dislike of God and his ways and servants, which is the devil's colors. 9. Unthankfulness, which tends to make them irreconcilable and unrecoverable. 10. Pride, which commands many regiments of lesser sins. 11. Worldliness, or love of money and wealth, which keeps his armies in pay. 12. Indulgence in physical pleasure, voluptuousness, or flesh-pleasing, which is the great commander of all the rest. [108] For selfishness is the devil's lieutenant-general, which consists chiefly in the three last named, but especially in pride and indulgence in physical pleasure. Some think that it is outward sins that bring all the danger; but these twelve heart sins, which I have named to you, are the twelve gates of the infernal city, which Satan loves above all the rest.

II. The matter and grounds of his temptations are these: 1. The devil first works upon the outward sense, and so upon the sensory appetite: he shows the cup to the drunkard's eye, and the bait of filthy lust to the fornicator, and the riches and pomp of the world to the greedy and proud. The glutton tastes the sweetness of the dish which he loves. Stage-plays, and tempting amusements, and proud attire, and sumptuous buildings, and all such physical pleasures, are the baits by which the devil angles for souls. Thus Eve first saw the fruit, and then tasted, and then did eat. Thus Noah, and Lot, and David sinned. Thus Achan says, Joshua 7:21, "I saw (the garments, silver, and gold) I coveted them, and I took them." The sense is the door of sin.

2. The tempter next works on the fantasy or imagination, and prints upon it the loveliest image of his bait that he possibly can, and engages the sinner to think on it, and to roll it over and over in his mind, even as God commands us to meditate on his precepts.

3. Next he works by these upon the passions or affections: which fantasy having inflamed, they violently urge the will and reason; and this according to the nature of the passion, whether fear or hope, sorrow or joy, love or hatred, desire or aversion; but by none does he work so dangerously as by delight, and love, and desire of physical pleasures.

4. Hence he proceeds to infect the will, (upon the simple apprehension of the understanding,) to make it excessively cling to the temporal good, and to neglect its duty in commanding the understanding to meditate on preserving objects, and to call off the thoughts from the forbidden thing: it neglects to rule the thoughts and passions according to its office and natural power.

5. And so he corrupts the understanding itself, first to omit its duty, and then to entertain deceit, and to approve of evil: and so the servant is put into the government, and the commanding powers do but serve it. Reason is blinded by indulgence in physical pleasure and passion, and becomes their servant, and pleads their cause.

By all this it appears, 1. That Satan's first bait is ordinarily some sensible or imaginary good, set up against true spiritual good. 2. That his first assault of the reason and will is to tempt them into a sluggish neglect and neutrality, to omit that restraint of sense, thought, and passion, which was their duty. 3. And that, lastly, he tempts them into actual compliance and committing of the sin: and herein, 1. The bait which he uses with the understanding is still "some seeming truth." And, therefore, his art and work is to color falsehood, and make it seem truth; for this is the deceiving of the mind: and therefore for a sinner to plead his mistake for his excuse, and say, I thought it had been so or so; I thought it had been no sin, or no duty; this is but to confess, and not to excuse: it is but as much as to say, My understanding sinned with my will, and was deceived by the tempter and overcome. 2. And the bait which he uses with the will is always some appearing good: and self-love and love of good is the principle which he abuses, and makes his ground to work upon; as God also uses it in drawing us to good.

III. The succors and auxiliaries of the devil, and his principal means, are these: 1. He does what he can to get a badly tempered body on his side; for as sin let in bodily disorders, so do they much befriend the sin that caused them. A choleric temper will much help him to draw men to passion, malice, murder, cruelty, and revenge. A sanguine and bilious temper mixed will help him to draw men to lust, and filthiness, and levity, and wantonness, and time-wasting pleasures: a sanguine temper mixed with a pituitous much helps him to make men dull, and heedless, and insensible of the great concerns of the soul. A phlegmatic temper helps him to draw people to drowsy sluggishness, and to an idle, slothful life, and so to bad means to maintain it, and to a reluctance toward every work that is good. A healthful temper much helps him to draw people to gluttony, drunkenness, lust, ambition, greed, and neglect of life eternal: a sickly temper helps him to tempt us to peevishness and impatience: and a melancholy temper helps him in all the temptations mentioned just now.

2. He uses his greatest skill to get the greatest fleshly interest on his side: so that it may be a matter of great pleasure, great advancement, and honor, and applause, or great advantage to a man, if he will sin; or a matter of great suffering, and great disgrace, and great loss to him that will not sin, or that will be holy and obedient to God: for fleshly interest being the common matter of all his temptations, his main business is to magnify this as much as may be.

3. He makes very great advantage of the common customs of the country that men live in: this carries away thousands and millions at once. When the common vote and custom are for sin, and against Christ and holiness, particular persons think themselves excused, that they are no wiser or better than all the country about them. And they think they are much the safer for sinning in so great a crowd, and doing but as most men do; and he that contradicts them comes on great disadvantage in their eye, when he is to oppose an army of adversaries, and seems to think himself wiser than so many.

4. Also he is exceedingly industrious to get education on his side; he knows how apt men are to retain the form which they were molded or cast into at first: if he gets the first possession, by actual as well as original sin, he is not easily cast out. Especially when education conspires with common custom, it delivers most of the people and kingdoms of the earth into his hands.

5. Also he is industrious to get the approved doctrine of the teachers of the people on his side. If he can get it to pass once for a revelation or command of God, he will quickly conquer conscience by it, and take down all resistance: he never does war more successfully against God than when he bears the name of God in his colors, and fights against him in his own name. Mahometans, Jews, papists, and all heretics are the trophies and monuments of his victories by this way. Mischief is never so much revered, nor proceeds so successfully, as when it is made a religion! When the devil can charge men to do his business in the name of God, and on pain of damnation, he has got the strongest weapons that ever he can make use of. His ordinary bait is some fleshly pleasure; but he goes high indeed when he presumes to offer the everlasting pleasures; he tempted Christ with all the kingdoms and glory of the world; but he tempts many millions of souls with the offers of the kingdom of heaven itself. For he will offer it to those whom he is endeavoring to keep from it, and make it the bait to draw men from it into the way to hell.

6. He is exceedingly diligent to get the wealth and prosperity of the world on his side; that he may not seem to flatter his servants with empty promises, but to reward them with real happiness and wealth. And then he would make the sinner believe that Christ is the deceiver, and promises a kingdom which none of them ever saw, and which he will not give them; but that he himself will not deceive them, but make good his promises even in this life without delay: for they see with their eyes the things which he promises, and they shall have them at once in possession, to secure them from deceit.

7. He is exceedingly industrious to get common fame and reputation on his side; that he may be able to keep his cause in credit, and to keep the cause of Christ and holiness in disgrace. For he knows how exceedingly prone men are to fall into the way of honor and esteem, and which most men praise; and how reluctant they are to go in the way which is hated and evil spoken of by the most of men.

8. He is very diligent to get the sword and government of kingdoms, and states, and countries, and cities, and corporations into his hands, or on his side; for he knows the multitude of the ignorant and common people are exceedingly prone to be of the religion of those that are able to help or hurt them, and to follow the stronger side; and that the will and example of the ruler is like the first sheet or stamp, which all the rest are printed after. Therefore he will do his worst, to give the greatest power to the most ungodly: if the Turk is the emperor, most of the common people are like quickly to be Turks: if a papist is their king, most of them are likely to be papists. Look into the present state of the heathen, unbelieving, Muslim, papal, and profane parts of the world, and into the history of all ages past, and you will see with grief and astonishment how much the devil has got by this.

9. Also he is very desirous to get our society and companions on his side; who are near us, and have frequent opportunities to do us good or hurt. For he knows by long and great experience how powerfully they draw, and how frequently they succeed.

10. And he is very industrious to get our friends that have power over us, and greatest interest in us, on his side. For then he has won our outworks already.

11. Lastly, he is desirous sometimes to get the name and appearance of virtue and piety on his side; that those that are to do his work may have a winning manner, and so a venerable name, and the cloak of virtue may serve his turn for the promoting of the destruction of piety itself.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **2 Corinthians 10:4 (flagged):** Baxter's "are not carnal" is the King James. The NET has "the weapons of our warfare are not human weapons, but are made powerful by God for tearing down strongholds." Baxter's argument is that the Christian's weapons are not fleshly ones, so we must not use them in rebellion against rulers; "human" is a little broader than "carnal" and loses the flesh/spirit contrast he often leans on. We kept Baxter's quoted words ("carnal," a technical term) and give the NET here.
- **Joshua 7:21 (flagged):** Baxter's quotation is a paraphrase, with his own parenthesis, so we kept his wording, modernized. The NET reads: "I saw among the goods we seized a nice robe from Babylon, 200 silver pieces, and a bar of gold weighing 50 shekels. I wanted them, so I took them." The NET has "I wanted them" where the King James has "I coveted them," so the word "coveted," which Baxter fits into his chain from sight to desire to taking, does not appear in the NET.
- **Unmarked Scripture allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Eve seeing, tasting, and eating (Genesis 3:6); Noah (Genesis 9:20-21), Lot (Genesis 19:30-38), and David (2 Samuel 11) as examples of sinning; God's command to meditate on his precepts (compare Psalm 1:2; Joshua 1:8); the devil's offer of the kingdoms of the world to Christ (compare Matthew 4:8-9). These identifications are ours and not in Baxter's text.
- **carnal:** kept as Baxter's term (see GLOSSARY.md). **fleshly interest, flesh-pleasing:** kept.
- **sensuality, sensual (his words):** now "indulgence in physical pleasure" and "physical pleasures" (by context); **sensitive appetite:** now "sensory appetite"; **sensible** ("sensible or imaginary good"): left, in the old sense "perceived by the senses."
- **fantasy:** kept beside "imagination," as Baxter pairs them. **affections:** kept as a technical term (emotions and desires).
- **preserving objects** (point 4): left as printed; the sense seems to be things that would keep the soul from the forbidden object, but we are not sure.
- **mortal** (heart sins "most mortal"): kept as Baxter's technical word for deadly sins.
- **temper / temperament:** Baxter's "temper" is the bodily constitution of his day's theory of the four humors. His terms "choleric," "sanguine," "bilious," "pituitous," "phlegmatic," and "melancholy" are kept. "Pituitous" and "phlegmatic" are both his words, and he treats them separately. "Ill tempered body" became "badly tempered body" and "distempers" became "disorders." He ties these claims to the medicine of his time; we assert nothing about them.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): holds, strongholds ("holds and helps of sin"); pretence, pretext; inordinately, excessively; spawners (kept); intelligencers, informants; disaffection to, dislike of; unrecoverable, kept; sensuality, see above; covetous, greedy; greaten, magnify; blockish, dull; regardless, heedless; concernments, concerns; commodity, advantage; loth, reluctant; speed, succeed; admiration, astonishment; carriage, manner; felicity, happiness; presently, at once; vulgar, common; reverenced, revered; infidel, unbelieving; Mahometans, Muslims (the first use in point 5 keeps his "Mahometans"; his word was "Mahometan").
- **colors** (as "the devil's colors," "in his colors"): military flags or banners.
- **common vote** (point 3): the common voice or verdict. Left as written.
- **corporations** (point 8): incorporated towns and bodies with a charter. Left as Baxter's word.
- **first sheet or stamp, which all the rest are printed after:** a printing image; kept.
- **papists, Turk, heathen:** his words, kept; this is Baxter's polemic, and the claims about whole nations following their rulers are his own.
- **outworks:** outer defenses of a fortress.
- **Sentence boundaries** were kept. Baxter's lists use "I. II. III." and "1. 2. 3." and we kept them as printed.
- **Roman numerals and source oddities:** "Secondly, As to our defence, I. The ends..." begins a new numbered series inside the "Secondly"; kept as Baxter has it.
- **"holy war":** Baxter's image for the Christian life; the battle language continues through the section.
