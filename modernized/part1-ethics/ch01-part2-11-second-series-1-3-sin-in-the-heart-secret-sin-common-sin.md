# Chapter 1, Part 2: Second Series, Temptations 1–3

*Baxter's second series of ten subordinate Temptations, each answering the excuse that a sin is "only an infirmity." He restarts the numbering at 1. Labels below say "second series" to keep them apart from the first thirty.*

**Temptation 1 (second series).** But here are many subordinate temptations by which Satan persuades them that their sins are but infirmities: one is because their sin is only in the heart, and does not appear in outward deeds: and they take *restraint* for sanctification.

**Direction 1.** Alas! man, the life and reign of sin is in the heart; that is its garrison and throne: the life of sin lies in the prevalence of your lusts within, against the power of reason and will. All outward sins are but acts of obedience to the reigning sin within; and a collecting of tribute for this, which is the king. For this it is that they make provision (Romans 13:14). On this all is consumed (James 4:3). Original sin may be reigning sin (as a king may be born a king). Sin certainly reigns until the soul is converted and born again.

**Temptation 2 (second series).** The devil tells them it is but an infirmity because it is no open, gross, disgraceful sin: it is hard to believe that they are in danger of hell for sins which are accounted small.

**Direction 2.** But do you think it is no mortal, heinous sin to be void of the love of God and holiness? to love the flesh and the world above him? to value earth more than heaven, and do more for it? However they show themselves, these are the great and mortal sins. Sin is not less dangerous for lying secret in the heart. The root and heart are usually unseen. Some kings (as in China, Persia, etc.) keep out of sight for the honor of their majesty. Kings are the spring of government; but actions of state are executed by officers. When you see a man walk, or work, you know that it is something within which is the cause of all. If sin appeared without as it is within, it would lose much of its power and majesty. Then ministers, and friends, and every good man would cast a stone at it; but its secrecy is its peace. The devil himself prevails by keeping out of sight. If he were seen, he would be less obeyed. So it is with the reigning sins of the heart. Pride and greed may be reigning sins, though they do not appear in any notorious, disgraceful course of life. David's hiding his sin, or Rachel her idol, did not make them better. It is a mercy to some men that God permits them to fall into some open, scandalous sin, which may tend to humble them, who would not have been humbled or convinced by heart-sins alone. See Jeremiah 4:14; Hosea 7:6–7. An oven is hottest when it is stopped.

**Temptation 3 (second series).** Satan tells them that these are not unpardoned, reigning sins, because they are common in the world. If all that are as bad as I am must be condemned, they say, God help a great number.

**Direction 3.** But do you not know that reigning sin is much more common than saving holiness? and that the gate is wide, and the way is broad, that leads to destruction, and many go in at it? Salvation is as rare as holiness; and damnation as common as reigning sin, where it is not cured. This sign therefore makes against you.

## Translator's notes

- **No Scripture is quoted in quotation marks.** References only: Romans 13:14; James 4:3; Jeremiah 4:14; Hosea 7:6–7, converted to modern form. Scripture quotations elsewhere are from the NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "the gate is wide, and the way is broad, that leads to destruction, and many go in at it" (Matthew 7:13; the NET reads "the gate is wide and the way is spacious that leads to destruction, and there are many who enter through it"); "cast a stone at it" (John 8:7); David hiding his sin (2 Samuel 11–12); Rachel's idol (Genesis 31:19, 34–35).
- **reigning sin** (throughout): Baxter's term for a sin that rules a person's heart and will, as opposed to an infirmity that the person resists. See the notes to Directions 2 and 30. **mortal:** left in his sense of a sin inconsistent with holiness.
- **"they take *restraint* for sanctification"** (Temptation 1): Baxter's italics. He means they mistake being held back from sinful acts (by fear or circumstance) for being made holy.
- **Original sin may be reigning sin (as a king may be born a king)** (Direction 1): left as written. The point is that sin rules from birth until grace overthrows it.
- **a collecting of tribute:** Baxter's "a gathering tribute." **consumed:** as in James 4:3, "that ye may consume it upon your lusts."
- **greed:** Baxter's "covetousness." **value … more than:** his "set more by … than." **walk:** his "go." **did not make them better:** his "made them not the better."
- **Some kings (as in China, Persia, etc.) keep out of sight** (Direction 2): Baxter's comparison, left as written. His point is that rulers stay hidden to protect their majesty, and so does sin.
- **actions of state are executed by officers** (Direction 2): "executed" here means carried out.
- **scandalous** (Direction 2): in the older sense of causing offense or disgrace in public. **heart-sins:** sins of the heart.
- **An oven is hottest when it is stopped** (Direction 2): a proverb. A closed-up oven burns hotter than an open one; a sin kept secret burns hotter.
- **God help a great number** (Temptation 3): the speaker means that God help the many who would be condemned if all as bad as he is were. Left as written.
