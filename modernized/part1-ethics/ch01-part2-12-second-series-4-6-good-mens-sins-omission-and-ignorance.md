# Chapter 1, Part 2: Second Series, Temptations 4–6

**Temptation 4 (second series).** But, says the tempter, they are such sins as you see good men commit: you play at the same games as they do: you do but what you see them do; and they are pardoned.

**Direction 4.** You must judge the man by his works, and not the works by the man. And there is more to be looked at than the bare matter of an act. A good man and a bad may play at the same game, but not with the same end, nor with the same love of sport, nor so frequently and long to the loss of time. Many drops may wear a stone: many stripes with small twigs may draw blood. Many humble men in a senate have been as powerful as kings: you may have many of these little sins set all together, which plainly make up a carnal life. The power of a sin is more significant than the outward show. A poor man, if he is in the place of a magistrate, may be a ruler. And a sin materially small, and such as better men commit, may be a sin in power and rule with you, and join with others which are greater.

**Temptation 5 (second series).** But, says the tempter, they are but sins of omission, and such are not reigning sins.

**Direction 5.** Sins of omission are always accompanied with some positive, physical desire for created things, which diverts the soul, and causes the omission. And so omission is no small part of the reigning sin. The not using of reason and the will for God, and for the mastering of sensuality, is much of the state of ungodliness in man. Denying God the heart and life is no small sin. God made you to do good, and not only to do no harm: else a stone or corpse would be as good a Christian as you; for they do less harm than you. If sin has a veto in your religion, whether God shall be worshipped and obeyed or not, it is your king: it may show its power as well by commanding you not to pray, and not to consider, and not to read, as in commanding you to be drunk or swear. The wicked are described by omissions: such as "will not seek after God: God is not in all his thoughts" (Psalm 10:4). Such as the nations "that do not acknowledge you" and the peoples "who do not worship you" (Jeremiah 10:25). That have "neither faithfulness nor loyalty," and do not "acknowledge God" (Hosea 4:1). That "feed not, clothe not, visit not" Christ in his members (Matthew 25); that hide their talents (Matthew 25). Indeed, if God does not have your heart, the creature has it; and so it is omission and commission that go together in your reigning sin.

**Temptation 6 (second series).** But, says the tempter, they are but sins of ignorance, and therefore they are not reigning sins: at least you are not certain that they are sins.

**Direction 6.** And indeed do you not know that it is a sin to love the world better than God? and fleshly pleasure better than God's service? and riches better than grace and holiness? and to do more for the body than for the soul, and for earth than for heaven? Are you uncertain whether these are sins? And do you not feel that they are your sins? You cannot claim ignorance for these. But what causes your ignorance? Is it because you would gladly know, and cannot? Do you read, and hear, and study, and inquire, and pray for knowledge, and yet cannot know? Or is it not because you would not know, or do not think it worth the effort to get it; or because you love your sin? And will such willful ignorance as this excuse you? No; it makes your sin the greater. It shows the greater dominion of sin, when it can use you as the Philistines did Samson, put out your eyes, and make a drudge of you; and conquer your reason, and make you believe that evil is good and good is evil. Now it has mastered the principal fortress of your soul, when your understanding is mastered by it. He is reconciled indeed to his enemy who takes him to be a friend. Do you not know that God should have your heart, and heaven should have your chiefest care and diligence; and that you should make the word of God your rule, and your delight, and meditation day and night? If you do not know these things, it is because you would not know them: and it is a miserable case to be given up to a blinded mind! Take care lest at last you commit the most horrible sins, and do not know them to be sins. For such there are that mock at godliness, and persecute Christians and ministers of Christ, and do not know that they do ill; but think they are doing God a service (John 16:2). If a man will make himself drunk, and then kill, and steal, and abuse his neighbors, and say, I knew not that I did ill, it shall not excuse him. This is your case. You are drunk with the love of fleshly pleasure and worldly things, and these carry you so far away that you have neither heart nor time to study the Scriptures, and hear, and think what God says to you, and then say that you did not know.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Psalm 10:4 (departure from the NET):** Baxter quotes the King James, "The wicked, through the pride of his countenance, will not seek after God: God is not in all his thoughts." His point in Direction 5 is that the wicked are known by what they fail to do, and this verse names an omission. The NET reads "The wicked man is so arrogant he always thinks, 'God won't hold me accountable; he doesn't care.'" That puts a different idea (denying accountability) where Baxter's argument needs an omission, so we kept Baxter's quotation, modernized, here and flag it. If you want the NET text used everywhere, say so.
- **Jeremiah 10:25; Hosea 4:1:** NET wording. Baxter's "know not God, and call not on his name" is "do not acknowledge you" and "do not worship you" in the NET. Baxter's "no truth, or mercy, or knowledge of God" is "neither faithfulness nor loyalty … nor do they acknowledge God." Baxter quotes the King James loosely; we kept the extent he quotes.
- **Matthew 25** (twice, Direction 5): Baxter's "feed not, clothe not, visit not" is his paraphrase of the sheep and goats (verses 31–46); "hide their talents" refers to the parable of the talents (verses 14–30). We kept his wording.
- **John 16:2** (Direction 6): cited for "think they are doing God a service." The NET reads "the one who kills you will think he is offering service to God." Baxter paraphrases without quotation marks.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): the Philistines putting out Samson's eyes (Judges 16:21); "evil is good and good is evil" (Isaiah 5:20); "meditation day and night" (Psalm 1:2); "drunk with the love of fleshly pleasure" (compare Ephesians 5:18).
- **materially small** (Direction 4): small in the matter of the act itself, as opposed to small in its power. Baxter's point is that an act can look small and still rule a person. **concur with:** Baxter's "concur with," meaning join with.
- **Many humble men in a senate have been as powerful as kings** (Direction 4): Baxter's "many mean men … as great kings," meaning that many small people acting together can hold the power of a king. "Mean" here means humble or lowly.
- **a veto** (Direction 5): Baxter's "a negative voice," meaning the power to say no to something.
- **physical desire for created things:** Baxter's "sensual affection to the creature." **sensuality:** kept as the older sense of the appetite of the senses.
- **omission and commission** (Direction 5): sins of not doing and sins of doing. Baxter's point is that they go together.
- **gladly:** Baxter's "fain." **claim ignorance:** his "pretend ignorance." **horrible:** his "horrid."
- **the most horrible sins, and do not know them to be sins** (Direction 6): Baxter warns that willful ignorance can end in doing wrong and honestly not recognizing it.
- **horridest:** rendered "most horrible." **worth the effort:** his "worth the pains."
- **blinded mind:** left as written; compare "given over to a reprobate mind" (Romans 1:28).
