# Chapter 1, Part 1: Directions 10 and 11

**Direction 10. When you have found out how grim a condition you are in, consider what there is in sin to make you amends or repair your loss, that should be any hindrance to your conversion.**

Certainly you will not continue for nothing (if you know it to be nothing) in so dangerous and sorrowful a case as this. And yet you do it for that which is much worse than nothing, not considering what you do. Sit down sometimes, and think well what recompense the world or sin will make you for your God, your souls, your hopes, and all, when they are lost and past recovery? Think what it will then avail or comfort you that once you were honored, and had a great estate; that once you fared of the best, and had your delightful cups, and merry hours, and sumptuous attire, and all such pleasures. Think whether this will lessen the horrors of death, or turn aside the wrath of God, or the sentence of your condemnation; or whether it will ease a tormented soul in hell? If not, think how small, and short, and foolish a thing and pleasure it is that you buy so dearly; and what a wise man can see in it that should make it seem worth the joys of heaven, and worth your enduring everlasting torments. What is it that is supposed to be worth all this? Is it the snare of advancement? Is it vexing riches? Is it fooling honors? Is it distracting cares? Is it swinish luxury or lust? Is it beastly pleasures? Or what is it else that you will buy at so wonderfully high a price? O lamentable folly of ungodly men! O foolish sinners! unworthy to see God! and worthy to be miserable! O strangely corrupted heart of man, that can sell his Maker, his Redeemer, and his salvation at so base a price!

**Direction 11. And when you are adding up your account, as you put all that sin and the world will do for you on one side of the scales, so put on the other the comforts both of this life, and of that to come, which you must part with for your sins.**

Search the Scriptures, and consider how happy the saints of God are there described. Think what it is to have a purified, cleansed soul; to be free from the slavery of the flesh and its desires; to have the sensory appetite in subjection to reason, and reason enlightened and set right by faith; to be alive to God, and disposed and enabled to love and serve him; to have access to him in prayer, with boldness and assurance of being heard; to have a sealed pardon of all our sins, and a share in Christ, who will answer for them all and justify us; to be the children of God, and the heirs of heaven; to have peace of conscience, and the joyful hopes of endless joys; to have communion with the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit, and to have that Spirit dwelling in us, and working to our further holiness and joy; to have communion with the saints; and the help and comfort of all God's ordinances, and to be under his many precious promises, and under his protection and provision in his family, and to cast all our care upon him; to delight ourselves daily in the remembrance and renewed experiences of his love, and in our too little knowledge of him, and love for him, and in the knowledge of his Son, and of the mysteries of the gospel; to have all things work together for our good, and to be able with joy to welcome death, and to live as in heaven in the foresight of our everlasting happiness. I would have given you here in order a particular account of the privileges of renewed souls, but that I have already done so much in that in my *Treatise of Conversion* and *Saints' Rest*. This taste may help you to see what you lose while you remain in an unconverted state.

## Translator's notes

- **No Scripture is quoted in quotation marks.** Two phrases are unmarked allusions: "cast all our care upon him" (1 Peter 5:7) and "all things work together for our good" (Romans 8:28). We kept Baxter's wording, modernized. Scripture quotations elsewhere are from the NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **grim** (Direction 10): Baxter's "sad." **hindrance:** his "hinderance."
- **advancement** (Direction 10): Baxter's "preferment," meaning promotion to a higher position. **fooling honors:** "befooling honours," meaning honors that make fools of those who chase them.
- **foolish … thing:** Baxter's "silly … commodity." In his day "silly" meant foolish or feeble, and "commodity" meant any thing of value or merchandise.
- **fared of the best:** lived on the best, ate and drank the best. **delightful cups:** Baxter's "delicious cups," where "delicious" meant "delightful."
- **adding up** (Direction 11): Baxter's "casting up," meaning totaling. **on one side of the scales:** Baxter's "in the one end of the scales."
- **sensory appetite** (Direction 11): Baxter's "sensitive appetite" (see the note on the presuppositions).
- **a share in Christ:** Baxter's "an interest in Christ," meaning a personal stake or claim.
- **desires:** Baxter's "concupiscence."
- ***Treatise of Conversion*** and ***Saints' Rest***: Baxter's own books. *Saints' Rest* is *The Saints' Everlasting Rest* (1650), his best-known work, on the joys of heaven.
