# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XV (part 2 of 4)

*This file begins partway through Direction 1 of Grand Direction 15 (on the duty of praising God), at point 3 of Baxter's numbered reasons for the excellence of the work; it then gives Directions 2, 3, and 4 and ends partway through the Grand Direction. The next file continues it.*

3. The praise of God is the highest and noblest work in itself. (1.) It has the highest object, even the glorious excellencies of God. Thanksgiving is somewhat lower, as having more respect to ourselves and the benefits received; but praise is terminated directly on the perfections of God himself. (2.) It is the work that is most immediately nearest to God, as he is our end: and as the end, as such, is better than all the means set together, as such, so are the final duties about the end greater than all the immediate duties. (3.) It is the work of the most excellent creatures of God, the holy angels: they proclaimed the coming of Christ, by way of praise, "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace among people with whom he is pleased!" (Luke 2:13–14): see Psalm 103:20; 148:2. And as we must be equal to the angels, it must be in equal praising of God, or else it will not be in equality of glory. (4.) It is the work of heaven, the place and state of all perfection; and that is best and highest which is nearest heaven; where "they never rest day or night, saying: 'Holy, Holy, Holy is the Lord God, the All-Powerful, Who was, and who is, and who is still to come!' ... 'You are worthy, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power, since you created all things, and because of your will they existed and were created!'" (Revelation 4:8, 10). "A voice came from the throne, saying: 'Praise our God, all you his servants, and all you who fear him, both the small and the great!'" (Revelation 19:5). "I heard what sounded like the voice of a vast throng, like the roar of many waters and like loud crashes of thunder. They were shouting: 'Hallelujah! For the Lord our God, the All-Powerful, reigns! Let us rejoice and exult and give him glory, because the wedding celebration of the Lamb has come, and his bride has made herself ready'" (Revelation 19:6–7).

4. It suits us, and concerns us greatly, to learn and exercise that work which in heaven we must do forever; and that is, to love and joyfully praise the Lord: for earth is but the place of our apprenticeship for heaven. The preparing works of mortifying repentance must in their place be done; but only as subservient to those which we must always do: when we shall sing the "new song" before the Lamb, "You are worthy ... because you were killed, and at the cost of your own blood you have purchased for God persons from every tribe, language, people, and nation. You have appointed them as a kingdom and priests to serve our God" (Revelation 5:9–10). Therefore the primitive church of believers is described as most like heaven; "... were continually in the temple courts blessing God" (Luke 24:53). "Praise the Lord ... Praise him in the assembly of the godly. Let Israel rejoice in their Creator. Let the people of Zion delight in their King" (Psalm 149:1–2). "Let the godly rejoice because of their vindication ... May the praises of God be in their mouths" (Psalm 149:5–6).

5. Though we are still diseased sinners, and in our warfare, among enemies, dangers, and perplexities, yet praise is seasonable and suitable to our condition here, as the greatest part of our duty, which all the rest must only promote. Do not claim that it is not fit for you because you are sinners, and that humiliation alone is suitable to your state. For the design of your redemption, the tenor of the gospel, and your own condition, engage you to it. Are they not engaged to praise the Lord, that are brought so near him to that end? 1 Peter 2:5, 9;--that are reconciled to him?--to whom he has given and forgiven so much? 1 Timothy 1:15; Titus 3:3, 5; Psalm 103:1–3;--that have so many great and precious promises? 2 Peter 1:4;--that are the temples of the Holy Spirit, who dwells in them, and sanctifies them to God?--that have a Christ interceding for them in the highest? Romans 8:33–34;--that are always safe in the arms of Christ; that are guarded by angels; and devils and enemies forbidden to touch them, further than their Father sees necessary for their good?--that have the Lord for their God? Psalm 33:12; 4:8;--that have his saints for their companions and helpers?--that have so many ordinances to help their souls; and so many creatures and comforts for their bodies?--that live continually upon the plenty of his love?--that have received so much, and are still receiving? Should we not bless him every day with praise, who blesses us every day with benefits? Should we not praise the bridge that we go over?--the friend that we have tried so often? And resolve, as Psalm 145, "Every day I will praise you. I will praise your name continually." "Because experiencing your loyal love is better than life itself, my lips will praise you. For this reason I will praise you while I live; in your name I will lift up my hands" (Psalm 63:3–4). Are they not bound to praise him on earth, that must reign with Christ forever in heaven? Romans 8:17, 33; Revelation 1:5–6; Colossians 1:12; 1 Peter 1:4.

6. The praises of God exercise our highest graces: praise is the very breath of love, and joy, and gratitude: it tends to raise us above ourselves, and make our hearts burn within us, while the glorious name of God is magnified: it has the most pure, and spiritual, and elevating effect upon the soul; and therefore tends most effectively to make us more holy, by the increase of these graces.

7. To be much employed in the praise of God tends exceedingly to the vanquishing of all hurtful doubts, and fears, and sorrows. Joy and praise promote each other. And this it does, (1.) By keeping the soul near to God, and within the warmth of his love and goodness, Psalm 140:13. (2.) By the exercise of love and joy, which are the cordial, reviving, strengthening graces, Psalm 94:19; 116:1. (3.) By dissipating distrustful, vexing thoughts, and diverting the mind to sweeter things, Psalm 104:34. (4.) By keeping off the tempter, who usually is least able to follow us with his troubles, when we are highest in the praises of our God. (5.) By bringing out the evidences of our sincerity into the light, while the chief graces are in exercise, 2 Corinthians 3:18. (6.) And by way of reward from God, who loves the praises of his humblest servants. And here I would commend this experiment, to comfortless, troubled souls, that have not found comfort by long searching after evidences in themselves. Exercise yourselves much in the praises of God: this is a duty that you have no pretext against. Against thanksgiving for his grace, you claim that you do not know that you have received his grace; but to praise him in the excellency of his perfections, his power, and wisdom, and goodness, and mercy, and truth, is the duty of all people in the world. While you are doing this, you will feel your graces stir, and feel that comfort from the face of God, which you are not likely to meet with in any other way whatsoever. Evidences are exceedingly useful to our ordinary, settled peace and comfort; but it is often long before we confidently discern them: and they are often discerned when yet the soul is not excited to much sense of comfort and delight: and we quickly lose the sight of evidences, if we are not very wise and careful. But a life of praise brings comfort to the soul, as standing in the sunshine brings light and warmth: or as laboring warms the body: or as the sight and company of our dearest friend, or the hearing of glad tidings, rejoices the heart, without any great reasoning or arguing the case. This is the way to have comfort by feeling, to be much in the hearty praises of the Lord. When we come to heaven we shall have our joy, by immediate vision, and the delightful exercise of love and praise. And if you would taste the heavenly joys on earth, you must imitate them in heaven as near as possibly you can; and this is your work of nearest imitation.

8. To live a life of praising God will make religion sweet and easy to us, and take off the wearisomeness of it, and make the word of God a pleasure to us. Whereas they that set themselves only to the works of humiliation, and leave out these soul-delighting exercises, cast themselves into exceeding danger, by making religion seem to them a grievous and undesirable life. This makes people reluctant to every duty, and do it heartlessly, and easily yield to temptations of omission and neglect, if not at last fall off through weariness: whereas the soul that is daily employed in the high and holy praises of his God, is always drawn on by encouraging experience, and does all with a willing, ready mind.

9. No duty is more pleasing to God than the cheerful praises of his servants. He loves your prayers, tears, and groans; but your praises much more: and that which pleases God most must be most pleasing to his servants; for to please him is their end: this is the end of all their labor, that "whether present or absent, they may be accepted by him" (2 Corinthians 5:9). So that it is a final enjoying, and therefore a delighting duty.

10. To be much employed in the praises of God will acquaint the world with the nature of true religion, and remove their prejudice, and refute their dishonorable thoughts and accusations of it, and recover the honor of Christ, and his holy ways, and servants. Many are averse to a holy life, because they think that it consists only of melancholy fears or scrupulosity: but who dares open his mouth against the joyful praises of his Maker? I have heard and read of several enemies and murderers, that have broken in upon Christians with an intent to kill them, or carry them away, that finding them on their knees in prayer, and reverencing the work so much as to stay and hear them until they had done, have reverenced the persons also, and departed, and dared not touch the heavenly worshipers of God. This life of praise is a continual pleasure to the soul; clean contrary to a melancholy life. It is refreshing to the spirits, and healthful to the body, which is consumed by cares, and fears, and sorrows. It is the way that yields that "mirth which does good like a medicine, and is a continual feast" (Proverbs 17:22; 15:15). Therefore says the apostle, "Is anyone in good spirits? He should sing praises" (James 5:13). He cannot better exercise mirth than in singing praises to his God. This keeps the soul continually on the wing, desiring still to be nearer God, that it may have more of these delights: and so it overcomes the sense of persecutions and afflictions, and the fears of death, and is a most excellent cordial and companion in the greatest sufferings. Was it not an excellent hearing, to have been a witness of the joy of Paul and Silas, when in the prison and stocks, with their backs sore with scourges, they sang at midnight the praises of the Lord? Acts 16:25; so that all the doors were opened, and all the prisoners' bonds were loosed, who had been their hearers; so great was God's acceptance of their work. Oh that we would do that honor and right to true religion, as to show the world the nature and use of it, by living in the cheerful praises of our God, and did not teach them to blaspheme it, by our misdoings!

I have said the more of the excellency and benefits of this work, because it is one of your best helps to perform it, to know the reasons of it, and how much of your religion, and duty, and comfort consists in it: and the forgetting of this is the common cause that it is so boldly and ordinarily neglected, or done carelessly as it is.

**Direction 2. The keeping of the heart in the admiration and glorifying of God, according to the foregoing directions, is the principal help to the right praising of him with our lips.**

For out of the heart's abundance the mouth will speak: and if the heart does not bear its part, no praise is melodious to God.

**Direction 3. Read much those Scriptures which speak of the praises of God; especially the Psalms: and furnish your memories with store of those holy expressions of the excellencies of God, which he himself has taught you in his word.**

None knows the things of God but the Spirit of God; who teaches us in the Scripture to speak divinely of things divine. No other dialect so well becomes the work of praise. God, who best knows himself, best teaches us how to know and praise him. Every Christian should have a treasury of these sacred materials in his memory, that he may be able at all times, in conference and in worship, to speak of God in the words of God.

**Direction 4. Be much in singing psalms of praise, and that with the most heart-raising cheerfulness and melody; especially in the holy assemblies.**

The melody and the conjunction of many serious, holy souls tends much to elevate the heart. And where it is done intelligibly, reverently, in conjunction with a rational, spiritual, serious worship, the use of musical instruments are not to be objected to or refused; any more than the tunes or melody of the voice.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All quotations below were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where this file begins:** the source range opens at point 3 of Direction 1 (its heading, about conceiving of praise according to its excellence, is in the previous file). The numbered points 3 to 10 keep Baxter's numbers.
- **Luke 2:13–14:** Baxter gives the reference with the quotation ("good will towards men," King James). The NET reads "peace among people with whom he is pleased," which puts the weight on those God favors, not on a general goodwill. Baxter uses the verse only to show the angels praising, so his point is not affected.
- **Revelation 4:8, 10:** Baxter's quoted words from "Thou art worthy" fall in verse 11 (the King James numbers it the same way); his reference "8, 10" covers verse 10, where the speech begins. We keep Baxter's reference. The NET has "Who" capitalized in verse 8, as here. The NET has "the All-Powerful" where the King James has "Almighty," and "because of your will they existed" where Baxter has "for thy pleasure they are and were created": the NET loses the "pleasure" wording.
- **Revelation 19:6–7:** NET "bride" and "wedding celebration" for Baxter's "wife" and "marriage"; "Hallelujah" for his "Alleluiah"; "the Lord our God, the All-Powerful" for "the Lord God omnipotent." Sense unchanged for his point.
- **Revelation 5:9–10 (flag):** Baxter, following the King James, has "hast redeemed us to God ... and hast made us kings and priests unto our God." The NET reads "purchased for God persons from every tribe ..." and "appointed them as a kingdom and priests," in the third person. This changes how directly the verse speaks of "us" singing; Baxter's argument here is only that this is the song of heaven, so it still serves. (The NET's "them" reflects the manuscript reading it follows; we have not checked the textual notes.)
- **Luke 24:53 (flag):** Baxter quotes "With great joy they were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God." In the NET, "with great joy" belongs to verse 52 ("returned to Jerusalem with great joy"), and verse 53 reads only "were continually in the temple courts blessing God." Baxter's "praising and" is not in the NET text. Baxter leans on joy and praise as marks of the early church, so his point is weaker on the NET verse alone, though verse 52 supplies the joy.
- **Psalm 149:1–2, 5–6 (flags):** NET "Praise him in the assembly of the godly" for "congregation of the saints" (so "saints" becomes "godly"); "Creator" for "him that made him"; "delight in their King" for "be joyful in their King." In verse 5 the NET has "rejoice because of their vindication" where Baxter has "be joyful in glory," and in verse 6 the NET has "the praises of God" where Baxter has "the high praises of God." The NET verse 6 goes on with "a two-edged sword in their hands," which Baxter does not quote. Baxter's "therefore" in verse 1 is his own link and is not in the verse.
- **Psalm 145 (flag):** Baxter cites "Psal. cxlv." without a verse, and we add none in the text. The NET wording matches best at verse 2 (not in Baxter's text) ("Every day I will praise you. I will praise your name continually"); the King James has "bless thee" there and "for ever and ever," so Baxter's "bless" (as in his "bless him every day") is lost in the NET.
- **Psalm 63:3–4 (flag):** NET "experiencing your loyal love" for "thy loving-kindness"; NET repeats "praise" where the King James has "praise" and then "bless"; "For this reason" for "thus."
- **Proverbs 17:22; 15:15 (paraphrase):** Baxter blends two verses ("mirth which does good like a medicine, and is a continual feast"). We keep his wording. NET: "A cheerful heart brings good healing" (17:22) and "one with a cheerful heart has a continual feast" (15:15). The NET drops the word "medicine" that Baxter's phrase depends on.
- **James 5:13 (flag):** We give the NET wording, "Is anyone in good spirits? He should sing praises." Baxter, following the King James, has "Is any merry, let him sing psalms." The NET loses the word "psalms" and "merry" (which ties to Baxter's "mirth" in the next sentence), and the verse's first half ("Is anyone among you suffering? He should pray.") is not quoted by Baxter.
- **2 Corinthians 5:9 (flag, paraphrase):** Baxter's quotation, "whether present or absent, they may be accepted of him," is a loose version of the King James ("we may be accepted of him"). We keep his words ("of him" becomes "by him"). The NET reads "whether we are alive or away, we make it our ambition to please him," which changes "accepted" to "please." Baxter's next sentence ("it is a final enjoying") depends on the idea of God's pleasure, which the NET keeps, but the wording about being accepted is gone.
- **Acts 16:25:** Baxter gives only a reference. The NET reads "praying and singing hymns to God," whereas Baxter's text says "sang ... the praises of the Lord." The rest of the story (the doors opened, the bonds loosed) is Baxter's own continuation from Acts 16:26.
- **References only** (converted to modern form, no quotation in Baxter's text): Psalm 103:1–3, 20; 148:2; 140:13; 94:19; 116:1; 104:34; 33:12; 4:8; 1 Peter 2:5, 9; 1 Timothy 1:15; Titus 3:3, 5; 2 Peter 1:4; Romans 8:17, 33–34; Revelation 1:5–6; Colossians 1:12; 1 Peter 1:4; 2 Corinthians 3:18. We have not checked each against its use here. Baxter's "ver. 6, 7" and "ver. 5, 6" are rendered with their chapters.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "equal to the angels" (probably Luke 20:36); "our hearts to burn within us" (probably Luke 24:32); "out of the heart's abundance the mouth will speak" (probably Matthew 12:34); "None knows the things of God but the Spirit of God" (probably 1 Corinthians 2:11). These are our guesses; Baxter gives no references.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): "beseemeth" to "suits"; "concerneth" to "concerns"; "pretend not" to "do not claim"; "pretence against" to "pretext against"; "you pretend that" to "you claim that"; "uncomfortable" (souls) to "comfortless"; "molestations" to "troubles"; "meanest" (servants) to "humblest"; "converse" (of a friend) to "company"; "backward" to "reluctant"; "confute" to "refute"; "dishonourable" to "dishonorable"; "recreating" to "refreshing"; "auditors" to "hearers"; "slubbered over" to "done carelessly" (to slur over a thing); "scrupled" to "objected to"; "men" (in "all men in the world" and "makes men backward") to "people"; "still" (in "is still drawn on") to "always"; "most like to heaven" to "most like heaven"; "stated" (peace) to "settled."
- **Kept as Baxter's words:** mortifying repentance; subservient; humiliation (the work of self-abasement and sorrow for sin); "evidences" (the signs by which a person can see that he has true grace); "cordial" (a heart-reviving, strengthening thing); "melancholy" (see GLOSSARY.md); "scrupulosity" (anxious over-scrupulousness in conscience); "conference" (spiritual conversation); "religion" (worship and devotion); "experiment" (a trial, as of a remedy); "final enjoying" (enjoying God as the end).
- **"Immediate vision":** Baxter's phrase; in his usage "immediate" means direct, without anything in between.
- **"Apprenticeship for heaven"** and **"the bridge that we go over":** Baxter's figures, left as written.
- **Enemies who spared Christians at prayer:** Baxter says "I have heard and read" and gives no names or sources; we have not identified any, and we do not endorse or verify the report.
- **Grammar:** "the use of musical instruments are" (Direction 4) is Baxter's agreement; left as written.
- **Direction headings:** in the source, each of Directions 2 to 4 has its heading sentence, then "--", then the body. We bold the heading and begin the body on a new paragraph.
- **Dated language:** "man" and "men" in the later Directions ("he may be able...") are Baxter's general terms for a person, kept where the sense is clear.
