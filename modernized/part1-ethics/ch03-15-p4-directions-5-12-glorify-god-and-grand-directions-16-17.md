# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 15 (part 4 of 4), with Grand Directions 16 and 17

**Direction 5. It much honors God when the hopes of everlasting joys cause believers to live much more joyfully than the most prosperous worldly people.**

Not with their kind of doting mirth, in empty amusements and pleasures, and foolish talking, and unseemly jests; but in that constant cheerfulness and gladness which suits the heirs of glory. Let it appear to the world that you do hope to live with Christ, and to be equal with the angels. Does a dejected countenance, and a mournful, troubled, and complaining life, express such hopes? or rather tell people that your hopes are small, and that God is a hard master, and his service grievous? Do not dishonor him in this way by your excessive dejection; do not frighten and discourage sinners from the pleasant service of the Lord.

**Direction 6. When Christians live in readiness to die, and can rejoice in the approach of death, and love and long for the day of judgment, when Christ will justify them from the slanders of the world, and will judge them to eternal joys: this is to the glory of God and of our profession.**

When death, which is the king of fears to others, appears as disarmed and conquered to believers; when judgment, which is the terror of others, is their desire; this shows a triumphant faith, and that godliness is not in vain. It must be something above nature that can make a man have "a desire to depart and be with Christ, which is better by far," and "to be away from the body and at home with the Lord," and to "encourage one another" with the mention of the glorious coming of their Lord, and the day when he will judge the world in righteousness (Philippians 1:21, 23; 2 Corinthians 5:8; 1 Thessalonians 4:18; 2 Thessalonians 1:10).

**Direction 7. The humility, and meekness, and patience of Christians much honor God and their holy faith; as pride, and passion, and impatience dishonor him.**

Let people see that the Spirit of God casts down the devilish sin of pride, and makes you like your Master, who humbled himself to assume our flesh, and to the "death on a cross," and to the opposition and reproach of foolish sinners, and "emptied himself," but "endured the cross, disregarding its shame" of being derided, spit upon, and crucified (Philippians 2:7-9; Hebrews 12:2); and stooped to wash the feet of his disciples. It is not arrogance, and lifting up the head, and standing on your terms and on your honor in the world, that honors God. When you are as little children, and as nothing in your own eyes, and do not seek the honor that comes from men, but say, "Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name bring honor" (Psalm 115:1); and are content that your honor decrease and be trodden into the dirt, so that his may increase, and his name be magnified; this is the glorifying of God. So it is when you show the world that you are above the ungovernable passions of men, not by being insensible, but by being "angry and sin not," and by "giving place to wrath," and not resisting and "avenging yourselves" (Romans 12:19); and by being "gentle and humble in heart" (Matthew 11:29). It will appear that you have the wisdom which is "from above," if you are "first pure, then peaceable, gentle, accommodating, full of mercy and good fruit, impartial, and not hypocritical" (James 3:17). "But if you have bitter jealousy and selfishness in your hearts, do not boast and tell lies against the truth," as if this were the wisdom from above which glorifies God; for this wisdom "does not come from above but is earthly, natural, demonic" (James 3:14-15). "The lasting beauty of a gentle and tranquil spirit ... is precious in God's sight" (1 Peter 3:4); an ornament commended to women by the Scripture, which is lovable in the eyes of all.

**Direction 8. It honors God and our profession when you abound in love and good works; loving the godly with a special love, but all men with so much love as makes you earnestly desirous of their welfare, and to love your enemies, and put up with wrongs, and strive to do good to all, and hurt to none.**

To be abundant in love is to be like God, who is love itself (1 John 4:7, 11); and shows that God dwells in us (1 John 4:12). "All men may know that we are Christ's disciples, if we love one another" (John 13:35). This is the "new" and the "great commandment; the fulfillment of the law" (Romans 13:10; John 15:12, 17; 13:34). You will be known to be the "children of your heavenly Father, if you love your enemies, and bless them that curse you, and pray for them that hate and persecute you, and mistreat you" (Matthew 5:44). Do all the good that you possibly can, if you would be like him who does good to the evil, and whose mercies are over all his works. Show the world that you are "his creative work, having been created in Christ Jesus for good works that God prepared beforehand so we can do them" (Ephesians 2:10). "My Father is honored by this, that you bear much fruit" (John 15:8). "Let your light shine before people, so that they can see your good deeds and give honor to your Father in heaven" (Matthew 5:16). "Honor the Lord from your wealth and from the firstfruits of all your crops" (Proverbs 3:9). "For I will honor those who honor me" (1 Samuel 2:30); when barren, worldly hypocrites, who honor God only with their lips and flattering words, will be treated as those who really dishonor him.

**Direction 9. The unity, concord, and peace of Christians glorify God and their profession; when their divisions, contentions, and malicious persecutions of one another heinously dishonor him.**

Men respect that faith and practice which they see us unanimously agree in. And the same men will despise both it and us when they see us fighting among ourselves about it, and hear us in a Babel of confusion, one saying, This is the way, and another, That is it; one saying, Look, here is the true church and worship, and another saying, Look, it is there. Not that one man or a few must make a shoe to fit his own foot, and then say, All who will not dishonor God by discord must wear this shoe: think as I think, and say as I say, or else you are schismatics. But we must all agree in believing and obeying God, and "walking by the same rule so far as we have attained" (Philippians 3:15, 16). "We who are strong ought to bear with the failings of the weak, and not just please ourselves. Let each of us please his neighbor for his good to build him up ... unity with one another in accordance with Christ Jesus, so that together you may with one voice glorify the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. Receive one another, then, just as Christ also received you, to God's glory" (Romans 15:1-2, 5-7).

**Direction 10. Justice, commutative and distributive, private and public, in bargains, and in government, and judgment, honors God and our profession in the eyes of all: when we do no wrong, but do to all men as we would want them to do to us (Matthew 7:12): that no man overreach or defraud his brother in any matter; for the Lord is the avenger of all such (1 Thessalonians 4:6).**

That a man's word be his master, and that we do not lie to one another, nor equivocate or deal subtly and deceitfully, but in plainness and singleness of heart, and in simplicity and godly sincerity, conduct ourselves in the world. Perjured persons and covenant-breakers, who dissolve the bonds of human society, and take the name of God in vain, will find by his vengeance that he does not hold them guiltless.

**Direction 11. It much glorifies God to worship him rationally and purely, in spirit and in truth, according to the glory of his wisdom and goodness; and it dishonors him to be worshiped ignorantly and carnally, with spells, and mimicking, irrational actions, as if he were less wise than serious, grave, understanding men.**

The worshipers of God have great cause to be careful how they behave; lest they meet with the reward of Nadab and Abihu, and God tell them by his judgments that "among the ones close to me I will show myself holy, and in the presence of all the people I will be honored" (Leviticus 10:1-3). The second commandment is enforced by the jealousy of God about his worship. Ignorant, rude, unseemly words, or ungraceful gestures, which tend to raise contempt in the hearers; or levity of speech, which makes men laugh, is abominable in a preacher of the gospel. And so it is to pray irrationally, incoherently, confusedly, with empty repetitions and tautologies, as if men thought to be heard for their babbling over so many words, while there is not so much as an appearance of a well composed, serious, rational, and reverent address of a fervent soul to God. To worship God as the papists do, with images, Agnus Dei's, crucifixes, crossings, spittle, oil, candles, holy water, kissing the pax, counting off beads, praying to the Virgin Mary, and to other saints, repeating the name of Jesus nine times in a breath, and saying such and such sentences so often, praying to God in an unknown tongue, and saying to him they know not what, adoring the consecrated bread as no bread, but the very flesh of Christ himself, choosing the titular saint whose name they will invoke, fasting by feasting upon fish instead of meat, saying so many masses a day, and offering sacrifice for the living and the dead, praying for souls in purgatory, purchasing indulgences from the pope for their deliverance out of purgatory, carrying the supposed bones or other relics of their saints, the pope's canonizing now and then one for a saint, claiming miracles to deceive the people, going on pilgrimages to images, shrines, or relics, offering before the images, with a multitude more of such items of devotion, most heinously dishonor God, and, as the apostle truly says, make unbelievers say, "They are mad" (1 Corinthians 14:23), and that they are "children in understanding," and not "men" (1 Corinthians 14:20). So that it seems one of the greatest hindrances to the conversion of the heathen and Muslim world, and the chief means of confirming them in their unbelief, and making them hate and scorn Christianity, that the Romish, and the eastern, and southern churches, within their view, worship God as dishonorably as they do: as if our God were like a little child who must have pretty toys bought him at the fair, and brought home to please him. Whereas, if the unreformed churches in the east, west, and south were reformed, and had a learned, pious, able ministry, that clearly preached and seriously applied the word of God, and worshiped God with understanding, gravity, reverence, and serious spirituality, and lived a holy, heavenly, mortified, self-denying life, this would be the way to spread Christianity, and win the unbelieving world to Christ.

**Direction 12. If you will glorify God in your lives, you must be above a selfish, private, narrow mind, and must be chiefly set on the public good, and the spreading of the gospel through the world.**

A selfish, private, narrow soul brings little honor to the cause of God: it is always taken up with itself, or imprisoned in a corner, in the dark, to the interest of some sect or party, and does not see how things go in the world: its desires, and prayers, and efforts go no further than it can see or travel. But a larger soul beholds all the earth, and is desirous to know how it goes with the cause and servants of the Lord, and how the gospel gains ground among the unbelieving nations; and such souls are moved by the state of the church a thousand miles off, almost as if it were at hand, as being members of the whole body of Christ, and not only of a sect. They pray for the "hallowing of God's name," and the "coming of his kingdom," and the "doing of his will throughout the earth, as it is in heaven," before they come to their own needs, at least in order of esteem and desire. The prosperity of themselves, or their party or country, does not satisfy them while the church abroad is in distress. They live as those who know that the honor of God is more concerned in the welfare of the whole than in the success of any party against the rest. They pray that the gospel may have free course and be glorified abroad, as it is with them, and the preachers of it be "delivered from perverse and evil people" (2 Thessalonians 3:1, 2). The silencing of ministers, and the suppressing of the interest of Christ and souls, are the most grievous news to them: therefore they pray "for kings and all who are in authority," not for any carnal ends, but that "we may lead a peaceful and quiet life in all godliness and dignity" (1 Timothy 2:1-3). Thus God must be glorified by our lives.

* * * * *

**Grand Direction 16. Let your life on earth be a life lived in heaven, by the constant work of faith and love; even such a faith as makes things future as if now present, and the unseen world as if it were continually open to your sight; and such a love as makes you long to see the glorious face of God, and the glory of your dear Redeemer, and to be taken up with blessed spirits in his perfect, endless love and praise.**

My treatise *The Life of Faith*, and the fourth part of *The Saints' Rest*, being written wholly or mostly to this use, I must refer the reader to them, and say no more of it in this direction.

* * * * *

**Grand Direction 17. As the soul must be carried up to God, and devoted to him, according to all the foregoing directions, so it must be delivered from carnal selfishness, or flesh-pleasing, which is the grand enemy to God and godliness in the world; and from the three great branches of this idolatry, namely the love of pleasures of the flesh, the love of worldly wealth, and the proud desire and love of worldly honor and esteem: and the mortifying of these must be much of the labor of your lives.**

Of this also I have written so much in a *Treatise of Self-denial*, and in another called *The Crucifying of the World by the Cross of Christ*, that I shall now pass over all of it, except what will be more timely later under the more particular directions, in the fourth part, when I come to speak of selfishness, as opposed to the love of others. [133]

* * * * *

I have now given you the general grand directions, containing the very being and life of godliness and Christianity; with those particular sub-directions which are needed for the performance of them. And I must tell you that as your life, and strength, and comfort depend principally on these, so does your success in resisting all your particular sins: and therefore, if you do not first obey these general directions, the more particular ones that follow will be almost useless to you, even as branches cut off from the stock of the tree, which are deprived thereby of their support and life. But on the supposition that you will first maintain these vital parts of your religion, I shall proceed to direct you first in some particulars most nearly subordinate to the forementioned duties, and then to the more remote branches.

## Baxter's notes

English modernized; no Latin or Greek in this note.

[133] I do not pass this over as a small matter, to be passed over by the reader as well. For I take the love of God kindled by faith in Christ, with the full denial of our carnal selves, to be the sum of all religion. But because I would not injure so great a duty by saying only a little of it; and therefore desire the reader, who studies for practice, and needs such helps, to read the mentioned books of *Self-denial* and *Crucifying the World*.

## Translator's notes

- **Where the file begins.** The range opens at the start of Direction 5 (source: "_Direct._ V."), so it is not mid-Direction. These are Directions 5-12 on honoring (glorifying) God by the life, the end of Grand Direction 15; Grand Directions 16 and 17 follow, then Baxter's closing paragraph. Baxter's Roman numerals are rendered in Arabic. The source's run-in "--" after each Direction's heading sentence is replaced by a bold heading, with the rest as body. Baxter prints no margin notes in this range.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org; see NOTICE.md.
- **Philippians 1:21, 23.** Baxter cites only 1:21 (living is Christ, dying is gain). His quoted words ("desire to depart and be with Christ, as best of all") are in 1:23, so we cite "1:21, 23". NET: "I have a desire to depart and be with Christ, which is better by far." Baxter's "as best of all" is KJV-like wording ("which is far better"); the NET is close in sense.
- **2 Corinthians 5:8.** NET: "would prefer to be away from the body and at home with the Lord." Baxter's "absent from the body and present with the Lord" is the KJV; we quote the NET phrase "away from the body and at home with the Lord."
- **1 Thessalonians 4:18.** Baxter's "comfort one another" (KJV) is NET "encourage one another." In 17th-century English "comfort" included "strengthen," so the shift is small.
- **2 Thessalonians 1:10.** Cited by Baxter for the day of Christ's coming; not quoted. "Judge the world in righteousness" is unmarked: it matches Acts 17:31 in the NET ("judge the world in righteousness"); the reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **Philippians 2:7-9 (flag).** Baxter quotes the KJV "made himself of no reputation." NET: "emptied himself," followed by "by taking on the form of a slave." Baxter uses the phrase to stress Christ giving up reputation and honor; the NET wording is about self-emptying, so his point about reputation is not carried by the verse text as strongly. Baxter's "death of the cross" is NET "death on a cross."
- **Hebrews 12:2 (flag).** Baxter has "endured the shame." NET: "endured the cross, disregarding its shame." The KJV "despising the shame" is nearer the NET; Baxter's "endured the shame" leans on suffering, the NET on Christ's disregard of it. We kept the NET words and fitted Baxter's following clause ("of being derided ...") to them without changing its words. "Contradiction and reproach of foolish sinners" echoes Hebrews 12:3 (NET "such opposition against himself by sinners"); the reference is not in Baxter's text. "Contradiction" is swapped for "opposition" (the older sense of speaking against).
- **Foot-washing** (John 13) is an unmarked allusion.
- **Psalm 115:1.** Baxter: "give glory." NET: "bring honor." We use the NET.
- **"Angry and sin not."** Unmarked; it matches the King James of Ephesians 4:26 (and Psalm 4:4 as the KJV renders it). NET Ephesians 4:26: "Be angry and do not sin." We keep Baxter's wording, modernized, because he did not cite it.
- **"Give place to wrath," "avenge yourselves."** Baxter cites Romans 12:19; NET: "Do not avenge yourselves, dear friends, but give place to God's wrath." Our quotation keeps the extent of his short phrases.
- **Matthew 11:29.** Baxter's "meek and lowly in heart" is NET "gentle and humble in heart."
- **James 3:17 (flag).** Baxter's KJV "easy to be entreated" is NET "accommodating"; "without partiality, and hypocrisy" is NET "impartial, and not hypocritical." Meaning is close.
- **James 3:14-15 (flag).** Baxter's KJV "earthly, sensual, and devilish" is NET "earthly, natural, demonic." His "sensual" is the old sense (of the natural, unspiritual life), not mainly the sexual; "natural" in the NET carries that. Baxter's "ver. 14, 15" is kept as 3:14-15. His "glory not, and lie not against the truth" is NET "do not boast and tell lies against the truth." The words "as if this were the wisdom from above which glorifies God" are Baxter's own.
- **1 Peter 3:4 (flag).** Baxter has "A meek and quiet spirit is of great price in the sight of God." NET: "the lasting beauty of a gentle and tranquil spirit, which is precious in God's sight." The NET sentence begins with "but the inner person of the heart"; we quote the part Baxter uses, with "..." for the skipped words. Baxter's "ornament commended to women" matches the context of 1 Peter 3:3-4.
- **1 John 4:7, 11, 12.** Not quoted; Baxter's prose summarizes "love is from God" and "God resides in us." "God, who is love itself" is a paraphrase of 1 John 4:8, 16 (not cited by Baxter).
- **John 13:35 (paraphrase).** Baxter turns "ye" into "we." We keep his wording, modernized. NET: "Everyone will know by this that you are my disciples—if you have love for one another."
- **"New" and "great commandment; the fulfilling of the law" (blend).** Baxter joins John 13:34 ("new commandment"), Matthew 22:38 (the "first and greatest commandment" in the NET; Matthew 22 is not cited by Baxter) and Romans 13:10 (NET: "love is the fulfillment of the law"). We keep his wording, with "fulfilling" modernized to "fulfillment." The "great commandment" wording is the KJV's; the NET has "first and greatest." Baxter's references John 15:12, 17 are carried over as printed.
- **Matthew 5:44 (blend).** Baxter combines Matthew 5:44-45 and Luke 6:27-28 in KJV wording: "bless them that curse you" and "despitefully use you." NET Matthew 5:44 reads only "love your enemy and pray for those who persecute you" (the NET omits the clauses about cursing and mistreating, which are a textual variant in that verse); Matthew 5:45 reads "so that you may be like your Father in heaven." We keep his wording, modernized ("despitefully use" to "mistreat").
- **Matthew 5:45.** "Like him that does good to the evil, and whose mercies are over all his works" echoes Matthew 5:45 and Psalm 145:9 (not cited by Baxter; we have not checked the Psalm wording).
- **Ephesians 2:10 (flag).** Baxter has "his workmanship, created to good works in Christ Jesus, which he hath ordained for you to walk in." NET: "his creative work, having been created in Christ Jesus for good works that God prepared beforehand so we can do them." The NET drops "walk," the image of a way of life; Baxter's "ordained" becomes "prepared beforehand." We also give the NET's "we" rather than Baxter's "you."
- **John 15:8.** Baxter prints "Herein is your Father glorified" (the KJV has "my Father"). We use the NET, "My Father is honored by this," and note the pronoun difference as probably a slip or adaptation by Baxter; we have not seen his source.
- **Matthew 5:16; Proverbs 3:9.** NET wording. Baxter's "substance" and "increase" are KJV; NET has "wealth" and "crops."
- **1 Samuel 2:30 (flag).** Baxter: "those that honour him he will honour," third person. NET: "I will honor those who honor me" (God speaking). We use the NET, so the person changes from "he" to "I"; the sense is the same. The NET continues "but those who despise me will be cursed," which fits his next clause about those who honor God "only with their lips" (an unmarked allusion; Isaiah 29:13 and Matthew 15:8 come to mind, but Baxter gives no reference).
- **"Justice commutative and distributive."** Kept as Baxter's technical terms. As commonly used in his day, commutative justice governs fair dealing between individuals (exchange, contract) and distributive justice the fair giving out of goods and burdens by those in authority. This is the usual scholastic sense; we have not checked Baxter's own definition.
- **"Overreach"** replaces "go beyond" (1 Thessalonians 4:6, where the KJV has "go beyond and defraud"). NET: "violate the rights of his brother or take advantage of him." Baxter's sentence is his own prose, not a quotation, so we did not substitute the NET.
- **"A man's word be his master."** Kept in Baxter's wording; we take it to mean that a man's word should bind him.
- **"Godly sincerity," "singleness of heart"** are unmarked allusions (compare 2 Corinthians 1:12 and Ephesians 6:5 in the KJV; no reference in Baxter's text). "Covenant-breakers" and "holds them not guiltless" echo Romans 1:31 and Exodus 20:7 in the KJV, also unmarked. "Conversation" (conduct) is rendered "conduct" in a nearby clause, here as "conduct ourselves."
- **Leviticus 10:1-3 (flag).** Baxter has "that he will be sanctified in all them that come nigh him, and before all the people he will be glorified." NET: "Among the ones close to me I will show myself holy, and in the presence of all the people I will be honored." "Sanctified" becomes "show myself holy," and "come nigh" becomes "close to me." Baxter's argument (worship must honor God's holiness) is kept.
- **Worship ("rationally and purely").** "Rationally" means in a way suited to understanding, not "logically"; Baxter's own sense. "Carnally" stays. "Mimical" is rendered "mimicking."
- **Polemic against Rome (kept as Baxter's).** The list of "papist" practices and his judgments on them are his own, kept without softening or sharpening. "Papists" is his word for Roman Catholics. His account of what each practice is and means is his view; we have not checked each term. "Agnus Dei's" are, we believe, blessed wax medallions stamped with a lamb; "the pax" is, we believe, a small tablet kissed during Mass; "crossings" means making the sign of the cross. "Dropping beads" is rendered "counting off beads."
- **"Fasting by feasting upon fish instead of flesh"** is Baxter's complaint about abstaining from meat on fast days. "Flesh" is rendered "meat." "The quick and the dead" is "the living and the dead."
- **"Heathen and Muslim world."** Baxter has "heathen and Mahometan world." "Mahometan" is his term (and the usual one in his day) for a Muslim. "Infidel world" becomes "unbelieving world" (see GLOSSARY decision "infidel").
- **1 Corinthians 14:23 (paraphrase; flag).** Baxter's "They are mad" is adapted from the KJV "ye are mad." NET: "will they not say that you have lost your minds?" We keep his words, modernized. In Paul's passage the unbelievers see uninterpreted speaking in tongues; Baxter applies it to Roman worship in Latin.
- **1 Corinthians 14:20 (paraphrase; flag).** Baxter: "children in understanding," and "not men" (KJV "in understanding be men"). NET: "do not be children in your thinking ... in your thinking be mature." "Men" in the KJV means grown-ups; we kept Baxter's "men" in quotation marks as printed, and the NET's "mature" gives the sense.
- **"Romish, and the eastern, and southern churches."** Baxter's phrase; we take "eastern" and "southern" to mean the Eastern Orthodox and other eastern churches and those of Africa, but we have not confirmed what he has in mind.
- **"Pretty toys bought him at the fair"** keeps Baxter's image; "fair" means a market fair.
- **Lord's Prayer (unmarked).** "Hallowing of God's name," "coming of his kingdom," "doing of his will ... as it is in heaven" echo Matthew 6:9-10. Baxter gives no reference; we have kept his wording rather than quote the NET.
- **2 Thessalonians 3:1, 2.** Baxter's "unreasonable and wicked men" is NET "perverse and evil people." "Unreasonable" in the KJV means absurd or perverse; the NET word is closer to that. Baxter's "free course" and "glorified" are KJV for NET "spread quickly" and "honored"; those two phrases are in his prose, not in quotation marks, and are unchanged.
- **1 Timothy 2:1-3.** NET: "for kings and all who are in authority," "that we may lead a peaceful and quiet life in all godliness and dignity." Baxter's "honesty" is the KJV word, meaning honorable conduct; the NET "dignity" gives that sense.
- **Philippians 3:15, 16 (paraphrase; flag).** Baxter's "walking by the same rule so far as we have attained" is the KJV ("let us walk by the same rule"). The NET has no "rule": "let us live up to the standard that we have already attained." The NET's "standard" is close, but Baxter's argument about a shared rule for unity rests on the KJV wording. We keep his words, modernized.
- **Romans 15:1-2, 5-7 (flag).** NET wording, with "..." where Baxter skips. Baxter's "be like-minded one towards another ... with one mind and one mouth" is a command (and "us" in his version); the NET has "may the God of endurance and comfort give you unity with one another ... so that together you may with one voice glorify ..." The NET is a prayer for unity, not an order; "one mind" is "unity" and "one mouth" is "one voice." "Receiving one another, as Christ also received us" is NET "Receive one another, then, just as Christ also received you." The NET also says "bear with the failings" where Baxter has "infirmities," and "to build him up" where he has "edification."
- **Direction numbering and spelling.** Direction 9: "by the ears" (quarreling) is rendered "fighting among ourselves"; "meet" in "make a shoe meet for his own foot" is "to fit." Direction 10: "bargainings" is "bargains." "Subtilly" is "subtly."
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** worldlings, worldly people; beseemeth, suits; inordinate dejectedness, excessive dejection; affright, frighten; honour, honor; stoutness, arrogance; impotent passions, ungovernable passions (in Baxter's day "impotent" could mean lacking self-control); amiable, lovable; put up wrongs, put up with wrongs; study to do good, strive to do good; vain, empty; sports, amusements; unhandsome, ungraceful; invocate, invoke; pretended, supposed; pretending miracles to delude, claiming miracles to deceive; parcels of devotion, items of devotion; infidelity, unbelief; endeavours, efforts; propagate, spread; conversation, life or way of life; viz., namely; anon, later; subdirections, sub-directions.
- **"Profession" (kept).** In Directions 6, 8, and 9 "our profession" means the faith we publicly profess (our confession of Christianity), not a career.
- **Grand Direction 16 ("conversation in heaven").** Baxter's "conversation" echoes the KJV of Philippians 3:20 ("our conversation is in heaven"), where it means citizenship or way of life; the NET has "our citizenship is in heaven." Baxter does not cite the verse here. We render it "a life lived in heaven." His titles *The Life of Faith* and *The Saints' Rest* (fourth part) are as he names them; we have not checked the exact title forms.
- **Grand Direction 17.** "Idolatry" and "the three great branches" (pleasures, wealth, honor) are Baxter's own scheme; "love of sensual pleasures" is rendered "love of pleasures of the flesh." The titles are *Treatise of Self-denial* and *The Crucifying of the World by the Cross of Christ* (Baxter prints them in quotation marks; we use italics).
- **Footnote 133.** The English sentence beginning "But because I would not injure so great a duty ..." never reaches a main verb in the source ("and therefore desire the reader ..."); we kept the grammar as printed and changed only words.
- **Closing paragraph.** "The forementioned duties" is kept as Baxter's wording. "Religion" is kept (see GLOSSARY).
