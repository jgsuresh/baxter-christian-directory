# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 9

**Direction 9. When you have found yourselves in a state of sin and death, understand and consider what a state that is.**

It may be you will think it a tolerable condition, and linger in it as if you were safe; or delay your repentance as if it were a matter of no great haste; unless you open your eyes, and look around you, and see in how slippery a place you stand. Let me name some instances of the misery of an unregenerate, graceless state, and then judge it as the word of God directs you.

1. As long as you are unconverted, you must necessarily be loathsome and abominable to God. [29] His holy nature is irreconcilable to sin, and would be irreconcilable to sinners, if it were not that he can cleanse and purify them. If you knew what sin is, and knew God's holiness, you would understand this much better. Your own aversion to God, and your dislike of the holiness of his laws and servants, might tell you what thoughts he has of you. Scripture says that he hates "all who behave wickedly" (Psalm 5:5). Indeed he takes you for his enemies, and as such he will handle you if you are not converted. I know many persons that are most deeply guilty, especially men of honor and esteem in the world, would scorn to have this title given to themselves; but truly God is not fearful of offending them, nor so tender of their defiled honor as they are of their own, or as they expect the preacher should be. If those are the king's enemies that refuse his government and set up another, then those are the enemies of God, and of the Redeemer, and of the Holy Spirit, that set up the base desires of their flesh, and the honor and prosperity of this world, and the will of man, and refuse the government of God their Creator and Redeemer, and refuse the sanctifying teachings and operations of the Holy Spirit. Read Luke 19:27.

Some think it strange that any men should be called "haters of God;" and I believe you will find it hard to meet that man that will confess it of himself until converting grace or hell constrains him. And indeed if God himself had not charged men with that sin, and called them by that name, we should scarcely have found belief or patience when we had endeavored to convince the world of it. Only entreat the worst of men to repent of hating God, and see how they will take it. Yet they may read that name in Scripture (Romans 1:30; Psalm 81:15; Luke 19:14). Did not the Jews hate Christ, do you think, when they murdered him? and when they hated all his followers for his sake? (Matthew 10:22; Mark 13:13). And does not Christ say, "that they shall be hated for his sake, not only by the Jews, but also by all nations, and all men" (Matthew 24:9; 10:22); even by the "world" (John 17:14; 15:17–19, etc.). And this was a hating "both Christ and his Father" (John 15:23–24). But you will say it is not possible that any man can hate God. I answer, how then did the devils come to hate him? Yes, every ungodly man hates God: indeed no man hates him as good, or as merciful to them; but they hate him as holy and just, as one that will not let them have the pleasure of sin without damning them; as one engaged in justice to cast them into hell if they die without conversion; and as one that has made so pure and strict a law to govern them, and convicts them of sin, and calls them to that repentance and holiness which they hate. Why did the world hate Christ himself? He tells you, "The world cannot hate you, but it hates me because I am testifying about it that its deeds are evil" (John 7:7). And, "this is the basis for judging: that the light has come into the world and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their deeds were evil" (John 3:19). Nay, it is a wonder of blindness that this God-hating world and age should not perceive that they are God-haters, while they hate his servants to the death, and implacably rage against them, and hate his holy ways and kingdom, and bend all their power and influence in most of the kingdoms of the world against his cause and his people upon earth: while the devil fights his battles against Christ through the world by their hands, they will yet confess the devil's malice against God, but deny their own; as if he used their hands without their hearts. Well! poor, wretched worms! instead of denying your enmity to him, lament it, and know that he also takes you for his enemies, and will prove too hard for you when you have done your worst. Read Psalm 2 and tremble, and submit. This is especially the case of persecutors and open enemies; but in their measure also of all that would not have him reign over them. And therefore Christ came to reconcile us to God, and God to us; and it is only the sanctified that are reconciled to him. See Colossians 1:21; Philippians 3:18; 1 Corinthians 15:25; Romans 5:10. "The outlook of the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to the law of God, nor is it able to do so" (Romans 8:7). Note that text well.

2. As long as you are unsanctified, you are unjustified and unpardoned: you are under the guilt of all the sins that you have ever committed: every sinful thought, word, and deed, of which the least deserves hell, is on your account, to be answered for by yourself: and what this signifies, the threats of the law will tell you. See Acts 26:18; Mark 4:12; Colossians 1:14. There is no sin forgiven to an impenitent, unconverted sinner.

*In the margin: Romans 8:9.*

3. And no wonder, when the unconverted have no personal stake in Christ. The pardon and life that is given by God is given in and with the Son: "God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. The one who has the Son has this eternal life; the one who does not have the Son of God does not have this eternal life" (1 John 5:10–12). Until we are members of Christ, we have no part in the pardon and salvation purchased by him: and ungodly sinners are not his members. So that Jesus Christ, who is the hope and life of all his own, does leave you as he found you: and that is not the worst; for,

4. It will be far worse with the impenitent rejecters of the grace of Christ than if they had never heard of a Redeemer. For it cannot be that God, having provided so precious a remedy for sinful, miserable souls, should allow it to be despised and rejected without increased punishment. Was it not enough that you had disobeyed your great Creator, but you must also think lightly of a most gracious Redeemer who offered you pardon, purchased by his blood, if you would but have come to God by him? Indeed, the Savior that you despised shall be himself your Judge, and the grace and mercy which you thought so lightly of shall be the heaviest aggravation of your sin and misery. For "how will we escape if we neglect such a great salvation?" (Hebrews 2:3). "How much greater punishment" (than those who despised the law of Moses) "do you think that person deserves who has contempt for the Son of God" (Hebrews 10:29).

5. The very prayers and sacrifices of the wicked are abominable to God (except such as contain their turning from their wickedness). So that terror arises to you from that which you expect should be your help. See Proverbs 15:8; 21:27; Isaiah 1:13.

6. Your common mercies do but increase your sin and misery (until you return to God): your carnal hearts turn all to sin; "All is pure to those who are pure. But to those who are corrupt and unbelieving, nothing is pure, but both their minds and consciences are corrupted" (Titus 1:15).

7. While you are unsanctified, you are powerless, and dead to any holy, acceptable work: when you should redeem your time, and prepare for eternity, and examine your condition, or pray, or meditate, or do good to others, you have no heart for any such spiritual works: your minds are biased against them (Romans 8:7). And it is not the excusable powerlessness of those who would do good but cannot: but it is the malicious powerlessness of the wicked (the same as that of devils) who cannot do good because they will not; and will not because they have blind, malicious, and ungodly hearts, which makes their sin so much the greater (Titus 1:16).

8. While you have unsanctified hearts, you have at all times the seed and disposition toward every sin; and if you do not commit the worst, it is because some providence restraining the tempter hinders you. No thanks to you that you do not daily commit idolatry, blasphemy, theft, murder, adultery, etc. It is in your hearts to do it when you have but temptation and opportunity; and will be until you are renewed by sanctifying grace.

9. Until you are sanctified you are heirs of death and hell, [30] even under the curse, and condemned already as a matter of law, though judgment has not passed the final sentence. See John 3:18, 19, 36. And nothing is more certain than that you would have been damned and undone forever if you had died before you had been renewed by the Holy Spirit; and that yet this will be your miserable portion if you should die unsanctified. Think, then, what a life you have lived until now? and think what it is to live any longer in such a case, in which if you die, you are certain to be damned. Conversion may save you, but unbelief and self-flattery will not save you from this endless misery (Hebrews 12:14; 2:3; Matthew 25:46).

10. As long as you are unsanctified, you are hastening to this misery: sin is likely to get more rooting; and your hearts to be more hardened, and at enmity with grace; and God more provoked; and the Spirit more grieved; and you are every day nearer to your final doom, when all these things will be more keenly felt, and better understood (2 Timothy 3:13; 2 Peter 2:3).

Thus I have given you a brief account of the case of unrenewed souls, and but a brief one, because I have done it before more fully. (*Treatise of Conversion*.)

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced exactly as in the source, except that Baxter's English is modernized. A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[29] Mira Ciceronis fictio in li. de Universit. p. 358. Atque ille qui recte et honeste curriculum vivendi à natura datum confecerit, ad illud astrum, quo cum aptus fuerit, revertetur. Qui autem immoderate et intemperate vixerit, eum secundus ortus in figuram muliebrem transferet, et si ne tum quidem finem vitiorum faciet, gravius etiam jactabitur, et in suis moribus simillimas figuras pecudum, et ferarum transferetur: neque malorum terminum prius aspiciet, quam illam sequi cœperit conversionem, quam habebat in se, &c. cum ad primam et optimam affectionem animi pervenerit. (A remarkable fiction of Cicero's, in the book *On the Universe*, p. 358. And he who has rightly and honorably completed the course of life given by nature will return to that star to which he was suited. But he who has lived immoderately and intemperately, a second birth will move into a woman's form; and if even then he does not make an end of his vices, he will be tossed about still more heavily, and will be moved into the forms of cattle and wild beasts most like his own character; nor will he see an end of his evils before he begins to follow that revolution which he had within himself, etc., when he has come to the first and best condition of mind.)

[30] Unus gehennæ ignis et in inferno, sed non uno modo omnes excruciat peccatores. Uniuscujusque enim quantum exigit culpa, tantum illic sentitur et pœna: nam sicut hic unus sol non omnia corpora æqualiter calefacit, ita illic unus ignis animas pro qualitate criminum dissimiliter exurit. Hugo Etherianus de Anim. regres. cap. 12. (There is one fire of Gehenna, even in hell, but it does not torment all sinners in the same way. For as much as each one's guilt demands, so much punishment is felt there: for just as here the one sun does not warm all bodies equally, so there the one fire burns souls differently according to the quality of their crimes. Hugh Etherianus, *On the Return of Souls*, chapter 12.) "Idem undique in infernum descensus est," (The descent to the underworld is the same from every direction,) says Anaxagoras (in Laertius) to one that only lamented that he must die in a strange country.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Psalm 5:5:** Baxter quotes the King James, "He hateth all the workers of iniquity." The NET reads "you hate all who behave wickedly," addressed to God, so we quote the NET phrase "all who behave wickedly" inside Baxter's sentence.
- **"haters of God"** (item 1): Baxter's phrase for Romans 1:30, where the NET also has "haters of God."
- **Matthew 24:9 and 10:22; John 15:23–24** (item 1): the quotations "that they shall be hated for his sake, not only by the Jews, but also by all nations, and all men" and "both Christ and his Father" are Baxter's paraphrases and blends of those passages. We kept his wording, modernized, and cited them.
- **John 3:19** (item 1): the King James has "this is the condemnation." The NET reads "this is the basis for judging," which is milder and a less direct match for Baxter's argument about condemnation.
- **Romans 8:7** (item 1 and item 7): Baxter's "the carnal mind is enmity against God" appears in the NET as "the outlook of the flesh is hostile to God." The sense is close; "carnal mind" (the mind-set of the flesh) is a more literal reading of the Greek than the modern ear takes from "outlook."
- **1 John 5:10–12** (item 3): Baxter cites verses 10–12 but quotes 11–12. We kept his citation.
- **Hebrews 10:29** (item 4): the King James, which Baxter quotes, speaks of those who "have trodden under foot the Son of God." The NET reads "who has contempt for the Son of God," which loses the vivid image. We put Baxter's parenthesis "(than those who despised the law of Moses)" between the NET's two halves, as he did.
- **Hebrews 2:3:** the NET changes "you" to "we."
- **powerless / powerlessness** (item 7): Baxter's "impotent / impotency." We avoid "impotent," whose modern meaning is different.
- **personal stake** (item 3): Baxter's "special interest," meaning a particular share or claim. **base desires** (item 1): his "concupiscence." **threats:** his "threatenings."
- **aggravation** (item 4): kept; it means something that makes a matter worse.
- **irreconcilable:** Baxter's "unreconcilable." **truly:** his "verily." **converting grace:** kept.
- **Platonic note [29]:** Baxter's remarkable "fiction" is a passage in Cicero's version of Plato's *Timaeus*, in which souls are reborn as lower creatures. Baxter cites it as a pagan example of the idea of retribution after death. The reference to "the form of a woman" is in the Latin as given.
- **Hugh Etherianus** (note 30): a twelfth-century theologian and Pisan scholar in Constantinople. **Anaxagoras:** a Greek philosopher; the saying is in Diogenes Laertius.
