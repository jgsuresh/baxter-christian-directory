# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XIII (part 3 of 3)

*This section begins at Direction 10 of Grand Direction 13 (on holy delight in God), which Baxter had begun in the previous section; no new Grand Direction heading is introduced here.*

**Direction 10. It is only a life of faith that will be a life of holy, heavenly delight: exercise yourselves, therefore, in believing contemplations of the things unseen.**

It must not be now and then a glance of the eye of the soul toward God, or a seldom greeting, which you would give a stranger; but a walking with him, and frequent addresses of the soul to him, which must help you to the delights which believers find in their communion with him.

**Direction 11. Especially let faith go frequently to heaven for renewed matter of delight, and frequently think what God will be to you there forever, and with what full, everlasting delight he will satiate your souls.**

As heaven is the place of our full delight, so the foresight and foretaste of it is the highest delight which on earth is to be attained. And a soul that is strange to the foresight of heaven will be as strange to the true delights of faith.

**Direction 12. It is a great advantage to holy delight to be much in the more delightful parts of worship; as in thanksgiving and praise, and a due celebration of the sacrament of the body and blood of Christ.**

Of which I have spoken in the foregoing directions.

**Direction 13. A skillful, experienced pastor, who is able to open the treasury of the gospel, and publicly and privately to direct his flock in the work of self-examination and the heavenly exercises of faith, is a great help to Christians' spiritual delight.**

The experiences of believers teach them this: how often do they go away refreshed and revived, who came to the assembly, or to their pastors, in great distress, and almost in despair! See Job 33:23; 2 Corinthians 1:3-4. It is the office and delight of the ministers of Christ to be "helpers of his people's faith and joy," 2 Corinthians 1:24; Philippians 1:4, 25; 1 Thessalonians 2:20.

**Direction 14. Make use of all that prosperity and lawful pleasure which God gives you in outward things, for the increase and advantage of your delight in God.**

Though corrupted nature is more apt to abuse prosperity and earthly delights than any other state, to the diverting of the heart from God; and almost all the devil's poison is given in sugared or gilded enticements; yet the original, natural use of prosperity, of health, and plenty, and honor, and peace, is to lead the mind up to God, and give us a taste of his spiritual delights! That the nearness of the body might be the soul's advantage; and that God, who in this life will be seen by us only in a mirror, and will give out his comforts by his appointed means, might make use of sensory delights for his own reception, and for the communications of his love and pleasure to man: that, as soon as the eye, or ear, or taste perceives the delightfulness of their several objects, the holy soul might at once take the hint and motion, and be carried up to delightful thoughts of him who gives us all these delights. And, doubtless, so far as we can make use of a delight in friends, or food, or health, or homes, or any comforts of our bodies, to further our delight in God, or to remove those melancholy fears or sorrows which would hinder this spiritual delight, it is not only lawful, but our duty to use them, with that moderation which tends to this end.

**Direction 15. Make use of affliction as a great advantage for your purest and unmixed delight in God.**

The servants of Christ usually never have so much of the joy in the Holy Spirit as in their greatest sufferings; especially if they are for his sake. The soul never retires so readily and delightfully to God as when it has no one else that will receive it, or that it can take any comfort from. God comforts us most when he has made us see that no one else can or will relieve us. When all friends have forsaken us except one, that one is sweeter to us then than ever. When all our house is burned down except one room, that room is pleasanter to us than it was before. He who has lost one eye will love the other better than before. In prosperity our delights in God are too often corrupted by a mixture of physical delight; but all that remains when the creature is gone is purely divine.

**Direction 16. Labor by self-examination, deliberately managed under the direction of an able spiritual guide, to settle your souls in the well-grounded persuasion of your special interest in God and heaven; and then do not allow Satan, by his troublesome persistence, to renew your doubts or disturb your peace.**

An orderly, well-guided, diligent self-examination may quickly do much to show you your condition; and if you are convinced that the truth of grace is in you, do not let fears and suspicion pass for reason, and cause you to deny what you cannot deny without the contradicting of your consciences. You do not see the design of the devil in all this: his business is, by making you fear that you have no interest in God, to destroy your delight in him and in his service: and next that, to make you, through weariness, forsake him; and either despair, or turn to physical delights. Foresee and prevent these designs of Satan, and do not allow him at his pleasure to raise new storms of fears and troubles, and draw you to deny your Father's mercies, or to suspect his proved love.

**Direction 17. Do not dampen your delights by willful sin.**

If you grieve your Comforter he will grieve you, or leave you to grieve yourselves: in the measure that any known sin is cherished, delight in God will certainly decay.

**Direction 18. Make use of your observation of wicked men's physical delights, to provoke your souls to delight in God.**

Think with yourselves: Shall hawks, and hounds, and pride, and filthiness, and cards, and dice, and plays, and amusements, and indulgence, and idleness, and foolish talk, or worldly honors, be so delightful to these deluded sinners? and shall not my God and Savior, his love and promises, and the hopes of heaven, be more delightful to me? Is there any comparison between the matter of my delights and theirs?

**Direction 19. Labor to overcome those fears of death which would dampen your joys in the foresight of everlasting joys.**

As nothing feeds holy delights more than the forethoughts of heaven; so there is scarcely anything that more hinders our delight in those forethoughts than the fear of death stepping in between. See what I have written against this fear, in my *Treatise of Self-denial*, and *Saints' Rest*, and in my *Treatise of Death, as the Last Enemy*, and in my *Last Work of a Believer*.

**Direction 20. Do not claim any other religious duties against your delights in God and holiness; but use them all in their proper service to this.**

Penitent sorrow is only a purge to cast out those corruptions which hinder you from relishing your spiritual delights. Use it therefore as medicine, only when there is need; and not for itself, but only to this end; and do not turn it into your ordinary food. Delight in God is the health of your souls: do not say you cannot have time to be healthful, because you must take medicine, or that you take medicine against health, or instead of health, but for your health. So take up no sorrow against your delight in God, or instead of it, but for it, and only so much as promotes it. See the directions for love that precede this.

By this time you may see that holy delight, joined to love, is the principal part of our religion, and that they mistake it who place it in anything else. And therefore how inexcusable are all the ungodly enemies or neglecters of a holy life. If it had been a life of grief and toil, they would have had some excuse; but to flee from pleasure, and refuse delight, and such delight, is inexcusable. Let it be known to you, sinners, that God does not call you to forsake delight, but to accept it; to change your delight in sin and worthless things for delight in him. You dare not say but that this is better: you cannot have your houses and lands forever, nor your lust and indulgence forever; but you may have God forever. And do you hope to live forever with him, and have you no delight in him? Men deal with Christ as the papists deal with the reformed churches: because we reject their formalities and ceremonial trifles, they say we take down all religion. So because we would call men from their brutish pleasures, they say we would let them have no pleasure; for the epicure thinks that when his indulgence, lust, and amusement are gone, all is gone. Call a sluggard from his bed, or a glutton from his feast, to receive a kingdom, and he will complain, if he observes only what you would take from him, and not what you give him in its place. When earthly pleasures end in misery, then who would not wish they had preferred the holy, lasting delights?

*In the margin: For a life of thankfulness.*

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Where the range begins:** it starts at the opening words of Direction 10. Direction numbers are Baxter's own and continue from the previous section of Grand Direction 13. The material ends with Baxter's closing paragraph on the whole subject of delight. The last line of the range (line 18250) is a margin note, "For a life of thankfulness," which stands before the next Grand Direction (on thankfulness); it is kept as the final line. The row of asterisks before it in the source is a section break and is not reproduced. The next section's file begins just after it.
- **2 Corinthians 1:24 (Direction 13):** Baxter puts "helpers of his people's faith and joy" in quotation marks, but this is his own paraphrase of the verses he cites, not a quotation of any one verse. The King James of 1:24 has "helpers of your joy" (as commonly printed; we have not checked it here). The NET reads "I do not mean that we rule over your faith, but we are workers with you for your joy, because by faith you stand firm." It keeps joy and faith, but "helpers" becomes "workers with you," and "faith" is here something Paul does not rule over rather than something he helps. Flagged because Baxter leans on "helpers." We kept his wording in quotation marks.
- **Job 33:23 (Direction 13):** the NET reads "If there is an angel beside him, one mediator out of a thousand, to tell a person what constitutes his uprightness." The King James Version, as we recall it, has "messenger" and "interpreter" (not rechecked here). Baxter reads the verse as describing a minister who guides a distressed person. The NET's "angel ... mediator" is the same word-pair in different terms; Baxter's reading is not changed, but the NET's "mediator" is less obviously a pastor than the King James "interpreter." Flagged as a possible shift.
- **2 Corinthians 1:3-4; Philippians 1:4, 25; 1 Thessalonians 2:20:** cited only by Baxter, not quoted. Checked against the NET: they speak of God's comfort given so that we can comfort others (2 Corinthians), of Paul's joy in prayer for the Philippians and his continuing with them "for the sake of your progress and joy in the faith," and of the Thessalonians as "our glory and joy." They fit Baxter's use.
- **Baxter's references:** "2 Cor. i. 3, 4" became 2 Corinthians 1:3-4; "Phil. i. 4, 25" became Philippians 1:4, 25; "1 Thess. ii. 20" became 1 Thessalonians 2:20. In the Direction 13 text the references follow the quotation, as Baxter placed them, in modern form.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): *glass* to "mirror" (Direction 14; 1 Corinthians 13:12 is probably in mind, but Baxter gives no reference, so we have not cited it in the text); *allectives* to "enticements"; *primitive* to "original"; *sensitive delights* to "sensory delights"; *sensual delight* / *sensual delights* to "physical delight(s)"; *sports* to "amusements"; *luxury* to "indulgence"; *vanity* to "worthless things"; *pretend not* to "do not claim"; *physic* to "medicine"; *purge* kept; *have while to be healthful* to "find time to be healthy"; *beforegoing* to "that precede this"; *suffer not* to "do not allow"; *importunity* to "persistence"; *gainsaying* to "contradicting"; *grudge* to "complain"; *fired down* to "burned down"; *interposing death* to "death stepping in between"; *ceremonious toys* to "ceremonial trifles"; *accommodations* to "comforts"; *molest* to "disturb"; *improve* (Direction 18) to "make use of"; *pretence* to "excuse"; *neighbourhood* to "nearness"; *habitations* to "homes"; *the gainsaying of* to "the contradicting of". Kept as modern words: *satiate*, *foregoing*, *addresses* (turnings of the soul toward God), and *strange to* (unacquainted with).
- **Technical or kept terms:** *interest in God* (a claim to share in God's saving favor) kept; *epicure* (a pleasure-seeker, a follower of luxury) kept; *papists* is Baxter's word for Roman Catholics, kept with his polemic as written. The comparison of "the papists with the reformed churches" is his own argument, left as he wrote it.
- **Titles:** Baxter's quoted titles are given in italics: *Treatise of Self-denial*; *Saints' Rest*; *Treatise of Death, as the Last Enemy*; *Last Work of a Believer*. We have not confirmed their exact published titles and kept them as Baxter wrote them.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "the joy in the Holy Ghost" (compare Romans 14:17; 1 Thessalonians 1:6, probably); "grieve your Comforter" (compare Ephesians 4:30, probably); "see through a glass" (see above). We have not put references into the text.
- **Direction 20 ("the principal part of our religion"):** left as written; Baxter is arguing that delight, joined to love, is the central matter of religion.
- **The image of "hawks, and hounds ... cards, and dice, and plays":** Baxter lists the diversions he has in mind; left as written, with "sports" modernized to "amusements."
