# Chapter 6: Introduction and Title 1, Directions 1–8 (against evil and idle thoughts)

**Chapter 6. Directions for the Government of the Thoughts.**

I have shown you, in my *Treatise of Walking with God*, how much man's thoughts are regarded by God, and should be regarded by himself; and what agents and instruments they are of very much good or evil: this therefore I shall suppose and not repeat; but only direct you in the governing of them. The work having three parts, they must have separate directions. 1. For the avoiding of evil thoughts. 2. For the exercise of good thoughts. 3. For the improvement of good thoughts, that they may be effective.

**Title 1. Directions against evil and idle thoughts.**

**Direction 1. Know which are evil thoughts, and keep such an odious picture of them continually on your minds as may move you always to meet them with abhorrence.**

Evil thoughts are such as these: All thoughts against the being, or attributes, or relations, or honor, or works of God: atheistic and blasphemous, idolatrous and unbelieving thoughts: all thoughts that tend to disobedience or opposition to the will or word of God; and all that savor of unthankfulness, or lack of love to God; or of discontent and distrust, or lack of the fear of God, or that tend to any of these: also sinful, selfish, greedy, proud studies; to make a mere trade of the ministry for gain; to be able to outtalk others; searching into unrevealed, forbidden things; inordinate curiosity, and hasty conceitedness of your own opinions about God's decrees, or obscure prophecies, prodigies, providence, mentioned before about pride of our understandings.

All thoughts against any particular word, or truth, or precept of God, or against any particular duty; against any part of the worship and ordinances of God; that tend to irreverent neglect of the name, or holy day of God: all impious thoughts against public duty, or family duty, or secret duty; and all that would hinder or spoil any one duty: all thoughts of dishonor, contempt, neglect, or disobedience to the authority of higher powers set over us by God, either magistrates, pastors, parents, masters, or any other superiors. All thoughts of pride, self-exalting ambition, self-seeking greed: voluptuous, pleasure-loving thoughts, proceeding from or tending to the corrupt, inordinate pleasures of the flesh: thoughts which are unjust, and tend to the hurt and wrong of others: envious, malicious, reproachful, injurious, contemptuous, wrathful, revengeful thoughts: lustful, wanton, filthy thoughts: drunken, gluttonous, fleshly thoughts: inordinate, worried, fearful, anxious, vexing, unsettling thoughts: presumptuous, and secure, despairing, and dejecting thoughts: slothful, delaying, negligent, and discouraging thoughts: uncharitable, cruel, false, censorious, unmerciful thoughts; and idle, unprofitable thoughts. Hate all these as the devil's spawn.

**Direction 2. Do not be unaware what a great deal of duty or sin is in the thoughts, and of how dangerous a significance and consequence a course of evil thoughts is to your souls.**

They show what a man is, as much as his words or actions do: "For as he thinks in his heart, so is he" (Proverbs 23:7). A good man or evil is named by the good or evil treasure of the heart, though known to men only by the fruits. Oh the vile and numerous sins that are committed in men's thoughts, and proceed from men's thoughts! O the precious time that is lost in idle and other sinful thoughts! Oh the good that is hindered by this both in heart and life! But having spoken of this in the treatise aforementioned, I proceed.

**Direction 3. Above all be sure that you cleanse the fountain, and destroy those sinful inclinations of the heart from which your evil thoughts proceed.**

Otherwise you will strive in vain to stop the streams: or if you should stop them, that very heart itself will be loathsome in the eyes of God. Are your thoughts all upon the world, either coveting, or worrying, or grieving for what you lack, or pleasing yourselves with what you have or hope for? Bring down your deceived valuation of the world; cast it under your feet, and out of your heart; and count all, with Paul, as only loss and dung, for the excellent knowledge of God in Christ: for until the world is dead in you, your worldly thoughts will not be dead; but all will stand still once this weight is taken off: crucify it, and this breath and pulse will cease. So if your thoughts run upon matters of advancement, or honor, disgrace, or contempt, or if you are pleased with your own pre-eminence or applause; mortify your pride, and beg of God a humble, self-denying, contrite heart. For until pride is dead, you will never be quiet for it; but it will stir up swarms of self-exalting and yet self-vexing thoughts, which make you hateful in the eyes of God. So if your thoughts are running out upon your back and belly, what you shall eat or drink, or how to please your appetite or sense; mortify the flesh, and subdue its desires, and master your appetite, and bring them into full obedience to reason, and get a habit of temperance; or else your thoughts will be always upon your guts and throats: for they will obey the ruling power; and a violent passion and desire moves them so powerfully that it is hard for the reason and will to rule them. So if your thoughts are wanton and filthy, you must cleanse that unclean and lustful heart, and get Christ to cast out the unclean spirit, and become chaste within, before you will keep out your unchaste imaginings. So if you have confusion and emptiness in your thoughts, you must get a well-furnished and well-ordered mind and heart before you will well cure the malady of your thoughts.

**Direction 4. Keep at a sufficient distance from those tempting objects which are the fuel and incentives of your evil thoughts.**

Can you expect that the drunkard should rule his thoughts while he is in the alehouse or tavern, and sees the drink? or that the glutton should rule his thoughts while the pleasing dish is in his sight? or that the lustful person should keep his thoughts chaste in the presence of his alluring plaything? or that the wrathful person rule his thoughts among contentious, heated words? or that the proud person rule his thoughts in the midst of honor and applause? Away with this fuel, flee from this infectious air, if you would be safe.

**Direction 5. At least make a covenant with your senses, and keep them in obedience, if you will have obedient thoughts.**

For all know by experience how powerfully the senses move the thoughts. Job says, "I made a covenant with my eyes; how then could I entertain thoughts against a virgin?" Notice how the covenant with his eyes is made the means to rule his thoughts. Pray with David, "Turn my eyes away from what is worthless" (Psalm 119:37). Keep a guard upon your eyes, and ears, and taste, and touch, if you will keep a guard upon your thoughts. Do not let that come into these outer parts which you desire should go no further. Do not open the door to them if you would not let them in.

**Direction 6. Remember how closely related the thought is to the deed; and what a tendency it has toward it.**

Let Christ himself tell you (Matthew 5:22, 28): "But I say to you that anyone who is angry with a brother will be subjected to judgment. ... But I say to you that whoever looks at a woman to desire her has already committed adultery with her in his heart." A malicious thought and a malicious deed are from the same spring, and have the same nature: only the deed is the riper serpent, and can sting another; while the thought is like the younger serpent, that has only the venomous nature in itself. A lustful thought is from the same defiled puddle as actual filthiness: and the thought is only the passage to the action: it is only the same sin in its youth, tending to maturity.

**Direction 7. Keep out, or quickly cast out, all inordinate passions: for passions press the thoughts violently, and forcibly carry them away.**

If anger, or grief, or fear, or any carnal love, or joy, or pleasure is admitted, they will command your thoughts to run out upon their various objects. And when you rebuke your thoughts, and call them in, they will not hear you until you get them out of the crowd and noise of passion. As in the heat of civil wars no government is well exercised in a kingdom; and as violent storms make the sailors unable to steer the ship, and save it and themselves; so passions are too stormy a region for the thoughts to be well governed in. Until your souls are brought back to a calm condition, your thoughts will be in tumult, and hurried whichever way the tempests drive them. Until these wars are ended, your thoughts will be lawless, and partakers in the rebellion.

**Direction 8. Keep your souls in a constant and careful obedience to God.**

Observe his law; be continually aware that you are under his government, and awed by his authority. Man does not judge your thoughts: if you are subject to man only, your thoughts must be ungoverned: but the heart is the first object of God's government, and that which he chiefly regards. His laws extend to all your thoughts; and therefore if you know what obedience to God is, you must know what the obedience of your thoughts to him is; for he that obeys God as God will obey him in one thing as well as another, and will obey him as the governor and judge of thoughts. The powerful, searching word of Christ is a "discerner of the thoughts and intentions of the heart, and as a two-edged sword is sharp and living," and will "pierce" and "cut" as deep as the very "soul and spirit" (Hebrews 4:12, 13). "It casts down every imagination, and brings into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:5). Therefore David says to God, "Examine me, O God, and probe my thoughts. Test me, and know my concerns. See if there is any idolatrous way in me, and lead me in the everlasting way" (Psalm 139:23, 24). And you find God's laws and reproofs extending to the thoughts: Isaiah 59:7, "Their thoughts are sinful." The fool's heart-atheism is rebuked (Psalm 14:1). He rebukes a rebellious people "who lived in a way that is morally unacceptable and who did what they desired" (Isaiah 65:2). See how Christ opens the heart (Matthew 15:9). He charges them (Deuteronomy 15:9), "Be careful lest you entertain the wicked thought," against the mercy which they must show to the poor. In Psalm 49:11 he exposes the "inward thought" of the worldling, that "their houses shall continue forever." In Proverbs 24:9 he says, "A foolish scheme is sin." The old world was condemned because "every inclination of the thoughts of their minds was only evil all the time" (Genesis 6:5). And when God calls a sinner to conversion, he says, "The wicked need to abandon their lifestyle and sinful people their plans. They should return to the Lord, and he will show mercy to them" (Isaiah 55:6, 7). You see then, if you are subject to God, your thoughts must be obedient.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," that is, a heading or main division. Chapter 6 has six Titles; this file covers the chapter's introduction and the first eight Directions of Title 1. Direction 9 continues in the next file.
- ***Treatise of Walking with God*** (introduction): Baxter's quotation marks around the title are rendered as italics. He probably means his treatise on walking with God published within *The Divine Life* (1664); we have not confirmed this. "The treatise aforementioned" in Direction 2 refers to the same work.
- **improvement of good thoughts** (introduction): in Baxter's day "improve" often meant "put to profitable use," not "make better." His third heading is about making good thoughts bear fruit. **separate directions:** his "several directions." **effective:** his "effectual."
- **Proverbs 23:7 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he," and builds on it ("they show what a man is"). The NET reads differently: "for he is like someone who has calculated the cost in his mind" (a stingy host counting the cost of the meal). Baxter's words are not in the NET verse, so we kept his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. Flagged.
- **Job 31:1:** NET wording. Baxter gave no reference; it is Job 31:1 (reference added here only). The King James has "why then should I think upon a maid"; the NET's "entertain thoughts against a virgin" keeps the point about thoughts that Baxter draws out.
- **Psalm 119:37:** NET wording. King James "beholding vanity" becomes "what is worthless."
- **Matthew 5:22, 28 (important):** NET wording, with "..." for the verses Baxter skips. Baxter's King James text has "angry with his brother *without a cause*"; the NET omits "without a cause," following Greek manuscripts that lack the phrase (the NET's own textual notes give the details; we have not reviewed them here). The qualification matters in a passage about which angry thoughts are sinful. The NET also has "to desire her" where the King James has "to lust after her." Flagged.
- **Hebrews 4:12, 13:** Baxter's quotation is a blend and paraphrase of the King James ("a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart," "sharper than any twoedged sword," "quick," "piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit"). We kept his wording, modernized ("quick" to "living"), in his quotation marks. The NET reads: "For the word of God is living and active and sharper than any double-edged sword, piercing even to the point of dividing soul from spirit, and joints from marrow; it is able to judge the desires and thoughts of the heart."
- **2 Corinthians 10:5 (important):** Baxter adapts the King James ("Casting down imaginations ... and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ") and makes "it" (the word of Christ) the subject. We kept his adapted wording, modernized. The NET reads: "and every arrogant obstacle that is raised up against the knowledge of God, and we take every thought captive to make it obey Christ." In the NET the apostles take thoughts captive, and "imaginations" becomes "arrogant obstacle," which moves away from Baxter's theme of thoughts. Flagged.
- **Psalm 139:23, 24:** NET wording. The King James "know my heart; try me, and know my thoughts" becomes "probe my thoughts. Test me, and know my concerns," and "wicked way" becomes "idolatrous way." The word "thoughts," which Baxter needs, survives.
- **Isaiah 59:7:** NET wording. King James "thoughts of iniquity" becomes "Their thoughts are sinful."
- **Isaiah 65:2 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "walketh in a way that was not good, after their own thoughts." The NET reads "who did what they desired," so the word "thoughts," the reason Baxter cites the verse, is lost. We used the NET. Flagged.
- **Matthew 15:9:** cited as in the source. Matthew 15:9 ("they worship me in vain") does not speak of the heart's thoughts; Baxter may have meant Matthew 15:19 ("For out of the heart come evil ideas ..." in the NET; "evil thoughts" in the King James). We have kept his reference; the correction is a guess.
- **Deuteronomy 15:9:** NET wording, for Baxter's King James "Beware that there be not a thought in thy wicked heart." The NET's "the wicked thought" keeps the point about a thought.
- **Psalm 49:11 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "Their inward thought is, that their houses shall continue for ever." The NET reads "Their grave becomes their permanent residence, their eternal dwelling place," following a different reading of the Hebrew; Baxter's words, and the "inward thought" he points to, are not in the NET. We kept his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. Flagged.
- **Proverbs 24:9 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "The thought of foolishness is sin." The NET has "A foolish scheme is sin." The meaning is close, but the word "thought" that Baxter's argument rests on becomes "scheme." We used the NET. Flagged.
- **Genesis 6:5:** NET wording; Baxter's "imaginations of their hearts were only evil continually" becomes "every inclination of the thoughts of their minds was only evil all the time." The NET makes the reference to thoughts even plainer.
- **Isaiah 55:6, 7 (important):** Baxter cites verses 6–7 but quotes verse 7 only. NET wording. The King James "the unrighteous man his thoughts" becomes "sinful people their plans," so the word "thoughts" Baxter leans on is lost. Flagged.
- **Psalm 14:1** (cited only): the fool who says in his heart there is no God; Baxter calls it "heart-atheism."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "count all, with Paul, as only loss and dung, for the excellent knowledge of God in Christ" (Philippians 3:8); "the good or evil treasure of the heart ... known ... by the fruits" (Luke 6:45; Matthew 7:16–20); "what you shall eat or drink" (Matthew 6:31); "cast out the unclean spirit" (for example Mark 1:23–26).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): retain such an odious character, keep such an odious picture; provoke you still, move you always; want of, lack of; covetous / covetousness, greedy / greed; overtalk, outtalk; unreverent, irreverent; mar, spoil; sensual, pleasure-loving; careful (full of cares), worried; vexatious, vexing; discomposing, unsettling; be not insensible, do not be unaware; signification, significance; denominated, named; hereby, by this; caring, worrying; Get down your deceived estimation, Bring down your deceived valuation; preferment, advancement; still (always), always; cogitations, imaginings; vanity, emptiness; well composed, well-ordered; enamouring toy, alluring plaything; passionate words, heated words; fly, flee; potently, powerfully; Mark, Notice; near kin, closely related; minority, youth; several (respective) objects, various objects; disable the mariners to govern the ship, make the sailors unable to steer the ship; reduced (brought back), brought back; tumultuating, in tumult; licentious (unruly, lawless), lawless; sensible, aware; principally, chiefly; detecteth, exposes; reproveth, rebukes.
- **savor of** (Direction 1): kept; it means "have a trace or smell of."
- **prodigies** (Direction 1): kept; it means portents or strange signs taken as omens. "mentioned before about pride of our understandings" refers back to Baxter's earlier treatment of pride of the understanding, somewhere before this chapter; we have not checked the exact place.
- **holy day of God** (Direction 1): kept; Baxter probably means the Lord's Day.
- **secure** (Direction 1): kept as Baxter's term "security," careless confidence about one's spiritual state.
- **this weight is taken off** (Direction 3): Baxter's "poise," the weight that drives a clock; when it is removed the works stop. The image is followed by "breath and pulse."
- **carnal** (Direction 7): kept; unspiritual, ruled by the flesh.
- **Directions numbering:** Baxter's "Direct. I." to "Direct. VIII." become Directions 1–8; the numbering restarts in each Title as he has it.
