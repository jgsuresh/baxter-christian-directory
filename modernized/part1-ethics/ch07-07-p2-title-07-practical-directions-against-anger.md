# Chapter 7: Title 7, against sinful wrath or anger (Objections 5-6 and practical Directions 1-16)

*This section begins partway through Title 7, at Baxter's fifth Objection.*

*Objection 5.* But there are none that will not be angry sometimes; no, not the best of you all.

*Answer.* The sin is never the better because many commit it. And yet, if you do not live where grace is a stranger, you may see that there are many that will not be angry easily, frequently, furiously, nor misbehave themselves in their anger, by railing, or cursing, or swearing, or bad language, or doing wrong to anyone.

*Objection 6.* Does not the apostle say, "Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on the cause of your anger" (Ephesians 4:26)? My wrath is down before the sun; therefore I do not sin.

*Answer.* The apostle never said that anger is never sinful, except when it lasts after sunset. But entertain no sinful anger at all; but if you do, yet quickly quench it, and do not continue in it. Do not be angry without or beyond cause: and when you are, yet do not sin by uncharitableness, or any evil words or deeds, in your anger; nor continue under the justest displeasure, but hasten to be reconciled and to forgive.

These reasons improved may rule your anger.

**Directions practical against sinful anger.**

**Direction 1. The principal help against sinful anger is, in the right habituating of the soul, that you live as under the government of God, with the sense of his authority always upon your hearts, and in the sense of that mercy that has forgiven you, and bears with you, and under the power of his healing and assisting grace, and in the life of charity to God and man.**

Such a heart is continually fortified, and carries its preservatives within itself, as a wrathful man carries his incentives always within him: there is the main cause of wrath or meekness.

**Direction 2. Be sure that you keep a humbled soul, that does not overvalue itself; for humility is patient and does not exaggerate injuries: but a proud man takes all things as heinous or intolerable that are said or done against him.**

He that thinks little of himself thinks little of all that is said or done against himself. But he that magnifies himself does magnify his provocations. Pride is a most impatient sin: there is no pleasing a proud person without a great deal of wit, and care, and diligence. You must come about them as you do about straw or gunpowder with a candle. "With pride comes only contention" (Proverbs 13:10). "He that is of a proud heart stirs up strife" (Proverbs 28:25). "A proud and arrogant person, whose name is 'Scoffer,' acts with overbearing pride" (Proverbs 21:24). "May lying lips be silenced—lips that speak defiantly against the innocent with arrogance and contempt" (Psalm 31:18). Humility, and meekness, and patience live and die together.

**Direction 3. Beware of a worldly, greedy mind; for that sets so much store by earthly things that every loss, or cross, or injury will be able to disquiet him, and inflame his passion.**

Neither neighbor, nor child, nor servant can please a greedy man; every little trespass, or crossing of his interest, touches him to the quick, and makes him impatient.

**Direction 4. Stop your passion in the beginning, before it goes too far.**

It is most easily moderated at first. Watch against the first stirrings of your wrath, and at once command it down: reason and will can do much if you will but use them according to their power. A spark is sooner quenched than a flame; and this serpent is most easily crushed in the egg.

**Direction 5. Command your tongue, and hand, and countenance, if you cannot at once quiet or command your passion.**

And so you will avoid the greatest of the sin, and the passion itself will quickly be stifled for lack of vent. You cannot say that it is not in your power to hold your tongue or hands if you will. Do not only avoid that swearing and cursing which are the marks of the profane, but avoid many words till you are fitter to use them, and avoid expostulations, and contending, and bitter, abusive, cutting speeches, which tend to stir up the wrath of others. And use a mild and gentle speech, which savors of love, and tends to calm the heat that is kindled. "A gentle response turns away anger, but a harsh word stirs up wrath" (Proverbs 15:1). And that which softens and appeases another will much contribute to the appeasing of yourselves.

**Direction 6. At least command yourself into quietness till reason is heard to speak, and while you deliberate.**

Do not be so hasty as not to think what you say or do. A little delay will abate the fury, and give reason time to do its office. "Through patience a ruler can be persuaded, and a soft tongue can break a bone" (Proverbs 25:15). Patience will soothe another's wrath; and if you use it but so long as to stay yourselves a little, till reason is awake, it will soothe your own. And he is a fury, and not a man, that cannot stop while he considers.

**Direction 7. If you cannot more easily quiet or restrain yourselves, go away from the place and company.**

And then you will not be heated by contending words, nor exasperate others by your contending. When you are alone the fire will die down. "Go away from the presence of a foolish man when you do not perceive in him the lips of knowledge" (Proverbs 14:7). You will not stand still and stir in a wasps' nest when you have enraged them.

**Direction 8. Yes, ordinarily avoid much talk, or disputes, or business with angry men, as far as you can without avoiding your duty: and avoid all other occasions and temptations to the sin.**

A man that is in danger of a fever must avoid that which kindles it. Do not come among the infected, if you fear the plague; do not stand in the sun, if you are too hot already. Keep as far as you can from that which most provokes you. [342]

**Direction 9. Do not meditate on injuries or provoking things when you are alone; do not allow your thoughts to feed upon them.**

Else you will be devils to yourselves, and tempt yourselves when you have no one else to tempt you; and will make your solitude as provoking as if you were in company; and you will be angering yourselves by your own imaginations.

**Direction 10. Keep upon your minds the lively thoughts of the exemplary meekness and patience of Jesus Christ; who calls you to learn from him to be "gentle and humble" (Matthew 11:29).**

"When he was maligned, he did not answer back; when he suffered, he threatened no retaliation ... leaving an example for you to follow in his steps" (1 Peter 2:21, 23). Who has pronounced a special "blessing" on the "meek," that "they will inherit the earth" (Matthew 5:5).

**Direction 11. Live as in God's presence; and when your passions grow bold repress them with the reverend name of God, and bid them remember that God and his holy angels see you.**

**Direction 12. Look on others in their passion, and see how unlovely they make themselves, with frowning countenances, and flaming eyes, and threatening, devouring looks, and hurtful inclinations; and think with yourselves whether these are your most desirable patterns.**

**Direction 13. Without any delay confess the sin to those that stand by (if easier means will not repress it); and at once take the shame to yourselves, and shame the sin and honor God.**

This means is in your power if you will; and it will be an excellent, effective means. Say to those that you are angry with, I find a sinful anger kindling in me, and I begin to forget God's presence and my duty, and am tempted to speak provoking words to you, which I know God has forbidden me to do. Such an immediate disclosing of your temptation will break the force of it; and such a speedy confession will stop the fire so that it goes no further; for it will be a pledge upon you in point of honor, even the reputation of your wit and honesty, which will both suffer by it, if you go on in the sin just when you have thus disclosed it by confession. I know there is prudence to be used in this, that you do not do it so as may make you ridiculous, or harden others in their sinful provocations. But with prudence and due caution it is an excellent remedy, which you can use if you are not unwilling.

**Direction 14. If you have let your passion break out to the offense or wrong of anyone, by word or deed, freely and speedily confess it to them, and ask them forgiveness, and warn them to beware of the like sin by your example.**

This will do much to clear your consciences, to preserve your brother, to cure the hurt, and to engage you against the sin hereafter: if you are so proud that you will not do this, say no more that you cannot help it, but that you will not. A good heart will not think this too dear a remedy against any sin.

**Direction 15. Go at once (in the manner that the place allows you) to prayer to God for pardon and grace against the sin.**

Sin will not endure prayer and God's presence. Tell him how apt your peevish hearts are to be kindled into sinful wrath, and entreat him to help you by his sufficient grace, and engage Christ in the cause, who is your Head and Advocate; and then your souls will grow obedient and calm. Even as Paul (2 Corinthians 12:7-9), when he had the thorn in the flesh, prayed three times (as Christ did in his agony), so you must pray, and pray again and again, till you find God's grace sufficient for you.

**Direction 16. Covenant with some faithful friend that is with you to watch over you and rebuke your passions as soon as they begin to appear; and promise them to take it thankfully and in good part; and perform that promise, that you do not discourage them.**

Either you are so far weary of your sin and willing to be rid of it as to be willing to do what you can against it, or you are not: if you are, you can do this much if you please: if you are not, do not claim to repent, and to be willing to be delivered from your sin upon any lawful terms, when it is not so. Remember always, the harmful effects of it make it no contemptible sin. "Do not let yourself be quickly provoked, for anger resides in the lap of fools" (Ecclesiastes 7:9). "Better to be slow to anger than to be a mighty warrior, and one who controls his temper is better than one who captures a city" (Proverbs 16:32). "A quick-tempered person stirs up dissension, but one who is slow to anger calms a quarrel" (Proverbs 15:18). "A person's wisdom has made him slow to anger, and it is his glory to overlook an offense" (Proverbs 19:11).

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** "Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," his name for each of the ten main divisions of Chapter 7 ("Directions for the Government of the Passions"). This file continues Title 7 ("against sinful wrath or anger") from Objection 5 to the end of the Title's practical Directions; the Title's heading and earlier material are in part 1.
- **Labels:** Baxter numbers the Objections with Roman numerals (V, VI) and the practical Directions I-XVI; we give them in Arabic numerals. The practical Directions restart at 1 under the sub-heading "Directions practical against sinful Anger," as in the source.
- **Direction layout:** in the source each Direction's opening sentence runs straight into the rest of its paragraph. Following the project's format, the first sentence is set as the bold Direction line and the following sentences form the body. Directions 11 and 12 are single sentences and have no separate body. In Direction 10 the bold line ends at Baxter's first reference (Matthew 11:29); the body begins with his relative clause "Who being reviled..." (now the NET of 1 Peter 2), and his following sentence "Who has pronounced..." is kept as the fragment he wrote.
- **Ephesians 4:26 (NET flag):** the King James reads "let not the sun go down upon your wrath," and the objector's reply ("My wrath is down before the sun") plays on the word "wrath." The NET has "the cause of your anger." The objector's play on the word is weaker in the NET wording, though Baxter's answer does not depend on it.
- **Proverbs 28:25 (NET flag):** Baxter quotes the King James "He that is of a proud heart stirreth up strife," which is why he uses it in his Direction on pride. The NET reads "The greedy person stirs up dissension," with no mention of pride. Since the word he leans on is not in the NET, we keep Baxter's wording (modernized) and flag it here.
- **Proverbs 14:7 (NET flag):** Baxter quotes the King James "Go from the presence of a foolish man, when thou perceivest not in him the lips of knowledge," which is the basis of his Direction to go away from angry company. The NET reads "Walk abreast with a foolish person, and you do not understand wise counsel" (that is, keeping company with a fool leaves you without knowledge). The NET does not contain the command "go away," which is the point Baxter leans on, so we keep his wording (modernized: thou perceivest to you do not perceive) and flag it here.
- **Matthew 11:29 (NET flag):** King James "meek and lowly"; NET "gentle and humble." Baxter's Direction 10 is about meekness and later ties the verse to Matthew 5:5 ("the meek"), where the NET keeps "meek." The sense is close, but the verbal link between the two verses is lost in the NET. Baxter's "to learn of him to be 'meek and lowly'" is rendered "to learn from him to be 'gentle and humble,'" keeping his construction with the NET's two words. In the NET (as in the King James) the words describe Christ himself ("I am gentle and humble in heart"); Baxter applies them to what we are to learn.
- **Psalm 31:18:** NET "the innocent" where the King James has "the righteous"; Baxter's point (proud and contemptuous speech) is unchanged.
- **1 Peter 2:21, 23:** Baxter quotes verse 23 first, then the end of verse 21, as one sentence; we follow his order with the NET wording and mark the join with "...". NET "threatened no retaliation" for King James "threatened not."
- **Proverbs 13:10; 21:24; 15:1; 25:15; Ecclesiastes 7:9; Proverbs 16:32; 15:18; 19:11:** NET wording. In Direction 5 Baxter's own words just before Proverbs 15:1 ("a mild and gentle speech") match the NET "gentle response" well; the King James had "a soft answer." In Direction 6, Baxter's "Patience will lenify..." follows Proverbs 25:15, where the King James had "long forbearing" and the NET has "patience."
- **2 Corinthians 12:7-9:** cited, not quoted. Baxter's "prick in the flesh" (the King James has "thorn in the flesh") is rendered "thorn in the flesh," the wording of both the King James and the NET, since "prick" now reads oddly. "As Christ did in his agony" alludes to Christ's threefold prayer in Gethsemane (Matthew 26:39-44); the reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **Unmarked allusion:** "God's grace sufficient for you" (Direction 15) echoes 2 Corinthians 12:9 (NET "My grace is enough for you"); kept in Baxter's wording.
- **These reasons improved may rule your anger:** kept. "Improved" means "put to use, applied"; the "reasons" are the considerations against anger given earlier in Title 7 (in part 1).
- **habituating** (Direction 1): kept; it means forming a settled habit or disposition of the soul. **incentives:** kept in its older sense of things that incite or kindle (here, kindle wrath).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): lasteth after sun-setting (lasts after sunset); "but when" (except when); ill language (bad language); still (always, in Directions 1 and 16); forbeareth (bears with); over-valueth (overvalues); aggravateth not (does not exaggerate; in 1673 "aggravate" meant to make heavier or worse, not to annoy); thinks meanly (thinks little); covetous (greedy); setteth so much by (sets so much store by); crossing his commodity (crossing of his interest; "commodity" meant advantage or profit); presently (at once); easiliest crushed in the spawn (most easily crushed in the egg); for want of (for lack of); opprobrious (abusive); savoureth of (savors of); assuage (calm, in Direction 5; die down, in Direction 7); mollifieth (softens); conduce to (contribute to); till reason be heard speak (till reason is heard to speak); lenify (soothe); a little to stay yourselves (to stay yourselves a little); easilier (more easily); suffer not (do not allow); a present opening (an immediate disclosing); opened (disclosed); engagement (pledge); take heed of (beware of); thrice (three times); pretend not (do not claim); mischievous (harmful).
- **Kept words:** "cross" (Direction 3: a setback or vexation); "wit" (Directions 2 and 13: intelligence, good sense, tact); "expostulations" (Direction 5: angry complaints and remonstrances with someone); "a fury" (Direction 6: a raging creature, after the Furies of Greek myth); "reverend name of God" (Direction 11: the name to be revered); "contemptible" (Direction 16: "no contemptible sin" means no small or negligible sin); "entertain" (Objection 6: to harbor or keep).
- **Grammar:** in Direction 16, "the mischievous effects of it do make it to be no contemptible sin" is rendered "the harmful effects of it make it no contemptible sin"; in Direction 13, "that it go no further" is rendered "so that it goes no further"; in Direction 14, "say no more you cannot help it" is rendered "say no more that you cannot help it."
- **Direction 7:** "a wasp's nest" in the source; given as "a wasps' nest" since "them" refers to the wasps.
- **Note 342:** "Thauler." is probably Johannes Tauler, the fourteenth-century German preacher; "flor." probably refers to a collection of extracts (*Flores*) from his works. We have not confirmed the edition Baxter used.
