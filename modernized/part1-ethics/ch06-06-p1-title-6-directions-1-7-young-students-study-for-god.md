# Chapter 6: Title 6, Directions 1-7 (young students: regeneration, the right end, and holiness)

**Title 6. Directions for young students, for the most profitable ordering of their studying thoughts.**

**Direction 1. Let it be your first and most serious study to make sure that you are regenerate, and sanctified by the Holy Spirit, and justified by faith in Christ, and love God above all, as your reconciled Father, and so have right to the heavenly inheritance.**

For, 1. You are nearest to yourselves, and your everlasting happiness is your nearest and your highest interest: what will it profit you to know all the world, and to lose your own souls? to know as much as devils, and be forever miserable with devils?

2. It is a most doleful employment to be all day at work in Satan's chains! to sit studying God and the holy Scriptures, while you are in the power of the devil, and have hearts that are at enmity to the holiness of that God and that Scripture which you are studying! It is a most preposterous and incongruous course of study, if you do not first study your own deliverance. And if you knew your case, and saw your chains, your trembling would disturb your studies.

3. Until you are renewed you study in the dark, and without that internal sight and sense, by which the life, and spirit, and kernel of all that you study must be known. All that the Scripture says of the darkness of a state of sin, and of the illumination of the Spirit, and of the marvelous light of regenerate souls, and of the natural man's not receiving the things of the Spirit, and of the carnal mind that is enmity against God, and is not subject to his law, nor can be; [316] all these and such other passages are not insignificant, but most weighty truths from the Spirit of truth. You have only that light that will show you the shell, and the dead letter, but not the soul, and life-giving sense, of any practical holy truth. As the eye knows food which we never tasted, or as a mere grammarian, or logician, reads a law book, or medical book, (who gather nothing out of them that will save a man's estate or life,) so will you pursue all your studies.

4. You are likely to have but poor success in your studies, when the devil is your master, who hates both you, and the holy things which you are studying. He will blind you, and pervert you, and possess your minds with false notions, and put distracting, pleasure-seeking thoughts into you, and will keep your own souls from being ever the better for it all.

5. You will lack the true end of all right studies, and set up wrong ends; and therefore whatever is the matter of your studies, you are still out of your way, and know nothing rightly, because you do not know it as a means to the true end. (But of this shortly.)

**Direction 2. When you have first laid this foundation, and have the true principle and end of all right studies, be sure that you intend this end in all, even the everlasting sight and love of God, and the promoting of his glory, and pleasing his holy will; and that you never meddle with any studies separated from this end, but as means to it, and as animated by it.**

If every step in your journey is but loss of time and labor which is not directed to your journey's end; and if all that you have to mind or do in the world is only about your end or the means; and all creatures and actions can have no other moral goodness than to be the means of God your ultimate end; then you may easily see that whenever you leave out God as the end of any of your studies, you are but sinning, or doting; for in those studies there can be no moral good, though they may tend to your knowledge of natural good and evil. And when you think you grow wise and learned men, and can dispute and talk of many things, which contribute to your renown, while your "wills do not consent to the wholesome words of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the doctrine which is according to godliness; you are proud, knowing nothing, but doting about questions and quarrels over words, from which come envy, strife, abusive talk, evil suspicions, perverse disputings of men of corrupt minds, supposing that gain is godliness: from such turn away" (1 Timothy 6:3–6). As there is no knowledge but from God, so it is not knowledge but dotage if it does not lead to God.

**Direction 3. See therefore that you choose all your studies according to their tendency to God your end, and use them always under the notion of means, and that you estimate your knowledge by this end, and judge yourselves to know no more indeed than you know of God and for God: and so let practical divinity be the soul of all your studies.**

Therefore, when life is too short for the studies of all things which we desire to know, make sure of the chief things, and prefer those studies which contribute most to your end; do not spend your time on things unprofitable to this end; do not spend your first and chiefest time on things unnecessary to it; for the near connection to God the end is what ennobles the matter of your studies. All true knowledge leads to God; but not all alike: the nearest to him is the best. [317]

**Direction 4. Remember that the chief part of your growth in knowledge is not in knowing many smaller things, of no necessity; but in a growing downwards in a clearer insight into the foundation of the Christian faith, and in taking better rooting than you had at your first believing; and in growing upward into a greater knowledge of God, and into a greater love of him, and heavenly-mindedness, and then in growing up to greater skill, and ability, and readiness to do him service in the world.**

Know as much as you can know of the works of God, and of the languages and customs of the world; but always remember that to know God in Christ better is the growth which you must daily study: and when you know them most, you have still much more need to know better these great things which you know already, than to know more things which you never knew. The roots of faith may still increase, and the branches and fruits of love may be still greater and sweeter! As long as you live, you may still know better the reasons of your religion, (though not better reasons,) and you may know better how to use your knowledge. And whatever you know, let it be that you may be led up to know God more, or love him more, or serve him better.

**Direction 5. With fear and detestation watch and resolve against all carnal, worldly ends; and see that your hearts are not captivated by your fleshly interest; nor grow to a high esteem of the pleasures, or profits, or honors of this world, nor to relish any fleshly comforts, as very pleasant and desirable: but that you rest content with God and the hopes of glory as your satisfying portion, and follow Christ as cross-bearers, denying yourselves, and dead to the world, and resolved and prepared to forsake all for his sake.**

These are words that you can easily say yourselves; but these are things that are so hard to learn that many of the most learned and reverend perish for lack of being better acquainted with them (and I shall never take that man to be wisely learned that has not learned to escape damnation). Christ's cross is to be learned before your alphabet. To impose the cross is quickly learned, but to learn to bear it is the difficulty. To lay the cross on others is to be the followers of Pilate; but to bear it when it is laid on us is to be the followers of Christ. If you grow corrupted with a love of honor, and riches, and promotion, and come to the study of divinity with a fleshly, worldly mind and end, you will but serve Satan while you seem to be seeking after God, and damn your souls among the doctrines and means of salvation, and go to God for materials to chain you faster to the devil, and steal a nail from divinity to fasten your ears to his door. And you little know how Judas's gain will wring and torment the awakened conscience! and how the rust will witness against you, and how it will eat your flesh as fire (James 5:3).

**Direction 6. Digest all that you know, and turn it into holy habits, and expect that success first on yourselves which, if you were to preach, you would expect in others. Remembering that knowing is not the end of knowing; but it is as eating to the body, where health, and strength, and service are the end.**

[318]

Every truth of God is his candle which he sets up for you to work by; it is as food that is for life and action. You lose all the knowledge which ends in knowing. To fill your head and commonplace book is not all that you have to do. But to fortify, and enliven, and inflame your hearts. Good habits are the best provision for a preacher. The habits of mind are better than the best library. But if the habits of heavenly love, and life in the heart, do not accompany them, the heart and life of a preacher and a scholar are still lacking, for all your knowledge. Study Paul's words, 1 Corinthians 8:1, "Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up." If he had said that knowledge builds up others, and love saves ourselves, he would have said nothing that is strange. But even as to building up, love has the precedence.

**Direction 7. Indeed, see that you excel the unlearned as much in holiness as you do in knowledge: unless you will persuade them that your knowledge is a useless, worthless thing; and unless you would be judged as unprofitable servants.**

Every degree of knowledge is for a further degree of holiness: ten talents must be improved to ten more. They that know and do not are beaten with many blows. The devil's scholars look on the godly that are unlearned with hatred and disdain, and preach to their discouragement and disgrace, and strive to set and keep true godliness in the stocks. But Christ's ministers love holiness wherever they see it, and are ashamed to think that the unlearned should be more holy and heavenly than they; and strive to go beyond them as much in the use and ends of knowledge as in knowledge itself; and with Augustine lament that while the unlearned take heaven by violence, the learned are thrust out into hell, as thinking it is their part to know and teach, and other men's to practice.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** "Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," that is, a heading or division. Chapter 6 has six such divisions; this file begins Title 6, the last, addressed to young students. Baxter restarts his Direction numbers at 1 here.
- **1 Timothy 6:3–6 (Direction 2):** Baxter does not quote the verses straight; he turns them into the second person ("your wills consent not … you are proud") and fits them into his own sentence, so we keep his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET of verses 3–5 reads: "If someone spreads false teachings and does not agree with sound words (that is, those of our Lord Jesus Christ) and with the teaching that accords with godliness, he is conceited and understands nothing, but has an unhealthy interest in controversies and verbal disputes. This gives rise to envy, dissension, slanders, evil suspicions, and constant bickering by people corrupted in their minds and deprived of the truth, who suppose that godliness is a way of making a profit." Baxter's closing words "from such turn away" correspond to the King James "from such withdraw thyself" at the end of verse 5, which the NET does not have (it follows a text without those words). His quotation stops at verse 5 though he cites 3–6; we keep his reference. Flagged: Baxter leans on "doting" (the King James for what the NET calls "an unhealthy interest in"), playing on it in "sinning, or doting" before the quotation and "not knowledge but dotage" after it; the NET wording would lose that link, which is one reason we keep his own wording here.
- **doting, dotage:** kept for the wordplay just described. Baxter means foolish, obsessive preoccupation, not fondness (doting) or senility (dotage).
- **1 Corinthians 8:1 (Direction 6), important:** Baxter quotes the King James, "Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth," and then builds on the word "edifieth": "If he had said that knowledge edifieth others, and charity saveth ourselves … But even as to edification charity hath the precedency." We use the NET ("Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up") and carry "builds up" and "love" into Baxter's following sentences ("knowledge builds up others, and love saves ourselves"; "as to building up, love has the precedence") so his argument still reads. His words were "edifieth," "edification," and "charity." Flagged.
- **James 5:3 (Direction 5):** Baxter paraphrases in his own sentence ("how the rust will witness against you, and how it will eat your flesh as fire"), so we keep his wording. The NET reads: "their rust will be a witness against you. It will consume your flesh like fire."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording; references are ours, not in his text): "what will it profit you to know all the world, and to lose your own souls?" (adapting Matthew 16:26); "as much as devils" (compare James 2:19); "marvelous light" (1 Peter 2:9); "the natural man's not receiving the things of the Spirit" (1 Corinthians 2:14); "the carnal mind that is enmity against God, and is not subject to his law, nor can be" (Romans 8:7); "the dead letter" (compare 2 Corinthians 3:6); "follow Christ as cross-bearers, denying yourselves" (Matthew 16:24; Luke 14:27); "forsake all for his sake" (Luke 14:33); "fasten your ears to his door" (the servant's ear pierced at the door, Exodus 21:6, here turned to service of the devil); "Judas's gain" (Matthew 26:14–16; 27:3–5); "unprofitable servants" (Matthew 25:30; Luke 17:10); "ten talents must be improved to ten more" (the parable of the talents, Matthew 25:14–30, where the servant with five talents gains five more; Baxter's "ten" is his own figure, and compare Luke 19:16); "beaten with many blows" (Luke 12:47); "take heaven by violence" (Matthew 11:12).
- **Augustine** (Direction 7): Baxter's "Austin," the usual English name for Augustine of Hippo. The lament is probably the one in Augustine's *Confessions*, Book 8, where he says the unlearned rise and take heaven while the learned wallow in flesh and blood; we have not checked Baxter's exact source.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): Holy Ghost, Holy Spirit; for ever, forever; till, until; meat, food; physic book, medical book; prosecute, pursue; ill success, poor success; like to have, likely to have; conceits, notions; diverting, distracting; sensual, pleasure-seeking; want (lack), lack; anon, shortly; thereto / thereby, to it / by it; make to (your renown, your end), contribute to; railing, abusive talk; evil surmisings, evil suspicions; strifes of words, quarrels over words; still (remember), always; so hardly learned, so hard to learn; preferment, promotion; take up with, rest content with; accommodations, comforts; gripe, wring; quicken, enliven; concur, accompany them; stripes, blows; connexion, connection; charity, love (see 1 Corinthians 8:1 above).
- **most considerable truths** (item 3): rendered "most weighty truths." Baxter's "considerable" means worth considering, not large.
- **quickening sense** (item 3): rendered "life-giving sense."
- **preposterous** (item 2): kept; in Baxter's day it still carried its literal sense, putting last what should come first (the backwards order of studying God before seeing to one's own deliverance), as well as absurd.
- **doleful:** kept (mournful, wretched).
- **estate** (item 3): property or fortune, as in "save a man's estate or life."
- **divinity, practical divinity:** Baxter's technical term for theology, and for theology as it bears on life and practice. Kept.
- **commonplace book** (Direction 6): kept; a notebook in which a student copied out quotations and notes under headings.
- **in the stocks** (Direction 7): kept; the wooden frame used to hold and publicly shame offenders.
- **natural good and evil** (Direction 2): Baxter distinguishes it from moral good; kept as written.
- **Note [316]:** a list of references, converted to modern form.
- **Note [317]:** Cicero, *De Natura Deorum* (On the Nature of the Gods); the passage appears to come from the opening of Book 1, but "page 5" refers to whatever edition Baxter used and we have not identified it.
- **Note [318]:** "Paul. Scalig." is probably Paulus Scaliger (Paul Skalić), a sixteenth-century writer; we have not confirmed the work "Thes." (perhaps a *Thesaurus*) or checked the quotation. "dedicere" as printed is probably a misprint for "dediscere" (to unlearn), and "loquitari" (to chatter) is a rare word; the translation assumes both readings. Kept exactly as printed.
- **Reverend** (Direction 5): "the most learned and reverend" means the most respected clergy; kept.
