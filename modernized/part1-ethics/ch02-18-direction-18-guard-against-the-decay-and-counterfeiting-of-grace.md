# Chapter 2: Direction 18

**Direction 18. Watch diligently both against the more noticeable decays of grace, and against the degenerating of it into some carnal affections, or something counterfeit, and of another kind. And so also of religious duties.**

We are no sooner warmed with the celestial flames, but natural corruption is inclining us to grow cold; like hot water, which loses its heat by degrees, unless the fire is continually kept under it. Who does not feel that as soon as in a sermon, or prayer, or holy meditation, his heart has got a little heat, as soon as it is gone, it is prone to its former earthly temper, and by a little remissness in our duty, or thoughts, or business about the world, we at once grow cold and dull again. Be watchful, therefore, lest it decline too far. Be frequent in the means that must preserve you from declining: when faintness tells you that your stomach is emptied of the former food, supply it with another, lest strength abate. You are rowing against the stream of fleshly interest and inclinations; and therefore do not stop too long, lest you go faster down by your ease, than you get up by labor.

*In the margin: How grace may degenerate.*

The degenerating of grace is a way of backsliding, very common, and too little observed. It is, when good affections do not directly cool, but turn into some carnal affections somewhat like them, but of another kind: as, if the body of a man, instead of dying, should receive the life or soul of a beast, instead of the rational, human soul. For instance: 1. Have you believed in God, and in Jesus Christ, and loved him accordingly? You will seem to do so still as much as formerly, when your corrupted minds have received some false representation of him; and so it is indeed another thing that you thus corruptly believe and love. 2. Have you been fervent in prayer? You will be fervent still; if Satan can but corrupt your prayers, by corrupting your judgment or affections, and get you to think that to be the cause of God, which is against him; and that to be against him, which he commands; and those to be the troublemakers of the church, who are its best and most faithful members: turn but your prayers against the cause and people of God by your mistake, and you may pray as fervently against them as you will. The same I may say of preaching, and conference, and zeal: corrupt them once, and turn them against God, and Satan will join with you for zealous and frequent preaching, or conference, or disputes. 3. Do you have a confidence in Christ and his promise for your salvation? Take heed lest it turn into carnal security, and a persuasion of your good condition upon ill grounds, or you do not know why. 4. Do you have the hope of glory? Take heed lest it turn into a careless recklessness of your soul, or the mere laying aside of fear and cautious suspicion of yourselves. 5. Do you have a love for those who fear the Lord? Watch your hearts, lest it degenerate into a carnal or a partial love. Many careless young persons of different sexes, at first love each other with an honest, chaste, and pious love; but unwisely using too much familiarity, before they were well aware it has turned into a fleshly love, which has proved their snare, and drawn them further into sin or trouble. Many have honored those who fear the Lord, who unnoticed have declined to honor only those of them who were eminent in wealth and worldly honor, or who were esteemed for their abilities or position by others, and little honored the humble, poor, obscure Christians, who were at least as good as they: forgetting that "what is highly prized among men is utterly detestable in God's sight" (Luke 16:15); and that God values not men by their positions and dignities in the world, but by their graces and holiness of life. Abundance who at first did seem to love all Christians, as such, as far as anything of Christ appeared in them, have first fallen into some sect, and and over-admiring their party, and have thought little of others as good as they, and censured them as unsound, and then withdrawn their special love, and confined it to their party, or to some few; and yet thought that they loved the godly as much as ever, when it was degenerate into a factious love. 6. Are you zealous for God, and truth, and holiness, and against the errors and sins of others? Take heed lest you lose it, while you think it is increasing in you. Nothing is more apt to degenerate than zeal: in how many thousands has it turned from an innocent, charitable, peaceable, tractable, healing, profitable, heavenly zeal, into a partial zeal for some party, or opinions of their own; and into a fierce, censorious, uncharitable, scandalous, turbulent, disobedient, unruly, hurting, and destroying zeal, ready to wish for fire from heaven, and kindling contention, confusion, and every evil work. Read well James 3:7. So if you are meek or patient, take heed lest it degenerate into stupidity or contempt of those you suffer by. To be patient is not to be merely insensible of the affliction; but by the power of faith to bear the sense of it, as overruled by things of greater moment.

How apt men are to corrupt and debase all duties of religion, is too visible in the face of the far greatest part of the Christian world. Throughout both the eastern and the western churches, the papists, the Greeks, the Armenians, the Abassines, and too many others, (though the essentials of religion through God's mercy are retained, yet,) how much is the face of religion altered from what it was in the days of the apostles! The ancient simplicity of doctrine is turned into abundance of new or private opinions, introduced as necessary articles of religion: and, alas, how many of them false! So that Christians, being too proud to accept the ancient test of Christianity, cannot now agree among themselves what a Christian is, and who is to be esteemed a Christian; and so they deny one another to be Christians, and destroy their charity to each other, and divide the church, and make themselves a scorn by their divisions to the unbelieving world: and thus the primitive unity, charity, and peace is partly destroyed, and partly degenerate into the unity, charity, and peace of several sects among themselves. The primitive simplicity in government and discipline is with most turned into a forcible secular government, exercised to advance one man above others, and to satisfy his will and lusts, and make him the rule of other men's lives, and to suppress the power and spirituality of religion in the world. The primitive simplicity of worship is turned into such a mask of ceremony, and such a task of formalities and bodily exercise, that if one of the apostolic Christians should come among them, he would scarcely think that this is the same employment which the church was formerly exercised in, or scarcely know religion in this grotesque dress. So that the lovable, glorious face of Christianity is so spotted and defiled, that it is hidden from the unbelieving world, and they laugh at it as irrational, or think it to be but like their own: and the principal hindrance of the conversion of heathens, Muslims, and other unbelievers, is the corruption and deformity of the churches that are near them, or should be the instruments of their conversion. And the most likely way to the conversion of those nations, is the true reformation of the churches, both in east and west: which, if they were restored to the ancient spirituality, rationality, and simplicity of doctrine, discipline, and worship; and lived in charity, humility, and holiness, as those whose hearts and conduct are in heaven, with all worldly glory and honor as under their feet; they would then be so illustrious and lovable in the eyes even of heathens and other unbelievers, that many would flock into the church of Christ, and desire to be such as they: and their light would so shine before these men, that they would see their good works, and glorify their heavenly Father, and embrace their faith.

The commonest way of the degenerating of all religious duties is into this dead formality, or lifeless image of religion. If the devil can but get you to cast off the spirituality and life of duty, he will give you leave to seem very devout, and make much fuss with outward actions, words, and beads; and you will have so much zeal for a dead religion, or the corpse of worship, as will make you think that it is indeed alive. By all means take heed of this turning the worship of God into lip-service. The commonest cause of it is a carnality of mind (fleshly men will think best of the most fleshly religion); or else a slothfulness in duty, which will make you sit down with the easiest part. It is the work of a saint, and a diligent saint, to keep the soul itself both regularly and vigorously employed with God. But to say over certain words by rote, and to lift up the hands and eyes, is easy: and hypocrites, who are conscious that they are void of the life and spirituality of worship, do think to make all up with this formality, and quiet their consciences, and delude their souls with a handsome image. Of this I have spoken more fully in a book called *The Vain Religion of the Formal Hypocrite*.

Yet do not run here into the contrary extreme, as to think that the body must not worship God as well as the soul, or that the decent and edifying determination of the outward circumstances of religion, and the right ordering of worship, is a needless thing, or sinful; or that a form of prayer in itself, or when imposed, is unlawful: but let the soul and body of religion go together, and the alterable adjuncts be used, as things alterable, while the life of holiness is still kept up.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). The NET text of Luke 16:15 was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Luke 16:15:** Baxter's King James quotation ("the things that are highly esteemed among men, are abomination in the sight of God") is given in the NET wording, keeping the extent he quotes. The sense he leans on is unchanged: the NET's "utterly detestable" renders the same idea as "abomination."
- **James 3:7** ("Read well James iii. 7"): reference kept as Baxter printed it, with the doubt flagged. In the NET, James 3:7 is about humankind taming animals, birds, reptiles, and sea creatures, which does not obviously bear on zeal. The sentence is about zeal turning into a "turbulent" and "unruly" thing, and James 3:13-18 (especially 3:16-17, on jealousy and "every evil practice" against the wisdom from above that is "peaceable, gentle") fits it better. A misprint for 3:17 is possible, but we have not confirmed it. We did not change the reference.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "ready to wish for fire from heaven" (Luke 9:54, where the disciples ask whether to call down fire on a Samaritan village); "every evil work" (compare James 3:16); "their light would so shine before these men, that they would see their good works, and glorify their heavenly Father" (Matthew 5:16); "lip-service" (compare Isaiah 29:13).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): loseth/feeleth/telleth/commandeth etc. to modern verb forms; presently to at once; meat (food) to food; intermit to stop; labour to labor; venturousness to recklessness; cautelous to cautious; unheedful to careless; imprudently to unwisely; insensibly to unnoticed; parts or place to abilities or position; set light by to thought little of; troublers to troublemakers; discernible to noticeable; celestial and remissness kept; good estate (condition) to good condition; "Take heed" kept; largely (fully) to fully; venturousness to recklessness ("of your soul" kept as Baxter wrote it, meaning risking the soul); reasonable (soul) to rational; ado to fuss; antic (dress) to grotesque; amiable to lovable; probablest to most likely; scarce to scarcely; hinderance to hindrance; infidel world to unbelieving world; Mahometans to Muslims; conversations (conduct) to conduct; "of the apostolical christians" to "of the apostolic Christians."
- **Kept as Baxter's words:** *carnal* and *fleshly* (see GLOSSARY.md); *affections* (emotions and desires); *carnal security* (careless confidence; Baxter's technical use of "security"); *conference* (religious conversation among believers); *zeal*; *stupidity* (here dullness or numbness of feeling); *scandalous* (here causing offense and disgrace to the faith); *tractable* (teachable, easily led); *censorious* (quick to condemn); *papists* (Baxter's word for Roman Catholics, as in the other files); *heathens* (non-Christians, as he uses it); *beads* (the rosary); *lusts* (strong desires); *formality* (outward form without inner life); *adjuncts* (things added to worship that can be changed). The sentences on the Roman and Eastern churches, and on forms of prayer and ceremony, are Baxter's own polemic and are kept as he wrote them.
- **Abassines:** Baxter's spelling, probably for the Abyssinians (the Christians of Ethiopia). We have not confirmed this. **Armenians; Greeks:** the Armenian and Greek (Eastern Orthodox) churches, as Baxter lists them.
- **"conference, and zeal... disputes"** (second paragraph): "conference" here means serious religious conversation, not a meeting.
- **Item 5** (second paragraph): the sentences on young people of "different sexes" are kept as written; Baxter's point is that a godly affection can slide into a fleshly one through too much familiarity.
- **Numbered items 1-6:** Baxter's own numbering, kept in the running paragraph as he printed it.
- **"Direct. XVIII."** is rendered "Direction 18." Baxter's heading sentence ends "And so also of religious duties," which introduces the third and fourth paragraphs on the decay of worship into mere form.
- **The Vain Religion of the Formal Hypocrite:** Baxter's own earlier book (title modernized by capitalization only); we have not checked the details of its publication.
- **Philippians 3:20 (flag):** Baxter's "hearts and conversations are in heaven" echoes the King James "our conversation is in heaven." The NET reads "our citizenship is in heaven," which fits his point about conduct and allegiance but is not the same word.
