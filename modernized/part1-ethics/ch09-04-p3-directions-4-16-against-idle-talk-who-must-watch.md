# Chapter 9: Title 4, Directions 4-16 against idle talk, and who should most watch against it

**Direction 4. If you would not be idle talkers, see that your hearts are taken up with something that is good; and that your tongues are acquainted with and accustomed to their proper work and duty.** [537]

An empty head and heart are the causes of empty, frothy, worthless talk. Conscience may tell you when your tongues run on empty things that at that time there is no sense of sin or duty, or the presence of God on your hearts; no holy love; no zeal for God: but you are asleep to God and all that is good; and in this sleep you ramble and talk idly of anything that comes into your mind. Also you are not conscientious about speaking of that which is good, or else it would keep out emptiness and evil. Remember what an abundance of greater matters you have to talk of! You have the evil of sin, the multitude and subtlety of temptations, and the way of resisting them, to talk of; you have your faults to lament, your evidences to inquire after, your mercies thankfully to set forth, the greatness and goodness, and all the attributes of God to praise; you have all the works of God to admire, even all the creatures in the world to contemplate, and all God's admirable providences and government to observe; you have the mystery of redemption, the person, and office, and life, and miracles, and sufferings, and glory, and intercession, and reign of Christ to talk of; and all the secret sanctifying operations of the Holy Spirit; and all the ordinances of God, and all the means of grace, and all our duties to God and man, and all the holy Scripture; besides death and judgment, and heaven and hell, and the concerns of the church of God, and the case of the persons you speak to, who may need your instruction, exhortation, admonition, reproof, or comfort: and is not here work enough to employ your tongues, and keep them from idle talk? [538] Be conscientious about those duties commanded: "You must let no unwholesome word come out of your mouth, but only what is beneficial for the building up of the one in need, that it would give grace to those who hear. And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God" (Ephesians 4:29). "And do not get drunk with wine, which is debauchery, but be filled by the Spirit, speaking to one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making music in your hearts to the Lord, always giving thanks to God the Father for all things in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ" (Ephesians 5:18, 19). "Whoever speaks, let it be with God's words" (1 Peter 4:11). [539] Sinful omission of good talk is the cause of sinful commission of emptiness. Especially when the heart itself is empty; for as a man is, so is he apt to speak. "They are from the world; therefore they speak from the world's perspective" (1 John 4:5). "For a fool speaks disgraceful things; his mind plans out sinful deeds. He commits godless deeds and says misleading things about the Lord" (Isaiah 32:6).

**Direction 5. Walk always with God, as in his presence, and in the awe of his laws and judgment, that conscience may be kept awake and tender.** [540]

You will be restrained from empty talk if you perceive that God is hearing you, and if you remember that your tongue is under a law, and that "on the day of judgment, people will give an account for every worthless word they speak" (Matthew 12:36, 37), and that "by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned." If the law of God were in your hearts (Psalm 40:8), and hidden there (Psalm 119:11), your heart would be fixed (Psalm 57:7). His word then would be the rejoicing of your heart (Psalm 119:111); and your tongues would then be talking of judgment (Psalm 37:30). A tender conscience will smart more with an idle word than a seared, senseless conscience with an oath, or lie, or slander. For the fear of God is clean (Psalm 19:9), and by it men depart from evil (Proverbs 16:6). Therefore "be zealous in fearing the Lord all the time" (Proverbs 23:17).

**Direction 6. Avoid idleness, if you would avoid idle talk.** [541]

The drones of the commonwealth that have nothing else to do but visit, and compliment, and chatter about other men's matters, and that can have time to sit whole hours together on no business, are they that are most guilty of idle chat. Idle gentlemen, and beggars, and idle gossiping women, and old men that are devoid of the fear of God, and children that have no business to do, are they that can sit talking away their time to as little purpose as if they had been all the time asleep. All idle persons swarm with the vermin of idle thoughts and words.

**Direction 7. If you would avoid idle talk, avoid idle, talkative companions: or if you cannot avoid them, do not answer them, but let them talk alone, unless it is to rebuke them, or turn them to more profitable talk.** [542]

For when you hear emptiness, it will incline you to speak emptiness: and these ungodly persons "speak every one emptiness to his neighbor," as if their tongues were so much their own that no lord might control them (Psalm 12:1-6). The philosopher could say, That which you would not hear, do not speak; and that which you would not speak, do not hear. Most are like parrots, that will most often speak the words which they most often hear. How hard it is to avoid idle talk among idle talkers! One empty word draws on another, and there is no end.

**Direction 8. Avoid empty works, if you would avoid empty words.**

For a man that engages himself in empty employment loses all the words as empty which he uses about that employment. What a life then do they live, that have an unlawful calling! When their very business and trade is sin, the adjuncts, the words about it, must be sin, and so all their lives are a continued sin. I would rather therefore be the lowest drudge than one of these men. Especially actors should think of this: and those that spend whole hours, yes, half-days, if not nights, in gaming, or empty and sinful amusements: what an abundance of idle words they use about them! every cast of the dice, and every card they play, has an idle word; so that a sober man would be weary and ashamed to hear them.

**Direction 9. Do not plunge yourselves into excess of worldly business, as some do, that undertake more without necessity than they can discharge: for such necessitate a variety of thoughts and words.**

And all that are spent in serving them in those their empty employments are empty; though the work in the matter of it is not empty.

**Direction 10. Do not let a corrupt mind make that seem necessary or suitable which is empty.**

Carnal hearts that are acquainted with no better things think nothing empty that pleases their physical inclinations, or which their carnal interest requires. A man-pleaser thinks civility obliges him to observe his unnecessary visits and compliments, and to answer idle talkers, and not sit silent by them, nor contradict them: and so it must be a point of good manners to break the law of God: and as they think it uncivil not to pledge every drinker in his toasts, so not to answer every chatterer in his talk.

**Direction 11. Beware of a proud, self-conceited mind, that thinks too well of your own talk.**

Only get humility, and you will rather choose to hear than to speak. But when all your fancies and irrelevancies seem some excellent matters to you, then you are with child till you are delivered of them, and then all must reverence and silently attend to your pride and folly; or be taken as neglecters of you for disregarding it.

**Direction 12. Avoid passion and emotional companions: for passion is talkative, and will not be checked, but resists the restraint of reason, and multiplies words that are worse than empty.** [543]

**Direction 13. Beware of an inordinate joking vein: [544] for it habituates the mind to foolish levity, and knows no bounds, and breeds idle words as thick as putrefied flesh breeds vermin: and it is the greater sin, because it is habitual, and with a certain pleasure and pride, and glorying in emptiness, and sinful levity and folly.**

**Direction 14. Understand particularly what service you have to do for God or men in every company you come into, and so fit your words to the present duty and company.** [545]

For those words are empty and unsuitable in one company that are necessary or suitable in another. If you are to converse with the ignorant and ungodly, turn your talk into a compassionate way of instruction or exhortation. If with men wiser and better than yourselves, inquire and learn from them, and draw that from them which may edify you.

**Direction 15. Do not affect an unnecessary elaborateness of speech, but take those for the fittest words which are suited to the matter, and to your heart, and to the hearers.** [546]

Otherwise your speech will be studiedly and affectedly empty; and you will glory in that as elegant which is your shame. Hypocritical words that do not come from the heart are dead and corrupt, and are only the image of true speech, as lacking that truth and meaning of the mind which is their life. Words are like laws, that are valued by the authority, and matter, and end, more than by the elaborateness and elegance; or like money, that is valued by the authority, metal, and weight, and not by the elaborateness of its engraving, imagery, or matter. All that is counterfeit, though elaborate, is empty.

**Direction 16. Suppose you had written down the idle words of a day (your own or any other chatterers'), and read them all over at night!**

Would you not be ashamed of such a volume of emptiness and confusion? Oh what a book it would be, that one should thus write from the mouth of idle talkers! What a shame it would be to human nature! It would tempt some to question whether man is a rational creature, or whether all are so, at least? Remember then that all is recorded by God and conscience; and all this hodgepodge of emptiness must be reviewed and answered for.

The rest that is necessary for direction against idle words you may find in chapter 5, part 2, in the government of the thoughts, and in my book of *Self-denial*. In a word (for I must not commit the fault which I am reproving), do not account a course of idle talk a small sin. Never allow so loose and slippery a member as your tongue to be unguarded; and never speak that of which you dare not say, as Psalm 19:14, "May my words and my thoughts be now and always acceptable in your sight, O Lord, my sheltering rock and my redeemer."

*In the margin: Who should be most watchful here.*

But especially, above others, these persons should watch against empty words: 1. Preachers, who are doubly sanctified persons, and whose tongues, being consecrated to God, must not be sacrilegiously alienated to emptiness: which is worse than sacrilegious alienation of the places, or utensils, or revenues of the church. Hate it therefore more than these.

2. Old people, whose words should be grave and wise, and full of instruction to suppress the levity of youth; childhood and youth are emptiness; but age should not be so. [547]

3. Parents and masters, who should be examples of gravity and staidness to their families; and by their reproofs and chastisements should repress such faults in their inferiors.

4. Those that are better qualified than others, with knowledge and utterance, to use their tongues to edification. Empty speech is a double sin in them.

5. Those that are noted as persons of holiness and religion: for it is supposed that they pray and speak much against idle talk, and therefore must not themselves be guilty of it. "If someone thinks he is religious yet does not bridle his tongue, and so deceives his heart, his religion is futile" (James 1:26). (See my sermon on that text.)

6. Those that are ignorant, and greatly need the edifying speech of others.

7. Those that live among wise and holy persons, by whom they may be much edified.

8. Those that are among chatterers, where they know they have more need to watch their tongues than their purses among pickpockets.

9. Those (women especially) that are naturally inclined to overmuch talk, who therefore should be the more watchful, as knowing their disease and danger.

10. Both empty and angry persons, who carry a continual temptation about them. All these should be especially watchful against idle talk.

And for the time, 1. Especially when they are among those that may receive most hurt by it. 2. And when you are going to holy duty, or newly come from it, etc.

## Translator's notes

- **Scope:** this file covers the end of Title 4 of Chapter 9 (against idle talk), from the start of Direction 4 to the end of the Title. "Title" (Baxter's "Tit.") is his word for one of the six headed divisions of Chapter 9; the Title 4 heading itself falls in an earlier part.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All quoted verses were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **"vain," "vanity" (throughout):** rendered "empty," "emptiness," per the glossary. "idle" is kept wherever Baxter wrote "idle."
- **Matthew 12:36-37 (Direction 5):** the King James "every idle word" is the phrase this whole Title rests on; the NET has "every worthless word." The sense (useless, careless speech) is close, but the verbal link to "idle talk" is lost in the NET. Baxter cites "36, 37" after the first quotation; the second quotation is 12:37.
- **Ephesians 4:29 (Direction 4):** Baxter cites only 4:29 but quotes the start of 4:30 ("grieve not the holy Spirit of God"), which we give in NET wording. The King James "corrupt communication" and "edifying ... minister grace to the hearers" become NET "unwholesome word" and "building up ... give grace to those who hear"; the sense he uses holds.
- **Ephesians 5:18-20 (Direction 4):** Baxter cites "18, 19" but quotes through verse 20 ("giving thanks always..."); we keep his reference. King James "speaking to yourselves" is NET "speaking to one another."
- **1 Peter 4:11 (Direction 4):** King James "as the oracles of God"; NET "with God's words." Same point.
- **1 John 4:5 (Direction 4):** King James "speak they of the world"; NET "speak from the world's perspective." Baxter seems to take it as speaking *about* worldly things; the NET reads it as speaking *from* a worldly outlook. His point (as a man is, so he speaks) still stands, but the NET weakens the link to the *topics* of talk.
- **Isaiah 32:6 (Direction 4):** the NET differs markedly: King James "the vile person will speak villany, and his heart will work iniquity, to practise hypocrisy, and to utter error against the Lord"; NET "a fool speaks disgraceful things; his mind plans out sinful deeds ... says misleading things about the Lord." Baxter leans on "his heart" as the source of speech; the NET has "his mind," which keeps the inner-source point. We stop where Baxter stops.
- **Proverbs 23:17 (Direction 5):** Baxter's "Be thou therefore in the fear of the Lord all the day long"; "therefore" is his own word and is placed outside the quotation. NET "be zealous in fearing the Lord all the time."
- **Psalm 12 (Direction 7):** Baxter's quoted words, King James Psalm 12:2 "They speak vanity every one with his neighbour," are kept in his wording (modernized), because the NET reads a different point: "People lie to one another." Baxter's argument depends on "vanity" (empty talk), which the NET does not have. "as if their tongues were so much their own that no lord might control them" alludes to Psalm 12:4.
- **Psalm 19:14 (end of Direction 16):** NET wording. "now and always" is Baxter's own addition; it is not in the King James or the NET. King James "the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart ... my strength, and my redeemer" becomes NET "my words and my thoughts ... my sheltering rock and my redeemer."
- **James 1:26 (list item 5):** NET "futile" for King James "vain"; Baxter's point (vain religion goes with an unbridled tongue) holds.
- **Unquoted references in Direction 5** (kept in Baxter's wording, since he does not put them in quotation marks): Psalm 40:8 (NET "Your law dominates my thoughts"); Psalm 119:11 (NET "In my heart I store up your words"); Psalm 57:7 "fixed" (NET "I am determined"); Psalm 119:111 "rejoicing of your heart" (NET "they give me joy"); Psalm 37:30 "talking of judgment" (NET "promote justice," so the NET loses the speech sense Baxter uses); Psalm 19:9 "the fear of God is clean" (NET "The commands to fear the Lord are right"); Proverbs 16:6 (NET "through fearing the Lord one avoids evil").
- **Unmarked allusion:** "childhood and youth are emptiness" (list item 2) echoes Ecclesiastes 11:10 (King James "childhood and youth are vanity"), which Baxter lists in note 547.
- **Note 540:** "Psal. ... cxlix. 11": Psalm 149 has only nine verses, so this reference is wrong as printed. We have not identified the intended verse (Psalm 119:11, cited in the body of Direction 5, is one possibility) and keep Baxter's reference.
- **Note 541:** "1 Tim. vi. 13" does not obviously bear on idleness; Baxter may have meant 1 Timothy 5:13 (on idle people going from house to house), but we have not confirmed this and keep his reference.
- **Note 542:** the Latin saying is given without a source; we do not identify one. Our translation is a draft; the sense seems to be that refusing to answer a chatterer is itself a reproof, which fits Direction 7.
- **Note 546:** Baxter does not name the person who used the phrase "clever triflers," and we have not identified him.
- **"The philosopher" (Direction 7):** Baxter does not name him; we have not identified the saying.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): discourse, talk; moither, ramble (a dialect word for talking confusedly or being muddled); make conscience of, be conscientious about; subtilty, subtlety; concernments, concerns; Holy Ghost, Holy Spirit; open (your mercies), set forth; prate, chatter; have while, have time; reprehend, rebuke; oftest, most often; stage-players, actors; sports, amusements; basest, lowest; vicious (given to vice), corrupt; convenient / inconvenient, suitable / unsuitable; sensual, physical; healths, toasts; twattler / prattlers, chatterer(s); take heed of, beware of; impertinencies, irrelevancies; passionate, emotional; jesting, joking; putrified, putrefied; ordinary (customary), habitual; curiosity / curious (of speech, of a coin), elaborateness / elaborate; sculpture (on a coin), engraving; verity and significancy, truth and meaning; reasonable creature, rational creature; suffer, allow; ancient people, old people; cut-purses, pickpockets; addicted, inclined.
- **Kept:** "evidences" (Direction 4), Baxter's term for the marks by which a person judges whether grace is real in him; "adjuncts" (Direction 8), the things that go along with a trade; "pledge" (Direction 10), to drink in answer to someone's toast; "staidness" (sober steadiness); "masters" and "inferiors" (the heads and members of a household, including servants); "carnal"; "levity."
- **Baxter's polemic and social views:** his judgment on actors and gaming (Direction 8), and his remarks on "idle gossiping women" (Direction 6) and women as "naturally addicted to overmuch talk" (item 9), are his own and are kept as he wrote them.
- **Cross-references not checked:** "chapter 5, part 2, in the government of the thoughts" (in this *Christian Directory*); his book *Self-denial*; "my sermon on that text" (James 1:26). We have not confirmed these.
- **Final "1. ... 2."** (the times to watch): Baxter shifts from "they" to "you" within this short list; kept as printed.
