# Advertisement to the Readers (part 3 of 3)

*Objection 4.* Your Ecclesiastical Cases are dangerously reconciling, tending to abate men's zeal against error.

*Answer.* The world has long enough escaped the danger of peace and reconciliation. It would have been well if they had as long escaped the danger of your conceited, orthodox strife, which has brought in confusion and all evil works. I take it to be a zeal effectively against love, and against unity, and against Christ, which, with the preachers of extremes, goes under the name of a zeal against error, and for truth.

*Objection 5.* Are all these numerous directions to be found in Scripture? Show us them in Scripture, or you trouble the church with your own inventions.

*Answer.* 1. Are all your sermons in the Scripture? and all the good books of your library in the Scripture? 2. Will you have none but readers in the church, and put down preachers? Surely it is the reader that delivers all and only the Scripture. 3. Are we not men before we are Christians? And is not the light and law of nature divine? And was the Scripture written to be instead of reason, or of logic, or other supporting sciences? Or must they not all be sanctified and used for divinity? 4. But I think that as all good commentaries, and sermons, and systems of theology, are in Scripture, so is the Directory here given, and is proved by the evidence of the very thing discussed, or by the plainest texts.

*Objection 6.* You confound your reader by over-subtlety of distinctions.

*Answer.* 1. If they are empty or false, shame them by exposing it, or you shame yourselves by blaming them, when you cannot show the error. Do not expose yourselves to laughter by avoiding just distinction to escape confusion: that is, avoiding knowledge to escape ignorance, or light to escape darkness. 2. It is ambiguity and confusion that breeds and feeds almost all our pernicious controversies; and even those that bring in error by empty distinction must be refuted by better distinguishers, and not by ignorant confounders. I will believe the Holy Spirit, 2 Timothy 2:14–16, that logomachy is the plague by which the hearers are subverted, and ungodliness increased; and that orthotomy, or right dividing the word of truth, is the cure. And, Hebrews 5:14, discerning both good and evil is the work of long and well exercised senses.

*Objection 7.* Is this your reducing our faith to the primitive simplicity, and to the creed? What a toilsome task do you make religion by overdoing? Is any man able to remember all these numberless directions?

*Answer.* 1. I beg you, do not mistake all these for articles of faith. I am more zealous than ever I was for the reduction of the Christian faith to the primitive simplicity; and more confident that the church will never have peace and concord till it is so done, as to the rest of men's faith and communion. But he that will have no books but his creed and Bible may follow that sectarian who, when he had burned all his other books as human inventions, at last burned the Bible, when he grew learned enough to understand that the translation of that was human too.

2. If men do not think all the tools in their shops, and all the furniture of their houses, or the number of their sheep, or cattle, or lands, nor the number of truths received by a learning intellect, etc. to be a trouble and toil, why should they think so of the number of helps to make easier the practice of their duty? If all the books in your libraries make your studies or religion toilsome, why do you keep them? and do not come to the common people's religion, that would hear no more than, Think well, speak well, and do well, or, Love God and your neighbor, and do as you would be done by. He that does this truly shall be saved. But there goes more to the building of a house than to say, Lay the foundation, and raise the superstructure: universals do not exist except in individuals; and the whole consists of all the parts.

3. It is not expected that any man remember all these directions. Therefore I wrote them, because men cannot remember them, that they may, upon every necessary occasion, go to that which they have present use for, and cannot otherwise remember.

In sum, to my quarrelsome brothers I have two requests: 1. That instead of their unconscionable, and yet unreformed custom of backbiting, they would tell me to my face of my offenses by convincing evidence, and not tempt the hearers to think them envious. And, 2. That what I do amiss they would do better: and not be such as will neither laboriously serve the church themselves, nor allow others; and that they will not be guilty of idleness themselves, nor tempt me to be a slothful servant, who have so little time to spend; for I dare not stand before God under that guilt. And that they will not join with the enemies and resisters of the publication of the word of God.

And to the readers my request is, 1. That whatever for quantity or quality in this book is an impediment to their regular, universal obedience, and to a truly holy life, they would neglect and cast away. 2. But that which is truly instructing and helpful, they would diligently digest and practice; and I encourage them by my testimony that by long experience I am assured that this PRACTICAL RELIGION will afford both to church, state, and conscience, more certain and more solid peace than contending disputers, with all their claims of orthodoxness and zeal against errors for the truth, will ever bring, or did ever attain to.

I beg your pardon for this long defense: it is an age where the objections are not invented, and where our greatest and most costly services of God are charged on us as our greatest sins; and where at once I am accused by conscience for doing no more, and by men for doing so much. Being really

A most unworthy servant of so good a Master,

RICHARD BAXTER.

## Translator's notes

- **Where this ends:** the last lines of the range (from "A" at line 1966) are the start of the title "A Christian Directory" that heads the next section; they are not part of the Advertisement and are not rendered here.
- **Scripture translation:** quoted Scripture in this project is from the NET Bible®. See [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). In this section Baxter paraphrases rather than quotes, so his wording is kept in the body and the NET is given here.
- **2 Timothy 2:14–16 (flagged):** Baxter's sentence paraphrases the King James ("strive not about words to no profit, but to the subverting of the hearers … rightly dividing the word of truth … they will increase unto more ungodliness"). His argument for careful distinctions leans on the King James "rightly dividing." The NET reads: "not to wrangle over words. This is of no benefit; it just brings ruin on those who listen" (v. 14); "teaching the message of truth accurately" (v. 15); "those occupied with it will stray further and further into ungodliness" (v. 16). "Dividing" is gone from the NET, so the link Baxter draws between Scripture and "distinction" does not show in the modern wording.
- **logomachy; orthotomy:** words formed from the Greek of these verses, "word-fighting" (v. 14) and "cutting straight" (v. 15). Baxter glosses the second himself ("right dividing the word of truth"). Kept.
- **Hebrews 5:14 (corrected reference):** the 1846 text prints "Heb. v. 15," but Hebrews 5 has only 14 verses, and the words are from 5:14. We corrected it. Baxter paraphrases; the NET reads: "whose perceptions are trained by practice to discern both good and evil." His "well exercised senses" is the King James "senses exercised."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "confusion and all evil works" recalls James 3:16; "slothful servant" recalls Matthew 25:26.
- **when inconsistent** (first paragraph): left as written; Baxter seems to mean pleasing men when their demands contradict one another.
- **Ecclesiastical Cases** (Objection 4): left as printed. Probably the cases of conscience on church matters in a later part of this book; we have not checked the cross-reference.
- **abate:** kept, in the sense "lessen."
- **conceited** (Answer to Objection 4): kept. In Baxter's day it could mean "fanciful" or "self-opinionated," so "conceited, orthodox strife" is strife that only fancies itself orthodox, as well as proud strife.
- **readers** (Objection 5, point 2): people who only read Scripture aloud in church, as opposed to preachers. **put down:** do away with.
- **supporting sciences:** Baxter's "subservient sciences" (disciplines that serve theology). **divinity:** kept, Baxter's technical term for theology.
- **the Directory:** this book, *A Christian Directory*. **discussed:** Baxter's "discoursed of."
- **over-subtlety:** Baxter's "curiosity" (excessive subtlety or elaborateness). **confound, confounders:** kept, in the sense "confuse, confusers," keeping his play on "confusion."
- **exposing:** Baxter's "detecting." **refuted:** his "confuted." **empty:** his "vain."
- **primitive simplicity:** the simplicity of the earliest church. Kept.
- **I beg you:** Baxter's "I pray."
- **as to the rest of men's faith and communion** (Objection 7, point 1): left as printed; the sense is unclear. Baxter may mean "as far as the remainder of what men must believe and hold in common is concerned." It is possible "rest" is a misprint (for example for "test"), but we have not checked an earlier edition.
- **sectarian:** Baxter's "sectary," a member of a sect. The story of the man who burned his Bible is Baxter's; we have not identified its source.
- **make easier:** Baxter's "facilitate." **common people's religion:** his "vulgar religion." **etc.:** his "&c."
- **brothers:** Baxter's "brethren," fellow ministers and Christians.
- **regular** (requests to readers): according to rule. **claims:** Baxter's "pretences." **PRACTICAL RELIGION:** capitals as printed.
- **defense:** Baxter's "apology," in the older sense of a defense or explanation. **invented:** his "feigned." **accused by conscience … by men:** Baxter's "accused of conscience … of men," where "of" means "by."
- **Book titles:** none are named in this section.
