# Chapter 1, Part 2 (continued): Temptations 13–15

**Temptation 13.** Lest the soul should be converted, the devil will do all that he can to keep you from the acquaintance and company of those whose holiness and instructions might convince and strengthen you; and especially from a lively, convincing minister; and to cast you under some dead-hearted minister and society.

**Direction 13.** Therefore, if it is possible, even if it is to your loss or inconvenience in the world, live under a searching, heavenly teacher; and in the company of those who are resolved for heaven. It is a dead heart indeed that does not feel the need of such assistance, and is not the better for it when it has it. If ever you are well on your way to heaven, and likely to be converted, it will be among such helps as these.

**Temptation 14.** But one of the strongest temptations of Satan is by making their sin exceedingly pleasant to them, for the gain, or honor, or fleshly satisfaction; and so increasing the violence of their physical appetite and lust, and making them so much in love with their sin that they cannot leave it. Like the thirst of a man in a burning fever, which makes him cry for cold drink, though it would kill him; the fury of the appetite conquering reason. So we see many drunkards, fornicators, worldlings that are so deeply in love with their sin that, come what will, they will have it, though they have hell with it.

**Direction 14.** Against this temptation I ask you to read what I have said later, in chapter 4, part 7, and chapter 3, Directions 6 and 8. Oh that poor sinners knew what it is that they so much love! Is the pleasing of the flesh so sweet a thing to you? and are you so indifferent to God, and holy things? Are these less lovable? Do you foresee what both will be at last? Will your sin seem better than Christ, and grace, and heaven when you are dying? O do not be so in love with damning folly, and the pleasure of a beast, as to despise for it the heavenly wisdom and delights!

**Temptation 15.** Another great temptation is the prosperity of the wicked in this life; and the reproach and suffering which usually fall upon the godly. If God struck every notorious sinner dead on the spot as soon as he had sinned, or struck him blind, or mute, or lame, or inflicted at once some such judgment, then many would fear him, and refrain from their sin; but when they see no men prosper so much as the most ungodly, and that they are the persecutors of the holy seed, and that sentence against an evil work is not speedily carried out, then "the human heart is encouraged to do evil" (Ecclesiastes 8:11).

**Direction 15.** But alas, how short is the prosperity of the wicked! Read Psalm 73 and 37. Delay is no forgiveness: they wait only until the assize: and will that tempt you to do as they do? How unthankfully sinners deal with God! If he should kill you and plague you, that would not please you: and yet if he holds back from you, you are emboldened by it in your sin. Thus his patience is turned against him; but the stroke will be the heavier when it falls. Do you think those men will always flourish? Will they always domineer and revel? Will they always live in the houses where they now live, and possess those lands, and be honored and served as they are now? Oh how quickly and how dreadfully will the case be changed with them! Oh could you but foresee now what faces they will have, and what heavy hearts, and with what bitter exclamations they will at last cry out against themselves for all their folly, and wish that they had never been deceived by prosperity, but rather had the portion of a Lazarus! If you saw how they are but fattened for the slaughter, and in what sorrowful misery their wealth, and sport, and honors will leave them, you would lament their case, and think so great a destruction would come soon enough, and not desire to be partners in their lot.

## Baxter's notes

None in these three pairs. (Footnotes [43]–[45] belong to later Temptations.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Ecclesiastes 8:11:** Baxter's "then are their hearts set in them to do evil" is a close rendering of the King James, "the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil." The NET reads "the human heart is encouraged to do evil." The NET's wording is looser ("encouraged") than the King James ("fully set"), but the sense of Baxter's use, that delayed punishment emboldens sinners, is the same.
- **Psalm 73; Psalm 37** (Direction 15): cited only; both are about the apparent prosperity of the wicked.
- **"a Lazarus"** (Direction 15): the poor man in Jesus's story (Luke 16:19–25). **fatted for the slaughter:** an unmarked allusion to Jeremiah 12:3 and James 5:5; we rendered it "fattened for the slaughter."
- **assize:** the periodic court sessions at which criminals were tried. Kept as Baxter's word; see the note to Direction 14 of Part 1.
- **physical appetite** (Temptation 14): Baxter's "sensual appetite," meaning appetite for physical pleasure. **lovable:** his "amiable."
- **fleshly satisfaction; honor; gain:** left as Baxter's.
- **make them cry for cold drink** (Temptation 14): the image is of someone with a fever who demands a drink that will do him harm. Kept as written.
- **well on your way to heaven:** Baxter's "fair for heaven," meaning on a good course toward it. **likely:** his "like."
- **chapter 4, part 7; chapter 3, Directions 6 and 8** (Direction 14): references to later sections of Baxter's book. We have not checked these sections yet.
- **mute:** Baxter's "dumb." **at once:** his "presently." **refrain from:** his "forbear." **emboldened:** his "imboldened." **sorrowful:** his "dolorous."
- **domineer; revel:** left as Baxter's words.
- **holy seed** (Temptation 15): the godly. A biblical phrase (Isaiah 6:13; Ezra 9:2).
