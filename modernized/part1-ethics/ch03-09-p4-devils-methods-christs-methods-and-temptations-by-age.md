# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 9 (part 4 of 13)

*This section begins in the middle of Grand Direction 9, partway through Baxter's numbered list of the devil's methods for keeping sinners from conversion (items 9 to 14). It then gives the methods of Christ against the tempter, and begins Baxter's treatment of temptations to particular sins.*

9. Or if any ministers or godly persons warn the sinner, the devil will do what he can so that they may be so small a number in comparison with those of the contrary mind; so that he may tell the sinner, Do you think these few self-conceited fellows are wiser than such, and such, and all the country? Shall none be saved but such a few strict ones? "None of the members of the ruling council or the Pharisees have believed in him, have they? But this rabble who do not know the law are accursed!" (John 7:48-49); that is, as Dr. Hammond notes, "This illiterate multitude are apt to be seduced, but the teachers are wiser."

10. The devil does his worst to cause some falling out, or difference of interest or opinion, between the preacher or warner and the sinner; so that he may take him for his enemy. And how unwilling people are to receive any advice from an enemy or adversary, experience will easily convince you.

11. He works so that powerful preaching may be so rare, and the contradiction of wicked quibblers so frequent, that the sermon may be forgotten, or the impressions of it blotted out, before they can hear another to confirm them, and strike the nail home to the head; and so that the fire may go out before the next opportunity comes.

12. He labors to keep good books out of the sinner's hands, or to keep him from reading them, lest he fare as the eunuch in Acts 8, who was reading the Scripture as he rode in his chariot on the way. And instead of such books he puts romances, and plays, and trifling, or scornful, contradicting writings into his hands.

13. He does what he can to keep the sinner from close acquaintance with any who are truly godly; so that he may know them only by the image which ignorant or malicious slanderers or scorners give of such; and so that he may know religion itself only by hearsay, and never see it exemplified in any holy, diligent believers. A holy Christian is a living image of God, a powerful persuader and teacher of the ungodly; and the nearer people come to them, the greater excellence they will see, and the greater effect they will feel. Whereas in the devil's army, most must not be seen in the open light, and the hypocrite himself must be seen, like a picture, only by a side light, and not by a direct one.

14. The devil labors to frustrate the means which are used: (1.) By sluggish carelessness and disregard. (2.) By prejudice, and false opinions which take hold of the mind beforehand. (3.) By distractions of many sorts. (4.) By prior commitments to the opposite interest and way; so that Christ comes too late for them. (5.) By worldly prosperity and pleasures. (6.) By bad company. (7.) And by harassing and frightening the sinner when he merely forms any purpose to be converted; giving him all contentment and quietness in sin, and raising storms and terrors in his soul when he is about to turn.

**The Methods of Christ against the Tempter**

Before I proceed to Satan's particular temptations, I will show you the opposite methods of Christ in the conduct of his army, and in opposing Satan.

I. Christ's ends are, ultimately, the glory and pleasing of his Father and himself, and the saving of his church, and the destroying of the kingdom of the devil; and next, the purifying of his own special people, and the calling home of all who are ordained to eternal life.

But more particularly, he looks principally at the heart, to plant there, 1. Holy knowledge. 2. Faith. 3. Godliness, or holy devotion to God, and love to him above all. 4. Thankfulness. 5. Obedience. 6. Humility. 7. Heavenly-mindedness. 8. Love to others. 9. Self-denial, and mortification, and contentment. 10. Patience. And in all these, 1. Sincerity; 2. Tenderness of heart; 3. Zeal, and holy strength and resolution. And along with this, to make us actually useful, and diligent in our Master's work, for our own and others' salvation.

II. Christ's order in working is direct, and not backward, as the devil's is. He first reveals saving truth to the understanding, and moves the will by showing the goodness of the things revealed; and these engage the thoughts, and passions, and senses, and the whole body, bringing the lower faculties to obedience, and casting out by degrees those images which had deceived and taken hold of them beforehand.

The matter which Christ presents to the soul is, 1. Certain truth from the Father of lights, set up against the prince and kingdom of darkness, ignorance, error, and deceit. 2. Spiritual and everlasting good, even God himself, to be seen, and loved, and enjoyed forever, against the tempter's temporal, bodily, and seeming good. Christ's kingdom and work are advanced by light: he is for promoting all useful knowledge; and therefore for clear and convincing preaching, for reading the Scriptures in a known language, and meditating on them day and night, and for exhorting one another daily; all of which Satan is against.

III. The means by which he works against Satan are such as these: 1. Sometimes he makes use of the very temperament of the body as a preparation; and (being Lord of all) he gives such a temperament as will be most useful to the soul; as a sober, deliberate, meek, quiet, and patient disposition. But sometimes he honors his grace by the conquest of such sins as even bodily disposition welcomes and cherishes.

2. Sometimes by his providence he withdraws the matter of temptations, so that they will not be too strong for feeble souls: but sometimes his grace makes use of them all, and leaves them for the magnifying of its frequent victories.

3. Sometimes he gives his cause the majority vote among the people, so that it will be a matter of dishonorable oddness not to be a professed Christian; and sometimes, but exceedingly rarely, it is so with the life of godliness and the practice of Christianity also. But ordinarily, in most places of the world, custom and the multitude are against him, and his grace is honored by prevailing against these bonds of Satan.

4. He makes his ministers his principal instruments, qualifying, disposing, and calling them to his work, and helping them in it, and prospering it in their hands.

5. He makes it the duty of every Christian to do his part to carry on the work; and furnishes them with love, and compassion, and knowledge, and zeal in their several measures.

6. He gives a very strict charge to parents to devote their children, along with themselves, to God; encouraging them with the promise of his accepting and blessing them; and commands them to teach them the word of God with the greatest diligence, and to bring them up in the discipline and fear of God.

7. He gives princes and magistrates their power, to promote his kingdom, and protect his servants, and encourage the good, and suppress iniquity, and further obedience to his laws; though, in most of the world, they turn out to be his enemies, and he carries on his work without them, and against their cruel persecuting opposition.

8. His light uncovers the nakedness of the devil's cause, and among the sons of light it is hateful, and a common shame. And as "wisdom is justified by her children," so the judgment of holy people condemning sin does much to keep it under in the world.

9. His providence usually casts the sinner whom he will do good to into the bosom and communion of his holy church, and into the familiar company and acquaintance of the godly, who may help him by instruction, affection, and example.

10. His providence fits all conditions to their good; but especially helps them by timely, quickening afflictions. These are the means which he ordinarily uses. But the powerful inward workings of his Spirit give effect to them all.

**Temptations to Particular Sins, with Directions for Preservation and Remedy**

In chapter 1, part 2, I have opened up the temptations which hinder sinners from conversion to God: I will now proceed to those which draw people to particular sins. Here Satan's art is exercised, 1. In fitting his baits to his particular use. 2. In applying them to it.

**Temptation 1.** The devil fits his temptations to the sinner's age. The same bait is not suitable to all. Children he tempts to excess of playfulness, lying, disobedience, unwillingness to learn the things that belong to their salvation, and a senselessness of the great concern of their souls. He tempts youth to wantonness, rudeness, gluttony, unruliness, and foolish thoughtlessness. In the beginning of manhood he tempts to lust, indulgence in physical pleasure, and luxury; or if these do not work, to schemes of worldliness and ambition. The aged he tempts to greed, and immovability in their error, and unteachableness and stubbornness in their ignorance and sin. Thus every age has its own special snare.

**Direction 1.** The remedy against this is, 1. To be distinctly acquainted with the temptations of your own age; and to watch against them with special care and fear. 2. To know the special duties and advantages of your own age, and turn your thoughts wholly to those. Scripture has various precepts for the various ages; study your own part. The young have more time to learn their duty, and less care and business to divert them; let them therefore be taken up with obedient learning. The middle-aged have the most vigor of body and mind; and therefore should do their Master's work with the greatest vigor, activity, and zeal. The aged should have the most judgment, and experience, and familiarity with death and heaven; and therefore should teach the younger, both by word and holy life.

## Translator's notes

- **Range and position.** The range begins mid-list at Baxter's item 9 of the devil's methods against conversion, and ends partway through his treatment of "Temptations to particular sins": the text stops after Direction 1 against Temptation 1, and the next file continues with Temptation 2.
- **Headings.** "The Methods of Christ against the Tempter" and "Temptations to particular Sins, with Directions for Preservation and Remedy" are Baxter's centered sub-headings, rendered in bold. Baxter's "Tempt. I." and "Direct. I." are rendered "Temptation 1." and "Direction 1."; his Roman numerals I, II, III for the Methods of Christ are kept as printed. No margin notes ("[Sidenote: ...]") fall in this range.
- **John 7:48-49 (NET).** Baxter quotes the King James style: "Do any of the rulers or of the Pharisees believe in him? But this people that knoweth not the law are cursed." The NET has "None of the members of the ruling council or the Pharisees have believed in him, have they? But this rabble who do not know the law are accursed!" The sense Baxter relies on is not changed: the NET's "rabble" and "accursed" match the contempt of the Pharisees' words, and Baxter's own gloss (Hammond's "illiterate multitude") agrees. The Hammond quotation is kept as Baxter gives it, with only the verb modernized; we have not checked it against Hammond's text, and Baxter gives no page.
- **"Wisdom is justified of her children."** Baxter puts this in quotation marks but gives no reference. It is an unmarked allusion; the nearest verses are probably Luke 7:35 and Matthew 11:19 (this reference is not in Baxter's text). He keeps the old sense "justified" (shown to be right). The NET has "vindicated by all her children" in Luke 7:35, and "vindicated by her deeds" in Matthew 11:19. I kept Baxter's wording, modernized lightly ("justified by her children"), since it is a loose quotation. The NET's "vindicated" gives the same sense Baxter uses here: the holy lives and judgments of God's people prove wisdom right and so shame sin.
- **Acts 8** (the eunuch reading Scripture in his chariot) is an allusion with no quoted words, kept as Baxter has it. Baxter gives "Acts viii." with no verse, kept as chapter only. Item 6 of the Methods of Christ (bringing children up in the "nurture and fear of God") echoes Ephesians 6:4 and Deuteronomy 6, unmarked by Baxter; the NET of Ephesians 6:4 has "discipline and instruction of the Lord" (reference not in Baxter's text).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then mine): vigour to vigor; monitor to warner; cavillers to quibblers; gulosity to gluttony; voluptuousness to indulgence in physical pleasure (see GLOSSARY, "sensual"); covetousness to greed; unmovableness to immovability; unapt to unwilling; precise ones to strict ones (his "precise" is probably the period jibe at strict Puritans; we have not confirmed this); sluggish heedlessness to sluggish carelessness; heedfulness to care; prepossess to "take hold of the mind beforehand"; diversions to distractions; pre-engagements to prior commitments; molesting to harassing; speed (fare) to fare; play-books to plays; concernment to concern ("a senselessness of the great concern of their souls"); major vote to majority vote; singularity to oddness; nurture to discipline; corporal to bodily; quickening afflictions kept as "quickening". "Gulosity" and "unmovableness" are rare words and my renderings are by sense.
- **Terms kept:** mortification, zeal, heavenly-mindedness, providence, the church, "sons of light," "Father of lights" (an echo of James 1:17, unmarked by Baxter). "Prevailing" in item 3 of the Methods of Christ is kept in its sense of winning out.
- **"Religion"** in item 13 ("religion itself") means devotion and practiced godliness, as in GLOSSARY; kept.
- **Item 14** and the **"Methods of Christ"** are Baxter's own summaries; his "reducing the inferior faculties to obedience" is rendered "bringing the lower faculties to obedience" (the soul's lower powers, the senses and passions, under the will and understanding).
- **Cross-references not checked:** "chap. i. part 2" (rendered "chapter 1, part 2") refers to Baxter's earlier discussion of temptations that hinder conversion; we have not verified the numbering in this edition. Baxter's "as I showed, p. 93" for bodily temperaments falls just after this range, in Temptation 2.
- **His polemic:** Baxter's contrast of the devil's army and Christ's is his own rhetoric and kept as he wrote it.
