# Chapter 1, Part 2 (continued): Temptations 23–25

**Temptation 23.** Indeed, Satan will do his worst to make them heretics, and teach them some doctrine of licentiousness suited to their lusts. It is hard to go on being wicked against conscience in the open light. This is kicking against the goads; too painful work to be easily borne; therefore the devil will make them a religion which shall please them and do their sins no harm. Either a religion made up of loose opinions, like the Familists, Ranters, Libertines, and Antinomians, and the Jesuits too much; or else made up of trifling formalities, and a great deal of bodily exercise, and stage actions, and outward courtesies, as much of popish devotion is: and a little will draw a carnal heart to believe a carnal doctrine. It is easier to get such a new religion than a new heart. And then the devil tells them that now they are in the right way, and therefore they shall be saved. A great part of the world think their case is good because they are of such or such a sect or party, and of that which (they are told by their leaders) is the true church and way.

**Direction 23.** But remember that whatever law you make for yourselves, God will judge you by his own law. Falsifying the king's coin is no good way to pay a debt, but an addition of treason to your former misery. It is a new and a holy heart and life, and not a new creed, or a new church or sect, that is necessary to your salvation. It will never save you to be in the soundest church on earth, if you are unsound in it yourselves, and are but the dust in the temple that must be swept out: much less will it save you to make yourselves a rule, because God's rule seems too strict.

**Temptation 24.** Another way of the tempter is to draw men to settle for mere convictions instead of true conversion. When they have but learned that it is necessary to salvation to be regenerate, and have the Spirit of Christ, they are as quiet as if this were indeed to be regenerate, and to have the Spirit. As some think they have attained perfection when they have but received the opinion that perfection may be had here; so a great many think they have had sanctification and forgiveness because now they see that these must be had, and that without sanctification there is no salvation: and thus the knowledge of all grace and duty shall pass with them for the grace and duty itself; and their judgment of the thing, instead of the possession of it: and instead of having grace, they force themselves to believe that they have it.

**Direction 24.** But remember God will not be mocked: he knows a convinced head from a holy heart. To think you are rich will not make you rich: to believe that you are well, or to know the remedy, is not enough to make you well. You may dream that you eat, and yet awake hungry (Isaiah 29:8). All the land or money which you see is not therefore your own. To know that you should be holy leaves your unholiness without excuse. Ahab did not escape by believing that he would return in peace. Self-flattery in so great and weighty a case is the greatest folly. "If you understand these things, you will be blessed if you do them" (John 13:17).

**Temptation 25.** Another great temptation is by hiding from men the intrinsic evil and odiousness of sin. What harm, says the drunkard, and adulterer, and self-indulgent pleasure-seeker, is there in all this, that preachers make so great a fuss against it? what harm is this to God or man, that they would make us believe that we must be damned for it, and that Christ died for it, and that the Holy Spirit must put it to death? "Why has the Lord threatened us with such great disaster? What wrong have we done? What sin have we done to offend the Lord our God?" the Jews say (Jeremiah 16:10). He that does not know God does not know what sin against God is; especially when the love of it and delight in it blinds them.

*In the margin: Psalm 40:12; Psalm 51.*

**Direction 25.** Against this I ask you to ponder those forty intrinsic evils in sin, which I name later, in chapter 3, Direction 8, and the aggravations. If the devil can but once persuade you that sin is harmless, all faith, all religion, all honesty, and your souls and all are gone. For then all God's laws and government must be fictions; then there is no work for Christ as a Savior, or the Spirit as a Sanctifier, to do; then all ordinances and means are troublesome, empty things, and godliness and obedience deserve to be banished from the earth as unnecessary troublers of mankind; then this poison may be safely taken and made your food. But oh how mad a notion this is! How quickly will God make the proudest know what harm it was to refuse the government of his Maker, and set up the government of his beastly appetite and misguided will! and that sin is bad, if hell is bad.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **John 13:17:** Baxter's "happy are ye if ye do them" is "you will be blessed if you do them" in the NET. Baxter's "If you know these things" is "If you understand these things."
- **Jeremiah 16:10:** NET wording. In the NET these are the questions the people will ask. Baxter quotes them as what "the Jews say." The NET's "What sin have we done to offend the Lord our God?" is a looser rendering of the King James "what is our sin that we have committed against the Lord our God?"
- **Isaiah 29:8:** cited only. **Psalm 40:12; Psalm 51:** margin references, reproduced as printed.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "kicking against the pricks" (Acts 26:14, "goads" in the NET); "God will not be mocked" (Galatians 6:7); "Ahab … believing that he should return in peace" (1 Kings 22:28).
- **Familists, Ranters, Libertines, Antinomians** (Temptation 23): groups Baxter treats as promoting loose living in the name of religion. The Familists were the "Family of Love," a sixteenth-century sect; Ranters were radical groups of the 1640s–50s; Antinomians held that believers are free of the moral law. Baxter adds that the Jesuits belong in the same list "too much" (to a considerable degree); he does not say why here, though in his polemics he often charged Jesuit casuistry with making sin easy. We have not confirmed his meaning. His language is polemical. Kept as written.
- **popish devotion:** kept as Baxter's term for Roman Catholic worship, which he calls "trifling formalities, bodily exercise, stage actions." His polemic, left as written.
- **goads:** Baxter's "pricks," from the old English of Acts 26:14. **outward courtesies:** his "compliments." **licentiousness:** kept; it means moral looseness.
- **Falsifying the king's coin** (Direction 23): counterfeiting. Baxter's comparison: a counterfeit religion cannot pay what God is owed.
- **the dust in the temple that must be swept out** (Direction 23): left as written; a person who attends the right church but is not holy is merely dirt to be cleaned out of it.
- **settle for:** Baxter's "take up with." **a great many:** his "abundance." **pass with them for:** his "go with them for."
- **put it to death:** Baxter's "mortify it." **make so great a fuss:** his "make so great ado." **self-indulgent pleasure-seeker:** his "voluptuous sensualist."
- **troublesome, empty things:** Baxter's "troublesome vanities." **notion:** his "conceit." **beastly:** left as written.
- **forty intrinsic evils** (Direction 25): Baxter's list of forty intrinsic evils of sin, named in a later chapter (chapter 3, Direction 8). We have not yet drafted it. **aggravations:** the things that make a sin worse.
- **blessed:** Baxter's "happy."
