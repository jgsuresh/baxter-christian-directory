# Chapter 1, Part 2 (continued): Temptations 6–9

**Temptation 6.** But, says the tempter, holiness is the special excellence of holy persons; but ordinary, unlearned people may be saved without such high matters, which are above them.

**Direction 6.** But God tells you that without holiness "no one will see the Lord" (Hebrews 12:14). The unlearned may be saved, but the ungodly cannot (Psalm 1:6). Holiness is to the soul as life to the body: he that has it not is dead; though all have not the same degree of health: sin is sin, and hated by God, in learned or unlearned. All men have souls that need regenerating at the first: and as all bodies that live must live on the earth, by the air, and food, etc.; so all souls that live do live upon the same God, and Christ, and heaven, by the same word and Spirit; and all this may be had by the unlearned.

**Temptation 7.** But, says the tempter, God is not so unmerciful as to damn all that are not holy: this is but talk to keep men in awe, and not to be believed.

**Direction 7.** But if God's threats are necessary to keep men in awe, then they are necessary to be carried out. For God does not need to awe men by a lie. He best knows to whom he will be merciful, and how far. Did you never read, "For these people lack understanding, therefore the one who made them has no compassion on them; the one who formed them has no mercy on them" (Isaiah 27:11)? And "Have no mercy on any treacherous evildoers" (Psalm 59:5). Is he not just, as well as merciful (Exodus 34:6–7)? Do you not see that men are sick, and pained, and die, for all that God is merciful? And do not merciful judges condemn criminals? Are not angels made devils by sin for all that God is merciful? The devil knows this to his sorrow. "If God did not spare the angels who sinned, but threw them into hell" (2 Peter 2:4), will he be unjust for you?

**Temptation 8.** But Christ died for all; and God will not punish him and you both for the same fault.

**Direction 8.** Christ died for all that have the gospel to this extent: to procure and seal for them a free and general pardon of all their sins, if they will repent and take him for their Savior, and so to bring salvation to their choice. But will this save the ungodly, stubborn refusers? Christ died to sanctify as well as to forgive (Ephesians 5:27), and "to purify for himself a people who are truly his, who are eager to do good" (Titus 2:14); and "to destroy the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8); and to bring all men under his dominion and government (Romans 14:9; Luke 19:27). "If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, this person does not belong to him" (Romans 8:9).

**Temptation 9.** No man can be certain of his salvation; but all must hope for the best: and to raise doubts in men's hearts whether they shall be saved or not will not help them, but puzzle them, and cast them into despair.

**Direction 9.** But is there so little difference between a child of God and of the devil, and between the way to heaven and the way to hell, that they cannot be told apart? Has not Christ taught us plainly how to know them (Psalm 1 and 15; 1 John 3)? and told us to "make every effort to be sure of your calling and election" (2 Peter 1:10)? If all men must hope that they shall be saved, then most must hope for that which they shall never have: but it is no hope of God's making which deceives men. Should so great a matter as our everlasting joy or misery be cast out of our care, and risked so carelessly in the dark? When is it that we have life, and time, and all in order to make it sure? And what harm can it do you to find out the truth of your own condition? If you are indeed unregenerate and unholy, discover it now in time, and you have time to be recovered. You must despair of being saved without conversion; but that prevents absolute, final despair. Whereas if you do not find out your case until time is past, then hope is past, and the devil has you in endless desperation, where he wants you.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Hebrews 12:14:** NET wording ("for without it no one will see the Lord"), where "it" is holiness. We put "without holiness" before the quotation so the reference is clear.
- **Isaiah 27:11:** NET wording. Baxter quotes only the last clauses of the verse. In the NET the verse begins with an image of brittle branches broken for kindling, which Baxter does not use. The NET's "these people lack understanding" matches his "a people of no understanding."
- **Psalm 59:5:** NET wording. In the NET this is a prayer to God to punish the nations ("Have no mercy on any treacherous evildoers"); Baxter, like the King James, quotes it as a general statement.
- **2 Peter 2:4:** NET wording. Baxter's "cast them down to hell" is "threw them into hell" in the NET.
- **Titus 2:14; 1 John 3:8; Romans 8:9:** NET wording, as in earlier sections.
- **2 Peter 1:10 (assurance):** Baxter quotes "give diligence to make our calling and election sure." The NET reads "make every effort to be sure of your calling and election." Baxter is using the verse to argue that Christians should seek assurance of their state, and the NET's "be sure of" fits that. The King James "make … sure" can also mean "make certain by one's conduct," which is not the point Baxter is making.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "the way to heaven and the way to hell" (compare Matthew 7:13–14); "children of God and of the devil" (1 John 3:10).
- **ordinary people:** Baxter's "vulgar," meaning common people. **special excellence:** his "the excellency."
- **hated by God:** Baxter's "hated of God." **at the first:** left as in the source.
- **carried out:** Baxter's "executed," meaning put into effect.
- **criminals:** Baxter's "malefactors." **for all that:** in spite of the fact that.
- **to this extent** (Direction 8): Baxter's "so far … as to," meaning that Christ's death secures a pardon offered to all who hear the gospel, which will not save those who refuse it.
- **stubborn:** Baxter's "obstinate."
- **will he be unjust for you?** (Direction 7): Baxter means, will God act unjustly to spare you, when he did not spare the angels who sinned?
- **make it sure; risked so carelessly:** Baxter's "for to make it sure," "ventured so regardlessly." **told apart:** Baxter's "known asunder."
- **prevents** (Direction 9): Baxter's "preventeth," meaning heads off or stops; here, discovering one's state in time prevents final despair.
- **hope for the best:** Baxter's "hope well."
