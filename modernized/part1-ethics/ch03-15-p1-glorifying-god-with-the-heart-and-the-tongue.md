# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XV (part 1 of 4)

**Grand Direction 15. Let your very heart be set to glorify God, your Creator, Redeemer, and Sanctifier; both with the esteem of your mind, the praises of your mouth, and the holiness of your life.**

The glorifying of God, being the end of man and the whole creation, must be the highest duty of our lives; and therefore deserves our distinct consideration. "Whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God" (1 Corinthians 10:31). "...so that in everything God will be glorified through Jesus Christ. To him belong the glory and the power forever and ever. Amen" (1 Peter 4:11). I shall therefore first show you what it is to glorify God, and then give directions how to do it.

To glorify God is not to add to his essential perfections, or happiness, or real glory. [127] The glory of God is a word that is taken in these various senses: 1. Sometimes it means the essential, transcendent excellencies of God considered in himself; so Romans 6:4; Psalm 19:2. 2. Sometimes it means that glory which the angels and saints behold in heaven: what this is, a soul in the flesh cannot formally conceive or comprehend. It seems not to be the essence of God, because that is everywhere, and so is not that glory; or if anyone thinks that his essence is that glory, and is everywhere alike, and that the creature's capacity is all the difference between heaven and earth, he seems to be refuted in that the glory of heaven will be seen by the glorified body itself, which it is thought cannot see the essence of God. Whether, then, that glory is the essence of God, or any immediate emanation from his excellency, as the beams and light that are sent forth by the sun, or a created glory for the happiness of his servants, we shall know when we enjoy it with the blessed. 3. Sometimes it is taken for the appearance of God's perfections in his creatures, either natural or free agents, as discerned by man, and for his honor in the esteem of man. John 11:4, 40; 1 Corinthians 11:7; 2 Corinthians 4:15; Philippians 1:11; 2:11; Isaiah 35:2; 40:5, etc. And so to glorify God is, 1. Objectively, to represent his excellencies or glory; 2. Mentally, to conceive of them; 3. and Verbally, to declare them. I shall therefore direct you separately, 1. How to glorify God in your minds. 2. By your tongues. 3. By your lives.

### Directions for glorifying God with the Heart

**Direction 1. Abhor all blasphemous representations and thoughts of God, and do not think of him lamely, unequally, or diminutively, nor as under any corporeal shape; nor think to comprehend him, but reverently admire him.**

Conceive of him as incomprehensible and infinite: and if Satan would tempt you to think meanly of anything in God, or to think highly of one of his perfections, and meanly of another, abhor such temptations; and think of his power, knowledge, and goodness, equally, as the infinite perfections of God. [128]

**Direction 2. Behold his glory in the glory of his works of nature and of grace, and see him in all as the soul, the glory, the all of the whole creation.**

What a power is that which made and preserves all the world! What a wisdom is that which joined together the universal frame of heaven and earth, and keeps all things in their order! How good is he that made all good, and gave the creatures all their goodness, both natural and spiritual, by creation and renewing grace! Thus "The heavens declare the glory of God; the sky displays his handiwork" (Psalm 19:1). "His splendor has covered the skies, the earth is full of his glory" (Habakkuk 3:3). "The Lord's shout is heard over the water; the majestic God thunders" (Psalm 29:3; 145 [the verse number is missing in the source]).

**Direction 3. Behold him in the person, miracles, resurrection, dominion, and glory of his blessed Son:**

"...is the radiance of his glory and the representation of his essence, and he sustains all things by his powerful word, and so when he had accomplished cleansing for sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high. Thus he became so far better than the angels..." (Hebrews 1:3–4). "By him" it is that "glory is given to God in the church" (Ephesians 3:21). "As a result God highly exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee will bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord to the glory of God the Father" (Philippians 2:9–11). "Pray," therefore, that "the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the glorious Father, will give you spiritual wisdom and revelation in your growing knowledge of him—since the eyes of your heart have been enlightened—so that you can know what is the hope of his calling, what is the wealth of his glorious inheritance in the saints, and what is the incomparable greatness of his power toward us who believe, as displayed in the exercise of his immense strength. This power he exercised in Christ when he raised him from the dead and seated him at his right hand in the heavenly realms far above every rule and authority and power and dominion and every name that is named, not only in this age but also in the one to come. And God put all things under Christ's feet, and gave him to the church as head over all things" (Ephesians 1:17–22). "The Father has glorified his name in his Son" (John 12:28; 13:31, 32; 14:13; 17:1).

**Direction 4. Behold God as the end of the whole creation, and intend him as the end of all the actions of your life.**

You do not honor him as God if you do not practically esteem him as your ultimate end; even the pleasing of his will, and the honoring him in the world. If anything else is made your chief end, you honor it before him, and make a god of it.

**Direction 5. Respond to all his blessed attributes with suitable affections, (as I have directed in my "Treatise of the Knowledge of God," and here briefly in Direction 4) and to his relations to us with the duty which they command, (subjection, love, etc.) as I have set out in the foregoing directions.**

We glorify him in our hearts when the image of his attributes is received there.

**Direction 6. Behold him by faith as always present with you.**

And then every attribute will affect you the more, and you will not admit dishonorable thoughts of him. Pray to him as if you saw him, and you will speak to him with reverence. Speak of him as if you saw him, and you dare not take his name in vain, nor talk of God with a common frame of mind, nor in a common manner, as of common things. "By faith [Moses] left Egypt without fearing the king's anger, for he persevered as though he could see the one who is invisible" (Hebrews 11:27). God is despised by those who think they are behind his back.

**Direction 7. Think of him as in heaven, where he is revealed in glory to the blessed, and magnified by their high, everlasting praise.**

Nothing helps us so much to glorify God in our minds as to behold him by faith where he is most glorious. The very reading over of the description of the glory of the New Jerusalem, Revelation 21 and 22, will much affect a believing mind with a sense of the gloriousness of God. Suppose, with Stephen, we saw heaven opened, and the Ancient of days, the great Jehovah, gloriously lighting up the city of God, and Jesus in glory at his right hand, and the innumerable army of glorified spirits before his throne, praising and magnifying him with the highest wonder and the most joyful acclamations that creatures are capable of; would it not raise us to some of the same wonder? The soul that by faith is much above does most glorify God, as being nearest to his glory.

**Direction 8. Foresee by faith the coming of Christ, and the day of the universal judgment, when Christ shall come in flaming fire with thousands of his holy angels, to be glorified among his saints, and admired among all who have believed (2 Thessalonians 1:10).**

**Direction 9. Abhor all doctrines which blaspheme or dishonor the name of God, and would blemish and hide the glory of his majesty.**

I give you this rule for your own preservation, and not in imitation of uncharitable firebrands and dividers of the church, to exercise your pride and domineering disposition, in condemning all men to whose opinions you can maliciously attach a blasphemous consequence, which either follows only in your own imagination, or is not acknowledged, but hated, by those to whom you attach it. Let it suffice you to detest false doctrines, without detesting the persons that you imagine guilty of them, who profess to believe the contrary truth as steadfastly as you yourselves.

**Direction 10. Beware of sinking into flesh and earth, and of being diverted by things of the senses from the daily contemplation of the glory of God.**

If your belly becomes your god, and you set your mind on earthly things, and are set upon the honors, or profits, or pleasures of the world, when your conduct should be in heaven, you will be glorying in your shame, when you should be marveling at the glory of your Maker (Philippians 3:18–20); and you will have so much to do on earth that you will find no leisure (because you have no hearts) to look up seriously to God.

### Directions for glorifying God with our Tongues in his Praises

*In the margin: How great a duty praising God is.*

**Direction 1. Conceive of this duty of praising God according to its superlative excellencies, as being the highest service that the tongue of men or angels can perform.**

To bless, or praise, or magnify God, is not to make him greater, or better, or happier than he is; but to declare and extol his greatness, goodness, and happiness. And that your hearts may be inflamed to this excellent work, I will here show you how great and necessary, how high and acceptable a work it is.

1. It is the giving to God his chief due. [129] A speaking of him as he is; and when we have spoken the highest, how far we fall short of the due expression of his glorious perfections! Oh how great praise that almightiness deserves, which created and preserves all the world, and overrules all the sons of men, and is able to do whatever he will! "The Lord is great and certainly worthy of praise. No one can fathom his greatness. One generation will praise your deeds to another and tell about your mighty acts. I will focus on your honor and majestic splendor and your amazing deeds. They will proclaim the power of your awesome acts. I will declare your great deeds" (Psalm 145:3–6). What praise that knowledge deserves which extends to all things that are, or were, or ever shall be! and that wisdom which orders all the world! He knows every thought of man, and all the secrets of the heart, Psalm 44:21; 94:11. "Known to God are all his works, from the beginning of the world" (Acts 15:18). "...there is no limit to his wisdom" (Psalm 147:5). What praise that goodness and mercy deserve, which is diffused throughout all the world, and is the life, and hope, and happiness of men and angels! "For your loyal love extends beyond the sky, and your faithfulness reaches the clouds" (Psalm 57:10). "How great is your favor, which you store up for your loyal followers" (Psalm 31:19); and therefore how great should be his praise! "Who can adequately recount the Lord's mighty acts or relate all his praiseworthy deeds?" (Psalm 106:2). "...for the Lord's splendor is magnificent" (Psalm 138:5).

2. It is the end of all God's wondrous works, and especially the end which man was made for, that all things might praise him objectively, and men (and angels) in estimation and expression. That his glorious excellency might be visible in his works, and be admired and extolled by the rational creature: for this all things were created and are continued: for this we have our understanding and our speech: this is the fruit that God expects from all his works. Deny him this, and you are guilty of frustrating the whole creation, as much as in you lies. You would have the sun to shine in vain, and the heavens and earth to stand in vain, and man and all things to live in vain, if you would not have God have the praise and glory of his works. Therefore, sun, and moon, and stars, and firmament, are called on to praise the Lord, Psalm 148:2–4, as they are the matter for which he must by us be praised. "Praise him for his mighty acts; praise him for his surpassing greatness!" (Psalm 150:2). "Let them give thanks to the Lord for his loyal love and for the amazing things he has done for people" (Psalm 107:8), etc. Yes, it is the end of Christ in the redemption of the world, and in saving his elect, that God might, in the church, in earth and heaven, have the "praise and glory of his grace," Ephesians 1:6, 12, 14. "Through him then let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of our lips, acknowledging his name" (Hebrews 13:15). "And let the redeemed of the Lord say that his mercy endures forever" (Psalm 107:2). For this, all his saints "are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of his own, so that [they] may proclaim the virtues of the one who called [them] out of darkness into his marvelous light" (1 Peter 2:5, 9).

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Many of Baxter's quotations are loose King James wording, so the NET often reads differently. Where it shifts the sense, it is noted below. Where Baxter quotes a speech to God ("your"/"thy"), the NET's "your" is kept even where Baxter wrote "his."
- **Heading punctuation:** Baxter prints "thy Creator Redeemer, and Sanctifier"; we added a comma after "Creator" so it reads as three titles. **esteem:** Baxter's "estimation." **happiness:** Baxter's "felicity" (throughout). **honor:** Baxter's "honour."
- **Structure:** the source runs each Direction's heading sentence into its body with a dash; we render the heading in bold and the rest as the body. The two sets of Directions (for the heart, for the tongue) each restart at 1, as Baxter has them. Baxter's third part (glorifying God by the life) is not in this file.
- **Senses of "glory"** (opening): Baxter's "formally" (a soul cannot "formally conceive") is left as written. **immediate emanation:** a direct outflowing from God's excellence, without anything between. **Objectively; Mentally; Verbally:** Baxter's three headings, kept as written. He means: by showing God's excellence (in the creation, the objects), by thinking of it, by speaking of it.
- **1 Peter 4:11:** Baxter's "to whom be praise and dominion" is not what the NET has ("the glory and the power"). It is probably the King James reading, but we have not checked. The NET also drops "That" at the start, so it is shown with an ellipsis.
- **Romans 6:4; Psalm 19:2; John 11:4, 40; 1 Corinthians 11:7; 2 Corinthians 4:15; Philippians 1:11; 2:11; Isaiah 35:2; 40:5:** references only; Baxter quotes nothing, so we do not quote either.
- **Psalm 19:1:** the NET verse begins with the title ("For the music director, a psalm of David"); we quote only the sentence Baxter quotes. NET: "sky displays his handiwork," where he has "firmament showeth his handy work."
- **Habakkuk 3:3:** Baxter's "the earth is full of his praise" (King James); the NET has "the earth is full of his glory." This slightly changes his emphasis (praise versus glory), though the context is still God's glory.
- **Psalm 29:3:** Baxter's "the God of glory thundereth." The NET has "the majestic God thunders," which loses the word "glory" he is leaning on. The reference "cxlv." is cut off after "Psal. xxix. 3; cxlv." in the source; we cannot tell which verse he meant.
- **Hebrews 1:3–4:** Baxter's quotation runs together with some omissions. NET "representation of his essence" for Baxter's "express image of his person" ("person" in the older sense, as the divine being; rendered loosely); the NET has no "by himself" before "cleansing for sins," where Baxter has "having by himself purged our sins." The NET verse 4 begins "Thus he became so far better than the angels as he has inherited a name superior to theirs"; Baxter ends at "being made better than the angels," so we stop at "angels" and mark the cut with an ellipsis.
- **Ephesians 3:21:** Baxter's "By him it is that glory is given to God in the church" is his own wording (probably a paraphrase of the King James, "Unto him be glory in the church by Christ Jesus"; we have not checked); the NET reads "to him be the glory in the church and in Christ Jesus," so it does not say "by him." Baxter's wording kept.
- **Philippians 2:9–11:** NET wording. Baxter's "of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth" is King James; the NET has "in heaven and on earth and under the earth." The NET's "and every tongue confess" depends on the earlier "will" in the sentence (the NET prints it that way).
- **Ephesians 1:17–22:** NET wording; Baxter's "Pray" is his own word (the NET has "I pray that"), and his "&c." is replaced by the verse range 17–22 that he in fact quotes. Three shifts: Baxter's "the spirit of wisdom and revelation" has "spiritual wisdom and revelation" in the NET (Baxter's wording could be read as the Holy Spirit; the NET's "spiritual" does not); "the eyes of your understanding" is "the eyes of your heart"; "in the celestials" is "in the heavenly realms."
- **John 12:28 (and 13:31–32; 14:13; 17:1):** Baxter's "The Father hath glorified his name in his Son" is a paraphrase, not a quotation. The NET of John 12:28 reads, "Father, glorify your name.' Then a voice came from heaven, 'I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.'" We have not looked up the other references.
- **Direction 5:** "my *Treatise of the Knowledge of God*" is Baxter's own work; we have not checked the title as printed. "here briefly direct. iv." could mean Direction 4 or Grand Direction 4 (on the knowledge of God, earlier in this chapter); we have rendered it "Direction 4" and have not checked which he means. **Respond to:** Baxter's "Answer." **set out:** Baxter's "opened."
- **Direction 4: intend:** to aim at as an end. **Direction 2: joined together:** Baxter's "set in joint."
- **Direction 6: Hebrews 11:27:** NET wording. Baxter has "Moses" in the quotation; the NET verse says only "he." We put "[Moses]" in brackets. NET: "left Egypt without fearing the king's anger" for "forsook Egypt, not fearing the wrath of the king"; "persevered" for "endured." **despised:** Baxter's "contemned."
- **Direction 7: Stephen** (Acts 7:55–56; see also note [127]), **the Ancient of days** (Daniel 7:9, as we read it) and the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21–22): allusions. **lighting up:** Baxter's "illustrating." **wonder:** Baxter's "admirations" (the old sense of "admire" is "marvel at"; the same for "admire" in Direction 1 and "admiring" in Direction 10, which we render "marveling").
- **Direction 8: 2 Thessalonians 1:10.** The clause "to be glorified among his saints, and admired among all who have believed" is NET wording. Baxter wrote "in his saints" and "in all them that do believe"; the NET has "among" in both places, and adds "on that day" (omitted in his clause). "In flaming fire with thousands of his holy angels" is an unmarked allusion (compare 2 Thessalonians 1:7 and Jude 14; we have not verified these). This Direction is one sentence in Baxter, without a separate body.
- **Direction 9:** Baxter is warning against condemning persons for consequences he says they do not accept. **firebrands:** troublemakers who stir up strife. **domineering disposition:** Baxter's "imperious humour." **attach:** Baxter's "affix."
- **Direction 10: Beware of:** Baxter's "Take heed of." **diverted:** kept as Baxter wrote it. **Philippians 3:18–20** is a reference, not a quotation, but the wording follows it ("belly... god," "glory... shame," "earthly things"). Baxter has "when your conversation should be in heaven" (King James); we say "conduct," per the glossary, but the NET has "our citizenship is in heaven," which, as we understand it, is closer to the Greek sense (a commonwealth or citizenship; we have not checked this). The NET also has "exult in their shame" where Baxter has "glorying in your shame," close enough that his point is not changed. **things of the senses:** Baxter's "things sensible."
- **Tongues, Direction 1:** "its superlative excellencies" left as written.
- **Psalm 145:3–6:** Baxter gives "cxlv. 3–5," but his quotation runs through "I will declare thy greatness," which is the NET's verse 6, so we cite 3–6. The NET reads "No one can fathom his greatness" for Baxter's "his greatness is unsearchable"; its "honor and majestic splendor" for "the glorious honour of thy Majesty"; and "great deeds" for "greatness" in the last line.
- **Psalm 44:21; 94:11:** Baxter's sentence is his own statement, not a quotation; references only. The NET of 44:21 says "he knows a person's secret thoughts"; of 94:11, "the Lord knows that peoples' thoughts are morally bankrupt."
- **Acts 15:18:** Baxter's "Known unto God are all his works, from the beginning of the world" (we have modernized "unto" to "to" in the text) is not in the NET, which reads only "known from long ago." Baxter's wording is probably the King James reading, which differs from the NET's here (we have not checked); it is kept, modernized, and not replaced.
- **Psalm 147:5:** NET "there is no limit to his wisdom"; Baxter has "His understanding is infinite." The NET's "wisdom" for his "understanding" is a shift, though he is using it on God's knowledge. The quote therefore stands with an opening ellipsis.
- **Psalm 57:10:** NET "loyal love extends beyond the sky" for Baxter's "mercy is great unto the heavens"; "faithfulness reaches the clouds" for "truth unto the clouds."
- **Psalm 31:19:** NET "How great is your favor, which you store up for your loyal followers" for Baxter's "how great is his goodness to them that fear him." The NET does not have "fear."
- **Psalm 106:2:** NET "adequately recount... mighty acts" and "relate all his praiseworthy deeds" for Baxter's "utter the mighty acts... show forth all his praise."
- **Psalm 138:5:** Baxter quotes "For great is the glory of the Lord." The NET has "for the Lord's splendor is magnificent." The word "glory" is not in it, and the clause begins the NET's second half of the verse (the first half is "Let them sing about the Lord's deeds"), so it stands with an opening ellipsis.
- **Psalm 148:2–4:** a reference only. **Psalm 150:2:** NET wording; Baxter's "O" and "therefore" belong to his sentence, not to the verse.
- **Psalm 107:8:** NET "give thanks to the Lord for his loyal love and for the amazing things he has done for people." Baxter's "Oh that men would praise the Lord for his goodness, and declare his wondrous works for the children of men" (King James) is different: "praise" versus "give thanks," "goodness" versus "loyal love."
- **Psalm 107:2:** Baxter's "let the redeemed of the Lord say, that his mercy endureth for ever" is the King James wording plus words that, as we read it, come from verse 1. The NET of verse 2 is "Let those delivered by the Lord speak out, those whom he delivered from the power of the enemy." We keep Baxter's wording, modernized, as his own and do not give it as the NET's.
- **Ephesians 1:6, 12, 14:** Baxter's "praise and glory of his grace" is a blend. The NET has "to the praise of the glory of his grace" (1:6), and "to the praise of his glory" (1:12, 14). Baxter's wording kept.
- **Hebrews 13:15:** NET "Through him then" for Baxter's "By him therefore." The NET has "acknowledging his name" where Baxter has "giving thanks to his name." Baxter's point is about thanks; the NET sense is closer to "confessing" or "acknowledging."
- **1 Peter 2:5, 9:** the quotation is from verse 9 (verse 5 is the "spiritual sacrifices" verse). NET "a chosen race" for "a chosen generation"; "a people of his own" for "a peculiar people"; "proclaim the virtues of the one who called you" for "show forth the praises of him that hath called you." The NET has "you may proclaim," where Baxter has "they should show forth"; we put "[they]" and "[them]" in brackets. The NET's "virtues" for Baxter's "praises" loses the sense of praise, which is his argument here.
- **Tongues, Direction 1, point 2: objectively:** as in the opening, meaning that all things, by showing God's excellence, praise him. **in estimation and expression:** in esteem and in speech. **as much as in you lies:** as far as you are able.
- **Baxter's notes:** [127] is a list of references only, converted to modern form. [128] and [129] are explained above in the notes themselves. Bucholtzer is not identified here; we have not confirmed which author is meant.
