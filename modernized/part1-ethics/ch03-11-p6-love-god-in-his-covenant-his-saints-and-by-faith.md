# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XI (part 6 of 8)

*This section begins in the middle of Direction 10 (points 2 and 3 of its list); the Direction's heading is in the previous file. It then gives Direction 11 in full.*

**(Direction 10, continued)**

2\. Behold God also in his covenant of grace, which he has made in Christ. In that you may see such sure, such great and wonderful mercies, freely given out to a world of sinners, and to yourselves among the rest, as may afford abundant matter for love and thankfulness to feed on while you live. There you may see how unwilling God is that sinners should perish; how he delights in mercy; and how great and unspeakable that mercy is. There you may see an act of pardon and amnesty granted upon the reasonable condition of believing, penitent acceptance, to all mankind; the sins that men have been committing many years together, their willful, heinous, aggravated sins, you may there see pardoned by more aggravated mercy; and the enemies of God reconciled to him, and condemned rebels saved from hell, and brought into his family, and made his sons. Oh what an image of the goodness of God is apparent in the tenor of his word and covenant! Holiness and mercy make up the whole--they are expressed in every leaf and line! The precepts, which seem too strict to sinners, are but the perfect rules of holiness and love, for the health and happiness of man. What loveliness did David find in the law itself! and so should we, if we read it with his eyes and heart: it was sweeter to him than honey; he loved it above gold, "I love your commands more than gold, even purest gold" (Psalm 119:127); and, verse 97, he cries out, "O how I love your law! All day long I meditate on it" (Psalm 119:97). And must not the Lawgiver then be much more lovely, whose goodness here appears to us? "The Lord is both kind and fair; that is why he teaches sinners the right way to live" (Psalm 25:8). "I will find delight in your commands, which I love. I will lift my hands to your commands, which I love, and I will meditate on your statutes" (Psalm 119:47-48). How delightfully then should I love and meditate on the blessed Author of this holy law! But how can I read the history of love, the strange design of grace in Christ, the mystery which the angels eagerly pry into, the promises of life to lost and miserable sinners, and not feel the power of love transform me? "See what sort of love the Father has given to us: that we should be called God's children--and indeed we are!" (1 John 3:1). How does God shed abroad his love upon our hearts, but by opening to us the superabundance of it in his word, and opening our hearts by his Spirit to perceive it? Oh when a poor sinner that first had felt the load of sin, and the wrath of God, shall feelingly read or hear what mercy is offered to him in the covenant of grace, and hear Christ's messengers tell him, from God, that all things are now ready; and therefore invite him to the heavenly feast, and even compel him to come in, what melting love must this affect the sinner's heart with! When we see the grant of eternal life sealed to us by the blood of Christ, and a pardoning, justifying, saving covenant, so freely made and surely confirmed to us, by that God whom we had so much offended, oh what an incentive is here for love!

When I mention the covenant I imply the sacraments, which are but its appendages or confirming seals, and the investing of the believer solemnly with its benefits. But in these God is pleased to condescend to the most familiar communion with his church, that love and thankfulness might lack no help. There it is that the love of God in Christ applies itself most closely to particular sinners; and the food or drink will be sweet in the mouth, which was not sweet to us on the table at all. Oh how many a heart has this affected! How many have felt the stirrings of that love, which before they did not feel, when they have seen Christ crucified before their eyes, and have heard the minister, in his name and at his command, bid them "take," and "eat," and "drink;" commanding them not to refuse their Savior, but take him and the benefits of his blood as their own; assuring them of his good will and readiness to forgive and save them.

3\. Behold also the loveliness of God in his holy ones, who bear his image, and are advanced by his love and mercy. If you are Christians indeed, you are taught by God to love his servants, and to see an excellence in the saints on earth, and make them the people of your delight (Psalm 16:1-2; 1 Thessalonians 4:9). And this must needs acquaint you with the greater loveliness in the most holy God, who made them holy. Oh how often have the feeling and heavenly prayers of lively believers excited those affections in me which before I did not feel! How often have I been warmed with their heavenly talk! How lovely is that holy, heavenly disposition and conduct which appears in them! Their faith, their love, their trust in God, their cheerful obedience, their hatred of sin, their desire for the good of all, their meekness and patience; how much do these advance them above the ignorant, pleasure-loving, proud, malignant, and ungodly world! How good then is that God who makes men good! And how little is the goodness of the best of men, compared to his unmeasurable goodness! Whenever your contact with holy men stirs up your love for them, rise by it at once to the God of saints, and let all be turned to him who gives all to them and to you.

And as the excellence of the saints, so their privilege and great advancement, should show you the goodness of God, who advances them. As often as you see a saint, however poor and mean in the world, you see a living monument of the abundant kindness of the Lord. You see a child of God, a member of Christ, an heir of heaven. You see one who has all his sins forgiven him, and is snatched as a brand out of the fire, and delivered from the power of Satan, and transferred into the kingdom of Christ. You see one for whom Christ has conquered the powers of hell; and one who is freed from the bondage of the flesh; and one who, from being the devil's slave, is made a priest, to offer up the sacrifices of praise to God. You see one who has the Spirit of God within him; and one who has daily intercourse with heaven, and access to God, and is dearly beloved by him in Christ. You see in flesh a companion of angels, and one who has the divine nature, and must shortly be above the stars in glory, and must be with Christ, and must love and magnify God forever. And is not the loveliness of God apparent, in such mercy bestowed upon sinful man? And should we not now begin to admire him in his saints, and glorify him in believers, who will come with thousands of his angels, to be glorified and admired in them at the last? (2 Thessalonians 1:10). Oh the abundant deliverances, preservations, provisions, encouragements, which all his servants receive from God! Who ever saw the just forsaken, even while they think themselves forsaken? "For the Lord promotes justice, and never abandons his faithful followers. They are permanently secure ... The law of their God controls their thinking; their feet do not slip. Take note of the one who has integrity. Observe the upright. For the one who promotes peace has a future" (Psalm 37:25, 28, 31, 37). "The Lord values the lives of his faithful followers" (Psalm 116:15). "You who love the Lord, hate evil! He protects the lives of his faithful followers; he delivers them from the power of the wicked. The godly bask in the light; the morally upright experience joy" (Psalm 97:10-11). "Love the Lord, all you faithful followers of his! The Lord protects those who have integrity, but he pays back in full the one who acts arrogantly" (Psalm 31:23).

**Direction 11. Do not insist so much on your desires for vision as to undervalue the lower apprehensions of faith; but love God by the way of faith, as in order to the love of intuition.**

We are exceedingly apt to be over-desirous of sight; and to take nothing as an object fit to affect us, which the senses do not perceive. When we have the surest evidence of the truth of things unseen, it hardly satisfies us, unless we may see or feel. And hereupon, our love for God is hindered; while we think of him as if he were not, or take the apprehensions of faith as if they were uncertain, and little different from a dream. Indeed, it proves the ground of most dangerous temptations to unbelief itself. While we take that knowledge which we have of God, in the way of faith, and the love and communion which is exercised by it, to be as nothing; we are next tempted to think that there is no true knowledge of God, and communion with him, to be attained. And when we have been searching and striving long, and find that we can reach no more, we are tempted to think that the soul of man is made but as the beasts, for present things, and is incapable of those higher things which are revealed in the gospel; and that if it were indeed a life to come, and man were made to enjoy his God, we should get nearer to him than we are, and know him more, and love him better.--But is it nothing, O presumptuous soul, to see God in a mirror, in order to a nearer sight? Is it nothing to have the heavenly Jerusalem described and promised to you, unless you see it and possess it? Will you travel to no place, but what you see all the way? Would you have no difference between earth and heaven? What can you have more in heaven, than immediate intuition? Would you have no life of trial, in the obedience of faith, before the life of enjoyment and reward? Or can you think that a life of sight and sense is fit for trial and preparation, to show who is fit for the rewarding life? Unthankful soul! Compare your state with that of animals: is it nothing for you to know your Maker in the works of his creation and providence, and in the revelations of grace, and the belief of promised immortality, unless you at once see him in his glory; when these your fellow-creatures know him not at all? Compare yourself now, with yourself as before, in the days of your ignorance and carnality. Did you then have any such knowledge of God, as you now undervalue? or any such communion with him, as you now account next to none? When the light first shone in your eyes, and you first had experience of the knowledge of God, you thought it something, and rejoiced in the light: if then you could have suddenly attained but to so much as you have now attained, would you have called it nothing? Would it not have seemed a greater treasure to you, than to have known both the Indies as your own? O do not be unthankful for the little which you have received, when God might have shut you out in that darkness which the greatest part of the world lies in, and have left you to yourself, to have desired no higher knowledge, than such as may feed your imagination, and pride, and lust. Are you so far drowned in flesh and sense, as to take intellectual apprehensions for dreams, unless your senses may see and feel? Will you take your soul, yourself, for nothing, because you are not to be seen or felt? Shall no subjects honor and obey their king, but they that have seen his court and him? Desire the fullest and the nearest sight, the purest and the strongest love; and desire and do not spare the life where all this will be had: but be careful not to be too hasty with God, and unthankful for the mercies of the way. Know better the difference between your travel and your home; and know what is fit for passengers to expect. Humbly submit to an obedient waiting in a life of faith; and make much of the testament of Christ, until you are of age to possess the inheritance. You must live, and love, and run, and fight, and conquer, and suffer by faith, if ever you will come to see and to possess the crown.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible(R); see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording below was fetched from labs.bible.org, not quoted from memory.
- **Where this file begins:** the range opens at point 2 of Direction 10 ("Study to know God as he appears more clearly to sinners"; the heading itself is in the file before this one). Direction 10's points 2 and 3 are carried here in full; Direction 11 begins at the bold heading.
- **Psalm 119:127 and "sweeter than honey":** Baxter's "it was sweeter to him than honey; he loved it above gold" is his own paraphrase of David. The honey comparison is not in Psalm 119:127; it is probably an allusion to a different verse (we have not confirmed which, possibly Psalm 19:10 or 119:103). We quote the NET only for the gold clause, which is what he cites.
- **Psalm 119:97:** Baxter's "it is my meditation all the day" is replaced by NET "All day long I meditate on it." Same sense. The NET text begins with the Hebrew letter heading "Mem," which we omit.
- **Psalm 25:8:** Baxter's (King James) "Good and upright is the Lord; therefore will he teach sinners in the way" is NET "kind and fair ... the right way to live." Baxter's point, that God's goodness is shown in his teaching sinners, is kept; the NET says "kind and fair" where the King James has "good and upright."
- **Psalm 119:47-48:** NET has "find delight in your commands." Baxter wrote "delight myself in thy commandments" and "lift my hands to thy commandments." Same sense.
- **1 John 3:1:** NET has "God's children," where Baxter (King James) had "sons of God." The NET clause also includes "and indeed we are!", which Baxter's quotation ends before in the printed text; we kept the NET clause whole because it is one sentence. The sense is the same. In the surrounding prose, "made his sons" is Baxter's own wording and is kept.
- **Psalm 16:1, 2:** Baxter's reference is kept as printed. His clause ("make them the people of your delight") matches the thought of the saints "in whom is all my delight" in the King James Psalm 16:3. In the NET, verses 1-2 do not mention the saints (verse 3 does: "God's chosen people who are in the land ..."), so the printed reference may be a slip for verse 3 or a verse-range habit; we kept his numbers and have not corrected them. **1 Thessalonians 4:9:** Baxter gives only the reference, so we give no quotation. (NET: "you yourselves are taught by God to love one another," which fits his phrase "taught by God to love.")
- **2 Thessalonians 1:10:** Baxter paraphrases ("to be glorified and admired in them"), and we keep his words. The NET reads "glorified among his saints and admired on that day among all who have believed." Baxter's "in" (as in the King James) is a little stronger than the NET's "among": he reads the saints as the ones in whom Christ is glorified; the NET's "among" is less specific. His point (we should begin to admire him in his saints now) is not changed. His printed reference "2 Thess. 1. 10" is given in modern form.
- **Psalm 37 (a composite):** Baxter strings four verses together in the King James wording, citing "25, 28, 31, 37." The sentence "Who ever saw the just forsaken" echoes verse 25; the NET has "I have never seen the godly abandoned." We give NET wording for verses 28, 31, and 37, with "..." between non-adjacent verses. **This flag matters:** Baxter's "the end of that man is peace" (King James, verse 37) is NET "the one who promotes peace has a future." The NET shifts the thought from the peaceful end of the upright (a peaceful death) to the future of the person who makes peace. Baxter's line "Mark the perfect man" is NET "Take note of the one who has integrity." Also, Baxter's "preserved for ever" (from verse 28) is NET "permanently secure"; and his "The law of his God is in his heart" is NET "The law of their God controls their thinking," which is a more interpretive rendering.
- **Psalm 116:15:** **flag.** Baxter's "Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints" is the King James. The NET reads "The Lord values the lives of his faithful followers," which does not mention death. Baxter's use of it (a comfort in the saints' deaths) depends on the King James reading; we have followed the convention and used the NET text, but it does not say "death." We have not looked at the NET's translator note on this verse, so we do not assert what it says.
- **Psalm 97:10-11:** **flag.** Baxter's "Light is sown for the righteous, and gladness for the upright in heart" (King James) is NET "The godly bask in the light; the morally upright experience joy." The NET loses the image of light being sown, which Baxter takes as a promise for the future. Baxter's "he preserveth the souls of his saints" is NET "He protects the lives of his faithful followers." His "Ye that love the Lord, hate evil" matches.
- **Psalm 31:23:** NET "pays back in full the one who acts arrogantly" replaces Baxter's "plentifully rewardeth the proud doer"; Baxter's "preserveth the faithful" is NET "protects those who have integrity." The sense of God paying back the proud is the same.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording; references are ours and are probable, not confirmed): "the mystery which the angels desirously pry into" (probably 1 Peter 1:12); "shed abroad his love upon our hearts" (probably Romans 5:5); "all things are now ready" and "compel him to come in" (probably Luke 14:17, 23); "take," "eat," "drink" (the words of institution of the Lord's Supper, probably Matthew 26:26-27 and 1 Corinthians 11:24-25); "a brand out of the fire" (probably Zechariah 3:2); "translated into the kingdom of Christ" (probably Colossians 1:13); "sacrifices of praise" (probably Hebrews 13:15); "the divine nature" (probably 2 Peter 1:4); "see God in a mirror" (probably 1 Corinthians 13:12); "the heavenly Jerusalem" (probably Hebrews 12:22). Baxter gave no references for these.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): loth, unwilling; delighteth, delights; oblivion, amnesty (an act of pardon and amnesty; compare the GLOSSARY entry for "act of oblivion"); wilful, willful; tendered, offered; appendants, appendages; Saviour, Savior; sensual, pleasure-loving; discourse, talk; conversation, conduct (in "disposition and conversation"); converse (contact), "your converse with holy men" is now "your contact with holy men"; amiable/amiableness, lovable/loveliness; presently, at once; audience (with God), access; translated (into the kingdom), transferred; desirously, eagerly; fruition, enjoyment; brutes, animals; fancy, imagination; infidelity, unbelief; meet (fit), fit; heretofore, before; betwixt, between; honour, honor; "pardoning, justifying, saving covenant" kept.
- **intuition** ("the love of intuition"; "immediate intuition"): kept. For Baxter it means direct, immediate sight or knowledge of God in heaven, in contrast to knowing him now through faith, which he calls "seeing God in a mirror."
- **vision; sight; apprehensions:** "vision" and "sight" are used for direct seeing of God; "apprehensions" (grasping with the mind) is kept as Baxter's word, and "lower apprehensions of faith" means the less direct knowing that faith gives.
- **"over-desirous of sight":** kept as written; he means being too eager for sight.
- **the Indies:** East and West Indies, a stock phrase for fabulous riches in his day. We keep it.
- **carnality:** kept (see GLOSSARY, "carnal"). **flesh and sense:** kept; "flesh" is his usual broad term for sinful or merely physical appetites.
- **testament of Christ:** kept; Baxter's image is of a will that a minor heir cannot yet claim, so the believer must live by faith until he comes of age for the inheritance.
- **"made his sons":** kept as Baxter's gendered language for believers adopted into God's family (the NET's "God's children" in 1 John 3:1 is broader).
- **"the sacraments, which are but its appendages"** and the passage about the Lord's Supper: these are Baxter's own view of the sacraments as seals of the covenant; he assumes the minister's words of invitation at the Lord's Table.
- **"good-will"** is now "good will" (spelling only).
- **Punctuation:** Baxter's "--" run-in marks are kept where they join two sentences' worth of thought ("make up the whole--they are expressed in every leaf and line"; "love him better.--But is it nothing"). Two parenthetical Scripture references that stood in the middle of sentences ("Psal. cxix. 127;" and "ver. 97") are now in the modern form with a closing period inside the parentheses; the sentence boundaries are as he had them.
- **Numbered points "2." and "3.":** these continue Direction 10's list as Baxter numbers it; point 1 is in the previous file.
