# Chapter 7: Against sinful despair, Directions 10-26

*This section continues Title 10 (against sinful despair) from the previous file; it begins at the start of Direction 10.*

**Direction 10. Be careful of being so blind or proud in your humility, as to think that you can be more willing to be a servant of Christ, than he is to be your Savior, or more willing to have grace, than God is to give it you, or more willing to come home to Christ, than he is to receive and welcome you.**

Either you are willing or unwilling to have Christ and grace, to be sanctified and freed from sin; if you are willing, Christ and his grace shall certainly be yours: indeed if you would have pardon without holiness, this cannot be, nor is there any promise of it; but if you would have Christ to be your Savior and King, and his Spirit to be your Sanctifier, and had rather be perfect in love and holiness than to have all the riches of the world, then you are in sincerity that which you would be in perfection: understand that God counts you to be what you truly desire to be. The great work of grace lies in the renewing of the will; if the will is sound, the man is sound. I do not mean the conquered, ineffectual faint wish of the wicked, that wish they could be free from pride, indulgence in physical pleasure, gluttony, drunkenness, lust, and greed, without losing any of their beloved honor, wealth, or pleasure; that is, when they think on it as the way to hell they do not like their sin, but wish they were rid of it, but when they think of it as pleasing their fleshly minds, they love it more, and will not leave it, because this is the prevailing thought and will. So Judas was unwilling to sell his Lord, as it was the betraying of the innocent, and the way to hell, but he was more willing as it was the way to get his pay. So Herod was unwilling to kill John the Baptist, as it was the murder of a prophet; but his willingness was the greater, as it was the pleasing of his girl, and the freeing himself from a troublesome reprover. But if your willingness to have Christ and perfect holiness is more than your unwillingness, and more than your willingness to keep your sin, and enjoy the honor, wealth, and pleasures of the world, then you have an undoubted sign of uprightness, and that love to grace, and desire after it, which nothing but grace itself gives. And if you are thus willing, it is great wrong to Christ to doubt of his willingness. For, 1. He is a greater lover of holiness than you are; and therefore cannot come behind you, in being willing of your holiness. 2. He is more merciful to you, than you are to yourself: his love and mercy are beyond your measure. 3. He has begun to you, and fully showed his willingness first. He died to prepare you a full remedy; he has drawn up the covenant; he has therein expressed his own consent, and entreats yours; he is the first in consenting, and is a suitor to you. Never did sinner yet begin to him in the world. Never was any willing of the match before him: his general offer of mercy, and covenant tendered to all, shows his willingness before they can show theirs by their acceptance. Never did man outdo him in willingness, and was more willing than he. Take this, sinner, as God's infallible truth. If the match breaks between Christ and you, and you are lost, it shall not be through his refusal, but through yours: and it cannot break any other way, no, not by the craft or force of all the devils in hell, but either because Christ is unwilling, or because you are unwilling; and on Christ's part it shall never break. And therefore if you are willing the match is made; and there is no danger but lest your heart draw back. If you are not willing, why do you complain for lack of that which you would not love? If you are willing, the covenant is then made, for Christ is more willing, and was willing first.

**Direction 11. Write out those sentences that contain the sense and substance of the gospel, and often read them.**

Write them on your very bedroom walls, and keep them always before your eyes; [375] and try whether they agree with the words of him that tempts you to despair: such as these which I here transcribe for you. "For this is the way God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life" (John 3:16).—"Now this is the basis for judging: that the light has come into the world and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their deeds were evil" (John 3:19).—"The one who believes in the Son of God has the testimony in himself; the one who does not believe God has made him a liar because he has not believed in the testimony that God has testified concerning his Son. And this is the testimony: God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. The one who has the Son has this eternal life; the one who does not have the Son of God does not have this eternal life" (1 John 5:10–12).—"He came to what was his own, but his own people did not receive him. But to all who have received him—those who believe in his name—he has given the right to become God's children" (John 1:11–12).—"And let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who wants it take the water of life free of charge" (Revelation 22:17).—"But you are not willing to come to me so that you may have life" (John 5:40).—"Everyone whom the Father gives me will come to me, and the one who comes to me I will never send away" (John 6:37).—"If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me, and ... drink" (John 7:37).—"Come, because everything is now ready" (Luke 14:17). And read Luke 15 often.

**Direction 12. Distinguish between sin seen and felt, and sin reigning to death; that you may not be so blinded as to think your sin greatest or your condition worst, when your sight and feeling of it are greatest.**

To see and feel your sin and misery is at least the ordinary preparation for recovery. To be dead is to be past feeling. [376] They that are most forsaken of God are most willing of their present condition, and most love their sin, and hate holiness and all that would reform them, and if they have power, will persecute them as enemies.

**Direction 13. Do not think that the troublesome strivings and temptations which weary you are the worst condition, or a sign of the victory of sin.**

It is rather a sign that you are not yet forsaken of God, while he bears witness in you against sin, and is yet following you with his dissuasions. Paul says, "For the flesh has desires that are opposed to the Spirit, and the Spirit has desires that are opposed to the flesh, for these are in opposition to each other, so that you cannot do what you want" (Galatians 5:17). Read Romans 7 from verse 14 to the end.

**Direction 14. Understand the difference between weak grace and no grace; that you may not think every lack of grace is a sign of total gracelessness.**

When you have opened in your complaints a long catalog of weaknesses, consider whether yet there is not a true desire to be better, and some degree of life with all these?

**Direction 15. Think well of the excellency of the least degree of special grace; that it is a seed of glory; the beginning of life eternal; the divine nature, and the image of God, and of greater worth than all the learning, wealth, and honor in the world.**

And do not be unthankful for so great a mercy, because you do not have more.

**Direction 16. Make a matter of conscience of observing the grace and mercy received, as well as the needs remaining and the sins committed, and of the thankful remembrance and mention of mercy, as much as the humble mention of sin.**

Think as often of mercy as of sin: talk of it as much to others; and mention it to God as much in prayer: this is your plain duty: if you will not do it, your willful unthankfulness for what you have received, may well leave you in distress without the comfort of it.

**Direction 17. Let your thoughts of God's goodness bear some proportion with your thoughts of his knowledge and his power.**

And then you will not be so apt to entertain false suspicions of it, and think of him as a man-hater, like the devil, nor to run away from him, that is the infinite, most attractive good.

**Direction 18. Record the particular kindnesses to yourself, by which God has testified his particular love to you; that they may stand as near and constant witnesses of his mercy and readiness to do you good, against your excessive fearfulness and despair.**

**Direction 19. Think how few there are in the world so likely for mercy as yourself.**

Do not look only on a few that are better than yourself; but think how five parts of the world are open unbelievers and heathens; and of the sixth part that are Christians, how few are reformed from popish and barbarous ignorance and superstition: and among Protestants how small is the number of them that are less in love with sin than yourself! I know that many wicked men abuse this comparison to presumption, but I know also that a Christian may and must use it against despair, and not think of God and the Redeemer as if he would save so few as are next to none at all.

**Direction 20. Remember that God commands faith and hope, and forbids unbelief and despair, [377] and that it is your sin: and will you sin more when you have sinned so much already?**

What if you see no other reason why you should hope, and why you should not despair, but God's command? Is not that enough? I charge you in the name of God obey him and do not despair. Do not sin willfully thus against him (Psalm 146:5; 31:24; Romans 8:24; 15:4, 13; Colossians 1:23; 1 Thessalonians 5:8; Hebrews 3:6; 6:11, 18, 19; Titus 1:2). Hope is your duty; and dare you plead against duty? Despair is your sin, and will you justify it? Indeed, consider what a great deal of comfort is in this; for if there were no hope of your salvation, God would never have made it your duty to hope, nor forbidden you to despair. He does not bid the devils nor the damned hope as he does you; he does not forbid them to despair as he does you: there is cause for this; he would have done it, if your condition were as hopeless as theirs is.

**Direction 21. If God forbids you to despair, it is certainly the devil that bids it.**

And will you knowingly obey the devil? What if the devil persuaded you to it openly with his own mouth? would you not know that it is bad which such an enemy draws you to? It seems to me this should be a very great comfort to you, to think that it is the devil that persuades you to despair? For that proves that you should not despair; and that proves that your case is not desperate but hopeful.

**Direction 22. Think where it leads: to despair is to give up all hopes of your salvation; and when you have no hope you will use no means; for to what purpose should a man seek for that which he has no hopes to find?**

And so when this weight is taken off, all the wheels stand still. The meaning of the devil has two parts: the first is, Do not hear, nor read, nor pray, nor seek advice, nor talk any more about it with good people, for there is no hope. And the next part is, either do away with yourself, or else sin boldly and take the pleasure of sin while you may; for there is no hope of any better. And do you think that either of these is from God? Or is it for your good? What is the meaning of all, but cast away your soul? While you hope, you will seek, and use some means; but to cast away hope is to cast away all. And have you so far lost self-love as to be yourself the doer of such a deed?

**Direction 23. Think what a wrong you do to the Father, the Savior, and the Sanctifier of souls, to think so poorly and despairingly of his grace, as if it were not able to prevail against your sin; and to obscure thus the glory of his redemption; and to believe the devil in his slandering, belittling, and dishonoring that in God, which he will have most glorified by sinners!**

**Direction 24. Consider what one person you can name in all the world, that ever perished or was rejected, that was willing in this life to be saved and sanctified by Christ, and had rather have Christ and perfect holiness than the treasures or pleasures of the world.**

Name me any one such person if you can: but I am sure you cannot: and do you fear that which never was done to any one; or think that Christ will begin with you?

**Direction 25. Up, man, and be doing, and resolve in spite of the devil that you will wait on God in the use of means, and cast yourself on Christ, and if you perish you will perish there.**

Do this, and you shall never perish. You cannot do worse than despair and give up all; nor can you please the devil more, nor displease God more, nor wrong Christ and the Spirit more. You are certain that you can lose nothing by trusting your soul on Christ, and hoping in him, and patiently using his means; do but this, and hope shall save you, when Satan by despair would damn you.

**Direction 26. Understand in what time and order it is that Christ gives his grace and saves his people from their sins; that he does it not all at once, but by degrees, and takes all the time of this present life to do it in.**

As able as your Physician is, he will not finish the cure till your life is finished. The next life is the state of absolute perfection; all things are imperfect here: do not despair therefore of all that you have not yet attained; your sin may be more mortified yet, and your grace yet more strengthened. If it is done before you come to judgment it is well for you: do your part in daily diligence: you plant and water, and he will give the increase. Read more of this before, part 2, against melancholy.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All quoted verses were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** Baxter divides Chapter 7 ("Directions for the Government of the Passions") into ten divisions headed "Tit." (Title), meaning a heading or division. This file falls within Title 10, against sinful despair. No Title heading falls inside this range; the Title ends with Direction 26, the last in this file.
- **Direction headings:** as in the finished files, the opening sentence(s) of each Direction are set in bold and the rest follows as body. Where the source gives a Direction only one or two sentences that form its heading, there is no body paragraph (Directions 18 and 23).
- **Direction 10, sentence boundaries:** in the source Direction 10's long first sentence ("Take heed ... welcome thee.") is the heading and the rest runs on in the same paragraph; we split them at that sentence boundary only.
- **John 3:19 (flag):** Baxter's King James "This is the condemnation" is in the NET "this is the basis for judging." Baxter sets the verse beside John 3:16 to show that people are condemned for refusing the light, not for lack of an offer; the NET's wording keeps that sense but loses the word "condemnation."
- **1 John 5:10–12:** the NET prints verse 10 in parentheses; we have dropped the parentheses inside Baxter's quotation. The King James "record" is "testimony," and "hath life" is "has this eternal life."
- **John 1:11–12 (flag):** the King James "power to become the sons of God" is in the NET "the right to become God's children." Baxter's quotation ends with "to them that believe on his name"; the NET puts "those who believe in his name" in the middle of verse 12.
- **Revelation 22:17 (flag):** Baxter quotes from "Let him that is athirst come" (the second half of the verse). His King James "whosoever will" is the point he leans on throughout this Title (that the willing are welcome); the NET's "the one who wants it" keeps that sense. "Freely" is "free of charge."
- **John 5:40 (flag, in Baxter's favor):** the King James "ye will not come unto me" is in the NET "you are not willing to come to me," which states even more plainly the point about willingness that Baxter makes in Direction 10.
- **John 6:37:** the King James "I will in no wise cast out" is in the NET "I will never send away."
- **John 7:37:** Baxter quotes the King James "let him come to me and drink." The NET divides the sentence differently: "let him come to me, and let the one who believes in me drink" (verses 37–38). We give the NET's words from verse 37 with "... drink" to keep the extent of Baxter's quotation.
- **Luke 14:17:** NET wording ("Come, because everything is now ready"). **Luke 15** is cited only.
- **Galatians 5:17 (flag):** the King James "the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh" is in the NET "the flesh has desires that are opposed to the Spirit." Baxter's point (the inner fight itself is a sign the Spirit is at work) is kept. "So that ye cannot do the things that ye would" is "so that you cannot do what you want." **Romans 7:14–25** is cited only.
- **Direction 12, "past feeling":** this is the King James phrase of Ephesians 4:19, which Baxter cites in note 376. The NET reads "Because they are callous." Baxter's own sentence is kept in his words.
- **Direction 20 references** (cited only, converted to modern form): Psalm 146:5; 31:24; Romans 8:24; 15:4, 13; Colossians 1:23; 1 Thessalonians 5:8; Hebrews 3:6; 6:11, 18, 19; Titus 1:2. In the source the references follow "Sin not wilfully thus against him," with only a comma; we put them in parentheses after that sentence.
- **Note 377** lists Psalms passages; the NET follows the English (King James) verse numbering of the Psalms, so Baxter's references stand as printed.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Judas's "hire" (Matthew 26:14–16); Herod and John the Baptist (Mark 6:17–28; Matthew 14:3–11); "the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4); "the image of God" (Colossians 3:10); "if you perish you will perish there" (perhaps an echo of Esther 4:16); "you plant and water, and he will give the increase" (1 Corinthians 3:6–7); "saves his people from their sins" (Matthew 1:21); "sin reigning to death" (Baxter's "unto death"; Romans 5:21; 6:12).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): velleity, faint wish (a weak, ineffectual wishing that does not carry the will); uneffectual, ineffectual; sensuality, indulgence in physical pleasure; covetousness, greed; accounteth, counts; hire, pay; damsel, girl (Herodias's daughter, who danced before Herod); John Baptist, John the Baptist; overwent, outdo (Baxter's "Never man overwent him" is given as "Never did man outdo him"); Take this sinner, Take this, sinner (a comma added to show "sinner" is the person addressed); complainest thou for want of, do you complain for lack of; chamber walls, bedroom walls; set them still before, keep them always before ("still" meaning always); dissuasives, dissuasions; want of grace, lack of grace; the wants remaining, the needs remaining (what is still lacking); catalogue, catalog; Make conscience of observing, Make a matter of conscience of observing; infidels, unbelievers; Methinks, It seems to me; Yea, Indeed; what a deal of comfort, what a great deal of comfort; whither it tendeth, where it leads; make away thyself, do away with yourself (that is, take your own life); Bethink thee, Consider; in despite of, in spite of; extenuating, belittling.
- **Kept as written:** "suitor" and "the match" (Baxter's image of Christ courting the soul in marriage); "willing of" (willing for); "Sanctifier"; "man-hater"; "Up, man, and be doing"; "the wheels stand still"; "mortified"; "prevailing"; "melancholy" (see GLOSSARY.md).
- **Direction 10, "if the will is sound, the man is sound":** Baxter's "man" here means a person generally.
- **Direction 19:** "five parts of the world are open unbelievers and heathens" and "the sixth part that are Christians" is Baxter's own estimate of the world's religions in his day; we make no claim about it. "Popish" is his polemical word for Roman Catholic teaching and practice; kept as his polemic.
- **Direction 21:** Baxter ends "Methinks this should be a very great comfort ... to despair?" with a question mark; kept.
- **Direction 22:** "the meaning of the devil" is the devil's intention. The first part is given in Baxter's form as a quoted "Do not ..." without quotation marks; kept so.
- **Direction 26, "As able as your Physician is":** Baxter capitalizes "Physician," meaning Christ; kept. "Read more of this before, part ii. against Melancholy" is Baxter's cross-reference, apparently to an earlier part of the work dealing with melancholy; a likely candidate is Chapter 6, Title 5, "Directions to the Melancholy about their Thoughts," but we have not confirmed which section Baxter means by "part ii." "Do you plant and water" (an emphatic command) is given as "you plant and water."
