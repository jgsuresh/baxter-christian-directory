# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 14

**Direction 14. In the present performance of your covenant with God, away with your former sinful life; and see that you do not sin willfully any more; but as far as you are able, avoid the temptations which have deceived you.**

God will never be reconciled to your sins: if he is reconciled to your person, it is as you are justified by Christ, and sanctified by the Spirit: he receives you as one that turns with repentance from sin to him. If you willfully or negligently go on in your former course of sin, you show that you were not sincerely resolved in your covenant with God.

I know infirmities and imperfections will not be so easily cast off, but will cling to you in your best obedience until the day of your perfection comes. But I speak of gross and willful sin; such as you can refrain from if you are but sincerely, though imperfectly, willing. [32]

Have you been a profane swearer or curser, or been in the habit of taking God's name in vain, or of backbiting, slandering, lying, or of ribald, filthy talk? It is in your power to refrain from these sins if you are but willing. Do not say, I fall into them through custom before I am aware; for that is a sign that you are not sincerely willing to forsake them. If you were truly penitent, and your will sincerely opposed to these sins, you would be more sensitive and afraid to offend, and resolved against them, and make a greater matter of them, and abhor them, and not commit them, and say, I did it before I was aware; no more than you would spit in the face of your father, or curse your mother, or slander your dearest friend, or speak treason against the king, and say, I did it through custom before I was aware. Sin will not be so played with by those that have been soundly humbled for it, and resolved against it.

Have you been a drunkard, or tippler, spending your precious hours in an alehouse, chattering over a mug, in the company of foolish, tempting sinners? It is in your power, if you are truly willing, to do so no more. If you love and choose such company, and places, and actions, and talk, how can you say you are willing to forsake them, or that your heart is changed? If you do not love and choose them, how can you commit them, when none compels you? No one carries you to the place; no one forces you to sin; if you do it, it is because you will do it, and love it. If you are in earnest with God, and will be saved indeed, and are not content to part with heaven for your cups and company, away with them at once, without delay.

Have you lived in lewdness, fornication, impurity, gluttony, gambling, idle amusements, indulgence in physical pleasure, to the pleasing of your flesh, while you have displeased God? O praise the patience and mercy of the Lord that you were not cut off all this while, and damned for your sin before you repented! And, as you love your soul, delay no longer; but make a stand, and go no further, not one step further in the way which you know leads to hell. If you know that this is the way to your damnation, and yet will go on, what pity do you deserve from God or man?

If you have been a greedy worldling, or an ambitious seeker of honor or advancement in the world, so that your gain, or rising, or reputation has been the game which you have followed, and has taken up your attention instead of God and eternal life; away now with these known deceits, and do not hunt after emptiness and vexation. You know beforehand what it will prove when you have overtaken it, and have enjoyed all that it can yield you; and how useless it will be as to your comfort or happiness at last.

Surely, if men were willing, they are able to refrain from such sins, and to make a stand, and look ahead, to prevent their misery: therefore God thus pleads with them: "Wash! Cleanse yourselves! Remove your sinful deeds from my sight. Stop sinning. Learn to do what is right," etc. (Isaiah 1:16–18). "Why pay money for something that will not nourish you? Why spend your hard-earned money on something that will not satisfy? Listen carefully to me and eat what is nourishing! Enjoy fine food. Pay attention and come to me. Listen, so you can live. Then I will make an unconditional covenantal promise to you" (Isaiah 55:2–3). "Seek the Lord while he makes himself available; call to him while he is nearby! The wicked need to abandon their lifestyle and sinful people their plans. They should return to the Lord, and he will show mercy to them, and to their God, for he will freely forgive them" (Isaiah 55:6–7). Christ supposes that the foresight of judgment may restrain men from sin when he says, "Don't sin any more, lest anything worse happen to you" (John 5:14; 8:11). Can the presence of men restrain a fornicator; and the presence of the judge restrain a thief, indeed, or the foresight of the assizes? And shall not the presence of God, with the foresight of judgment and damnation, restrain you? Remember that impenitent sin and damnation are joined together. If you will cause one, God will cause the other. Choose one, and you shall not choose whether you shall have the other. If you will have the serpent, you shall have the sting.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced exactly as in the source. A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[32] Næ illi falsi sunt, qui diversissimas res pariter expectant, ignaviæ voluptatem et præmia virtutis. Sallust. (Truly they are mistaken who expect two very different things alike: the pleasure of idleness and the rewards of virtue. Sallust.) Tenebit te diabolus sub specie libertatis addictum, ut sit tibi liberum peccare, non vivere: Captivum te tenet author scelerum, compedes tibi libidinis imposuit, et undique te sepsit armatâ custodiâ; Legem tibi dedit ut licitum putes omne quod non licet; et vivum te in eternæ mortis foveam demersit. Hugo Etherianus de Animar. regressu, cap. 9. (The devil will hold you bound under the appearance of freedom, so that you are free to sin, not to live. The author of crimes holds you captive; he has put the fetters of lust on you and has hedged you in on every side with an armed guard; he has given you a law, so that you think lawful everything that is not lawful; and he has plunged you, alive, into the pit of eternal death. Hugh Etherianus, *On the Return of Souls*, chapter 9.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Isaiah 1:16–18:** Baxter cites verses 16–18 but quotes only the start. We quote the NET for the same stretch: "Wash! Cleanse yourselves! Remove your sinful deeds from my sight. Stop sinning. Learn to do what is right."
- **Isaiah 55:3 (important for Baxter's wording):** Baxter's King James quotation ends "I will make an everlasting covenant with you." The NET reads "I will make an unconditional covenantal promise to you, just like the reliable covenantal promises I made to David." We stopped the quotation where Baxter did, so it ends "unconditional covenantal promise to you." This loses the word "everlasting," which Baxter, writing about the covenant of grace, may be leaning on.
- **Isaiah 55:6–7:** "Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts" is rendered in the NET as "the wicked need to abandon their lifestyle and sinful people their plans." The NET's "lifestyle" is a modern word for what the King James calls "his way." **he will abundantly pardon** becomes "he will freely forgive."
- **John 5:14; 8:11:** Baxter's "Sin no more, lest a worse thing come unto thee" matches John 5:14. The NET text of John 8:11 reads "do not sin any more." The passage about the woman caught in adultery (John 7:53–8:11) is bracketed in the NET because it is missing from many early manuscripts. Baxter cites it as Scripture without any doubt.
- **profane swearer or curser** (paragraph 3): someone who uses God's name carelessly, or curses. **ribald:** coarse, crude.
- **tippler** (paragraph 4): someone who drinks small amounts frequently, a habitual drinker. **alehouse:** a tavern. **chattering over a mug:** Baxter's "prating over a pot."
- **lewdness:** Baxter's "wantonness." **impurity:** his "uncleanness." **gambling:** his "gaming." **idle amusements:** his "pastimes." **indulgence in physical pleasure:** his "sensuality."
- **greedy:** Baxter's "covetous." **advancement:** his "preferment." **emptiness and vexation:** his "vanity and vexation," an echo of Ecclesiastes.
- **assizes** (last paragraph): the periodic sessions of the royal courts where criminal cases were tried. Kept as Baxter's word.
- **sensitive** (paragraph 3): Baxter's "tender," meaning conscience-sensitive, quick to feel sin.
- **Hugh Etherianus; Sallust** (note 32): the same Hugh Etherianus as in note [30]; Sallust is the Roman historian.
