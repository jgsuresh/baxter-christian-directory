# Chapter 6: Title 2, Directions 1-3 (your end, God, and the world to come)

**Title 2. Directions to furnish the mind with good thoughts.** [302]

To have the mind well furnished with matter for holy and profitable thoughts is necessary to all that have the use of reason, though not to all alike. But I shall here present you only with such materials as are necessary to a holy life, and to be used in our daily walk with God; and not deal with such as are proper to pastors, magistrates, or other special callings, though I may give some general directions also for students at the end of this.

*In the margin: Our own interest and end.*

**Direction 1. Understand well your own interest and great concerns, and be well resolved what you live for, and what is your true happiness and end; and then this will command your thoughts to serve it.**

The end is what the means are all chosen for, and used for. A man's estimation directs his intention and designs; and his intention and designs command his thoughts. These will certainly have the first and chief, the most serious, and practical, and effective thoughts; though some side-thoughts may run out another way: as the miller will be sure to keep so much water as is necessary to grind his grain, though he may let that run by which he thinks he has no need of; as you gather in all your grain and fruit for yourselves at harvest, though perhaps you will leave some scatterings which you do not value much, for any that will to gather; so whatever a man takes for his ultimate end and true happiness will have the store and stream of his thoughts, though he may scatter some few upon other things, when he thinks he may do it without any detriment to his main design. As a traveler's face is ordinarily toward his journey's end, though so far as he thinks it does not stop him, he may look behind him, or on each side; so our main end will in the main carry on our thoughts. And therefore unholy souls, that do not know practically any higher end than the prosperity and pleasure of the flesh, and the plenty and honor of the world, cannot possibly exercise any holy government over their thoughts; but their minds and consciences are defiled, and their thoughts made carnal as is their end. Nor is there any possibility of curing their vicious, wicked thoughts, and of ordering them acceptably to God, but by curing their worldly, carnal minds, and causing them to change their designs and ends. And this must be by understanding what is their interest. Only know well what it is that is most necessary for you, and best for you, and it will change your hearts, and save your souls. Know this, and your thoughts will never lack matter to be employed on; nor will they be allowed to wander much afield. Therefore it is that the expectation of death, and the thought of coming at once to judgment, do usually more effectively supply the mind with the wisest and most useful thoughts than the most learned book or ordinary means can. That which tells a man best what he has to do best tells him what he has to think about. But the approach of death, and the appearance of eternity, best tells a dull and fleshly sinner what he has to do; this tells, and tells him bluntly, that he must at once search his heart and life, and judge himself as one that is going to the final judgment; and that it is high time for him to look out for the remedy for his sin and misery, etc.; and therefore it will command his thoughts this way. Ask any lawyer, physician, or tradesman, what commands his thoughts; and you will find that his interest, and his ends, and work command them. Know what it is to have an immortal soul, that must live in joy or woe forever, and what it is to be always so near to the irreversible, determining sentence, and what it is to have this short uncertain time, and no more, to make our preparation in, and then it is easy to foretell which way your thoughts will go. A man that knows his house is on fire will be thinking how to quench it; a man that knows he is entering into a mortal sickness will be thinking how to cure it. There is no better way to have your thoughts both furnished and set in motion rightly than to know your interest, and right end.

*In the margin: God.*

**Direction 2. Know God rightly, and behold him by the eye of an effective faith, and you shall never lack matter for holy thoughts.**

His greatness and continual presence with you may command your thoughts, and awe them, and keep them from masterless wanderings. His wisdom will find them continual employment, upon the various, excellent, and delightful subjects of his natural and supernatural revelation; but nowhere so much as upon himself. In God you may find matter for your thoughts and affections, most high and excellent, delighting the mind with a continual sweetness, affording ever fresh delights, though you meditate on him a thousand years, or to all eternity. You may better say that the ocean has not water enough for you to swim in, or that the earth has not room enough for you to tread upon, than that there is not matter enough in God for your longest meditations, and most delighting, satisfying thoughts. The blessed angels and saints in heaven will find enough in God alone to employ their minds to all eternity. Oh horrid darkness and atheism that yet remains on our hearts! that we should lack matter for our thoughts, to keep them from feeding upon air or filth! or lack matter for our delight, to keep our minds from begging it at the creature's door, or hungering for the husks that feed the swine! when we have the infinite God, omnipotent, omniscient, most good and bountiful, our life, and hope, and happiness, to think on with delight.

*In the margin: The world to come.*

**Direction 3. If you have but an eye of faith, to see the things of the unseen world, as revealed in the sacred word, you cannot lack matter to employ your thoughts.**

Scripture is the mirror in which you may see the other world. There you may see the Ancient of Days, the Eternal Majesty shining in his glory, for the making happy of holy, glorified spirits. There you may see the human nature advanced above angels, and enjoying the highest glory next to the uncreated Majesty; and Christ reigning as the King of all the world, and all the angels of God obeying, honoring, and worshiping him. You may see him sending his angels on his gracious messages, to the lowest members of his body, the little ones of his flock on earth; you may see him interceding for all his saints, and procuring their peace and welcome with the Father; and preparing for their reception when they pass into those mansions, and welcoming them one by one as they pass from here. There you may see the glorious, celestial society attending, admiring, extolling, worshiping the Great Creator, the Gracious Redeemer, and the Eternal Spirit, with unceasing, glorious, and harmonious praise; you may see them burning in the delicious flames of holy love, drawn out by the vision of the face of God, and by the streams of love which he continually pours out upon them; you may see the magnetic attraction of the uncreated love, and the happy-making union of the attracted love of holy spirits, thus united to God by Christ, and feasting everlastingly upon him; you may see the raptures of joy, and the unspeakable pleasures, which all these blessed spirits have in this transporting sight, and love, and praise. You may see the ecstasies of joy which possess the souls of those that have newly passed from the body, and escaped the sins and miseries of this world, and find there such sudden rapturous welcome, unspeakable beyond their former expectations, conceptions, or belief. You may see there with what wonder, what pity, what loathing and detestation, those holy, glorified souls look down upon earth, on the negligence, contempt, indulgence in physical pleasure, and profaneness of the dreaming and distracted world! You may see there what you shall be forever, if you are the holy ones of Christ, and where you must dwell, and what you must do, and what you shall enjoy. All this you may so know by sound believing as to be carried to it as sincerely as if your eyes had seen it (Hebrews 11:1; 2 Corinthians 5:7). And yet can your thoughts be idle, or carnal, or worldly and sinful, for lack of work? Are your meditations dry and barren for lack of matter to employ them? Does the fire of love or other holy affections go out for lack of fuel to feed it? Are not heaven and eternity spacious enough for your minds to range in? Is not such a world as that sufficient for you to study, with fresh and delightful variety of discoveries from day to day? or that which is more delightful than variety? Would you have more matter, or higher and more excellent matter, or sweeter and more pleasant matter, or matter which more nearly concerns yourselves? Get that faith which all that shall be saved live by, which makes things absent as operative (in some measure) as if they were present, and that which will be as if it now were, and that which is unseen as if it were now open to your eyes; and then your thoughts will lack neither matter to work upon, nor altogether a stirring into action.

If this were not enough, I might tell you what faith can see also in hell, which is not unworthy of your serious thoughts. [303] What work is there? what dreadful complaints and lamentations? what self-tormentings, and what sense of God's displeasure, and for what? But I will wholly pass this by, that you may see there is delightful work enough for your thoughts, and that I set you no unpleasant task.

*In the margin: The work of love.*

## Translator's notes

- **Title:** "Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," that is, a heading or division. Chapter 6 has six such divisions; this file begins Title 2. The source prints the Title 2 heading on its own line with note [302] attached; we keep the marker after the bold heading.
- **Direction headings:** as in the finished files, the opening sentence of each Direction (what the source prints right after "_Direct._ I.", "II.", "III.") is set in bold, and the rest of Baxter's paragraph follows as body text. The words are unchanged by this layout.
- **Range end:** the last line of this range is the margin note "The work of love," which heads the next Direction (Direction 4). The Direction itself begins in the next file.
- **Scripture:** Baxter gives only references in this section (Hebrews 11:1; 2 Corinthians 5:7, in Direction 3), with no quoted words, so no NET text is quoted here. Scripture translation for the project is the NET Bible; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording, references not in his text): "minds and consciences are defiled" (Titus 1:15, where the King James has "defiled" and the NET has "corrupted"); "the husks that feed the swine" (Luke 15:16, the prodigal son; the King James has "husks that the swine did eat," the NET "the carob pods the pigs were eating"); "the Ancient of Days" (Daniel 7:9, 13, 22); "the little ones of his flock" (compare Luke 12:32, "little flock"); "those mansions" (John 14:2, where the King James has "mansions" and the NET has "dwelling places"; we keep Baxter's word); "interceding for all his saints" (compare Hebrews 7:25; Romans 8:34). These identifications are ours.
- **Note [302], "Part 2":** the source reads "Part ii." This probably points to the directions on prayer, hearing, reading, and the Lord's Supper in a later part of the *Christian Directory*; we have not checked the cross-reference.
- **Note [303], *The Life of Faith*:** Baxter's own treatise, which he says is on Hebrews 11:1. We have not checked the edition he means.
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** concernments, concerns; felicity, happiness; "The end is it that," "The end is what"; chiefest, chief; effectual, effective; by-thoughts, side-thoughts; grist and corn, grain; "for any that will to gather" kept (it means "for anyone who wishes to gather"); cogitations, thoughts (twice); want (lack), lack (throughout); suffered, allowed; "wander much abroad," "wander much afield"; presently, at once; "do use more effectually to supply" ("are accustomed to supply"), "do usually more effectively supply"; learnedst, most learned; think on, think about (in the sentence on what a man has to do); roundly, bluntly; &c., etc.; acted aright, set in motion rightly ("acted" here means "actuated," set working); aright, rightly; vagaries (wanderings), wanderings; delectable, delightful; suavity, sweetness; still (always), ever; glass, mirror; "the felicitating of," "the making happy of"; entertainment (reception, welcome), welcome; uncessant, unceasing; "felicitating closure," "happy-making union" ("closure" here means a joining or union); ravishments, raptures; ravishing entertainment, rapturous welcome; conceivings, conceptions; sensuality, indulgence in physical pleasure; expatiate (roam at large), range; nearlier, more nearly; direful, dreadful; "an actuating excitation," "a stirring into action."
- **Kept words:** *estimation* (what a person values or esteems); *interest* (what truly concerns or benefits a person, not curiosity); *end* (goal, purpose); *vicious* (full of vice, not savage); *carnal* (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh); *affections* (emotions and desires; see GLOSSARY.md); *the creature* ("the creature's door": created things, as opposed to the Creator); *operative* (having effect); *profaneness* (irreverence toward holy things); *determining sentence* (the final verdict at the judgment).
- **"And yet can your thoughts be idle...":** Baxter's run of questions is kept as printed, including the lowercase "or that which is more delightful than variety?" after a question mark.
