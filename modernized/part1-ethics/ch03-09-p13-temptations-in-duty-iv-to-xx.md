# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 13 of 13)

*This section begins partway through Grand Direction 9, in the list of temptations to neglect or spoil holy duty, each answered by a Direction; it opens with the end of Direction 3 (numbered here as Baxter has it) and goes on through Direction 20. The section's last line is the margin note that opens the next section.*

**Direction 3. Know well your sin, and vileness, and desert, and the justice and holiness of God; and then you will see that if Christ does not reconcile you to God, and does not justify you by his blood, and does not sanctify and help you by his Spirit, and make you sons of God, and does not intercede for you, there is no access to God, nor standing in his sight.**

**Temptation 4.** The tempter would have you pray hypocritically, with the tongue only, without the heart: to put off God in a few customary words, with seeming to pray (as people do the poor, James 2, with a few empty words) either in a form of words not understood, or not considered, or not felt and much heeded; or in more gross hypocrisy, praying for the holiness which they will not have, and against the sin which they will not part with.

**Direction 4.** O fear the holy, jealous, heart-searching God, who hates hypocrisy, and will be worshipped seriously in spirit and truth, and will be sanctified by all who draw near him (Leviticus 10:3); and says they "worship him in vain, who draw near him with the lips, when the heart is far from him" (Matthew 15:8-9). See God by faith, as present with you, and know yourself, and it will awaken you to seriousness. See Hebrews 4:13; Hosea 8:12, 13.

**Temptation 5.** He would destroy faith and hope, and make you doubt whether you will get anything by duty.

**Direction 5.** But, 1. Why should God command it, and promise us his blessing if he did not mean to perform it? 2. Remember God's infiniteness, and omnipresence, and all-sufficiency: he is as truly with you as you are there: he upholds you: he shows by his mercies that he regards you; and by his regarding lower things: and if he regards you, he does regard your duties. It is all one with him to hear your prayers, as if he had never another creature to regard and hear. Believe then, and hope and wait on him.

**Temptation 6.** Sometimes the tempter will promise you more by holy duty than God does, and make you expect deliverance from every enemy, want, and sickness, and a speedier deliverance of soul than God ever promised; and all this is to make you throw away all as empty, and think God fails you, when you miss your expectations.

**Direction 6.** But God will do all that he promises, but not all that the devil or you yourselves promise. See what God promises in his word. That is enough for you. Make that and no more the end of your duties.

**Temptation 7.** The tempter usually would draw you from the heart and life of duty, by ascribing too much to the outside: laying too much on the bare doing of the work, the giving of the alms, the hearing of the sermons, the saying of the words, the polished expression, order, manner; which in their places are all good, if animated with spirit, life, and seriousness.

**Direction 7.** Look most and first to the soul in duty, and the soul of duty. The picture of meat does not feed; the picture of fire does not warm; fire and shadows will not nourish us: God does not love dead carcasses instead of spiritual worship: we pay no attention to words ourselves, further than they express the heart. Let the outer part have but its due.

**Temptation 8.** He tempts you to rest in a forced, affected, counterfeit fervency, stirred up by a desire to please others.

**Direction 8.** Look principally at God and holy motives, and less at men, so that all your fire may be holy, fetched from heaven.

**Temptation 9.** He would keep you in a lazy, sluggish coldness, to read, and hear, and pray as if asleep, as if you did not do it at all.

**Direction 9.** Awake yourselves with the presence of God, and the great concern of what you are about, and do not yield to your sloth.

**Temptation 10.** He would make you bring a divided, distracted heart to duty, that is half occupied with your worldly business.

**Direction 10.** Remember God is jealous, your business with him is great, much depends on it; call off your hearts, and do not let them stay behind: all the powers of your souls are little enough in such a work (Ezekiel 33:31).

**Temptation 11.** Ignorance, lack of skill, and unfamiliarity with duty is a great impediment to most.

**Direction 11.** Learn by study joined with practice. Do not be weary, and difficulties will be overcome.

**Temptation 12.** Putting duty out of its place, and neglecting the season that is fittest, makes it often done carelessly.

**Direction 12.** Redeem time, and dispatch other business, so that idleness does not deprive you of leisure; and do everything in order.

**Temptation 13.** Neglecting one duty is the tempter's snare to spoil another. If he can keep you from reading, you will not understand well what you hear. If he keeps you from meditating, you will not digest what you hear or read. If he keeps you from hearing, you will lack both material and life for prayer, and meditation, and conversation. If he keeps you from godly company, you will be hindered in all, and in the practice: no one is left out, but you are disadvantaged by it in all the rest.

**Direction 13.** Observe how one duty helps another, and take all together, each one in its place.

**Temptation 14.** Sometimes the tempter calls you off to other duty, and puts in ill-timed urgings toward something that in its time is good; he interrupts prayer with meditation, he sets seeming truth against love, and peace, and harmony.

**Direction 14.** Always know which duties are greatest, and which is the right season for each, and do everything in order.

**Temptation 15.** He spoils duty, by causing you to do it only as a duty, and not as a means for the good of your own souls; or only as a means, and not as a duty. If you do it only as a duty, then you will not be quickened to it by the ends and benefits, nor carried by hope, nor suit everything to the end, nor be so fervent or vigorous in it, as the sense of your own good would make you be. And if you do it only as a means, and not as a duty, then you will give up or faint, when you lack or doubt the success: whereas the sense of both would make you vigorous and steady.

**Direction 15.** Keep under the sense of God's authority, so that you may feel yourselves bound to obey him, whatever the success; and may resolve to wait in an obedient way. And along with this, marvel at his wisdom in fitting all duties to your benefit, and commanding you nothing but what is for your own or others' good, or for his honor: and mark the reason and tendency of all, and your own need.

**Temptation 16.** The tempter hinders you in duty, as well as from duty, by setting you to quarrel with the minister, the words, the company, the manner, the circumstances; so that these things may divert your thoughts from the matter, or distract your mind with groundless scruples.

**Direction 16.** Pray and labor for a clear judgment, and an upright, self-judging, humble heart, which dwells most at home, and looks most at the spiritual part, and does not crave being different.

**Temptation 17.** The tempter spoils duty by your inconsistency; while you read or pray so seldom that you have lost the benefit of one duty before you come to another, and cool through the gaps between.

**Direction 17.** Remember that it is not your amusement, but your calling, and is to your soul as eating is to your bodies.

**Temptation 18.** Sometimes Satan corrupts duty by people's private passions, interests, and opinions, making them, in preaching and praying, vent their own notions and ill temper, and rail against those who differ from them or offend them, and profane the name and work of God; or proudly seek the praise of men.

**Direction 18.** Remember that God is most jealous in his worship, and hates hypocritical profaneness above all profaneness. Search your hearts, and mortify your passions; and especially selfishness, remembering that it is a poisonous and insinuating sin, and will easily hide itself with a cloak of zeal.

**Temptation 19.** False-hearted holding back is a most accursed corrupter of holy duty; when the soul is not wholly given up to God, but sets about duty from some ordinary motive; as, because it is in credit, or to please some friend, purposing to try it for a while, and leave it if they do not like it.

**Direction 19.** Fear God, you hypocrite, and do not waver between two opinions. If the Lord is God, obey and serve him with all your heart; but if the devil and the flesh are better masters, follow them, and let him go.

**Temptation 20.** Lastly, The tempter greatly hinders holy duty by wandering thoughts, and melancholy perplexities, and a rush of temptations, which torment and distract some Christians, so that they cry out, I cannot pray, I cannot meditate; and are weary of duty, and even of their lives.

**Direction 20.** This shows the malice of the tempter, and your weakness; but if you would rather be delivered from it, it does not hinder your acceptance with God. Read for this what I have said in Chapter 5, Part 2, at large; especially in my Directions to the Melancholy.

I have been forced to put off many things briefly here, which deserved a larger handling; and I must now omit the discovery of those temptations by which Satan keeps men in sin, when he has drawn them into it. 2. And those by which he causes declining in grace, and apostasy. 3. And those by which he takes away the comfort of true believers; because otherwise this direction would swell to a treatise; and most will think it too long and tedious already, though the brevity which I use, to avoid being long-winded, does wrong to the matter all through. Acquaintance with temptations is needed for our overcoming them.

*In the margin: For serving Christ our Master in good works.* (This margin note stands at the end of the range and belongs to the section that follows.)

## Translator's notes

- **Labels and numbering:** Baxter's "_Tempt._ IV." and "_Direct._ IV." are rendered "Temptation 4." and "Direction 4."; the Directions here are the numbered items inside Grand Direction 9, and the numbering follows his. The range begins at Direction 3, whose first part is in the previous section. Direction 3's heading is given in bold as Baxter's heading sentence; the text before it is not in this range.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Leviticus 10:3 (Direction 4), NET wording changes Baxter's sense.** Baxter's "will be sanctified of all that draw near him" is the King James idea ("I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me"). We kept his words, modernized, because the NET reads differently: "Among the ones close to me I will show myself holy, and in the presence of all the people I will be honored." Baxter leans on the thought that God requires worshippers to treat him as holy; the NET says God will show himself holy to those near him, which is a related but not identical point.
- **Matthew 15:8-9 (Direction 4):** Baxter's quotation is a blend and loose paraphrase of the verses (his order runs "in vain" first, then "draw nigh with the lips, when the heart is far from him"). We kept his wording, modernized, and in his quotation marks. The NET reads: "This people honors me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me, and they worship me in vain, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men." The NET's "teaching as doctrines the commandments of men" is not in Baxter's quotation.
- **Ezekiel 33:31 (Direction 10):** cited as a reference only, with no quotation. NET: "They come to you in crowds, and they sit in front of you as my people. They hear your words, but do not obey them. For they talk lustfully, and their heart is set on their own advantage." Baxter cites it for the hearer whose heart is elsewhere.
- **Hebrews 4:13; Hosea 8:12, 13 (Direction 4):** references only, converted to modern form; not quoted.
- **James 2 (Temptation 4):** Baxter's "as they do the poor, James ii." alludes to James 2:15-16, where someone says "Go in peace, keep warm and eat well" to a needy person but gives nothing. We converted his chapter reference and did not add verses to the text; the verse numbers are our identification, not Baxter's.
- **Unmarked allusion:** Direction 19, "halt not between two opinions. If the Lord be God, obey and serve him" echoes 1 Kings 18:21 (Elijah on Mount Carmel). Baxter gives no reference; we kept his wording, modernized ("do not waver between two opinions"), and did not replace it with NET wording.
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** *fetched* kept; *unskilfulness and unacquaintedness* to "lack of skill and unfamiliarity"; *concernment* to "concern"; *vain* (empty) to "empty"; *conceits* to "notions"; *spleen* to "ill temper"; *inveigh against* to "rail against"; *intermissions* to "the gaps between"; *divertisement* to "amusement"; *handsome* (expression) to "polished"; *to take with others* to "to please others"; *unseasonable motions* to "ill-timed urgings"; *causeless scruples* to "groundless scruples"; *slightly* (done slightly) to "carelessly"; *reservedness* to "holding back"; *common motive* to "ordinary motive"; *hurry of temptations* to "rush of temptations"; *discomforteth* to "takes away the comfort of"; *admire* to "marvel at"; *affecteth not singularity* to "does not crave being different"; *inconstancy* to "inconsistency"; *much regarded* to "much heeded"; *regard not words* to "pay no attention to words". These are our best judgments of his sense.
- **Terms kept:** *tempter* (the devil); *jealous* (zealous for his own honor; a standing sense in Baxter, not suspicion); *mortify* (put to death); *melancholy* (see GLOSSARY); *meditation* (his regular term for focused reflection on spiritual truth). In Temptation 13, Baxter's "conference" is rendered "conversation." "Redeem time" (Direction 12) probably echoes Ephesians 5:16; it is unmarked and we kept his phrase.
- **Direction 3 (opening)** has "make you sons of God" as Baxter wrote it; "sons" is kept as in his text.
- **Temptation 13:** "no one is omitted" is rendered "no one is left out"; the sense is that no duty can be dropped without harm to the rest.
- **Temptation 15:** "nor fit all to the end" rendered "nor suit everything to the end"; Baxter's meaning is that the person does not shape all his actions toward the goal. We keep the grammar as he has it; the "to" and "then you will not" run is slightly uneven in the source, and we left it.
- **Cross-reference not checked:** "Chapter 5, Part 2" and "my Directions to the Melancholy" (Direction 20) refer to another place in the *Christian Directory*; we have not verified the reference.
- **"I have been forced to put off ..." paragraph:** the "2." and "3." are Baxter's own run-in numbers continuing a list begun in the first sentence (with the first item implicit); kept as printed.
- **Final line:** the margin note "For serving Christ our Master in good works" is in the source at line 14565, at the end of this range; it heads the next section and is kept here only so no line is dropped.
