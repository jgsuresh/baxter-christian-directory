# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 11 of 13)

*This section begins at Direction 39 of Grand Direction 9 (Baxter's Directions against Satan's temptations) and runs through Direction 48, the end of that series. It then opens a new series, "Temptations to draw us off from Duty," in which Baxter restarts the numbering at 1 and runs through Direction 6. The next file continues from Temptation 7.*

**Direction 39.** Remember that the least degree of sin is sin, and "the payoff of sin is death" (Romans 6:23). Do not think that you have escaped well if your hearts have taken any of the infection, or if you have been wounded anywhere, though it might have been worse. If the tempter had tempted you no further than to a lustful, malicious, or proud thought or word, you would perceive that if he prevails, he conquers: so may you when he gets this much, by a shameless asking more.

**Temptation 40.** He tempts us sometimes to be so fearful and careful against one sin, or about some one danger, as to be mindless of some other, and lie open to his temptation. Like a fencer, who will seem to aim all at one place, so that he may strike you in another while you are guarding that. Or like an enemy, who gives all the alarm at one end of the city, so that he may draw the people there, while he storms in another place. So Satan makes some so afraid of worldliness that they do not watch against idleness; or so fearful of hardheartedness, and deadness, and hypocrisy, that they do not watch against passion, neglect of their callings, or dejectedness; or so fearful of sinning or being deceived about their salvation, that they do not fear the lack of love, and joy, and thankfulness for all the mercy they have received, nor the neglect of holy praise to God.

**Direction 40.** Remember that as obedience must be entire and universal, so is Satan's temptation against all parts of our obedience; and our care must extend to all if we will escape. It would cure your excessive fear in some one point if you extended it to all the rest.

**Temptation 41.** Sometimes, by the suddenness of a temptation, he surprises men before they are aware.

**Direction 41.** Be never unarmed nor off your watch; especially as to thoughts, or sudden passions, or rash words, which are usually committed for lack of deliberation.

**Temptation 42.** Sometimes he uses a violent earnestness, especially when he gets passion on his side. So that reason is borne down; and the sinner says, I could not help it.

**Direction 42.** But remember that the very eager unruliness of your passion is a sin itself: and that none can compel you to sin: and that reason must deliberate and rule; or else any murder or wickedness may have the excuse of urgent passions.

**Temptation 43.** Sometimes he uses the violence of men: they threaten men, to frighten them into sin.

**Direction 43.** But are not God and his threatenings more to be feared? Do men threaten imprisonment, or death, or ruin? And does not God threaten everlasting misery? And can he not defend you from all that man shall threaten, if it is best for you? See the portion of the fearful, Revelation 21:8.

**Temptation 44.** Sometimes a variety of temptations distracts men, so that they do not look to all at once.

**Direction 44.** Remember that one part of the city unguarded may lose the whole in a general assault.

**Temptation 45.** Sometimes he stops, to make us secure, and lay aside our arms, and then he surprises us.

**Direction 45.** Take heed of security, and Satan's ambushes. Distinguish between cessation and conquest. You do not conquer every time that you have rest and quietness from temptation. Until the sin is hated, and the contrary grace or duty is in practice, you have not at all overcome: and when that is done, yet do not trust the devil or the flesh; nor think the war will be shorter than your lives, for one assault will begin where the former ended. Make use of every cessation only to prepare for the next encounter.

**Temptation 46.** He will tempt you to take striving for overcoming; and to think, because you pray and make some resistance, that sin is conquered; and because your desires are good, all is well.

**Direction 46.** But all who fight do not overcome. "If anyone competes as an athlete, he will not be crowned as the winner unless he competes according to the rules" (2 Timothy 2:5). "...many, I tell you, will try to enter and will not be able to" (Luke 13:24).

**Temptation 47.** He follows the sinner with frequency and persistence, until he wearies him and makes him yield.

**Direction 47.** 1. Remember that Christ is as persistent with you to save you as the devil can be to damn you; and which then should prevail? 2. Be as constant in resistance; be as often in prayer and other confirming means. Do as Paul, 2 Corinthians 12:7, 8, who prayed three times (as Christ did in his agony) when the thorn in the flesh was not removed. 3. Do not tempt the tempter by giving him encouragement. A faint denial is an invitation to ask again. Give him quickly a flat denial, and put him out of hope, if you would shorten the temptation.

**Temptation 48.** Lastly, the devil would sink the sinner in despair, and persuade him that now it is too late.

**Direction 48.** Observe his design, that it is only to take away that hope which is the weight that sets the wheels of the soul going. In everything he is against God and you. In other sins he is against God's authority: in this he is against his love and mercy. Read the gospel, and you will find that Christ's death is sufficient; the promise is universal, full, and free; and that the day of grace is so far continued until the day of death, and no one shall be denied it who truly desires it. And that the same God who forbids your presumption also forbids your despair.

### Temptations to draw us off from Duty

*A new series begins here; Baxter restarts the numbering at 1.*

**Temptation 1.** The greatest temptation against duty is, by persuading people that it is no duty. Thus in our days we have seen almost all duty cast off by this erroneous notion. One says, That the holy observance of the Lord's day is not commanded by God in Scripture. Another says, What Scripture have you for family prayer, or singing psalms, or baptizing infants, or praying before and after sermon, or for your office, ordination, tithes, churches, etc. Another says, That church government and discipline are not of divine institution. Another says, That baptism and the Lord's supper were only for that age. And thus all duty is taken down, instead of doing it.

**Direction 1.** Read and fear, Matthew 5:19, "anyone who breaks one of the least of these commands and teaches others to do so will be called least in the kingdom of heaven, but whoever obeys them and teaches others to do so will be called great in the kingdom of heaven." Denying duty is too easy a way of evading obedience, to serve one's purpose. Denying the laws that bind you to public payments will not save you from them; but for all that, if you deny, your goods must be seized. And God will make it cost you more if you put him to seizing your goods for duty. Must he go to law with you for it? He will quickly show you law for it, and prove that it was your duty. Open your doubts to able men, and you will hear more evidence than you know; but if pride and insincerity blind you, you must bear your punishment.

**Temptation 2.** The tempter says, It is a duty to weak ones, but not for you: you must not be still under ordinances, in the lower grade: every day must be a sabbath to you, and every bit a sacrament, and every place as a church: you must live above ordinances in Christ.

**Direction 2.** We must live above Mosaic ordinances, Colossians 2:18, 21; but not above Christ's ordinances: unless you will live above obedience and above the government of Christ. [109] Has not Christ appointed the ministry, and church helps, "until we all attain ... a mature person" (Ephesians 4:13); and promised to "be with them to the end of the world" (Matthew 28:20)? It is fool-making pride that can make you think you have no need of Christ's instituted means.

**Temptation 3.** But you are unworthy to pray or to receive the sacrament: it is not for dogs.

**Direction 3.** The willful, impenitent refusers of grace are unworthy. The willing soul, who would gladly be what God would have him be, has an accepted worthiness in Christ.

**Temptation 4.** But while you doubt, you do not do it in faith; and therefore for you it is sin.

**Direction 4.** But is it not a greater sin to leave it undone? Will doubting of all duty excuse you from it? Then you have an easy way to be free from all! Only doubt whether you should believe in God, or Christ, or love him, or live a godly life, and it seems you think it will excuse you. But if you doubt whether you should feed your child, you deserve to be hanged for murdering it, if you starve it. If you doubt of duty, it is duty still, and you are first bound to lay aside your doubts. But things indifferent, left to your choice, must not be done with a doubting conscience: it was of such things that Paul spoke.

**Temptation 5.** The devil puts something still in the way, that seems necessary, to thrust out duty.

**Direction 5.** God has not set you work which he allows you no time for. Is all your time spent in better things? Is it not your carnal mind that makes you think carnal things most needful? Christ says, "one thing is needed" (Luke 10:42). "... above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well" (Matthew 6:33). Had you that love and delight in holiness as you should, you would find time for it. An unwelcome guest is put off with any excuse. Others, as poor as you, can find time for duty, because they are willing. Set your business in order, and let everything keep its proper place, and you may have time for every duty.

**Temptation 6.** But you are so unable and unskilled to pray, to learn, that it is as good never meddle with it.

**Direction 6.** Set yourselves to learn, and mark those who have skill; and do what you can. You must learn by practice. The least skilled duty is better than none. Unworded groans often come from the Spirit of God, and God understands and accepts them, Romans 8:26, 27.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All passages below were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Labels:** Baxter's "_Tempt._" and "_Direct._" are rendered "Temptation" and "Direction," with his numbers (39-48, then 1-6). In this file the label alone is bold. The sub-heading "Temptations to draw us off from Duty" is Baxter's.
- **Romans 6:23:** Baxter quotes "death the wages of it"; the NET has "the payoff of sin is death." The sense is the same ("wages" and "payoff" translate one Greek word).
- **2 Timothy 2:5 (reference corrected):** Baxter's printed reference is "1 Tim. ii. 5." The words he quotes ("If a man strive for masteries, yet is he not crowned, except he strive lawfully") are not in 1 Timothy 2:5 (which is about one mediator between God and humanity) but in 2 Timothy 2:5, so we correct it. The King James "strive for masteries" and "strive lawfully" become "competes as an athlete" and "competes according to the rules" in the NET. Baxter's point (striving does not secure victory) is kept, but the NET makes it clear that the image is an athletic contest.
- **Luke 13:24:** Baxter quotes only the second half ("Many will seek to enter, and shall not be able"). The NET reads "many ... will try to enter and will not be able to," so "seek" becomes "try." The first half of the verse (KJV "Strive to enter in at the strait gate") is not quoted by Baxter; the NET there reads "Exert every effort to enter through the narrow door." Baxter's "strive" in the Direction ties in with it.
- **Matthew 5:19:** NET wording from "anyone" onward; Baxter's opening "Whosoever shall" is the King James. No change of sense.
- **Ephesians 4:13:** Baxter has "till we all come to a perfect man." The NET has "a mature person"; the sense Baxter leans on (church ministry is needed until believers are grown up) is intact, but "perfect" is a stronger word than "mature."
- **Matthew 28:20:** Baxter's words ("be with them to the end of the world") are his paraphrase, adapted to speak of the ministry, so we keep his wording. The NET has "remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age." The NET's "age" for the King James "world" does not change the point he relies on.
- **Luke 10:42:** King James "One thing is needful"; NET "one thing is needed."
- **Matthew 6:33:** King James "Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things shall be added unto you"; NET "But above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well." Baxter's quotation begins at "Seek first," so we begin the NET wording at "above all" and mark the skip with "...". The NET omits "of God" (it reads "his kingdom") and puts "above all" for "first"; the priority of the kingdom over other needs, which Baxter relies on, is still there.
- **Revelation 21:8 (reference only):** Baxter does not quote the verse; he refers to "the portion of the fearful." The NET reads "cowards" where the King James has "the fearful," so the word Baxter points to is not found in the NET, though the same Greek word is meant (the fearful who shrink from faithfulness under threat, which is Baxter's point in Direction 43). We keep Baxter's "fearful."
- **2 Corinthians 12:7, 8 (reference only, Direction 47):** Baxter's "the prick in the flesh" (thorn) becomes "thorn in the flesh"; the NET has "a thorn in the flesh" and "asked the Lord three times." "(as Christ did in his agony)" is Baxter's parenthesis, an allusion to Christ praying three times in Gethsemane (Matthew 26:36-44; he gives no reference).
- **Colossians 2:18, 21 (reference only, Direction 2):** Baxter refers to these verses for living "above Mosaic ordinances" (the ceremonial rules of the law of Moses). The NET of verse 21 reads "Do not handle! Do not taste! Do not touch!" and verse 18 speaks of false humility and the worship of angels; Baxter's use of the passage as about Mosaic rules is his own reading.
- **Romans 8:26, 27 (reference only, Direction 6):** "Unworded groans" is Baxter's paraphrase; the NET has "inexpressible groanings."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "Not for dogs" (Matthew 15:26; Mark 7:27); "things indifferent ... of such things Paul spoke" (Romans 14, especially verse 23); "One saith ... Another saith" (the sects of his day, not named).
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** surpriseth, conquereth, and other -eth forms to plain verbs; ambushments to ambushes; inordinate to excessive; importunate/importunity to persistent/persistence; fancy to notion; wilful to willful; fain to gladly; unskilfullest to least skilled; "I could not forbear" to "I could not help it"; "famish" to "starve"; "somewhat" to "something"; "lower form" to "lower grade" (the school image of a class or level); "Mosaical" to "Mosaic"; "false-heartedness" to "insincerity"; "befooling pride" to "fool-making pride" (his coinage); "serve turn" to "serve one's purpose"; "must be distrained on" to "your goods must be seized," and "put him to distrain on you" to "put him to seizing your goods" ("distrain" was the legal seizure of goods to enforce a debt or duty); "show you law for it" to "show you the law for it"; "weight to set the wheels of the soul a going" to "weight that sets the wheels of the soul going"; "lay by" to "lay aside"; "want of" to "lack of"; "are used to be committed" to "are usually committed."
- **Technical or kept terms:** **security** (Direction 45; careless confidence, as elsewhere in the Directory); **callings** (a person's vocation or station of work); **ordinances** (the means of grace appointed by Christ: preaching, sacraments, prayer, discipline); **things indifferent** (matters neither commanded nor forbidden); **carnal** kept; **the day of grace** (the period in which repentance is still possible).
- **"So may you when he gets this much, by a shameless asking more"** (Direction 39): the grammar is compressed; we keep it as written. It seems to say that you, too, may be conquered when he gets even this small thing, since he goes on to ask for more without shame.
- **Direction 47:** Baxter's "thee" in "with thee to save thee" is turned to "you" along with his "thy" in Direction 48; his own plain "you" elsewhere in the same Direction shows he moves between forms.
- **Temptation 1:** "your office, ordination, tithes" refers to the Church of England's ministry and its support; Baxter lists the things some of his contemporaries said had no Scripture warrant. "The Lord's day" is Sunday.
- **Footnote [109]:** "my two sheets for the Ministry" is Baxter's reference to another of his writings; we have not identified it and do not supply a title.
- **Words kept close to Baxter:** "men" is kept where he means people generally (STYLE_GUIDE); "Be never unarmed nor off your watch" keeps his order, with "off" for "from"; "the lower grade" is for "the lower form"; "bit" (a morsel) is kept; "observation" (of the Lord's day) becomes "observance"; "wearies" and "Take heed of" are kept as still understood.
- **Luke 10:42 and Matthew 6:33:** the quotations are partial; Baxter's own punctuation ends each at the quoted words.
