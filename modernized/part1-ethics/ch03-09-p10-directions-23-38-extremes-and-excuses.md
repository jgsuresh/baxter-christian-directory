# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 10 of 13)

*This section begins at Direction 23 of Grand Direction 9 and ends partway through Temptation 39, whose Direction (and the rest of the series) continues in the next file.*

**Direction 23. Understand all your danger; and mark the latitude or extent of God's commands; and watch on every side: and you must know in what duties you are in danger of extremes and in what not.** In those acts of the soul that are purely rational, about your ultimate end, you cannot do too much; as in knowing God, and loving him, and being willing and resolved to please him. But passions may possibly go too far, even about God, especially fear and grief; for they may be such as nature cannot bear without distraction, death, or hindrance of duty: but few are guilty of this. But toward the creature, passions may easily exceed: and in external actions toward God or man there may be excess. But especially in point of judgment, it is easy to slide from extreme into extreme. And you must know in every duty you do, and every sin which you avoid, and every truth you receive, what is the contrary or extreme to that particular truth, or sin, or duty; and keep it in your eye. If you do not thus watch, you will reel like a drunken man from side to side, and never walk uprightly with God. You will turn from extravagance to greed, from cruel persecution to libertinism, or from libertinism to persecuting cruelty; from hypocritical formality to hypocritical, supposed spirituality, or from enthusiasms and faction to dead formality. But of this I have spoken at large, chapter 5, part 2, "Direction to Students."

**Temptation 24.** On the contrary, the tempter usually pleads moderation and prudence against a holy life, and careful, zealous obedience to God; and would make you believe that to be so diligent in duty and scrupulously afraid of sin, is to run into an extreme, and to be righteous overmuch, and to make religion a vexatious or distracting thing, and that it is more fuss than is needed.

**Direction 24.** This I have answered so often that I shall here say but this: that God cannot be too much loved; nor heaven too much valued, nor too diligently sought or obeyed; nor sin and hell be too much avoided: nor does any man need to fear doing too much, where he is sure when he has done his best to do too little. Listen to what people say of this at death.

**Temptation 25.** The tempter would persuade us that one sin is necessary to avoid another; and that of two evils you must choose the lesser, as if there were no other way. Thus James and John did, by sinful, uncharitable zeal, desire to punish sin (Luke 9:54). Peter would sinfully fight against the sinful Jews (Matthew 26:52). Thus he bids men lie, to avoid some dishonor to God and religion; and persecute, to preserve the unity of the church, and keep out sin; and commit a lesser sin themselves to escape a greater.

**Direction 25.** This is to abuse God, as if he had made that necessary which he forbids, and had not provided you lawful means enough to use against every sin. This is willfully to do that which you claim you are unwilling to do, even to sin. Of two evils avoid both, but be sure you consent to neither.

**Temptation 26.** He pleads Christian liberty to entice to sin, especially to indulgence in physical pleasure. Has not Christ purchased you liberty to use the creatures? all things are yours. No men but the godly have just title to them.

**Direction 26.** He never purchased us liberty to abuse the creature, as poison to hurt ourselves; to hinder mortification, and strengthen our enemy, and our snare, and to steal away our hearts from God. It is a liberty from sin, and not a liberty to sin, that Christ has purchased us.

**Temptation 27.** He pleads the necessity of wife, children, estate, life, etc. Necessity makes it lawful.

**Direction 27.** There is no necessity of sinning. He cannot be Christ's disciple, who thinks it more necessary to save his life, or provide for wife and children, than to obey his Lord (Luke 14:26, 33). God must be trusted with these.

**Temptation 28.** But, says the tempter, it is natural to lust, to love honor, ease, pleasure, etc.; therefore it is no sin.

**Direction 28.** Nature is corrupted and sinful; and it is natural to you to be rational, and to rule your sense and appetite by reason, and not to do what lust or appetite desires. Otherwise man is but a beast.

**Temptation 29.** But, says the tempter, authority commands it; it is your parent's or master's will, and you must obey.

**Direction 29.** There is no power, but from God; therefore none against him or above him. They must be obeyed in all things lawful, but not in sin. They cannot save you nor themselves from the wrath of God.

**Temptation 30.** But, says the tempter, you have promised or vowed that you will do it, and are not at liberty.

**Direction 30.** The vow of a lawful thing must be kept; but if you vow to sin, it is another sin to perform it, and to wrong God or man because you have vowed to wrong him.

**Temptation 31.** But, says the tempter, it is a controversy, and many learned and good men think it is no sin.

**Direction 31.** You have the more reason to be fearful and cautious, when you see that the case is so obscure, and the snare so subtle, and are sure that many learned and good men on one side or the other are deceived before you. Remember God is your King and Judge; who will not take it for an excuse for sin, that learned or good men did it, or defended it. Do not consult with flesh and blood, but with God.

**Temptation 32.** But, says the tempter, will you be singular, and be pointed or hooted at by all.

**Direction 32.** In doctrine I will not be singular from the holy catholic church of God; in worship I will not in singularity or schism separate from the communion of saints: but in doctrine I will be singular from unbelievers and heretics; and in a holy life I will be singular from the ungodly, and profane, and pleasure-seeking; lest if I do as they, to avoid their scorns, I fare as they.

**Temptation 33.** But you are weak, and you cannot help it, until God will give you grace to do it.

**Direction 33.** Therefore I must not be willful, and negligent, and rash, and do that evil which I may refrain from, nor resist and refuse that grace, and help, and mercy without which I can do nothing.

**Temptation 34.** But you repent, and ask God's forgiveness through Christ, every night, for the sins of the day.

**Direction 34.** Repenting is a sorrowful turning of the heart from sin to God. You do not repent if you do not turn. To mock God with such hypocritical praying and repenting is itself a heinous sin. Will you take it for repenting, if a man that spits in your face and beats you, shall do it every day, and ask you forgiveness at night, and purpose to do it still, because he asked forgiveness.

**Temptation 35.** But every man sins daily: you do but as the best men in the world do.

**Direction 35.** No true Christian that is justified has any sin but what he hates more than loves, and would gladly be rid of, and strives against in the use of holy means. He has no beloved sin which he would not part with, but had rather keep than leave.

**Temptation 36.** But those that seem strict and godly are hypocrites, and secretly as bad as you.

**Direction 36.** This is just like the devil, the accuser of those that are sanctified and justified by Christ, the father of malice and lies; to charge that on them, which he confesses is secret and he cannot prove. So he said of Job, that if he were touched in his estate or body, he would forsake his godliness; but he was found a liar. But be it how it will, I am sure I must be holy or I shall not see God, and if "I live according to the flesh, I will die" (Hebrews 12:14; Romans 8:9, 13); and other men's misery will be no ease to me.

**Temptation 37.** But, says the tempter, if you will not sin, come but near it, and do that which is lawful.

**Direction 37.** Indeed we must not run into a contrary extreme, under pretense of fleeing far enough from sin; but if you keep out of other sin, you cannot go too far from any. To be near sin is to be near God's wrath, and near that which tends to hellfire. And to come near it is the common way of coming to it. He that could wish he might do it is infected at the heart already. Keep a tender conscience, and a constant sense of the danger of sinning.

**Temptation 38.** It is a great snare, when sin has gotten into good standing, 1. By putting fair names upon it, calling luxury and gluttony keeping a good house, and a good table; tippling is called drinking a cup with a friend; lust and filthiness are called love; worldliness is called thriftiness and good management; idleness and loss of time are called the leisure of a gentleman; slothfulness is called a not being too worldly; time-wasting amusements are called recreations; pride is called decency and handsomeness; proud revenge is called honor and gallantry; Romish cruelty, and persecution, and wasting the church, are called keeping up order, obedience, and unity; disobedience to superiors is called not fearing man; church divisions are called strictness and zeal. 2. Especially if a sin is not in disgrace among the stricter sort, it greatly prepares men to commit it: as breaking the Lord's day, overseas, in many reformed churches: and at home, spiritual pride, censoriousness, backbiting, disobedience, and church divisions are not in half that disgrace among many professors of strictness, as they deserve, and as swearing, etc. are.

**Direction 38.** Remember, that whatever the name or cloak, God judges righteously, according to the truth; names may deceive us, but not our Judge. And sin is still in disgrace with God, however it may be with men. Remember, the more attractive the paint and cover are, the greater is the danger, and the more watchful and cautious we should be. It is not imperfect man, but the perfect law of God, which must be our rule. The great success of this temptation should deter us from entertaining it. What an abundance of mischief it has done in the world!

**Temptation 39.** Sometimes, the devil tempts men to some heinous sin, so that, if he does not prevail, at least he may draw them into a lesser one. As cheating charterers will ask twice the price of their commodity, so that, by abating much, they may make you willing to give too much. He that would get a little must ask a great deal. He will tempt you to drunkenness, and if he draws you but to tippling or time-wasting, he has gotten something. If he tempts you to fornication, and he gets you but to some filthy thoughts, or immodest, lascivious talk or actions, he has done much of that which he intended. If he tempts you to some horrid cruelty, and you yield but to some lesser degree, or to some unjust or uncharitable judgments, you think you have conquered, when it is he that conquers.

## Translator's notes

- **Labels:** Baxter's "_Direct._ XXIII." and "_Tempt._ XXIV." are rendered Direction 23, Temptation 24, and so on, with Baxter's numbering. The source has no Latin or Greek in this range.
- **Direction 23 heading:** the first sentence is bolded as the Direction's heading, as in the finished files; the rest of the paragraph is Baxter's discussion under it.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Most of Baxter's references here are bare citations with no quoted words (Luke 9:54; Matthew 26:52; Luke 14:26, 33), and are only converted to modern form.
- **Romans 8:13 (Direction 36):** Baxter quotes "if I live after the flesh I shall die" in the first person. NET Romans 8:13 reads "if you live according to the flesh, you will die"; we use the NET phrase (with the comma it needs in the first person) and keep Baxter's "I" and quotation marks, since he has turned it into his own resolve. The sense is unchanged. **Hebrews 12:14** is not quoted: Baxter's "I must be holy or I shall not see God" is his paraphrase. NET reads "Pursue peace with everyone, and holiness, for without it no one will see the Lord"; Baxter says "God" where the verse says "the Lord," and does not mention the "peace" clause. **Romans 8:9** is cited without quoted words.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "righteous over-much" (Temptation 24; cf. Ecclesiastes 7:16, NET "So do not be excessively righteous or excessively wise" (the King James has "righteous over much"); Baxter's argument is that this is a tempter's plea, not a command); "all things are yours" (Temptation 26; cf. 1 Corinthians 3:21, NET "everything belongs to you"); "There is no power, but from God" (Direction 29; cf. Romans 13:1, NET "there is no authority except by God's appointment"); the devil's charge against Job (Direction 36; cf. Job 1:11 and 2:5; NET Job 1:11 "strike everything he has", 2:5 "strike his bone and his flesh").
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): pleadeth, saith, hath, etc. to modern verb forms; hinderance to hindrance; cautelous to cautious; wilful to willful; pretend (claim) to claim; pretence to pretense; sensuality to "indulgence in physical pleasure"; sensual (Direction 32) to pleasure-seeking; infidels to unbelievers; prodigality to extravagance; covetousness to greed; pretended spirituality to "supposed spirituality"; ado to fuss; fain to gladly; hearken to listen; comelier to more attractive; sports to amusements; "speed as they" to "fare as they do"; honour/dishonour to honor/dishonor; "beyond sea" to overseas; "good husbandry" to good management; censures to judgments (Temptation 39); accurate to careful (Temptation 24, with a comma added between "careful" and "zealous"); "more ado than needs" to "more fuss than is needed"; forbear to "refrain from" (Direction 33); "ask God forgiveness" to "ask God's forgiveness" (Temptation 34); sense (Direction 28) kept singular as printed; "gotten into credit" to "gotten into good standing."
- **Terms kept:** *distraction* (Direction 23) is mental derangement, not mere interruption. *libertinism* is moral looseness; *enthusiasms* are claims to direct inspiration; *formality* is empty outward religion. *professors* (Temptation 38) are people who profess a strict religion. *Romish* is Baxter's polemical word for Roman Catholic; it is his polemic. *luxury* here is Baxter's word for self-indulgent excess. *tippling* is habitual light drinking. *mortification* is the putting to death of sinful desires. *singular* means standing apart from others. *catholic* means the universal church.
- **charterers** (Temptation 39): kept as written. We have not confirmed exactly who Baxter means; the sense is dishonest bargainers who open with an inflated price.
- **Direction 29, "They":** refers to the parents and masters of Temptation 29.
- **Punctuation oddities kept:** Temptation 32 and the last sentence of Direction 34 are questions in sense but end with a period in the source; we keep Baxter's mark. Direction 34's "ask you forgiveness at night, and purpose to do it still" is kept as printed.
- **Cross-reference:** Baxter points to "chap. v. part II. 'Direction to Students'" in Direction 23. We have not checked it and give it as he wrote it, with the numbers converted.
- **Temptation 38, "a not being too worldly":** kept as printed (Baxter's phrase is awkward but clear).
- **Temptation 38, "as swearing, etc. are":** the sentence ends in a plain comparison; we leave it as Baxter wrote it.
