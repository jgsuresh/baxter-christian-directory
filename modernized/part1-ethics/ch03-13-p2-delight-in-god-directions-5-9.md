# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XIII (part 2 of 3)

*This file begins partway through Grand Direction 13, in the middle of the series of Directions on delighting in God. It opens with Direction 5, which began under that heading, and ends partway through the Grand Direction; the next file continues it.*

**Direction 5. Take special notice of the reasons why God commands you to delight in him, and consequently how much of religion consists in these delights.**

1. You vilify and dishonor him if you judge him not the worthiest for your delights. 2. If you do not delight in him, your thoughts of God will be seldom, or unwelcome and unpleasant thoughts. 3. And your speeches of him will be seldom, or heartless, forced speeches. Who does not know how readily our thoughts and tongues follow our delight? Be it house, or land, or books, or friends, or actions, which are our delight, we need no force to bring our thoughts to them. The worldling thinks and tastes of his wealth and business; the proud man, of his dignities and honor; the voluptuous beast, of his lusts, and amusements, and meats, and drinks; because they most delight in these. And so must the Christian of his God, and hopes, and holy business, as being his delight. [125] 4. It will keep you away from holy duties, in which you should have communion with God, if you have no delight in God and them. This makes so many neglect both public and secret worship, because they have no delight in it; when those that delight in it are ready in taking all opportunities. 5. It will corrupt your judgments, and draw you to think that a little is enough, and that serious diligence is unnecessary preciseness, and that one quarter of your duty is an excess. A man that has no delight in God and godliness is easily drawn to think that little, and seldom, and cold, and formal, and heartless, lifeless preaching and praying may serve the turn, and any lip-service is acceptable to God, and that more is more ado than needs. And hence he will be further drawn to reproach those that go beyond him, to quiet his own conscience, and save his own reputation; and at last be a forlorn, Satanical reviler, hater, and persecutor of the serious, holy worshippers of God. Jeremiah 6:10, "Behold, the word of the Lord is a reproach to them: they have no delight in it: therefore I am full of the fury of the Lord." 6. If you do not delight in it, you will do what you do without a heart, with backwardness and weariness: as your ox draws unwillingly in the yoke, and is glad when you unyoke him: and as your horse that goes against his will, and will go no longer than he feels the spur, when delight would cause alacrity and unweariedness. 7. It makes men apt to quarrel with the word, and every weakness in the minister offends them, as sick stomachs that have some fault or other still to find with their meat. 8. It greatly inclines men to carnal and forbidden pleasures, because they do not taste the higher and more excellent delights. Taverns, and ale-houses, plays, and whores, cards, and dice, and excess of recreation must be sought out for them, as Saul sought a witch and a musician instead of God. It would be the most effective answer to all the silly reasonings of the voluptuous, when they are pleading for the lawfulness of their unnecessary, foolish, time-wasting amusements, if we could but help them to the heavenly nature and hearts that delight more in God. [126] This better pleasure is an argument that would do more to refute and banish their sinful pleasure than a twelvemonth's disputing or preaching will do with them, while they are strangers to the soul's delight in God. Then they would rather say to their companions, O come and taste those high delights, which we have found in God! 9. The lack of a delight in God and holiness leaves the soul as a prey to sorrows: every affliction that assaults it may do its worst, and has its full blow at the naked, unfortified heart: for creature delights will prove but a poor preservative to it. 10. This lack of a delight in God and holiness is the way to apostasy itself. Few men will hold on in a way that they have no delight in, when all other delights must be forsaken for it. The caged hypocrite, while he is cooped up to a stricter life than he himself desires, even while he seems to serve him, is loathsome to God; for the body without the will is but a carcass or carrion in his eyes. If you had rather not serve God, you do not serve him while you seem to serve him. If you had rather live in sin, you do live in sin, reputatively, while you forbear the outward act: for in God's account the heart, or will, is the man: and what a man had rather be (habitually) that he is indeed. And yet this hypocrite will be still looking for a hole to get out of his cage, and forsake his unbeloved outside of religion: like a beast that is driven in a way that he is loath to go, and will be turning out at every gap. All these mischiefs follow the lack of delight in God.

On the contrary, the benefits that follow our delight in God (besides the sweetness of it) are unspeakable. Those which are contrary to the aforementioned hurts, I leave to your own consideration. 1. Delight in God will prove that you know him, and love him, and that you are prepared for his kingdom; for all that truly delight in him shall enjoy him. 2. Prosperity, which is but the small addition of earthly things, will not easily corrupt you or carry you away. 3. Adversity, which is the withholding of earthly delights, will not much grieve you, or easily cast you down. 4. You will receive more profit by a sermon, or good book, or conference, which you delight in, than others who do not delight in them will do in many. 5. All your service will be sweet to yourself, and acceptable to God: if you delight in him, he certainly delights in you (Psalm 149:4; 147:11; 1 Chronicles 29:17). 6. You have a continual feast with you, which may sweeten all the crosses of your life, and afford you greater joy than your sorrow is, in your saddest case. 7. When you delight in God, your creature delight will be sanctified to you, and warrantable in its proper place; which in others is idolatrous, or corrupt. These, with many others, are the benefits of delight in God.

**Direction 6. Consider how suitable God and holiness are to be the matter of your delight, and take care to avoid all temptations that would represent him as unsuitable to you.**

He is, 1. Most perfect and blessed in himself. 2. And full of all that you can need. 3. He has all the world at his command for your relief. 4. He is nearest to you in presence and relation in the world. 5. He has fitted all things in religion to your delight, for matter, variety, and benefit. 6. He will be a certain and constant delight to you: and a durable delight, when all others fail. Your soul came from him, and therefore naturally should tend to him: it is from him, and for him, and therefore must rest in him, or have no rest. We delight in the house where we were born, and in our native country, and in our parents; and everything inclines to its own original: and so should the soul to its Creator.

**Direction 7. Do not corrupt your minds and appetites with contrary delights.**

Do not addict yourselves to fleshly pleasures: taste nothing that is forbidden. Sorrow itself is not such an enemy to spiritual delights as physical, sinful pleasures are. O leave your beastly and your childish pleasures, and come and feast your souls on God (Isaiah 55:1–3). Away with the delights of lust, and pride, and greed, and empty amusements, and gluttony, and drunkenness, if ever you would have the solid and durable delights! Do not think of joining both together. Think it over: can it be anything but the disease and wickedness of your heart that can make a play, or a feast, or drunken, wanton company more pleasant to you than God? What a heart is that which thinks it a toil to meditate on God and heaven; and thinks it a pleasure to think of the baits of pride and greed! What a heart is that which thinks that indulgence in physical pleasure, wantonness, and emptiness are the pleasure of their families, which must not be turned out; and that godliness, and heavenly talk and exercises, would be the sadness and trouble of their families, which must not be brought in, lest it mar their mirth; that thinks it an intolerable toil and slavery to love God, and holiness, and heaven, and to be employed for them; and thinks it a delightful thing to love a whore, or excess of meat, or drink, or amusements! Can you say anything of a man that is more disgraceful, unless you say he is a devil? It were not so vile for a child to delight more in a dog than in his parents, or a husband to delight more in the ugliest harlot than in his wife, as it is for a man to delight more in fleshly emptiness than in God. Will you be licking up this dung, when you should be solacing your souls in angelic pleasures, and foretasting the delights of heaven? Oh how justly will God thrust away such wretches from his everlasting presence, who so abhor his ways and him! Can they blame him for denying them the things which they hate, or set so light by, as to prefer a lust before them? If they were not haters of God and holiness, they would never be so averse even to the delights which they should have with him.

**Direction 8. Take care to avoid a melancholy bodily constitution; for melancholy people can scarcely delight in anything at all, and therefore not in God.**

Delight is as hard to them as it is to a pained member to find pleasure, or a sick stomach to delight in the food that it loathes. They can think of God with trouble, and fear, and horror, and despair; but not with delight.

**Direction 9. Take care to avoid an impatient, peevish, self-tormenting mind that can bear no cross; and of overvaluing earthly things, which causes impatience in the lack of them. Do not make too great a matter of fleshly pain or pleasure.**

Otherwise your minds will be called to a continual attendance on the flesh, and taken up with continual desires, or cares, or fears, or griefs, or pleasures; and will not be permitted to solace themselves with God. The soul that would have pure and high delights must abstract itself from the concerns of the flesh; and look on your body as if it were the body of another, whose pain or pleasure you can choose whether you will feel. When Paul was caught up into the third heaven, and saw the things unutterable, he was so far freed from the prison of sense that he did not know whether he was in the body or out of it. As the separated souls, that see the face of God and the Redeemer, leave the body to be buried, and to rot in darkness, and feel not all this to the interrupting of their joys; so faith can imitate such a death to the world, and such a neglect of the flesh, and some kind of elevating separation of the mind, to the things above. If in this near conjunction you cannot leave the body to rejoice or suffer alone, yet, as it is itself but a servant to the soul, so let not its pain or pleasure be predominant, and control the high operations of the soul. A manly, valiant, believing soul, though it cannot abate the pain at all, nor reconcile the flesh to its calamity, yet can do more, notwithstanding the pain, to its own delight than strangers will believe.

Some women, and emotional, weak-spirited men, especially in sickness, are so peevish, and of such impatient minds, that their daily work is to disquiet and torment themselves. One can scarcely tell how to speak to them, or look at them, but it offends them. And the world is so full of occasions of provocation that such persons are likely to have little quietness. It is unlikely that these should delight in God, who keep their minds in a continual, ulcerated, galled state, incapable of any delights at all, and cease not their self-tormenting.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Jeremiah 6:10 (Baxter's quotation, with "fury of the Lord" from the next verse):** this is the King James wording, run together from verses 10 and 11. The NET reads, "Indeed, the Lord's message is offensive to them. They do not like it at all" (Jeremiah 6:10), and "I am as full of anger as you are, Lord. I am tired of trying to hold it in" (Jeremiah 6:11). Two differences matter. First, the NET has "offensive" and "do not like it" where the King James has "reproach" and "no delight in it"; Baxter's argument rests on the word "delight," which the NET does not use. Second, in the NET the anger is Jeremiah's, held in "as you are, Lord," where the King James has "I am full of the fury of the Lord." We keep Baxter's wording, modernized, in his quotation marks, as a quotation of the King James and not of the NET.
- **Psalm 149:4; 147:11; 1 Chronicles 29:17:** Baxter gives references only. The NET has "the Lord takes delight in his people" (Psalm 149:4) and "The Lord takes delight in his faithful followers" (Psalm 147:11). In 1 Chronicles 29:17 the NET reads "you ... are pleased with integrity," so the verse speaks of God being pleased, and does not use the word "delight." Baxter's claim that God "delights in" the one who delights in him is clearly supported by the two Psalms; 1 Chronicles is a weaker fit for the word.
- **Isaiah 55:1-3:** a reference only, converted to modern form. The NET opens, "Hey, all who are thirsty, come to the water!" Baxter's "feast your souls on God" is his own application.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Saul seeking a witch (1 Samuel 28), and "a musician" (1 Samuel 16:14-23, where David played for Saul; Baxter's pairing is his own); the apostle caught up to the third heaven, "whether in the body or out of the body" (2 Corinthians 12:2-4, where Paul speaks of "a man in Christ" and the NET reads "caught up to the third heaven"). Baxter says "Paul was caught up" and "things unutterable"; we have not tried to match NET wording because he gives no quotation marks or reference.
- **Direction numbering:** the source opens at "Direct. V." and the Directions run V to IX in this file. We render them as Directions 5 to 9, as the file is a continuation of Grand Direction 13 and Baxter's own numbering restarts inside it.
- **Baxter's lists:** in Directions 5 and the benefits paragraph the numbers 1 to 10 and 1 to 7 are inline in Baxter's sentences and are kept inline.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): "vilifiest" to "vilify"; "sports" to "amusements"; "covetousness" to "greed"; "vain sports" to "empty amusements"; "vanity" (in "fleshly vanities") to "fleshly emptiness"; "vanities/wantonness and vanity" to "wantonness, and emptiness"; "sensual, sinful pleasures" to "physical, sinful pleasures"; "sensuality" to "indulgence in physical pleasure"; "concernments" to "concerns"; "passionate" to "emotional"; "discourse" to "talk"; "scarce" to "scarcely"; "habit of body" to "bodily constitution"; "transport thee" to "carry you away"; "confute" to "refute"; "forementioned" to "aforementioned"; "effectual" to "effective"; "loth" to "loath"; "angelical" to "angelic"; "wanting" ("the want of") to "the lack of"; "preservative" is kept.
- **Kept as Baxter's words:** carnal (here in the sense of fleshly and unspiritual); "serve the turn" (be enough); "preciseness" (excessive strictness in religion, the charge made against the godly); "reputatively" (in reputation, but not in fact); "conference" (spiritual conversation with others); "creature delights" (pleasures found in created things); "alacrity"; "twelvemonth's"; "unbeloved outside of religion" (religion he does not love, worn only on the outside); "member" (a part of the body). "Melancholy" is kept; see GLOSSARY.md.
- **"Satanical":** kept as Baxter's word, meaning devilish. **"Whores," "harlot," "dung":** his blunt words, kept as he wrote them.
- **"the body without the will is but a carcass":** the thought is that God counts the willing heart as the person; we do not add to it.
- **"Some women, and emotional, weak-spirited men":** Baxter's generalization about groups, translated faithfully. It reads as dated, and he offers no evidence for it here.
- **Medical claim (Direction 8):** Baxter treats melancholy as partly bodily in origin, in line with the thought of his day; see GLOSSARY.md. We do not endorse or correct it.
- **"Taverns, and ale-houses, plays, ... cards, and dice":** Baxter's list of amusements, which he treats as seats of sinful pleasure; this is his own judgment, left as written.
- **Cross-reference:** the Direction heading "Direct. V." appears under a Grand Direction whose earlier Directions (1 to 4) are in the previous file; we have not checked how the heading was rendered there.
