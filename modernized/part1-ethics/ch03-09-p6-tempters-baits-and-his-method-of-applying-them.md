# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 9 (part 6 of 13)

*This range begins in the middle of Grand Direction 9, at Direction 8 of its list of directions against the devil's temptations (each Direction answers a numbered Temptation). It ends partway through the second list, "The Tempter's Method in Applying His Prepared Baits," after Direction 2 and Temptation 3; the next file continues with Direction 3.*

**Direction 8.** Discern in all men what there is of God to be your help, and make use of that; and what there is of Satan, sin, and self, and be careful of that. Look upon every man as a helper and a tempter; and be prepared always to draw out his help, and to resist his temptation. And remember that man is only the instrument; it is Satan that tempts you, and God that tests you, by that man! David says of Shimei, "The Lord has spoken to him" (2 Samuel 16:11); that is, he is only God's rod to scourge me for my sin, as my son himself is. As Satan was his instrument in testing Job, not by God's causing but by his allowing the sin: observe God and Satan in it, more than men.

**Temptation 9.** His temptations are also suited to our previously received opinions and thoughts. If you have let in only one lustful thought, or one malicious thought, he can make great use of that nest-egg to gather in more; as a little leaven to leaven the whole lump: he can roll it up and down, and do much to hatch it into a multitude. If you are only tainted with any false opinion, or prejudice against your teacher, your ruler, or your brother, he can make use of it to such increase, and raise such conclusions from it, and more from them, and bring them all into practice, as shall make observers with astonishment say, Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindles!

**Direction 9.** Be careful what thoughts you first admit into your mind; and above all, cherish and approve none except after very good trial and examination. And if they prove corrupt, sweep clean your imagination and memory of them, so that they do not become residents, and do not take up their lodgings in you, or have time to spawn and breed. And fill up the room with contrary thoughts, and useful truth, and cherish them daily, so that they may increase and multiply; and then your hearts will be like a well-populated kingdom, able to keep their possession against all enemies.

**Temptation 10.** Also he fits his temptations to your natural and acquired abilities. So that if you are weak, he may either discourage you; or (which is more usual and dangerous) make you think better of them than they are, and think you know much, when it is next to nothing; and make you wise in your own eyes, and ready to receive an error, and then be confident in it: not to discern between things that differ; but to be deceived into false zeal, and false ways, by the attractive claims and appearances of truth; and then to be zealous for the deceiving of others. Also so that you may be a dishonor to truth and godliness, by your weakness and poor handling of good causes; and may give them away through your lack of skill to the adversary. If you are of stronger wits and abilities, the tempter will draw you to despise the weak; to take common gifts for special grace; or to undervalue holiness and humility, and overvalue learning and cleverness: he will tempt you (dangerously) to loathe the simplicity of Christianity and of the Scriptures, as to style and method; and to be offended at the cross of Christ. So that such persons are usually in greater danger of unbelief, heresy, pride, and insolent domineering over the flock of Christ, than ordinary Christians who have lesser abilities.

**Direction 10.** Work to be well acquainted with yourselves. If you are weak, know your weakness, so that you may be humble, and fearful, and seek strength and help. If you are comparatively strong, remember how weak the strongest are; and how little it is that the wisest know. And study well the ends and use of knowledge; so that all you know may be digested into love and holiness; and use it as remembering that you have much to give account of.

**Temptation 11.** Moreover the tempter will draw advantage against you from your former life and actions. If you have gone out of the way to heaven, he would harden you by custom, and make you think it such a disgrace or trouble to return, that it is as good to go on, and put it to the venture. If you have done any work that was materially good, while your heart and course of life are carnal and worldly, he would quiet you in your sinful, miserable state, by applauding the little good that you have done. If a good man has erred or done wrong, he will engage his honor in it, and make him study to defend it, or excuse it, lest it prove his shame; and tempt men, as he did David, to hide one sin with another. If he gets hold of one link, he will draw on all the chain of sin.

**Direction 11.** Be careful, therefore, what you do; and foresee the end. Do not let the devil get in one foot: try your way, before you enter it. But if you have erred, come off, and that thoroughly and early, whatever it costs; for be sure it will cost more to go on. And if he would make a snare of the good that you have done, remember that this is to turn it into the greatest evil; and that there must be a concurrence and integrity of good to make you acceptable, and to save you: heart and life must be good to the end.

**Temptation 12.** Lastly, he fits his temptations to the season. He will take the season just when an evil thought is most likely to take hold of you; and when the winds and tide serve him: what will take hold at one time (when a man is at a loss in mind and heart) would be abhorred at another. In times of affliction he will draw you to deny Christ, with Peter, or look out for yourselves by sinful means; in times of prosperity he will tempt you to security, worldliness, and forgetfulness of the night and winter that approach: the timing his temptations is his great advantage.

**Direction 12.** Dwell as with God, and you dwell as in eternity, and will always see that as time, so all the pleasures, and advantages, and dangers, and sufferings of time, are things of little importance in themselves. Keep your eye upon judgment and eternity, where all the errors of time will be set right, and all the inequalities of time will be leveled, and the sorrows and joys that are passing will be no more; and then no reasons drawn from the frowns or flatteries of the times will seem of any force to you. And be always employed for God, and always armed and on your watch, so that Satan may never find you disposed to take the bait.

**The Tempter's Method in Applying His Prepared Baits.**

*A new list begins here; its numbering starts again at 1.*

**Temptation 1.** The devil's first work is to present the tempting bait in all its alluring, deceiving qualities; to make it seem as true as possible to the understanding, and as good and lovable as possible to the will. To say as much as can be said for an evil cause; he makes his image of truth and goodness as beautiful as he can: sin shall be sugared, and its pleasure shall be its strength (Hebrews 11:25). Sin shall have its wages paid down in hand (2 Peter 2:15). He will set it out with full-mouthed praises: O what a fine thing it is to be rich, and please the flesh continually! to have command, and honor, and lusts, and amusements, and what you desire! Who would refuse such a condition that may have it? All this I will give you, was the temptation he thought fit to assault Christ himself with. And he will corrupt the history of time past, and tell you that it went well with those who took his way (Jeremiah 44:17). And for the future, he will promise them that they shall be gainers by it (as he did Eve) and shall have peace, though they please their flesh in sinning: see Deuteronomy 29:19.

**Direction 1.** In this case, first inquire what God says of that which Satan so commends. The commendations and urgings of an enemy are to be suspected. God is most to be believed. 2. Then consider not only whether it is good, but how long it will be good; and what it will prove at the end; and how we shall judge of it at the parting; and at the same time consider what it tends to; whether it tends to good or evil; and whether it is the greatest good that we are capable of. And then you will see that if there were no good, or appearance of good in it, it could do a voluntary agent no harm, and would not be fit to be the matter of a temptation: and you will see that it is temporal good set up to deceive you of the eternal good, and to entice you into the greatest evil and misery. Does the devil show you the world, and say, "All this I will give you"? Look to Christ, who shows you the glory of the world to come, with all things good for you in this world, and says more truly, "All this I will give you." The world and hell are in one end of the balance, and pardon, holiness, and heaven are in the other. Which now will you prefer? If the devil has more to give you and bid for you than Christ, let him take you.

**Temptation 2.** The tempter works to keep God, and Christ, and heaven out of sight, so that they do not darken the splendor of his bait; and to hide those powerful reasons from them, by which they might easily repel the temptation; so that though they are well known and sure, and Scripture is full of them, none of them shall be ready at hand to use when the temptation comes; so that to them they shall all be as nothing: and this he does by unbelief and thoughtlessness.

**Direction 2.** Live by faith. See that God the Father, the Redeemer, and the Holy Spirit dwell within you, and take up your hearts, and that your hopes are all placed on heaven, and that these are your very life and business; and then you will always have at hand what may repel the tempter. A heart taken up with God and Christ, conversing in heaven, is always fortified, and prepared to meet every temptation with abhorrence. Let your souls be always possessed with as constant a sense of the evil of sin, the danger of sinning, the presence, authority, and holiness of God, the wrong that sin does him, the harm it does ourselves and others, and what it did to Jesus Christ, as you have of the danger of fire, and water, and poison; and then the tempter will not succeed.

**Temptation 3.** It is the great care of the devil to keep out of sight, so that he may not be seen himself in the temptation. As the angler keeps himself behind the bush, and the fowler hides himself from the birds, or else they would fear, and fly, and escape; so the devil uses all his art to hide himself from the sinner's notice; so that the deluded soul shall little think that the devil is so near him, and has so great a hand in the business. If the ambitious or greedy worldling saw the devil offer him the bait, and heard him say, "All this I will give you"; he would have the smaller desire to take the bait. If the devil appeared to the whoremonger, and brought him his whore, and encouraged him in his filthiness, it would cool his lust: or if he appeared to the drunkard, and presented him the cup, he would have but little desire to drink. If the proud and the malicious saw the devil at their backs, rejoicing in their sin, and urging them on, it might frighten them half into their wits. Therefore the great effort of the devil is to persuade men that it is not he who makes the suggestion to them: it is such a friend, or such a neighbor, or gentleman, or minister, or wise man; it is not the devil! until the fish is caught, and the bird is in the net, and then the author of all appears, to kill them, and carry them away without any concealment.

## Translator's notes

- **Where this range sits:** the source heading hint ("Direct. VIII. Discern in all men...") is the opening line of the range, so the file begins at Direction 8 itself. The numbered Temptations and Directions here come in pairs (each Direction answers the Temptation just above it). Baxter then starts a second list under the heading "The Tempter's Method in Applying His Prepared Baits," with numbering restarting at 1; we kept his numbers and added a small italic line to mark the restart. The labels "Temptation" and "Direction" render his "Tempt." and "Direct."
- **2 Samuel 16:11 (Shimei):** Baxter gives no reference and quotes the King James wording "The Lord hath bidden him." We supplied the reference (not in Baxter's text) and used the NET phrase "the Lord has spoken to him." The sense Baxter leans on is a command ("bidden"), which he glosses as God's rod to scourge David. The NET's "has spoken to him" is somewhat weaker on its own; the NET at 2 Samuel 16:10 does read "the Lord has said to him, 'Curse David!'", which carries the sense of a command.
- **"Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindles!"** (Temptation 9): an unmarked quotation of James 3:5 in the King James form. Baxter has built it into his sentence, so we kept his wording, modernized. The NET reads "Think how small a flame sets a huge forest ablaze," which says the same thing in different words. **"as a little leaven to leaven the whole lump"** is an unmarked allusion to 1 Corinthians 5:6 (also Galatians 5:9); the NET at 1 Corinthians 5:6 has "a little yeast affects the whole batch of dough." Kept as Baxter's wording.
- **"All this will I give thee"** (Temptation 1; Direction 1 twice; Temptation 3): Baxter gives no reference. It blends the devil's words to Christ in Matthew 4:9 and Luke 4:6-7 (NET: "I will give you all these things if you throw yourself to the ground and worship me"; "To you I will grant this whole realm... all this will be yours"). We kept Baxter's wording, modernized as "All this I will give you." The second use in Direction 1, which Baxter makes Christ's own words ("says more truly"), is his application, not a Scripture quotation.
- **Scripture references given without quotation:** Hebrews 11:25, 2 Peter 2:15, Jeremiah 44:17, and Deuteronomy 29:19 are cited by Baxter as support, not quoted, so we kept his sentences and gave the references in modern form. The NET at Hebrews 11:25 speaks of "sin's fleeting pleasure" (the King James has "pleasures of sin for a season"); at 2 Peter 2:15 "the wages of unrighteousness." Baxter's "wages paid down in hand" is his own gloss. His "Eve" promise ("shall be gainers by it") alludes to Genesis 3:4-5.
- **Unmarked allusions:** Job's testing (Job 1-2); "the night and winter which approacheth" (probably a general allusion to the coming of trouble or death; we have not identified a verse); "deny Christ, with Peter" (the accounts in the Gospels, for example Matthew 26:69-75); "tempt men, as he did David, to hide one sin with another" (2 Samuel 11); "the cross of Christ" (as in 1 Corinthians 1 and Galatians 5:11).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): fantasy to imagination; inhabitants to residents; well-peopled to well-populated; reduce them to practice to bring them into practice; make use of / improve it to make use of it; parts to abilities (his usual word for natural endowments); specious pretences and shows to attractive claims and appearances; ill management to poor handling; unskilfulness to lack of skill; vulgar christians to ordinary Christians; concocted into to digested into; betimes to early; shift for yourselves to look out for yourselves; speed to succeed; list (desire) to desire; inconsiderateness to thoughtlessness; whither/hath/doth and similar forms modernized throughout; honour to honor; sports to amusements; infidelity to unbelief; covetous to greedy; endeavour to effort; "motion" (an inner prompting or suggestion) to suggestion; "motions of an enemy" to urgings of an enemy; "labour" to work.
- **take heed to be careful** (Directions 8, 9, 11): Baxter's "take heed." **"when a man hath his wits and heart to seek"** (Temptation 12): an idiom for being at a loss; we rendered it "at a loss in mind and heart."
- **security** (Temptation 12): kept as Baxter's term for careless confidence.
- **materially good** (Temptation 11): kept as Baxter's term for an act good in itself, whatever the heart behind it. **study** (Temptation 11, "make him study to defend it"): kept in Baxter's sense of work at, strive.
- **conversing in heaven** (Direction 2): kept; Baxter's "conversing" here means having one's dwelling and dealings in heaven, not talking.
- **"the timing his temptations"** (Temptation 12): Baxter's text omits "of"; we kept his wording.
- **carnal** (Temptation 11) kept; **whoremonger** and **whore** kept as Baxter's blunt words.
- **"nest-egg"** (Temptation 9): kept; an egg left in a nest to encourage a hen to lay more (we have not confirmed the history of the term). His sense here is a starting stock that draws more.
- **"2."** (Direction 1): Baxter runs a "2." in the middle of the paragraph with no "1." before it (the first point begins "first inquire"); we left it where he put it.
- **"made observers say with astonishment"** and **"the angler... the fowler"**: Baxter's images, kept as written; "angler" is a person who fishes with a rod; "fowler," one who snares birds.
