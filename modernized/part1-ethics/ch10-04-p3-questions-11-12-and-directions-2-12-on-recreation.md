# Chapter 10: Part 2, Direction 1 (continued) and Directions 2–12: the last questions to gamblers, and directions for lawful recreation

*This section begins partway through Direction 1 of Part 2, at Question 11 of Baxter's questions to the "impenitent gamester or idle time-waster"; the earlier questions are in the previous file.*

*Question 11.* If you were sure yourselves that you do not sin in your games or amusements, are you sure that your companions do not? that they have no lust or emptiness of mind at stage-plays, no greed, or sinful pleasure, or anger at cards or dice? If you say, We are not bound to keep all other men from sin, I answer, You are bound to do your best towards it; and you are bound not to contribute willingly to their sin; and are bound to forgo a thing indifferent, though not a duty, to avoid the causing to stumble or tempting of another. If Paul would never eat meat while he lived rather than make a weak person offend, should not your amusements be subject to as great charity? He says, "It is good not to eat meat or drink wine or to do anything that causes your brother to stumble." [607] *Objection.* Then we must give up our food, and drink, and clothes, and all. *Answer.* It does not follow that we must forsake our duty to prevent another man's sin, because we must forsake our pleasure in things indifferent. If you knew what sin is, and what it is to save or lose one's soul, you would not make a sport of other men's sin, nor so easily contribute to their damnation, and think your physical pleasure to be a good excuse. Romans 15:1–3, in such cases, "we who are strong ought to bear with the failings of the weak, (that is, to show compassion to them as we do to children in their weakness,) and not just please ourselves (to their hurt). Let each of us please his neighbor for his good to build him up (that is, prefer the edifying of another's soul before the pleasure of your bodies). For even Christ did not please himself—" If Christ lost his life to save men from sin, will you not lose your amusement for it?

*Question 12.* What kind of men are they that are most addicted to your games and plays, and what kind of men are they that avoid them, and are against them? Are there not more fornicators, drunkards, swearers, cursers, coveters of other men's money, and profane neglecters of God and their souls, among gamblers and players, than among those that are against them? Judge by the fruits.

*In the margin: To amusement-loving youths.*

And what I say to idle gamblers is proportionately to be said to pleasure-loving youths, that run after wakes, and May-games, and dancings, and revelries, and are carried by the love of amusements and pleasure from the love of God, and the care of their salvation, and the love of holiness, and the love of their callings; and into idleness, dissipation, and disobedience to their superiors. For the cure of this love of pleasure (besides what is said in chapter 4, part 9) consider:

1. Do you not know that you have higher delights to mind? And are these trifles befitting a noble soul, that has holy and heavenly matters to delight in?

2. Do you not feel what a plague the very pleasure is to your affections? how it bewitches you, and makes a fool of you, and makes you out of love with holiness, and unfit for anything that is good?

3. Do you know the worth of those precious hours which you play away? have you no more to do with them? Look inwards to your soul, and forward to eternity, and think better.

4. Is it amusement that you most need? Do you not more need Christ, and grace, and pardon, and preparation for death and judgment, and assurance of salvation? Why then are these not your business?

5. Have you not a God to obey and serve? and does he not always see you? and will he not judge you? alas! you do not know how soon. Though you are now merry in your youth, and your "heart cheer you," and you "follow the impulses of your heart and the desires of your eyes, but know that God will judge your motives and actions," Ecclesiastes 11:9.

6. Observe in Scripture what God judges of your ways. Titus 3:3, "We, too, were once foolish, disobedient, misled, enslaved to various passions and desires—" 2 Timothy 2:22, "Keep away from youthful passions, and pursue righteousness, faithfulness, love, and peace, in company with others who call on the Lord from a pure heart." Read 1 Peter 4:1–4; 2 Peter 3:3; 2 Timothy 3:4, "loving pleasure rather than loving God." [608]

7. You are only preparing for your future sorrow, either by repentance or destruction; and the greater is your pleasure now, the greater will be your sorrow and shame in retrospect.

Having spoken this much for the cure of sinful amusements, I proceed to direct the more sober in their recreations.

**Direction 2. When you understand the true nature and use of recreations, labor to be acquainted with just how much and what sort of recreation is needful to yourselves in particular.**

In which you must have respect, 1. To your bodily strength. 2. To your minds. 3. To your labors. And when you have resolved on it, what and how much is needful and fit, to help you in your duty, allow it its proper time and place, as you do your meals, and see that you do not allow it to encroach upon your duty.

**Direction 3. Ordinarily join profit and pleasure together, that you lose no time.**

I do not know one person in a hundred, or in many hundreds, that needs any game at all: there is such variety of better exercises at hand to recreate them. And it is a sin to idle away any time which we can better improve! I confess my own nature was as much addicted to playfulness as most: and my judgment allows me as much recreation as is needful to my health and labor (and no more). But for all that I find no need of any game to recreate me. When my mind needs recreation, I have variety of recreating books, and friends, and business to do that. And when my body needs it, the hardest labor that I can bear is my best recreation: walking is instead of games and amusements; as profitable to my body, and more to my mind: if I am alone, I may improve that time in meditation; if with others, I may improve it in profitable, cheerful conversation. I do not condemn all amusements or games in others, but I find none of them all to be best for myself: and when I observe how far the disposition and life of Christ and his best servants was from such recreations, I avoid them with the more suspicion. And I see very few who do not dislike it in ministers (even shooting, bowling, and such more healthful games, to say nothing of chess and such others, as do not fit the purpose of a recreation). Therefore there is something in it that nature itself has some suspicion of. That student that needs chess or cards to please his mind, I suspect, has a carnal, empty mind. If God and all his books, and all his friends, etc. cannot suffice for this, there is some disease in it that should rather be cured than pleased. And for the body, it is another kind of exercise that profits it.

**Direction 4. Watch against inordinate, physical delight, even in the most lawful amusement.**

Excess of pleasure in any such empty pursuit very much corrupts and makes a fool of the mind. It puts it out of relish with spiritual things; and turns it from God, and heaven, and duty.

**Direction 5. To this end keep a watch upon your thoughts and fantasies, that they do not run after amusements and pleasures.**

Otherwise you will be like children that are thinking of their play, and longing to be at it, when they should be at their books or business.

**Direction 6. Avoid the company of revelers, gamblers, and such time-wasters.**

Do not come among them, lest you be ensnared. Keep company with those that delight themselves in God, 2 Timothy 2:22.

**Direction 7. Remember death and judgment, and the necessities of your souls.**

Usually these amusements seem only foolishness to serious men; and they say of this mirth, as Solomon, "It is folly," Ecclesiastes 2:2. And it is great and serious subjects which make serious men. Death and the world to come, when they are soberly thought on, put the mind quite out of relish with foolish pleasures.

**Direction 8. Be diligent in your honest callings.**

Laziness breeds a love of amusements; when you must please your slothful flesh with ease, then it must be further pleased with empty pursuits.

**Direction 9. Delight in your relations and family duties and mercies.**

If you love the company and conversation of your parents, or children, or wives, or kindred as you ought, you will find more pleasure in talking with them about holy things or honest business than in foolish amusements. But adulterers that do not love their wives, and unnatural parents and children that do not love one another, and ungodly masters of families that do not love their duty, are driven to seek their amusement away from home.

**Direction 10. See to the sanctifying of all your recreations, when you have chosen such as are truly suited to your need; and do not go to them before you need, nor use them beyond your need.**

See also that you lift up your hearts secretly to God, for his blessing on them; and mix them all along as far as you can with holy things; as with holy thoughts or holy speech. As for music, which is a lawful pleasure, I have known some think it profaneness to use it privately or publicly with a psalm, who did not scruple using it in common mirth; whereas all our mirth should be as much sanctified as is possible. All should be done to the glory of God; and we have much more in Scripture for the holy use of music (public and private) than for any other use of it whatever. And it is the excellence of melody and music, that they are recreations which may be more aptly and profitably sanctified by application to holy uses than any other. And I should think them little worth at all, if I might not use them for the holy exhilarating or elevating of my soul, or moving it towards God, or stirring it to duty.

**Direction 11. The sickly and the melancholy (who are usually least inclined to amusement) have much more need of recreation than others, and therefore may allow it a much larger time than those that are in health and strength.**

Because they take it only as medicine to recover them to health, it being to be reduced again when they are recovered.

**Direction 12. Be much more strict in regulating yourselves in your recreations than in censuring others for using some amusements which you dislike.**

For you do not know perhaps their case, and reasons, and temptations; but an idle, time-wasting, pleasure-loving amusement-seeker everyone should look on with pity as a miserable wretch.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Every NET quotation in this file was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where we are:** Chapter 10 (Directions for the Government of the Body), Part 2, whose heading (in the earlier file) is "Directions about sports and recreations, and against excess and sin therein." Baxter's long Direction 1 puts a series of questions to the "impenitent gamester or idle time-waster"; this file picks it up at Question 11 (*Quest.* XI) and finishes Part 2. Part 3 (on apparel) begins right after this range.
- **calling(s)** (paragraph to youths; Direction 8): Baxter's word for one's vocation or occupation; kept.
- **sports to amusements:** following the project's standing decision, "sports" becomes "amusements" throughout ("sportful youths" in the margin becomes "amusement-loving youths"; "sporter" in Direction 12 becomes "amusement-seeker"; children's "sport" in Direction 5 becomes "play"). We kept the idiom "make a sport of" (make light of) in Question 11, which is still current English.
- **gamesters:** "gamblers" (players of games for money, as with the cards and dice Baxter names). **players** (Question 12): kept; it may mean actors or those who play at games, and the source does not settle which.
- **a thing indifferent / things indifferent** (Question 11): kept as Baxter's technical term for actions neither commanded nor forbidden by God (morally neutral in themselves). **causing to stumble:** Baxter's "scandalizing," which in his English meant leading someone into sin, not shocking them. **forgo:** his "forbear." **anger:** his "passion" (at cards or dice), meaning heated temper. **emptiness of mind:** his "vanity of mind." **greed:** his "covetousness." **food:** his "meat" in the objection, which then meant food in general. **meat:** his "flesh" (Paul's eating of meat, 1 Corinthians 8:13, cited in note 607).
- **Romans 14:21** (Question 11): NET wording. Baxter's King James text ran "nor any thing whereby thy brother stumbleth, or is offended, or is made weak"; the NET, following a different Greek text, has only "causes your brother to stumble," and has no word for "weak." Baxter's sentence before the quotation leans on the "weak person," so the point survives, but the NET does not have the three-fold "stumbles, is offended, is made weak."
- **Romans 15:1–3** (Question 11): NET wording, with Baxter's own explanatory parentheses kept where he inserts them, and ending at "did not please himself" as he does (his dash). The NET's "not just please ourselves" softens the King James "not to please ourselves" slightly; Baxter's parenthesis "(to their hurt)" makes the same limit. The NET's "to build him up" stands for the King James "to edification"; we kept Baxter's "edifying" in his own parenthesis, since he is glossing that word. **show compassion to:** his verb "compassionate."
- **Ecclesiastes 11:9** (item 5): NET wording, fitted into Baxter's sentence ("Though you are now merry in your youth, and your..."), so "heart cheer you" is a phrase from the NET's "let your heart cheer you." The King James had "walk in the ways of thine heart, and in the sight of thine eyes: but know thou, that for all these things God will bring thee into judgment." The NET's "God will judge your motives and actions" is broader than "for all these things," which Baxter leans on as judgment for the very pleasures of youth; the warning of judgment is kept, but the direct link to "these things" is weaker in the NET.
- **Titus 3:3** (item 6): NET wording to the point where Baxter breaks off (his dash). The King James "serving divers lusts and pleasures" becomes "enslaved to various passions and desires"; the NET loses Baxter's word "pleasures," which fits his theme.
- **2 Timothy 2:22** (item 6): NET wording, with the opening "But" left off as Baxter's quotation begins at "Fly." The King James "faith, charity" becomes "faithfulness, love." Baxter cites the same verse again in Direction 6 (reference only) for keeping company with "them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart."
- **2 Timothy 3:4** (item 6): the source prints "1 Tim. iii. 4," but the quoted words ("lovers of pleasure more than lovers of God") are 2 Timothy 3:4; 1 Timothy 3:4 is about an overseer managing his household. We corrected the reference. NET: "loving pleasure rather than loving God"; the sense is unchanged.
- **Ecclesiastes 2:2** (Direction 7): Baxter's "it is madness" comes from the King James "I said of laughter, It is mad: and of mirth, What doeth it?" The NET reads "I said of partying, 'It is folly,'" which we use. "Folly" is milder than "madness," and the NET's "partying" fits Baxter's "this mirth" closely; the point is kept.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "Judge by the fruits" (Question 12; compare Matthew 7:16, 20); "All should be done to the glory of God" (Direction 10; compare 1 Corinthians 10:31); "If Paul would never eat flesh while he lived" (Question 11; 1 Corinthians 8:13, cited in note 607). References for these are not in Baxter's text.
- **chapter 4, part 9** (paragraph to youths): Baxter's cross-reference ("chap. iv. part ix."), presumably to his directions against sensuality in Chapter 4; we have not checked it.
- **wakes; May-games** (paragraph to youths): kept. Wakes were local parish festivals, with feasting and games; May-games were the May Day festivities. **revelries:** his "revellings." **dissipation:** his "riotousness" (dissolute, wasteful living, not rioting). **love of pleasure:** his "voluptuousness"; **pleasure-loving youths:** his "voluptuous youths." **proportionately:** his "proportionably."
- **Items 1–7:** **trifles:** his "toys." **befitting:** his "beseeming." **makes a fool of:** his "befooleth" (also "befool" in Direction 4). **think better:** his "bethink thee better." **in retrospect:** his "in the review."
- **Direction 2:** **do not allow:** his "suffer ... not." "labor to be acquainted with" adds "with," which modern English needs.
- **Direction 3:** **improve** (time): kept; in Baxter's English it meant to put to good use or turn to profit. **conversation:** his "conference." **disposition:** his "temper." **I see very few who do not dislike it in ministers:** his "I see but few but distaste it in ministers," where the second "but" means "who do not." **purpose:** his "end." **something:** his "somewhat." **I suspect:** his "I doubt" (in his English, to fear or suspect). **there is such variety:** the source has "there are such variety"; we used the singular verb. **recreate:** kept in its root sense, to refresh or restore.
- **Direction 4:** **inordinate** kept (excessive, beyond due measure). **out of relish with** kept here and in Direction 7: having lost the taste for.
- **Direction 6:** **Keep company with:** his "Accompany yourselves with."
- **Direction 8:** **diligent:** his "painful," which meant painstaking, taking pains.
- **Direction 9:** **conversation:** his "converse"; **talking:** his "discoursing"; **driven to seek ... away from home:** his "put to seek their sport abroad" ("abroad" meant out of doors or away from home, not overseas).
- **Direction 10:** **whereas:** his "whenas." **who did not scruple:** his "that scrupled not" (had no qualms about). **speech:** his "speeches" (in the sense of talk). **moving it towards God:** his "affecting it towards God." **stirring:** his "exciting." **excellence:** his "excellency."
- **Direction 11:** **melancholy** kept (see GLOSSARY.md). **medicine:** his "physic." "it being to be reduced again" renders "being to abate again" (the recreation is to be cut back once they recover). Baxter's sentence beginning "Because" is a fragment in the source, kept as it is.
- **Direction 12:** **strict:** his "severe." **dislike:** his "mislike." **pleasure-loving:** his "sensual."
- Baxter's polemic and judgments (for example on the kind of men found among gamblers and players, and on students who need chess or cards) are his own, kept as he wrote them.
