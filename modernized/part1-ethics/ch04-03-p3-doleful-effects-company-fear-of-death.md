# Chapter 4: The end of Direction 6, and Directions 7-12 against hardness of heart

*This range begins partway through Direction 6 ("Take notice of the doleful effects of hard-heartedness"), at item 15 of its numbered list; the earlier items and the Direction's heading are in the previous file.*

15. All the promises of salvation in the gospel do put in an exception against these men, "unless they are converted:" they are made to the penitent, and not to the impenitent. There is justification and life; but not for them. "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1). "The one who does not believe has been condemned already" (John 3:18, 36). And they that "after their hardness and impenitent hearts, do treasure up wrath against the day of wrath, shall have tribulation and anguish" (Romans 2:5-7). Here is comfort for repenting sinners, but none (but on condition they repent) for them: when others are welcomed to Christ's marriage feast, he says to these, "How did you get in here?" and yet they do not feel it.

16. They still carry about with them the doleful evidences of all this misery. One would think the ambitious, and greedy, and pleasure-seeking might see these death-marks on themselves; and the ungodly might feel that God does not have their hearts; especially they that hate the godly, and show their wolfish cruelty against them, and are the progeny of Cain; and yet they do not feel any of this, but live as quietly, and talk as pleasantly, as if all were well with them, and their souls were safe, and their calling and election were made sure. Alas! if these souls were not hardened in sin, we should see it in their tears, or hear it in their complaints; they would after sermon sometimes come to the minister, as they, "Sirs, what must we do to be saved?" (Acts 2:37; 16:30) or we should see it in their lives, or hear of it by report of others, who would observe the change that grace has made; and sermons would stick longer with them, and not at best be turned off with a fruitless commendation; and saying, it was a good sermon, and there is an end of it. Judge now by this true description which I have given you, what a hardened sinner is. And then the godly may so see cause to bewail the remnants of this evil, as yet to be daily thankful to God that they are not in the power of it.

**Direction 7. Live, if you can possibly, under a lively, quickening ministry, and in the company of serious, lively Christians.** It is true, that we should be deeply affected with the truths of God, however coldly they are delivered. But the question is not, what is our duty; but what are our disease, and our necessity, and the proper remedy. All men should be so holy, as not to need any exhortations to conversion at all: but shall the ministers therefore neglect such exhortations, or they that need them turn away their ears? Hear, if possible, that minister that first feels what he speaks, and so speaks what he feels, as tends most to make you feel. "Shout loudly! Don't be quiet! Yell as loudly as a trumpet! Confront my people with their rebellious deeds; confront Jacob's family with their sin" (Isaiah 58:1-2). Though such as "seek me day after day; they want to know my requirements, like a nation that does what is right and does not reject the law of their God." God is the chief agent; but he is accustomed to work according to the fitness of the instrument. O woeful case! to hear a dead minister speaking to a dead people, the living truths of the living God! As Christ said, "If someone who is blind leads another who is blind, both will fall into a pit." And if the dead must raise the dead, and the ungodly enemies of a holy life must bring men to godliness and to a holy life, it must be by such a power as once made use of clay and spittle, to open the eyes of the blind. It seems it was a proverb in Christ's days, "Let the dead bury their own dead:" but not, Let the dead raise the dead. God may honor the bones of the dead prophet, (2 Kings 13:21,) with the raising a corpse that is cast into its grave, and touches them. A meeting of a dead minister and a dead people, is like a place of graves: and though it be a lamentable thing to hear a man speak without any life, of life eternal, yet God can cooperate in the quickening of a soul. But surely we have no great reason to expect that ordinarily he should convert men so miraculously, without the moral aptitude of means. It is most incongruous for any man in his familiar conversation, to speak without great seriousness and reverence of things concerning life eternal. But for a preacher to talk of God, of Christ, of heaven, and hell, as coldly and sleepily, as if he were persuading men not to believe him, or regard him, that no more regards himself, is less tolerable. It is a sad thing to hear a man draw out a dreaming, dull talk, about such astonishing weighty things; and to speak as if it were the business of his art, to teach men to sleep while the names of heaven and hell are in their ears; and not to be moved while they hear the message of the living God, about their life or death everlasting. If a man tells in the streets of a fire in the town, or a soldier brings an alarm of the enemy at the gates, in a reading or jesting tone, the hearers will neglect him, and think that he does not believe himself. I know it is not mere noise that will convert a soul: a bawling fervency, which the hearers may discern to be but theatrical and affected, and not to come from a serious heart, does harden the listeners worst of all. A rude, unreverent noise is unbecoming an ambassador of Christ. But an ignorant saying of a few confused words, or a sleepy recital of the most pertinent things, do as little become them. Christ did not raise Lazarus by the loudness of his voice: but where the natural ears are the passage to the mind, the voice and manner should be suitable to the matter. Noise without seriousness and pertinent matter, is like gunpowder without bullet, that causes sound and no execution. And the weightiest matter without clear explication and lively application, is like bullet without powder. If you will throw cannon bullets at the enemy with your hands, they will sooner fall on your feet than on them. And it is deadness aggravated by hypocrisy, when a lifeless preacher will claim moderation, as if he were afraid of speaking too loud and earnestly, lest he should awake the dead, whom lightning and thunder will not awake: and when he will excuse himself by accusing those that are not as drowsy or dead as he; and would make men believe that seriousness is intemperate rage or madness. If you are cast upon a cold and sleepy minister, consider the matter more than the manner; but do not choose such a one for the cure of hardness and insensibility of heart.

**Direction 8. Take notice, how sensitive tender-hearted Christians are of sins far less than those that you make a jest of; and how close those matters come to their hearts, that do not touch yours.** And do you not have as much cause to be moved as they? and as much need to lay such things to heart? Did you but know what a trouble it is to them, to be haunted with temptations to the unbelief and atheism which prevails with you, though they are far from choosing them, or delighting in them; did you see how involuntary thoughts and frailties make some of them weary of themselves; and how they even hate their hearts for believing no more, and loving God no more, and for being so strange to God and heaven, when yet there is nothing in the world so dear to them, nor has so much of their esteem or effort; you would think, surely, that if such hearts had your sin and misery to feel, they would feel it to their grief indeed, unless the sin itself did hinder the feeling, as it does with you. Let tender-hearted Christians instruct you, and not be witnesses against you.

**Direction 9. Beware of hardening company, examples, and conversation.** To hear men rail and scoff at holiness, and curse, and swear, and blaspheme the name and truth of God, will at first make you tremble; but if you willfully cast yourself ordinarily into such company, by degrees your sense and tenderness will be gone, and you will find a very great hardening power, in the company, and frequent conversation, and practices, which yourselves condemn.

**Direction 10. Beware of willful sinning against knowledge; much more of lying in such sin, unrepented of.** It greatly hardens, to sin against knowledge; and much more to commit such sins over and over. This grieves and drives away the Spirit, and dangerously provokes God to leave men to themselves.

**Direction 11. Beware of being customary in the use of those means that must be the means of curing hardened hearts.** If once the lively preaching, and holy living, and fervent praying, of the servants of God, are taken by you but as matters of course, and you go with them to church and to prayers, but as to eat or drink, or kneel with them but for custom, you will be as the smith's dog, that can sleep by the anvil, while the hammers are beating, and the sparks are flying about his ears. It is dangerous to grow customary and dull, under powerful, lively helps.

**Direction 12. Be often with the sick, and in the house of mourning, and read your lesson in the churchyard, and let the grave, and bones, and dust instruct you.** When you see the end of all the living, perhaps you will somewhat lay it to heart. Sight will sometimes do more than the hearing of greater things. Fear may possibly touch the heart, that has not yet so much ingenuousness as to be melted by the force of love. And ordinarily, the humbling and softening of a hard, impenitent heart begins in fear, and ends in love. The work of preparation is in a manner the work of fear alone. The first work of true conversion is begun in a great measure of fear, and somewhat of love; but so little as is scarcely perceived, because of the more noticeable operations of fear. And as a Christian grows, his love increases, till perfect love in the state of perfection has cast out all tormenting fear, though not our reverence or filial fear of God. Look, therefore, into the grave, and remember, man, that you must die!—you must die!—it is past all controversy that you must die! And do you know where you must appear, when death has once performed its office? Do you not believe that after death comes judgment? Do you not know that you are now in a life of trial, in order to endless joy or misery? and that this life is to be lived but once? and if you miscarry now, you are undone forever? and that all the hope of preventing your damnation, is now, while this life of trial does continue? "Look, now is the acceptable time; look, now is the day of salvation!" If hell is prevented, it must be now prevented! If ever you will pray, if ever you will be converted, if ever you will be made an heir of heaven, it must be now! O man! how quickly will patience have done with you, and time be gone! and then, O then, it will be too late! Do you not know that all the care, and labor, and hope of the devil for your damnation, is laid out this way, if it is possible, to find you other work, or take you up with other thoughts, or keep you asleep with presumptuous hopes, and carnal mirth, and pleasures, and company, or quiet you by delays, till time is gone, and it is too late? And will you let him have his will, and please him with your own perdition? Do you think these are not things to be thought about? Do they not deserve your speediest and most serious thoughts? At least use your reason and self-love to the awakening, and moving, and softening your hardened heart.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Romans 8:1 (item 15):** Baxter has the King James full verse, "...that walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit." The NET text of verse 1 ends at "in Christ Jesus"; the "walk not after the flesh" clause is not in its verse 1. We quote the NET extent, so Baxter's second clause is not shown. His point, that the promise has a condition, leans on the clause he cites.
- **John 3:18, 36 (item 15):** Baxter's "But he that believeth not, is condemned already" is the wording of 3:18; we give the NET of that clause. Verse 36 says the unbeliever "will not see life, but God's wrath remains on him" (NET), which is close in sense but not in words.
- **Romans 2:5-7 (item 15):** Baxter's quotation is a blend, kept in his words, modernized. It joins phrases from 2:5 ("hardness and impenitent heart," "treasure up wrath against the day of wrath") with "tribulation and anguish," which in the King James is in 2:9. NET 2:5: "because of your stubbornness and your unrepentant heart, you are storing up wrath for yourselves in the day of wrath"; NET 2:9: "affliction and distress." We kept his reference "2:5-7" as printed.
- **"unless they are converted"** (item 15): Baxter's own quoted words, no reference given; possibly an allusion to Matthew 18:3 (we have not confirmed this). We keep his word, modernized.
- **"How did you get in here?"** (item 15; Matthew 22:12, not referenced by Baxter): NET reads "Friend, how did you get in here without wedding clothes?"; Baxter quotes only the first phrase of the question.
- **"calling and election were made sure"** (item 16): allusion to 2 Peter 1:10, not referenced by Baxter.
- **Acts 2:37; 16:30 (item 16):** Baxter's "Sirs, what must we do to be saved?" blends two verses. NET Acts 2:37: "What should we do, brothers?"; NET Acts 16:30: "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?" We keep his blend, modernized.
- **evil** (item 16): Baxter's "mischief" (harm, evil). **greedy:** his "covetous." **pleasure-seeking:** his "voluptuous." **death-marks; progeny of Cain:** kept. **bewail:** kept.
- **Direction 7, Isaiah 58:1-2:** Baxter has "house of Israel"; the NET has "Jacob's family." His second quotation ("as seek me daily, and delight to know my ways, ...") is from verse 2; the NET has "seek me day after day; they want to know my requirements," so his "delight to know my ways" does not appear as such. He gave no separate reference for it.
- **Matthew 15:14 (Direction 7, unmarked):** Baxter's "If the blind lead the blind, both will fall into the ditch" is rendered in NET wording ("...both will fall into a pit"). The reference is not in Baxter's text; it is probably Matthew 15:14 (we have not checked the parallel in Luke 6:39).
- **"Let the dead bury their own dead"** (Direction 7, unmarked): NET wording of Luke 9:60 and Matthew 8:22 (same words in both). The reference is not in Baxter's text. Baxter's "their dead" is the King James. **clay and spittle:** probably an allusion to John 9:6 (not confirmed).
- **2 Kings 13:21:** reference as Baxter gave it. The NET says the body "touched Elisha's bones"; Baxter says "the dead prophet." **Lazarus:** John 11 (unmarked).
- **cooperate:** Baxter's "concur." **moral aptitude of means:** kept as written; he seems to mean the fitness of the means for the moral end. **incongruous:** kept. **execution:** kept (effect, as of a shot). **theatrical:** his "histrionical." **listeners:** his "auditors." **unbecoming; become:** his "unbeseeming; beseem." **claim moderation:** his "pretend moderation," meaning to put on a show of it. **cannon bullets:** kept (cannonballs). **conversation; talk:** his "discourse." **explication:** kept (explanation). **insensibility of heart:** kept (lack of feeling).
- **Direction 8:** **sensitive:** his "sensible" (feeling keenly). **esteem or effort:** his "estimation or endeavour." **strange to God:** kept; it means distant from, unfamiliar with. **atheism:** kept as his word.
- **Take heed of / Take heed:** Baxter's opening of Directions 9-11, changed to "Beware of," as in earlier finished files. **Direction 7 sentence 3:** his "what are our disease, and our necessity, and the proper remedy" is kept in his word order. **"Though such as":** the "as" is Baxter's, placed outside the quotation marks because the NET reads "They seek me".
- **Direction 9:** **sense:** kept; it means sensitivity, ability to feel. **ordinarily:** kept (regularly). **yourself / yourselves:** both as printed.
- **Direction 11:** the source's "thou go" and "thou wilt" are rendered as "you go" and "you will."
- **Direction 12:** the source has "thou will somewhat lay it to heart," apparently a misprint for "wilt"; rendered "you will." **ingenuousness:** his "ingenuity," which probably meant noble, open character in his day (we have not confirmed this). **in a manner:** kept (in a way). **scarcely; noticeable:** his "scarce; sensible." **perfect love ... cast out all tormenting fear:** allusion to 1 John 4:18, not referenced. **after death comes judgment:** allusion to Hebrews 9:27 (not referenced). **Now is the accepted time:** 2 Corinthians 6:2; Baxter gives no reference. NET: "Look, now is the acceptable time; look, now is the day of salvation!" Baxter's "this is the day of salvation" is changed to the NET wording. **miscarry:** kept (fail). **prevented:** kept in the sense of stopped. **take you up with:** kept (occupy you with). **perdition:** kept.
- **"PART III."** The source line at the end of the range ("PART III.") is a heading for what follows; it is not part of this section and is not reproduced in the body.
