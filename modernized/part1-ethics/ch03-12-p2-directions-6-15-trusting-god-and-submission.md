# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XII (part 2 of 2)

*This file begins at Direction 6 within Grand Direction XII (on trusting God); Baxter's heading for the Grand Direction and Directions 1-5 fall in an earlier file. It ends with the margin note that belongs to Grand Direction XIII, whose heading falls in the next file.*

**Direction 6. Remember that trusting God does, as it were, oblige him, and distrusting him greatly offends him, especially when anything else is trusted before him.**

If any man trusts you on some encouragement given him by you, you will consider yourselves obliged to be trustworthy to him, and not to fail any honest trust; but if he does not trust you, or trusts another, you will turn him away to those he has trusted. God may say to you, Let them help you whom you have trusted: you do not trust in me, and therefore I do not fail your trust when I forsake you.

**Direction 7. Remember that you must trust in God, or in nothing.**

For nothing is more sure, nor more often experienced, than that all other things are utterly insufficient to be our help. Shall we choose a broken reed, which we know beforehand will both deceive and pierce us? Woe to the man who has no surer foundation for his trust than creatures! The greatest of them are unable; and the best of them are untrustworthy and deceitful. How sad is your situation, if God turns you away to these for help in the hour of your extremity! Then you will see that "it is better to take shelter in the Lord than to trust in people. ... It is better to take shelter in the Lord than to trust in princes" (Psalm 118:8-9). "When the godly see this, they will be filled with awe, and will mock the evildoer, saying: 'Look, here is the man who would not make God his protector. He trusted in his great wealth and was confident about his plans to destroy others'" (Psalm 52:6-7). "Those who trust in the Lord are like Mount Zion, which cannot be moved and will endure forever" (Psalm 125:1). Creatures will certainly deceive your trust, but God will not.

**Direction 8. Believe and remember the particular providence of God, which regards the falling of a sparrow on the ground, and numbers the very hairs of your heads (Matthew 10:30).**

And can you distrust him who is so exact in his regard for your smallest concerns? who is always present, and watches over you? You need not fear his absence, disregard, forgetfulness, or insufficiency. Does he number your hairs, and does he not number your groans, and prayers, and tears? How then does he wipe away your tears, and put them all as in his bottle! (Psalm 56:8; Revelation 7:17).

**Direction 9. Compare God with your dearest and most faithful friend, and then think how boldly you could trust that friend if your life or welfare were wholly in his hand; and how much more boldly you should trust in God, who is wiser, and kinder, and more merciful, and more trustworthy than any mortal man can be.**

When you are in want, in prison, in sickness, and in pain, expecting death, think now, if my life, or health, or liberty were absolutely in the power of my surest friend, how quietly could I wait, and how confidently could I cast away my fears, though I had no promise what he would do with me; for I know he would do nothing but what is for my good: and is not God to be trusted in much more? Indeed a friend would ease my pain, or supply my wants, or save my life, when God will not: but that is not because God is less kind, but because he is wiser, and better knows what tends to my hurt or good. My friend would pull off the plaster as soon as I complain of its sting; but God will wait until it has done the cure. But, surely, God is more to be trusted for my real, final good, though my friend may be more eager to give me ease. All friends may fail; but God never fails.

**Direction 10. Make use of your natural love of quietness, and your natural weariness of tormenting cares, and fears, and sorrows, to move you to cast yourself on God, and quiet your soul in trusting in him.**

For God has purposely made you and all other things insufficient, unsatisfying, and vexing to you, so that you might be driven to rest on him alone, when nothing else gives you rest. Cares, and fears, and restlessness of mind are such thorns and briers as nature cannot love or be content with: and you may be sure that you can in no way be delivered from them, but by trusting in God. And will you choose care and torment, when so sure and cheap a way of ease is set before you? Who can endure to have fears torment him, and cares feed daily upon his heart, when he may safely be delivered from it? An ulcerated, festered, pained mind is a greater calamity than any bodily distress alone. And if you are cast upon your own care, or committed to the trust of any creature, you can never rationally have peace. For your own ease and comfort then turn to God, and cast all your care and burden on him, who cares for you, and knows perfectly what you need (1 Peter 5:7; Matthew 6:32). Read often Matthew 6 from verse 24. How sweet an ease and quietness it is to the mind that can confidently trust in God! How quiet is he from the storms of trouble, and the sickness of mind, which others are distressed with! Isaiah 26:3-4: "You keep completely safe the people who maintain their faith, for they trust in you. Trust in the Lord from this time forward, even in Yah, the Lord, an enduring protector!" Psalm 112:7-8: "He does not fear bad news. He is confident; he trusts in the Lord. His resolve is firm; he will not succumb to fear." Psalm 31:19-20, 24: "How great is your favor, which you store up for your loyal followers. In plain sight of everyone you bestow it on those who take shelter in you. You hide them with you, where they are safe from the attacks of men; you conceal them in a shelter, where they are safe from slanderous attacks. ... Be strong and confident, all you who wait on the Lord." Psalm 56:3-4: "When I am afraid, I trust in you. In God--I boast in his promise--in God I trust; I am not afraid. What can mere men do to me?" How easy and sweet a life this is!

**Direction 11. Remember that distrust is a pregnant, multiplying sin, and will carry you to all iniquity and misery if you allow it to prevail.**

Distrusting God is but our entrance upon a life of error, sin, and woe. It at once sets us on idolatrous confidence in the flesh, and sinful expedients, and stretching the conscience; it deceives our judgments, and makes everything seem lawful which seems necessary to our safety and welfare; and everything seem necessary, without which a man cannot accomplish it. All sinful compliance, and time-serving, and man-pleasing, and believing sinful means to be no sin, proceed from this distrust of God.

**Direction 12. Do not allow distrustful thoughts and reasonings in your mind, but cast them out, and command them to be gone.**

Thoughts are the instruments of good and evil in the mind of man; they cannot be acted on except by thinking, and the will has more command of the thoughts than it has directly of the passions themselves. If you cannot trust God as quietly as you would like, nor keep down every fearful apprehension, yet keep out, or cast out, the thoughts which put your sin to work, and turn your thoughts to something else. If thoughts do not set it in motion, your distrustful fears and cares will vanish. What are your cares, but the turmoil of your thoughts? continually feeding upon difficulties and trouble, and tiring themselves with hunting about for help? Cast away the thoughts, and the cares are gone. You can do much in this if you will, though it is difficult. Matthew 6:25, "do not worry about your life, what you will eat or what you will drink, or about your body, what you will wear." Verses 27-28, "Which of you by worrying can add even one hour to his life? ... Why do you worry about clothing?"

**Direction 13. When commands will not prevail, rebuke and scold your unbelieving heart, and reason it out of its distrustful cares, and fears, and sorrows.**

Say to it, as David often did, Psalms 42 and 43, "Why are you depressed, O my soul? Why are you upset? Wait for God! For I will again give thanks to my God for his saving intervention." O foolish soul! have you still learned no better to know your God? Does he support the heavens and the earth, and the whole creation? and yet can you not rely on him? Is he not wise enough to be trusted with the conduct and disposal of you? Is he not good and gracious enough to be trusted with your life, property, and reputation, and welfare? Is he not great and powerful enough to be trusted against the greatest danger, or difficulties, or opposition that can ever befall you? Is he not true and faithful enough to be trusted, whatever improbabilities may arise before you? Where did the man live, and what was his name, who ever trusted him in vain, or was ever failed or deceived by him? Are not his Son, and Spirit, and covenant, and oath, sufficient pledges of his love for your security? How often has he performed his promises to you, and heard your cries, and helped and saved you in your distress! How often has he refuted your unbelief, and shamed your distrustful fears and cares! and then you could resolve to trust him better in the next distress. And shall all his wonders of mercy be forgotten? and all your confessions, thanksgivings, and promises be now repented of, contradicted, or taken back, by your renewed distrust and unbelief? Is he not the same God, who has so often and so abundantly had mercy on you? Is he not the same God, who has saved all who trusted in him, and worked such wonders for his servants on the earth, and brought so many safely to heaven? "In you our ancestors trusted; they trusted in you and you rescued them. To you they cried out, and they were saved; in you they trusted and they were not disappointed" (Psalm 22:4-5). And is he not sufficient for you, who is sufficient for all the world? Who ever fared badly who trusted in him? or who has prospered by trusting in themselves or any other, without him, or against him? Unworthy soul! will you atheistically deny the sufficiency, or truth, or goodness of your God? Shall your distrust deny him, or blaspheme him? Will you idolatrously set up a worm above him? Is there more in man, or anything else, to hurt or ruin you, than in God to save you? Whom will you trust, if you do not trust God? Do you dare to think that any other is fitter for your confidence? You would be quiet and confident if your dearest friend had your life or welfare in his hands; and are you troubled now that it is in the hands of God? Is he enough to be our endless happiness in heaven, and not to be your confidence on earth? Can you trust him to raise your body from the dust, and not raise your condition, or reputation, or troubled mind? Either take him for your rock and hope, or never claim to take him for your God. If you do not trust in him, you must despair, or trust against him; and whom will you trust to save you from him? Had you no more encouragement to trust him but this, that he has told you to trust him, you could be sure he would never deceive you. Lament, therefore, your disquiet and self-tormenting fears; lament your injurious distrust of your most dear Almighty Father. Do not choose vexation, when the harbor of his love is open to secure you. If men or devils are against you, say as those believers did, Daniel 3:16-17, "We do not need to give you a reply concerning this. If our God whom we are serving exists, he is able to rescue us." Go on, with Daniel, chapter 6, in praying to your God, and trust him with the lions' jaws. "Commit your future to the Lord. Trust in him, and he will act on your behalf" (Psalm 37:5). "Some trust in chariots and others in horses, but we depend on the Lord our God" (Psalm 20:7). "Trust in the Lord. He is their deliverer and protector" (Psalm 115:9-11).

**Direction 14. Do not take the sayings of the tempter or of your own distrustful heart for the sayings of God, or for any reason against your confidence in him.**

Some take all the malicious suggestions of the devil for reasons for their disquiet and fears, as if it were the Spirit of God that raised all the terrors and molestations in them, which are raised by the enemy of God and of them: and they fear when Satan tells them to, thinking it is the Spirit of God; and they dare not trust God when he commands them, for fear that it may be the will of Satan. Some are so strongly affected by their own notions and fancies, that they think God says all that their hearts or fancies say, and make one fear the reason of another. Your heart is not so wise or good that you should take all its words for the words of God. Your "flesh and your heart" may "fail you," when God, who is the "rock of your heart and your portion," will never fail (Psalm 73:26). Your heart may say, I have no grace, no help, no hope, when God never said so (Psalm 77:7-10). Your heart may say, I am a reprobate, forsaken by God, he will not hear me, the time of grace is past, when God never said so. Your heart may say, I am undone, I can find no comfort in any friend, no evidence of grace within me, no comfort in God, in Christ, or in the promises, no comfort in my life, which is but a burden to me; I cannot pray, I cannot believe, I cannot answer the objections of Satan, I can strive no longer against my fears, I cannot bear my wounded conscience. All this is the failing of the heart; but it proves no failing of God, whose grace is sufficient for you, and his strength is shown perfect in your weakness. The heart has a thousand sayings and notions, which God is utterly against.

**Direction 15. When you cannot exercise a trust of assurance, exercise the trust of general faith, and hope, and the quiet submission of yourself to the holy will of God.**

The common excuse for distrust is, I do not know that I am a child of God; and, it is fitting for the ungodly to fear his wrath. But, as the gospel is tidings of great joy to any people where it comes, so it is a word of hope and trust. At least trust God so far as Infinite Goodness should be trusted, who will damn none but the finally obstinate refusers of his saving grace. [120] And with Aaron, Leviticus 10:3, hold your peace, when he is glorifying himself in his corrections. Remember that the will of God is never misguided; that it is the beginning and end of all things (Revelation 4:11; Romans 11:36); that it never wills anything but good; that it is the center and end of all our wills. There is no rest or quietness for our wills, but in the will of God: and his will is always for the good of those who truly desire to be conformed to it, by obedience to his commands, and submission to his disposal. Say, therefore, with your Savior, "Father, if it be your will, let this cup pass from me; but not as I will, but as you will." There is nothing gained by struggling against the will of God; nor anything lost by a quiet submission to it. And if you love it, and desire to obey and please it, trust in it, for it will surely save you.

*In the margin: For a delight in God.*

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible(R), fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Psalm 118:8-9 (flag):** Baxter's King James has "trust in the Lord ... put confidence in princes." The NET has "take shelter in the Lord" in the first half and "trust in people / princes" in the second. The word "trust" is Baxter's theme here, so the NET's "take shelter" loses some of the echo. The NET's "people" is also a change from the "man" Baxter's King James has in verse 8. The quotation skips from verse 8 to verse 9 with "...".
- **Psalm 52:6-7:** NET wording. The King James has "The righteous also shall see, and fear, and laugh at him" and "strengthened himself in his wickedness." The NET has "the godly," "filled with awe," "mock," and "was confident about his plans to destroy others." The sense of the wicked man "strengthening himself" in sin is only loosely kept. The NET verse numbers match Baxter's.
- **Psalm 125:1:** NET wording. The NET's heading "A song of ascents" is part of its verse 1 and is left out, as Baxter does. The King James "abideth for ever" is the NET's "will endure forever."
- **Matthew 10:30; Psalm 56:8; Revelation 7:17; 1 Peter 5:7; Matthew 6:32 (paraphrases):** Baxter paraphrases these in his own words with the reference after. We kept his wording, modernized. For comparison, the NET reads: Matthew 10:30 "Even all the hairs on your head are numbered"; Psalm 56:8 "You keep track of my misery. Put my tears in your leather container. Are they not recorded in your scroll?" (Baxter's "bottle" is the King James wording for the container); Revelation 7:17 "God will wipe away every tear from their eyes"; 1 Peter 5:7 "by casting all your cares on him because he cares for you." Baxter's "sparrow" (Matthew 10:29) is also paraphrased.
- **Matthew 6:24ff:** cited only. Baxter's "Read often Matt. vi. from ver. 24" is turned into "Read often Matthew 6 from verse 24."
- **Isaiah 26:3-4 (important flag):** Baxter's King James has "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee." The NET has "You keep completely safe the people who maintain their faith, for they trust in you." The NET has no word for "peace" or for the mind "stayed" on God, which fits Baxter's argument about quietness of mind in Direction 10; the NET has "completely safe" instead. Also the King James "Jehovah ... everlasting strength" is the NET's "Yah, the Lord, an enduring protector." Baxter's Hebrew-derived "Jehovah" is not carried over.
- **Psalm 112:7-8:** NET wording. Baxter's King James has "his heart is fixed, trusting in the Lord. His heart is established." The NET has "He is confident; he trusts in the Lord. His resolve is firm." Baxter's quotation stops at "he shall not be afraid"; the NET verse 8 continues ("before he looks in triumph on his enemies"), which we did not quote.
- **Psalm 31:19-20, 24:** NET wording. Baxter's King James has "pavilion," "pride of man," and "strife of tongues"; the NET has "shelter," "attacks of men," and "slanderous attacks." Verse 24: Baxter's "Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart" is in the NET only as "Be strong and confident," so the promise that God "shall strengthen your heart" is lost. The NET also has "wait on the Lord" where the King James has "hope in the Lord."
- **Psalm 56:3-4:** NET wording. Baxter's King James has "In God I will praise his word" and "what flesh can do unto me." The NET has "I boast in his promise" and "What can mere men do to me?" The "flesh" of the King James (a word Baxter often uses elsewhere in a spiritual sense) is not in the NET here.
- **Matthew 6:25, 27-28 (important flag):** Baxter's King James has "Take no thought for your life." The NET has "do not worry about your life." Baxter's Direction 12 plays on the word "thought," that is, the King James "thought" in the old sense of anxious worry, and on the thoughts of the mind, so the NET's "worry" loses that play of words. Verse 27: Baxter has "add one cubit to his stature"; the NET has "add even one hour to his life." This changes the picture (height versus length of life). The NET has "Therefore I tell you" at the start of verse 25, which Baxter's quote omits.
- **Psalms 42 and 43 (important flag):** Baxter's quote blends the refrain of Psalm 42:5, 42:11, and 43:5 as the King James has it: "Why art thou cast down, O my soul? ... Trust in God ... who is the health of my countenance." The refrain is the same word for word in the NET in each of the three places; we quote the NET of Psalm 43:5. In the NET the call is "Wait for God!" and not "Trust in God," and the "health of my countenance" becomes "his saving intervention." Baxter builds his whole Direction on the "trust" of the psalm, so this matters. "Depressed" is the NET's word for what the King James calls "cast down"; it is not meant in the modern clinical sense (compare "melancholy" in GLOSSARY.md).
- **Psalm 22:4-5:** NET wording. The King James "were not confounded" is the NET's "were not disappointed."
- **Daniel 3:16-17 (important flag):** Baxter's King James has "We are not careful to answer thee in this matter; our God whom we serve is able to deliver us." The NET has "We do not need to give you a reply concerning this. If our God whom we are serving exists, he is able to rescue us." The NET's "If ... exists" is a conditional that the King James does not have, and Baxter takes the words as plain confidence in God's power. We kept the NET quotation to the extent Baxter quotes it; he does not quote the rest of verse 17.
- **Daniel 6:** cited only (Daniel's praying and the lions' den).
- **Psalm 37:5:** NET wording. The King James "Commit thy way unto the Lord ... he shall bring it to pass" is the NET's "Commit your future to the Lord ... he will act on your behalf."
- **Psalm 20:7:** NET wording. Baxter's "but I will remember the name of the Lord our God" (King James) is the NET's "but we depend on the Lord our God." The NET's "depend" is closer to Baxter's point about trust than "remember the name" is.
- **Psalm 115:9-11:** Baxter quotes only "Trust in him, for he is thy help and shield." The NET repeats a similar line three times (to Israel, to the family of Aaron, to the loyal followers): "trust in the Lord. He is their deliverer and protector." We quote the NET of verse 9. The NET has "their" where Baxter has "thy," and "deliverer and protector" where he has "help and shield."
- **Psalm 73:26 (flag):** Baxter's quoted fragments ("flesh and thy heart," "fail thee," "rock of thy heart and thy portion") are the King James, worked into his sentence, so we kept them as his quotation (modernized) and did not replace them with the NET. The NET reads "My flesh and my heart may grow weak, but God always protects my heart and gives me stability." The NET has no "rock" and no "portion" here; Baxter's phrase "rock of thy heart" and "portion" is lost.
- **Psalm 77:7-10:** cited only. The NET verses are the psalmist's questions "Will the Lord reject me forever?" and so on; Baxter uses them as the example of a heart saying what God never said.
- **Leviticus 10:3:** Baxter's "hold your peace" is the King James (Aaron "held his peace"); the NET has "So Aaron kept silent." Baxter's reference stands.
- **Revelation 4:11; Romans 11:36:** cited only, for the thought that God's will is the beginning and end of all things. The NET of Romans 11:36 is "from him and through him and to him are all things."
- **The prayer of Christ (Matthew 26:39; Luke 22:42), unmarked:** Baxter gives no reference. His wording ("Father, if it be thy will, let this cup pass from me; but not as I will, but as thou wilt") blends the Gospel accounts, so we kept it as his, modernized. The NET of Matthew 26:39 reads: "My Father, if possible, let this cup pass from me! Yet not what I will, but what you will." The NET of Luke 22:42 reads: "Father, if you are willing, take this cup away from me. Yet not my will but yours be done." (References added by us, not in Baxter's text.)
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "a broken reed" (compare Isaiah 36:6); "Let them help you whom you have trusted" (compare the language of Judges 10:14; we have not confirmed that Baxter has this verse in mind); "the gospel is tidings of great joy" (compare Luke 2:10); "put my tears in his bottle" (Psalm 56:8, cited); "his strength is shown perfect in your weakness" and "grace is sufficient for you" (compare 2 Corinthians 12:9); "forsaken by God" (compare Psalm 22:1).
- **Direction 6, "oblige him" and "greatly offends him":** Baxter has "oblige him" and "disoblige him." In 1673 "oblige" means to bind by a favor or promise; "disoblige" means to offend or fail someone one is bound to. We kept "oblige" and changed "disoblige" to "offends." **Direction 7, "creatures":** kept; it means created things and beings, as in Baxter's contrast of God and creatures.
- **Direction 8:** "concernments" became "concerns" (GLOSSARY). "Punctually" became "exact." "Providence" is kept as a theological term.
- **Direction 9, "the plaster":** a medical dressing laid on a wound or sore; Baxter's comparison is to a friend who pulls it off at the first complaint of pain. Kept. "Sure" in "my surest friend" is left as "surest." "Forwarder" became "more eager."
- **Direction 10:** "Betake yourselves" became "turn." "Unsatisfactory" became "unsatisfying." "Unquietness" became "restlessness." "Ulcerated, festered" kept. "Rationally" kept.
- **Direction 11, "pregnant, multiplying sin":** kept as Baxter's image of a sin that is fruitful and breeds more sin. "Presently" became "at once." "Suffer" became "allow." "Shifts" became "expedients"; "temporizings" became "time-serving" (going along with whatever the time demands). "Prevail" is kept.
- **Direction 12, "Cogitations":** Baxter's word became "Thoughts." "Actuate" became "set in motion." "Passions" is kept as his technical term for the emotions (compare "affections" in GLOSSARY.md). "Suffer not" became "Do not allow."
- **Direction 13:** "chide" became "scold." "Estate" became "property," "name" became "reputation." "Conduct and disposal" kept. "Sped ill" became "fared badly." "Recanted" became "taken back." "Confuted" became "refuted." "Pretend" became "claim" (GLOSSARY). "Harbour" became "harbor." "Careful" in the Daniel quote is replaced by the NET. "Atheistically" and "idolatrously" kept. "Worm" kept (a human being as a worm). "Disquietment" became "disquiet."
- **Direction 14, "conceits":** became "notions" (CONVENTIONS). "Reprobate" is kept as a theological term. Baxter's "make one fear the reason of another" is left as written; it seems to mean that one fancy makes a person fear what another suggests, but the syntax is compressed. "Disquietness" became "disquiet."
- **Direction 15:** "Common pretence" became "common excuse." "Beseems" became "is fitting for." "Tidings" kept ("tidings of great joy"). "Centre" became "center." "Saviour" became "Savior."
- **Baxter's polemic and doctrine:** "will damn none but the finally obstinate refusers of his saving grace" is Baxter's own statement, kept as written; note [120] points to a later discussion we have not checked.
- **"Of hope and assurance I have spoken afterward" (note 120):** we have not looked up where this is; it is a cross-reference to another part of the *Directory*.
- **Margin note at the end:** "For a delight in God" is Baxter's margin note for Grand Direction XIII ("Diligently labor that God and holiness may be your chief delight"), whose heading falls in the next file.
