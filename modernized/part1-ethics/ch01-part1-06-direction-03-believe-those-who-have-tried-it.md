# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 3

**Direction 3. To know what a life of holiness is, believe the word of God, and those that have tried it; and do not believe the slanders of the devil and of ungodly men, that never tried or knew the things which they reproach.**

Reason cannot question the reasonableness of this advice. Who is wiser than God? or who is to be believed above him? And what men are more likely to know what they talk about than such as speak from their own experience? Nothing is more familiar with wicked men than to slander and reproach the holy ways and servants of the Lord. No wisdom, no measure of holiness or righteousness, will exempt the godly from their malice; otherwise Christ himself at least would have been exempted, if not his apostles and other saints, whom they have slandered and put to death. Christ has foretold us what to expect from them (John 15:18–21): "If the world hates you, be aware that it hated me first. If you belonged to the world, the world would love you as its own. However, because you do not belong to the world, but I chose you out of the world, for this reason the world hates you. Remember what I told you, 'A slave is not greater than his master.' If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you. If they obeyed my word, they will obey yours too."

The truth is, wicked men are the seed and children of the devil, and have his image, and obey him, and think, and speak, and do as he would have them; and the godly are the seed and members of Christ, and bear his image, and obey him: and do you think that the devil will bid his children speak well of the ways or followers of Christ? I must confess that until I had found the truth of it by experience, I was not aware how shameless in slandering, and cruel in abusing the servants of Christ, his worldly, malicious enemies are. [22] I had often read how early an enmity was put between the woman's and the serpent's seed, and I had read and wondered that the first man that was born into the world murdered his brother for worshipping God more acceptably than himself; "because his deeds were evil, but his brother's were righteous" (1 John 3:12). I had read the inference in verse 13, "do not be surprised, brothers and sisters, if the world hates you"; but yet I did not so fully understand that wicked men and devils are so very alike, and so closely related, until the words of Christ (John 8:44), expounded by visible demonstrations, had taught it to me. Indeed the apostle says (1 John 3:12) that Cain was of the evil one, that is, the devil: but Christ says more plainly, "You people are from your father the devil, and you want to do what your father desires. He was a murderer from the beginning, and does not uphold the truth because there is no truth in him. Whenever he lies, he speaks according to his own nature because he is a liar and the father of lies." Here note that cruel murder and lying are the principal actions of a devil; and that as the father of these, he is the father of the wicked, who are most notoriously addicted to these two courses against the most innocent servants of the Lamb. How just it is that they dwell together hereafter who are here so alike in disposition and action: even as the righteous shall dwell with Christ, who bore his image, and imitated his holy, suffering life.

I conclude, then, that if you will never turn to God and a holy life until wicked men stop slandering and reproaching them, you may as well say that you will never be reconciled to God until the devil is first reconciled to him; and never love Christ until the devil loves him, or bids you love him; or never be a saint until the devil is a saint, or will give you leave; and that you will not be saved until the devil is willing that you be saved.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **John 15:18–21:** Baxter quotes verses 18–20 and cites 18–21; we kept his citation and quoted what he quotes.
- **1 John 3:12–13:** the NET reads "brothers and sisters" where the King James has "my brethren." Baxter's phrase "of that wicked one" is rendered "of the evil one," the NET wording.
- **John 8:44:** the King James has the devil "abode not in the truth," which Baxter quotes. The NET reads "does not uphold the truth." The sense is close, but "abode not" suggests he once stood in the truth and fell, while the NET does not.
- **slandering** (paragraphs 2 and 3): Baxter's "belying," meaning lying about someone.
- **shameless:** Baxter's "impudent." **aware:** Baxter's "sensible."
- **the woman's and the serpent's seed** (paragraph 2): an allusion to Genesis 3:15. **the first man that was born into the world** is Cain (Genesis 4).
- **Victor Uticensis** (note 22): the name Baxter uses for the historian more often called Victor of Vita, who wrote about the persecution of Catholic Christians under the Arian Vandals in North Africa. **Arian** is Baxter's "Arrian." **consecrated virgins:** Baxter's "devoted virgins." **N. B.** is *nota bene*, "note well," and is Baxter's.
