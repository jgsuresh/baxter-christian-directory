# Chapter 8: Part 2, directions for the government of the eyes

**Part 2.**

**Particular directions for the government of the eyes.**

**Direction 1. Know the uses that your sight is given you for.**

For example: 1. To see the works of God, that thereby your minds may see God himself. 2. To read the word of God, that in it you may perceive his mind. 3. To see the servants of God whom you must love, and the poor whom you must relieve or pity, and all the visible objects of your duty; to conduct your body in the discharge of its office about all the matters of the world. [379] And in particular often to look up toward heaven, the place where your blessed Lord is glorified, and from which he will come to take you to his glory.

**Direction 2. Remember the sins which the eye is most in danger of, that you may be watchful and escape.**

1. You must take heed of a proud, and lofty, and scornful eye; which looks on yourselves with admiration and delight, as the peacock is said to do on his tail, and on others as below you with slighting and disdain. [380] 2. You must take heed of a lustful, wanton eye, which secretly carries out your heart to a befooling piece of dirty flesh, and steals from beauty and ornaments a spark to kindle that fire which prepares for everlasting fire. [381] 3. Take heed of a grasping, greedy eye, which with Achan and Gehazi looks on the bait to tempt you to unlawful love and desire, and to bring you by their sin to their ruin. [382] 4. Take heed of a self-indulgent, gluttonous, and drunken eye; [383] which is looking on the forbidden fruit, and on the tempting dish, and the delicious cup, till it has provoked the appetite of that greedy worm, which must be pleased, though at the price of your damnation. 5. Take heed of a gazing, wandering eye, [384] which, like a vagrant, has no home, nor work, nor master, but gads about to seek after death, and find out matter for temptation. "Wisdom is directly in front of the discerning person, but the eyes of a fool run to the ends of the earth" (Proverbs 17:24). 6. Take heed of an envious eye, which looks with dislike and discontent at the prosperity of others, especially such as stand opposed to your own interest. [385] "Is your eye evil because I am good?" (Matthew 20:15). It is the envious eye that in Scripture most usually is called by the name of an evil eye, πονηρος οφθαλμος. It is an eye that would see evil rather than good upon another: [386] as "Lest your eye be evil against your poor brother," etc. (Deuteronomy 15:9). Proverbs 23:6, it is an eye that grudges another anything that is ours. So Proverbs 28:22; Mark 7:22. 7. Take heed of an emotional, cruel eye, that kindles the hurting or reviling fire in your breast, or is kindled by it; that fetches matter for rage or malice from all that displeases you in another. [387] 8. Take heed of a self-conceited and censorious eye, that looks on all the actions of another with quarreling, undervaluing, censure, or reproach. [388] 9. Take heed of a doting and fanciful eye, that falls in love too much with houses, or friend, or child, or goods, or whatever pleases it. 10. Take heed of a sleepy, sluggish eye, that is shut to good, and would rather sleep than watch, and read, and pray, and labor. [389] 11. Abhor a malignant eye, which looks with hatred on a godly man, and upon the holy assemblies and communion of saints, and upon holy actions; and can scarcely see a man of exemplary zeal and holiness but the heart rises against him, and could wish all such expelled or cut off from the earth. [390] This is the heart that has the image of the devil in most lively colors, he being the father of such, as Christ calls him (John 8:44). 12. Abhor a hypocritical eye, which is lifted up to heaven, when the heart is on earth, on lusts, on honors, on amusements, or pleasure, or plotting mischief against the just. [391] Know the evil and danger of all these diseases of the eye.

**Direction 3. Remember that the eye, being the noblest, and yet the most dangerous sense, must have the strictest watch.**

Sight is often put in Scripture for all the senses; and living by sight is opposed to living or walking by faith. "We live by faith, not by sight" (2 Corinthians 5:7). And a pleasure-loving life is called a "walking in the ways of our heart and in the sight of the eyes" (Ecclesiastes 11:9). An ungoverned eye shows the power of the ungoverned senses. Abundance of good or evil enters in by these doors: all lies open if you do not guard these.

**Direction 4. Remember that as your sin or duty, so your sorrow or joy depend much on the government of your eyes; and their present pleasure is the common way to later sorrow.**

What a flood of grief David let into his heart by one unlawful look!

**Direction 5. Remember that your eye is much of your honor or dishonor, because it is the index of your minds.**

You see that which is next to the mind itself, or the most immediate beam of the invisible soul, when you see the eye. How easily a wandering eye, a wanton eye, a proud eye, a self-indulgent eye, a malicious eye, an emotional eye betrays the treasure of sin which is in the heart! [392] Your soul lies more open to the view of others in your eye than in any other part: your very reputation therefore should make you watch.

**Direction 6. Remember that your eye is of all the senses most subject to the will, and therefore there is the more of duty or sin in it; for voluntariness is the requisite to morality, both good and evil.**

Your will cannot so easily command your feeling, tasting, hearing, or smelling, as it can your sight; so easily can it open or shut the eye in a moment, that you are the more inexcusable if it is not governed; for all its faults will be proved the more voluntary. Ham was cursed for not turning away his eyes from his father's shame, and Shem and Japheth blessed for doing it. The righteous is thus described, "He who stops his ears from hearing of blood, and shuts his eyes from seeing evil; he shall dwell on high," etc. (Isaiah 33:15). Men's idols which they are commanded to cast away are called "The abomination of their eyes" (Ezekiel 20:7). Greed is called "The desire of the eyes" (1 John 2:16). It is said of the unclean that they have "eyes, full of adultery" (2 Peter 2:14). And as sin, so punishment is placed on the eye: [393] "The eyes of the lofty shall be humbled" (Isaiah 5:15). Yes, the whole bodies of the daughters of Zion are threatened to be dishonored with nakedness, scabs, and stink, and shame, because they walked with "wanton eyes, haughtily, and mincing as they go," etc. (Isaiah 3:16).

**Direction 7. Therefore let believing reason, and a holy, resolved, fixed will, keep a continual law upon your eyes, and let them be used as under a constant government.**

This Job calls the "making a covenant with them" (Job 31:1). Do not leave them at liberty; as if a look had nothing in it of duty or sin; or as if you might look on whatever you would. Will you go to foolish, tempting plays, and gaze on empty, alluring objects, and think there is no harm in all this? Do you think your eye cannot sin as well as your tongue? undoubtedly it is much sin that is both committed by it, and enters at it: keep away therefore from the bait, or command your eye to turn away.

**Direction 8. Remember always how much more easy and safe it is to stop sin here at the gates and outworks, than to beat it out again when it has once got in: if it has but tainted your very imagination or memory (as tempting sights will almost unavoidably do), it has there spawned the matter for a swarm of empty and sinful thoughts.**

It is almost impossible to rule the thoughts without ruling the eye: and then the passions are at once tainted; and the citadel of the heart is taken before you are aware. You little know, when a lustful look, or a greedy look, begins the game, to how sad an end it tends. Many a horrid adultery, and murder, and robbery, and wickedness, has begun but with a look: a look has begun that which has brought many a thousand to the gallows, and many millions to hell!

**Direction 9. Keep both eye and mind employed in continual duty, and do not let them be idle, and have leisure to wander upon empty things.**

Idleness and neglect of spiritual and bodily duty is the beginner and the nurse of much indulgence in physical pleasure. Let your spiritual work and your lawful bodily labors take up your time and thoughts, and command and keep your senses in their services.

**Direction 10. Beg daily of God the preserving assistance of his grace and providence.**

Of his inward grace to confirm you and assist you in your resolutions and watch; and of his providence and gracious ordering of you and objects, to keep the temptations from before your eyes: and when others will run and go on purpose to gaze on empty or tempting shows, or to admire like children the empty things of the playful, pompous world, you go to God with David's prayer, "Turn my eyes away from what is worthless. Revive me with your word" (Psalm 119:37). And imitate him: "My eyes anticipate the nighttime hours so that I can meditate on your word" (verse 148). And make every look a passage to your mind, to carry it up to God, and pray, "Open my eyes so I can truly see the marvelous things in your law" (Psalm 119:18). Observe these, with the general directions named before.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Headings:** the source prints "PART II." and the italic sub-heading "Particular Directions far the Government of the Eyes"; "far" is a misprint for "for," corrected here. As elsewhere in this chapter, each numbered "Direct." becomes a bold **Direction** line holding its directive sentence(s), and the rest of the paragraph follows. In Directions 3 to 8 this split falls at Baxter's own sentence or colon boundary; no words were moved between sentences.
- **Matthew 20:15:** Baxter quotes the King James "Is thine eye evil, because I am good?" The NET reads "Or are you envious because I am generous?" Since Baxter's point is the phrase "evil eye," we keep his wording (modernized). The NET's "envious" in fact supports his claim that the "evil eye" of Scripture means envy. The Greek he cites, πονηρος οφθαλμος (printed without accents), means "evil eye."
- **Deuteronomy 15:9:** Baxter's "Lest thine eye be evil against thy poor brother" is the King James. The NET has "and your attitude be wrong toward your impoverished fellow Israelite," with no eye. Kept in Baxter's wording, modernized.
- **Proverbs 23:6; 28:22; Mark 7:22:** cited by Baxter for the "evil eye." The King James has "evil eye" in all three; the NET reads "stingy person" (Proverbs 23:6; 28:22) and "evil" in Mark 7:22's list, so the eye image his argument depends on is not visible in the NET.
- **Proverbs 17:24:** NET wording. Baxter's King James "the eyes of a fool are in the ends of the earth" becomes "the eyes of a fool run to the ends of the earth"; the sense he uses is kept.
- **2 Corinthians 5:7:** NET "we live by faith, not by sight"; Baxter quotes the King James "We walk by faith." His surrounding words ("living or walking by faith") cover both. The source has no period after "2 Cor. v. 7"; we supply the sentence break that the capital "And" shows.
- **Ecclesiastes 11:9:** Baxter adapts the King James ("walk in the ways of thine heart, and in the sight of thine eyes") into "our heart." Kept as his adaptation, modernized. The NET reads "Follow the impulses of your heart and the desires of your eyes," which loses the word "sight" that Baxter is leaning on.
- **Isaiah 33:15:** Baxter's quotation is the King James. The NET reads very differently ("the one who does not plot violent crimes and does not seek to harm others— this is the person who will live in a secure place"), with no ears or eyes, so we keep Baxter's wording, modernized.
- **Ezekiel 20:7:** King James "the abominations of his eyes." The NET has "the detestable idols you keep before you," with no "eyes," so Baxter's wording is kept.
- **1 John 2:16:** NET "the desire of the eyes" for the King James "the lust of the eyes"; the sense Baxter uses is unchanged.
- **2 Peter 2:14:** NET "eyes, full of adultery," the same as the King James phrase.
- **Isaiah 5:15:** King James "the eyes of the lofty shall be humbled." The NET reads "the proud will be brought low," with no eyes, so Baxter's wording is kept; his point that punishment "is placed on the eye" depends on the King James wording.
- **Isaiah 3:16:** Baxter's "wanton eyes, haughtily, and mincing as they go" is a condensed King James phrase ("walk with stretched forth necks and wanton eyes, walking and mincing as they go"). Kept as his wording. The NET reads "They walk with their heads high and flirt with their eyes. They skip along." The "nakedness, scabs, and stink, and shame" refer to the threats later in the chapter (NET 3:17 "skin diseases"; 3:24 "A putrid stench"); Baxter gives no reference for them.
- **Job 31:1:** Baxter's "the making a covenant with them" is his paraphrase; the NET reads "I made a covenant with my eyes."
- **Psalm 119:37, 148, 18:** NET wording. In 119:37 the King James "Turn away mine eyes from beholding vanity: and quicken me in thy way" becomes "Turn my eyes away from what is worthless. Revive me with your word"; the NET has "word" where the King James has "way." In 119:148 the King James "prevent" (go before) becomes "anticipate." Baxter's "ver. 148" is rendered "verse 148."
- **Ham** (Direction 6): Baxter says Ham was cursed; in Genesis 9:25 the curse is pronounced on Canaan, Ham's son. Kept as Baxter wrote it.
- **Unmarked allusions:** Achan (Joshua 7:21) and Gehazi (2 Kings 5:20–27); the forbidden fruit (Genesis 3:6); David's "one unlawful look" (2 Samuel 11:2); Shem and Japheth (Genesis 9:23). None of these references is in Baxter's text.
- **Word swaps:** "As, 1." to "For example: 1."; "in special" to "in particular"; "whence" to "from which"; "covetous" to "grasping, greedy" in item 3 (Baxter's "greedy, covetous" doubles the idea), and "covetous look" to "greedy look," "Covetousness" to "Greed"; "luxurious" to "self-indulgent" (in 1673 it meant given to excess or lust, not costly); "at the rate of" to "at the price of"; "stand cross to" to "stand opposed to"; "passionate" to "emotional" (per the glossary; here the feeling is mainly anger, as the next clause shows); "fond" to "doting" (foolishly fond); "had rather" to "would rather"; "bewray" to "betrays"; "unexcusable" to "inexcusable"; "Remember still" to "Remember always"; "fantasy" to "imagination" (the faculty of images, not daydream); "presently" to "at once"; "period" to "end"; "vain"/"vanity" to "empty"/"empty things"; "sensual" to "pleasure-loving," "sensuality" to "indulgence in physical pleasure"; "corporal" to "bodily"; "disposals" to "ordering"; "forenamed" to "named before"; "&c." to "etc."
- **befooling** (item 2): kept; it means "making a fool of you." **that greedy worm** (item 4): Baxter's image for the appetite. **gads about:** wanders idly. **malignant** (item 11): Baxter's usual word for those who hate godliness and godly people. **index** (Direction 5): a pointer or indicator, the thing that shows what the mind holds. **watch** (items 10 and Direction 5): stay awake and on guard.
- **"This is the heart..."** (item 11): Baxter writes "heart" while describing the malignant eye; kept as printed.
- **"undoubtedly"** (Direction 7): lower case after the question mark as in the source; we have not made it a new sentence.
- **Dr. Hammond** (note 385): probably Henry Hammond, the seventeenth-century English divine whose annotations on the New Testament Baxter seems to have in mind; we have not confirmed the identification or the edition.
