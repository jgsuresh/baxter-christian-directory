# Chapter 5: Title 3, Directions 7-11 (little losses, readiness, uncertain life, holiness in all, and the thieves of time)

**Direction 7. Endure patiently some smaller inconvenience and loss, for the avoiding of greater, and for the redeeming of time for greater duties: and let little things be resolutely cast out of your way, when they would draw out your time by imperceptible degrees.**

The devil would cunningly steal that from you by drops, which he cannot get you to throw away lavishly at once; he who will not spend extravagantly by the pounds may run out by not regarding pence. You will have the pretexts of decency, and propriety, and civility, and good manners, and avoiding offense, and censure, and of some necessity too, to draw out your precious time from you little by little; and if you are so easy as to yield, it will almost all be wasted by this temptation. As, if you are ministers of Christ, whose time must be spent in your studies, and pulpits, and in conversation with your people, and visiting them, and watching over them; and it is your daily groans that time is short and work is long, and that you are forced to omit so many needed studies, and pass by so many needy souls, for lack of time; yet if you do not look well about you, and will not bear some censure and offense, you will lose even the rest of the time, which now you make good use of. Your friends about you will be tempting and telling you, O this friend really must be visited, and the other friend must be civilly treated; you must not shake them off so quickly; they expect more of your time and company: you are much obliged to them; they will say you are rude and morose. Such a scholar comes to be acquainted with you; and he will take it badly, and misrepresent you to others, if you do not allow him time for some friendly conversation. It is one who was never with you before, and never took up any of your time: and so says the next and the next as well as he. Such a one visited you, and you really must visit him in return. There is this journey or that which really must be made; and this business and that which really must be done. Indeed, one's very household affairs will steal away all his time, if he does not watch closely: we will have this servant to talk to, and the other to hear, and our relatives to attend to, and an abundance of little things to mind, so little as not to be named by themselves, about food, and drink, and clothes, and dressing, and house, and goods, and servants, and work, and tradesmen, and messengers, and marketing, and payments, and cattle, and a hundred things not to be counted up, that will every one take up a little of your time; and those littles set together will be all. As the greedy moneylender who, to purchase a place of honor, agreed for a month to give a penny to everyone that asked him; which being quickly spread abroad in the city, there came so many for their pence as took all that he had, and made him give up his place of honor, because he had nothing left to maintain it. So perhaps you are an eminent, much valued minister; and this draws upon you such a multitude of acquaintances, every one expecting a little of your time, that among them all they leave you almost none for your studies; by which not only your conscience is wounded, but your abilities are quenched, and your work is starved and poorly done, and so your admirers themselves begin to think as lightly of you as of others, for that which is the effect of their own pressing demands. And as in our yearly expenses of our money there goes nearly as much in little matters, not to be named by themselves, and incidental, unexpected costs, of which no account can be given beforehand, as does in food, and clothing, and the ordinary costs which we foresee and count on; just so it will be with your precious time, if you are not very thrifty and resolute, and do not look well to it: you will have such an abundance of little matters, scarcely fit to be named, which will every one require a little, and one begin where the other ends, that you will find in the review, when time is gone, that Satan was too cunning for you, and cheated you by drawing you into seeming necessities. This is the chief reason why marriage and housekeeping are so greatly inconvenient to a pastor of the church who can avoid them; because they bring upon him such an abundance of these little distractions, which cannot be foreseen. In this case a conscientious man (in whatever calling) must be resolute: and when he has tried with reason to satisfy those with expectations, and put off distractions, if that will not do he must neglect them, and cast them off, and break away, though he loses by it in his estate, or his reputation, or his peace itself, and though he is censured for it as imprudent, rude, morose, or neglectful of his friends. God must be pleased, whoever is displeased: we must satisfy our minds with his approval alone, instead of all: time must be saved, whatever is lost or wasted; and the great things must be done, whatever becomes of the less: though where both may be done, and the lesser does not hinder the greater, and does not rob us of time from necessary things, there we must take care of both.

**Direction 8. Labor to go always equipped and well provided for the performance of every duty which may occur.**

As he who will not lose his time in preaching must be well provided; so he who will not lose his time in solitude must be always equipped with matter for profitable meditation; and he who would redeem his time in company must be always equipped with matter for profitable conversation: he who is full will be ready to pour out to others, and not be silent and lose his time for lack of matter, or skill, or zeal; for in all these three your provision does consist. An ignorant, empty person lacks matter for his thoughts and words; an imprudent person lacks skill to use it; a careless, cold, indifferent person lacks life to set his faculties in motion, and oil and weights to set the wheels of his soul and body going. Consider in the morning what company you are likely to meet, and what occasions of duty you are likely to have; and provide yourselves accordingly before you go, with matter and resolution. Besides the general preparation of habitual knowledge, love, and zeal, which is the chief, you should also have your particular preparations for the duties of each day. [290] A workman who is strong and healthy, and has all his tools in readiness and order, will do more in a day than a sick man, or one who lacks tools, or keeps them dull and unfit for use, will do in many. "The godly speak wise words and promote justice"; and no wonder, when "The law of their God controls their thinking; their feet do not slip" (Psalm 37:30–31). "...the mouth speaks from what fills the heart. The good person brings good things out of his good treasury" (Matthew 12:34–35). "...every expert in the law who has been trained for the kingdom of heaven is like the owner of a house who brings out of his treasure what is new and old" (Matthew 13:52).

**Direction 9. Do not promise long life to yourselves, but live as those who are always uncertain of another day, and certain to be shortly gone from here.**

The groundless expectation of long life is a very great hindrance to the redeeming of our time. Men will spend extravagantly out of a full purse, who would be sparing if they knew they had only a little, or were likely to come to need themselves. Young people, and healthy people, are under the greatest temptation to the loss of time. They are apt to think that they have time enough before them, and that though it is possible that they may die soon, yet it is more likely that they will live long: and so, putting the day of death far from them, they lack all those awakenings which the face of death brings to those who still expect it; and therefore lack the wisdom, zeal, and diligence which are necessary to the redemption of their time. Pray therefore as Psalm 90:12, "So teach us to consider our mortality, so that we might live wisely." Do not dream of rest and plenty for many years, when you have no promise to live till the next morning (Luke 12:19–20). When they perceive death is at hand and time is near an end, almost all men seem highly to value time, and promise to spend it better if God would only try them once again. Therefore do continually perceive that death is even at hand, and time near an end, and then it will make you continually wiser than death makes most men; and to redeem your time as others intend to redeem it when it is too late.

**Direction 10. Sanctify all to God that you have and do, and let Holiness to the Lord be written upon all; whether you eat or drink, let it be intended and ordered ultimately to his glory.**

Make all your civil relationships, possessions, and occupations holy in this way; directing them to the service and pleasing of God, and to the everlasting good of yourselves or others, and mixing holy meditation and prayer with them all in season. [291] And so we are told to "constantly pray," and "in everything give thanks" (1 Thessalonians 5:17–18). And "in every situation, through prayer and petition with thanksgiving, tell your requests to God" (Philippians 4:6). And "all things are sanctified by the word and prayer." This sacred alchemy, that turns all our conduct, and possessions, and actions into holy, is an excellent part of the art of redeeming time.

**Direction 11. Lastly, be acquainted with the great thieves that rob men of their time, and with the devil's methods in enticing them to lose it, and live in continual watchfulness against them.**

It is a more necessary thrift to be sparing and saving of your time than of your money. It concerns you more to keep a continual watch against the things which would rob you of your time than against those thieves who would break into your house, and rob you on the highway. Those persons who would tempt you to the loss of time are to be regarded as your enemies, and avoided. I will here recite the names of these thieves, and time-wasters, that you may detest them, and save your time and souls from their deceits.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where this file sits:** it covers Directions 7 to 11 of Title 3 ("Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," a heading or main division of Chapter 5). The range begins exactly at the heading of Direction 7. It ends with Baxter's announcement that he will "recite the names of these thieves"; the list itself follows in the next section of the source and is not in this file.
- **Bold heading sentences:** as in the finished files, the first sentence of each Direction is set in bold. In Direction 10 that sentence runs through "ultimately to his glory."
- **Psalm 37:30–31 (flag):** Baxter puts the reference before the quotation ("Psal. xxxvii. 30, 31"); we follow the project's practice and put it in parentheses after. His point is that a good man's speech comes from what is stored within him ("The law of his God is in his heart"). The NET reads "The law of their God controls their thinking," so the image of the law stored "in his heart" (Baxter's "provision") is weaker. The NET also turns the King James singular ("the righteous ... his tongue") into the plural ("The godly ... their feet"), and "his tongue talketh of judgment" becomes "promote justice." The API text ran "wise wordsand" together; we added the space.
- **Matthew 12:34–35 (flag):** the NET begins verse 34 "Offspring of vipers! ..." and Baxter quotes only the last clause, so we begin with "...". The King James "Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" is "the mouth speaks from what fills the heart." In verse 35 Baxter quotes the King James "out of the good treasure of his heart"; the NET reads "out of his good treasury," without "of his heart" (probably because the NET follows a Greek text without those words; we have not checked the NET's own note). Since Baxter's point is the heart's stored provision, the loss is worth noting.
- **Matthew 13:52:** NET wording; the verse begins "Then he said to them, 'Therefore...'", so we begin with "...". "Every scribe which is instructed unto the kingdom of heaven ... an householder" is "every expert in the law who has been trained for the kingdom of heaven ... the owner of a house"; "things new and old" is "what is new and old."
- **Psalm 90:12 (flag):** Baxter's "So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom" is the King James. The NET reads "So teach us to consider our mortality, so that we might live wisely." The counting of days, which fits Baxter's theme of time, is gone, though the NET's "mortality" still fits his Direction about the uncertainty of life. Baxter's "Pray therefore as Psal. xc. 12" is kept as "Pray therefore as Psalm 90:12."
- **Luke 12:19–20:** cited only (the rich fool who promised himself "many years").
- **1 Thessalonians 5:17–18:** NET wording. "Pray continually" and "in all things give thanks" are, in the NET, "constantly pray" and "in everything give thanks."
- **Philippians 4:6 (flag):** Baxter's quotation is a sentence fragment that continues "we are bid": "in all things to make known our requests to God, in prayer, supplication, and giving of thanks." The NET verse is an imperative with a different order: "in every situation, through prayer and petition with thanksgiving, tell your requests to God." We used the NET wording, so the clause no longer depends grammatically on "we are told." The source prints the reference "Phil, iv. 6," with a comma for a period.
- **"all things are sanctified by the word and prayer" (paraphrase):** Baxter gives no reference in the text; his note [291] includes 1 Timothy 4:5 (reference not in Baxter's text at this point). His wording blends the King James 4:4 ("every creature of God is good") with 4:5 ("it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer"). We kept his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET reads "For every creation of God is good ... For it is sanctified by God's word and by prayer." In Paul the "it" is food received with thanksgiving; Baxter widens it to "all things."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "Holiness to the Lord" (Zechariah 14:20, the inscription on the horses' bells; the NET has "Holy to the Lord"; Exodus 28:36 has the same inscription on the high priest's turban); "whether you eat or drink" (1 Corinthians 10:31; NET "So whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God"); "Dream not of rest and plenty for many years" (Luke 12:19, which Baxter cites).
- **"As, if you are ministers of Christ":** the source reads "As if you be ministers of Christ," where "As" means "for example." We added a comma so that it does not read as "as though."
- **The moneylender story:** Baxter's sentence beginning "As the greedy moneylender" has no main verb; we kept it as he wrote it. He gives no source for the anecdote, and we have not identified one. "Usurer" (Baxter's word) meant a moneylender who charged interest; we use "moneylender."
- **Marriage and housekeeping:** Baxter's view that marriage and running a household are "so greatly inconvenient to a pastor of the church who can avoid them" is kept exactly as he states it.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): insensible, imperceptible; profusely, lavishly; prodigally, extravagantly; pretences, pretexts; seemliness, propriety; by little and little, little by little; conference, conversation; needful, needed; for want of, for lack of; improve (time), make good use of; must needs, really must; look for, expect; uncivil, rude; take it ill, take it badly; familiar discourse, friendly conversation; visit him again, visit him in return (that is, return the visit); gone (a journey), made; yea, indeed; family occasions, household affairs; watch not narrowly, does not watch closely; respect (our relations), attend to; meat, food; reckoned up, counted up; covetous usurer, greedy moneylender; noised abroad, spread abroad; quit, give up; parts, abilities; set as light by, think as lightly of; importunity, pressing demands; charges, costs; raiment, clothing; foreknow and reckon upon, foresee and count on; grand reason, chief reason; diversions, distractions; conscionable, conscientious; in what calling soever, in whatever calling; endeavoured, tried; expectants, those with expectations; put by, put off; serve, do; repute, reputation; neglective, neglectful; approbation, approval; spared (time), saved; furnished, equipped; solitariness, solitude; discourse, conversation; wants, lacks; on motion, in motion; poise, weights (the weights that drive a clock's wheels); a-going, going; bethink you, consider; like, likely; preparative, preparation; charity, love; healthful, healthy; wanteth, lacks; hence, here; come to want, come to need; quickly (die), soon; esteem of, value; but (only), only; purpose, intend; the most, most men; employments, occupations; designing, directing; bid, told; alchymy, alchemy; conversation, conduct; concerneth, concerns; break (your house), break into; by the highway, on the highway; taken as, regarded as.
- **civil relationships:** "civil" here means ordinary social and household relationships, as distinct from religious ones.
- **estate** (Direction 7): Baxter's word for a person's property and means; kept.
- **"into holy"** (Direction 10): Baxter's elliptical "turneth all our conversation ... into holy" is kept as written.
- **"and to redeem your time"** (end of Direction 9): Baxter's syntax is loose here ("it will make you ... wiser ... and to redeem your time"); kept as written.
- **Do you therefore continually perceive:** Baxter's emphatic imperative is rendered "Therefore do continually perceive."
