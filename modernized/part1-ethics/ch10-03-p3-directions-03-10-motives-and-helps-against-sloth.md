# Chapter 10: Title 3, Direction 3 (continued) to Direction 10: more motives to zeal, and helps against sloth

*This section begins partway through Direction 3, at item 6 of Baxter's list of motives to zeal and diligence; the previous file covers the Direction's heading and items 1–5.*

6. To remember how many millions are undone already by their sloth and negligence! how many are in hell lamenting their slothfulness on earth, while I am hearing, or reading, or praying to prevent it, one would think should waken me from my sloth: what if I saw them, and heard their cries? would it not make me serious? What if one of them had time and leave and hope again as I have? would he be cold and careless?

7. To think how many millions are now in heaven, that all came there by holy zeal and diligence, and are now enjoying the fruit of all their labor and sufferings! to think of the blessed end of all their pains and patience, and how far they are now from repenting of it! I think should stir us up to zeal and diligence.

8. To foresee what thoughts all the world will have of holy diligence at last! how the best will wish they had been better, and had done much more for God and their salvation! and how the worst will wish, when it is too late, that they had been as zealous and diligent as the best! How earnestly they will then knock and cry, "Lord ... Let us in!" when it is all in vain! and say to the watchful, diligent souls, "Give us some of your oil because our lamps are going out," Matthew 25. To think how glad the most ungodly would then be, if they might only have "died the death of the righteous, and their latter end might be like his!" Numbers 23:10. And what heart-tearing grief will seize upon them forever, to think how madly they lost their souls, and sluggishly went to hell to spare their pains of that sweet and holy work that should have prevented it! Will not such forethoughts awaken the most sluggish, stupid souls, that will only follow them till they can do their work?

9. Remember that you must be zealous and diligent in this or nothing; for there is nothing else that is worth your seriousness, in comparison with this. To be earnest and laborious for perishing empty things is the disgrace of your mind, and will prove your disappointment, and leave you at last in shame and sorrow; when holy diligence will repay all your pains.

10. Remember also that you have been slothful and negligent too long! And how do you repent of your former sloth, if you will be as slothful still? Are you grieved to think how many duties slothfulness has put aside, and how many it has murdered, and frustrated, and made nothing of, and how much grace, and mercy, and comfort it has already deprived you of? and how much better your case would be, if you had lived in as much holy diligence as the best you know? And yet will you be slothful still?

11. Remember that you have your life, and health, and wit, and abilities, for nothing else but by your present duty to prepare for everlasting joys: that all God's mercies bind you to be diligent; and every ordinance, and all his helps and means of grace, are given to further you in the work; and sun, and moon, and air, and earth, and all, attend you with their help. And yet will you be cold and slothful, and frustrate all these means and mercies?

12. Remember how diligent your enemy is: Satan "goes about even night and day, like a roaring lion seeking to devour!" 1 Peter 5:8. And will you be less diligent to resist him?

13. Think what an example of diligence Christ himself has left you! And how laboriously blessed Paul and all the holy servants of Christ did follow their Master's work! Did they pray, and watch, and work as slothfully as you do?

14. Remember how hot and earnest you were formerly in your sin! and will you now be cold and negligent in your duty, when God has set you in a better way?

15. Observe how eager and diligent worldlings are for the world, and flesh-pleasers for their amusements and pleasures, and proud persons for their greatness and honor, and malignant persons to oppose the gospel of Christ, and their own and other men's salvation; look on them; and think what a shame it is to you to be more cold and remiss for God.

16. Observe how an awakening pang of conscience, or the sight of death when it seems to be at hand, can waken the very wicked to some kind of serious diligence for the present; so that by their confessions, and cries, and promises, and amendments, while the fit was on them, they seemed more zealous than many that were sincere. And shall not saving grace do more with you than a fit of fear can do with the ungodly?

17. Remember of how sad importance it is, and what it signifies, to be cold and slothful! If it is predominant, so as to keep you from a holy life, it is damnable. The spirit of slumber is a most dreadful judgment. But if it does not so prevail, yet, though you are a child of God, it signifies a great weakness of soul, and foretells some sharp affliction to befall you, if God means to do you good by a recovery. The decay of natural heat is a sign of old age, and is accompanied with the decay of all the powers. And sicknesses and pains do follow such decays of life. And as you will make your horse feel the rod or spur when he grows dull and heavy, expect when you grow cold and dull, to feel the spur of some affliction, to make you stir and mend your pace.

18. Remember that your sloth is a sinning against your knowledge, and against your experience, and against your own covenants, promises, and profession; and therefore an aggravated sin. These and suchlike serious thoughts will do much to stir up a slothful soul to zeal and diligence.

**Direction 4. Do not drown your hearts in worldly business or delights; [601] for these breed a loathing, and aversion, and weariness of holy things.**

They are so contrary one to the other that the mind will not be eagerly set on both at once: but as it relishes the one, it more and more dislikes the other. There is no heart left for God when other things have carried it away.

**Direction 5. Do all you can to raise your hearts to the love of God, and a delight in holy things, and then you will not be slothful, nor weary, nor negligent.**

Love and delight are the most excellent remedy against a slow, unwilling kind of duty. Know only how good it is to walk with God, and do his work, and you will do it cheerfully.

**Direction 6. A secret root of unbelief is the mortal enemy of zeal and diligence; labor for a well-grounded belief of the word of God and the world to come, and stir up that belief into exercise, when you would have your slothful hearts stirred up.**

When there is a secret questioning in the heart, What if there should be no life to come? What if the grounds of religion are unsound? This blasts the vigor of all efforts, and inclines men to serve God only with hypocritical halving and reserves; and makes men resolve to be no further religious than is consistent with present, fleshly happiness.

**Direction 7. Take heed of corrupting conscience by venturing on doubtful things, much more by known and willful sin. [602]**

For when once conscience is taught to comply with sin, and is mastered in one thing, it will do its duty well in nothing, and zeal will quickly be extinct; diligence will die when conscience is corrupted or fallen asleep.

**Direction 8. Live in a constant expectation of death.**

Do not foolishly flatter yourself with groundless notions that you shall live long. There is a great power in death to rouse up a drowsy soul, when it is taken to be near; and a great force in the notion of living long, to make even good men grow more negligent and secure.

**Direction 9. Live among warm and serious Christians; especially as to your intimate familiarity. [603]**

There is a very great power in the zeal of one to kindle zeal in others; as there is in fire to kindle fire. Serious, hearty, diligent Christians are excellent helps to make us serious and diligent. He that travels with speedy travelers will be willing to keep pace with them; and tired sluggards are drawn on by others; when he that travels with the slothful will go slowly as they do.

**Direction 10. Lastly, be often in the use of enlivening means: live, if you can attain it, under an enlivening, zealous minister.**

There is life in the word of God, which, when it is opened and applied in a lively way, will put life into the hearers. Read the holy Scriptures, and such lively writings as help you to understand and practice them. As going to the fire is our way when we are cold, to cure our numbness, so reading over some part of a warm and enlivening book will do much to warm and enliven a numbed soul: and it is not the smallest help to rouse us up to prayer or meditation, and put life into us before we address ourselves more nearly to God. I have found it myself a great help in my studies, and to my preaching: when studying my own heart would not serve the turn, to awake me to serious fervency, but all has been cold and dull that I have done, because all was cold and dull within, I have taken up a book that was much more warm and serious than I, and the reading of it has recovered my heat, and my warmed heart has been fitter for my work. Christians, take heed of a cold, and dull, and heartless kind of religion; and think no pains too much to cure it: death is cold, and life is warm; and labor itself does best excite it.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Every NET quotation below was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where we are:** this file is in Chapter 10 (Directions for the Government of the Body), Part 1 (Directions about our labor and callings), Title 3 (Directions against sloth and laziness). "Title" (*Tit.* in the source) is Baxter's word for a heading or division within a Part. Neither the Title heading nor the word "calling" (Baxter's word for one's vocation or occupation) occurs inside this range; the headings are in the earlier files. Directions 4–10 here are directions against sloth in the things of God (zeal and diligence in religion), continuing Direction 3's motives.
- **Matthew 25:11** (item 8): Baxter's "Lord, open to us" is the King James "Lord, Lord, open to us." NET: "Lord, lord! Let us in!" We give the NET words, with the second "lord" skipped ("..."), since Baxter quotes only one "Lord." The sense is unchanged. (Luke 13:25 has similar words; Baxter cites Matthew 25.)
- **Matthew 25:8** (item 8): NET wording. Baxter's King James "our lamps are gone out" becomes the NET "our lamps are going out" (going out, not yet out); his point does not depend on the difference.
- **Numbers 23:10** (item 8): Baxter adapts the King James "Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his!" into his own sentence ("if they might but have 'died the death of the righteous, and their latter end might be as his!'"), changing person and tense. Because his grammar depends on the adaptation, we keep his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET reads "Let me die the death of the upright, and let the end of my life be like theirs." Note the NET's "upright" for "righteous" and "theirs" (plural) for "his."
- **1 Peter 5:8** (item 12): Baxter's quotation, "goeth about even night and day, like a roaring lion seeking to devour," is the King James "as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour" with Baxter's own "even night and day" added. Since it is his blend, we keep his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET reads "Your enemy the devil, like a roaring lion, is on the prowl looking for someone to devour." "Night and day" is not in either version.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "the spirit of slumber" (item 17) is the King James phrase in Romans 11:8 (NET "a spirit of stupor"); the parable of the ten virgins (Matthew 25:1–13) behind item 8; "Satan ... like a roaring lion" (1 Peter 5:8, cited); "there is life in the word of God" (Direction 10; compare Hebrews 4:12, King James "the word of God is quick," i.e. living). These identifications are ours, not Baxter's.
- **Footnotes 601–603** are lists of Scripture references only and have been converted to modern form. Note 603's "Rom. xv. 14" is split across two lines in the source.
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** "thither" to "there"; "methinks" to "I think" (item 7); "but" in the sense "only" to "only" ("might but have," "will but follow," "Know but how good"); "vanities" to "empty things" (item 9); "in comparison of" to "in comparison with"; "recompense" to "repay"; "put by" to "put aside"; "parts" to "abilities" (natural gifts); "sports" to "amusements"; "honour" to "honor"; "at the present" to "for the present"; "debility" to "weakness"; "averseness" to "aversion"; "disrelisheth" to "dislikes" (loses its taste for); "endeavours" to "efforts"; "stands with" to "is consistent with"; "debauching" to "corrupting" (in Baxter's day to "debauch" meant to lead astray or corrupt, not only in sexual matters); "venturing upon" to "venturing on"; "conceits" to "notions"; "oft" to "often"; "quickening" to "enlivening" and "quicken" to "enliven" (making alive, as in the previous file); "livelily" to "in a lively way"; "benumbedness" to "numbness" and "benumbed" to "numbed"; "unto" to "to"; "such like" to "suchlike."
- **Kept words:** **leave** (item 6) in the sense "permission" (time, permission, and hope to repent); **undone** (ruined); **stupid** (item 8) in its older sense, dull and without feeling, not unintelligent; **wit** (item 11), mind or understanding; **ordinance** (item 11), the appointed means of worship such as preaching, prayer, and the sacraments; **malignant persons** (item 15), Baxter's term for people hostile to godliness and the gospel; **remiss**, slack or careless; **fit** (item 16), a passing attack, as of fear; **predominant** and **damnable** (item 17), meaning reigning so as to keep a person from holiness, and bringing damnation; **natural heat** (item 17), from the medicine of Baxter's day, which held that the body's innate heat declines with age (a dated medical idea, kept as his comparison); **aggravated sin** (item 18), a sin made heavier by its circumstances; **profession** (item 18), one's public profession of faith; **halving and reserves** (Direction 6), serving God by halves and with things held back; **secure** (Direction 8), in Baxter's sense, carelessly confident, without fear of danger (see the conventions on "security"); **familiarity** (Direction 9), close friendship; **opened** (Direction 10), explained; **serve the turn**, be enough for the purpose; **address ourselves more nearly to God**, come closer to God (in prayer); **heartless** (Direction 10), lifeless, without spirit; **excite it**, stir it up (the "it" most likely refers back to life or warmth).
- **"thou" and "you":** Baxter shifts between "thou," "you," and "your" in this range; all become "you," which loses his shift from the singular "thou" (items 9–14 and Direction 5's "thou wilt do it") to the plural "you."
- **Item 6:** the clause "one would think should waken me from my sloth" has no subject in Baxter's grammar ("to remember ... one would think should waken me"); we keep his construction. Item 7 has the same shape with "methinks should stir us up."
- **Direction 10, autobiographical passage:** Baxter speaks of his own practice of reading a "warm" book before study and preaching. We have kept it as his testimony and add nothing about which books he meant.
