# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 6 (part 2 of 3)

*This section runs from Direction 9 through the first seven of the "more special directions" for obedience, all within Grand Direction 6; Baxter's Direction 7 here is not the end of that list, and the next file continues it.*

**Direction 9. Mark what obedience is expected by men, and what influence government has upon the state and affairs of the world, and what the world would be without it.**

And sure this will make you think honorably and delightfully of the government of God. What would a nation be without government, but like a company of thieves and lawless murderers? or like the pikes in a pond, that first eat up the other fish, and then devour one another, the greater living upon the less. Bears and wolves would live more quietly together than ungoverned men (except those few that are truly subject to the government of God). Government maintains every man in his property; and keeps lust and madness from breaking out; and keeps peace and order in the world. What would a family be without government? Children and servants are kept by it in their proper place and work. Think then how necessary and excellent is the universal government of God.

**Direction 10. Think well of the endless rewards and punishments, by which God will procure obedience to his laws, or vindicate the honor of his government on the disobedient.**

That the world may see that he gives sufficient motives for all that he requires, he will reward the obedient with everlasting blessedness, and punish the rebels with endless misery. You shall not say that he bids you work for nothing. Though you can give him nothing but his own, and therefore can merit nothing of him, in point of commutative justice; yet, as he is a Governor and a Father, he will put so wide a difference between the obedient and the rebellious, that one shall be judged to everlasting joy, with a "Well done, good and faithful slave!" and the other to "eternal punishment" (Matthew 25:21, 46). Is there not enough in heaven, in a life of endless joys with God, to make obedience lovely to you, and to make sin loathsome? Is there not enough in hell to deter you from disobedience, and drive you to God? God will rule, whether you will or not. Consent to be obedient, or he will punish you without asking your consent.

\* \* \* \* \*

*In the margin: More special directions for obedience.*

The directions for the nearer stirring up of your obedience, and confirming your full subjection, are these:

**Direction 1. Keep the face of your souls always upon God, and in the sense of his greatness, and of his continual presence, and of his particular providence.**

And this will keep you in an obedient frame. You will easily then perceive that so great a God cannot be disobeyed without great iniquity and guilt. And that a God who is continually with you must be continually regarded. And that a God who exactly observes and attends to the thoughts and words of every man should by every man be exactly attended to and observed. This will help you to understand the meaning of the tempter, when you perceive that every temptation is an urging of you to offend, for nothing, so great a God, who is just then observing what you do.

**Direction 2. Always remember where you are going; that you are preparing for everlasting rest and joy, and must pass through the righteous judgment of the Lord; and that Christ is your Guide and Governor only to bring you safely home, as the Captain of your salvation; and that sin is a rejecting of his help, and of your happiness.**

Do not think that God rules you as a tyrant, to your hurt or ruin, to make his own advantage of you; or by needless laws that have no regard for your good and safety; but think of him as one who is conducting you to eternal life, and would now guide you by his counsel, and afterwards take you to his glory. Think that he is leading you to the world of light, and life, and love, and joy, where there are rivers of pleasure, and fullness of delight for evermore, that you may see his face, and feel his love, among a world of blessed spirits; and not be weeping and gnashing the teeth with ungodly, impenitent souls. And is not such a government as this desirable? It is but like the government of a physician, to save his patient's life. Or like your government of your children, which is necessary to their good, who cannot feed or rule themselves. Or like a pilot's governing the ship which is conveying you to possess a kingdom: if the sailors obey him, they may safely arrive at the desired port; but if they disobey him, they are all cast away and perish. And should such a government as this seem burdensome to you? or should it not be most welcome, and accurately obeyed?

**Direction 3. Keep thinking what dangers, difficulties, and enemies you must pass through to this rest, and that all your safety depends upon the conduct and assistance of your Guide.**

And this will bring over self-love to command your strict obedience. You are to pass through the army of your enemies; and will you here disobey the Captain of your salvation? or would you have him leave you to yourselves? Your disease is mortal, and none but Jesus Christ can cure it; and if he does not cure it, you are lost forever. No pain of gout or kidney stone is comparable to your everlasting pain; and yet will you not be obedient to your Physician? Think, when a temptation comes, If there were a narrow bridge over the deepest gulf or river, and all my friends and happiness lay on the further side, and I must needs go over whether I will or not; if Christ would take me by the hand and lead me over, would I be tempted to refuse his help, or to let go of his hand? or if he should offer to let go of me, and leave me to myself, should I not tremble, and cry out, as Peter, "Lord, save me!" (Matthew 14:30), or as the disciples, "Save, Master, we perish?" And should I not then hold him fast, and most accurately obey him, when he is leading me to life eternal, that I may escape the gulf of endless misery?

**Direction 4. Remember always how bad, and blind, and backward, and deceitful, and weak you are yourselves, and therefore what need you have of the greatest watchfulness, lest you should disobey your Pilot, and lose your Guide, before you are aware.**

O what a heart we have to watch! A lazy heart, that will be loitering or sitting down when we should be following our Lord. A foolish heart, that will let him go while we play with every playmate in our way. A cowardly heart, that will steal away, or draw back in danger, when it should follow our General. A treacherous heart, that will give us the slip, and deceive us, when we seemed surest of it. A half-blind heart, that even when it follows Christ, our Guide, is hardly kept from missing the bridge, and falling into the gulf of misery. Think well of these, and you will obey your Governor.

**Direction 5. Do not forget the fruits of your former obedience and disobedience, if you would be kept in an obedient frame.**

Remember that obedience has been sweetest afterward; and that you never yet found cause to repent or be ashamed of it. Remember that the fruit of sin was bitter, and that when your eyes were opened, and you saw your shame, you would gladly have fled from the face of God; and that then it appeared another thing to you than it seemed in the committing. Remember what groans and heartache it has cost you; and into what fears it brought you of the wrath of God; and how long it was before your broken bones were healed; and what it cost both Christ and you. And this will make the very name and first approach of sin throw you into a forestalling fear. A beast that has once fallen into a gulf or quicksand will hardly be driven into the same again: a fish that was once struck and escaped the hook will fear and fly from it the next time: a bird that has once escaped the snare, or the talons of the hawk, is afterwards afraid of the sight or noise of such a thing. Remember where you fell, and what it cost you, and what you escaped which it might have cost you, and you will obey more accurately hereafter.

**Direction 6. Remember that this is your day of trial, and what depends upon your accurate obedience.**

God will not crown untried servants. Satan is purposely allowed to tempt you, to try whether you will be true to God or not. All the hope that his malice has of undoing you forever consists in his hope to make you disobedient to God. I think these considerations should awaken you to the most watchful and diligent obedience. If you were told beforehand that a thief or pickpocket had undertaken to rob you, and would use all his cunning and industry to do it, you would then watch more carefully than at another time. If you were in a race to run for your lives, you would not go then at your ordinary pace. Does God tell you before that he will try your obedience by temptation, and that as you stand or fall, you shall fare for ever; and will not this keep you watchful and obedient?

**Direction 7. Avoid those tempting and deluding objects which are always enticing your hearts from your obedience; and avoid that diverting crowd and noise of company or worldly business, which drowns the voice of God's commands.**

If God calls you into a life of great temptations, he can bring you safely through them all; but if you rush into it willfully, you may soon find your own inability to resist. It is dangerous to be under strong and relentless temptations, lest the stream should bear us down; but especially to be long under them, lest we be weary of resisting. Those who are long urged to sin too often yield at last: it is hard to be always in a clear, and ready, and resolute frame: few men have their wits, much less their graces, always at hand, in a readiness to use. And if the thief comes when you are dropped asleep, you may be robbed before you can awake. The constant drawings of temptation often abate the habit of obedience, and diminish our hatred of sin and holy resolutions, by slow, imperceptible degrees, before we yield to commit the act. And the mind that will be kept in full subjection must not be so diverted in a crowd of distracting company or business, as to have no time to think on the motives of his obedience. This withdrawing of the fuel may put out the fire.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording below was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where this range sits:** Direction 9 and Direction 10 are the last two items of one numbered list of Directions. After the row of asterisks Baxter begins a new list ("more special directions for obedience") and restarts the numbering at 1. The range ends at Direction 7 of that second list; the rest of it is in the next file.
- **Matthew 25:21, 46:** Baxter cites only "Matt. xxv." and quotes "Well done, good and faithful servant" and "everlasting punishment." NET reads "Well done, good and faithful slave!" (verse 21) and "eternal punishment" (verse 46). We use the NET words and the verse numbers; the verse numbers are ours, not Baxter's. The change from "servant" to "slave" and "everlasting" to "eternal" does not alter his point. His "everlasting punishment" is the King James wording.
- **Matthew 14:30:** NET "Lord, save me!" matches Baxter's quotation.
- **"Save, Master, we perish":** Baxter gives no reference. It is probably his blend of the disciples' cry in the storm (Luke 8:24 in the NET: "Master, Master, we are about to die!"; Matthew 8:25: "Lord, save us! We are about to die!"). We keep his wording, modernized, as his paraphrase.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "Captain of your salvation" (compare Hebrews 2:10, where the NET reads "pioneer of their salvation"; the sense of a leader who goes ahead is close, but the NET word differs from the King James "captain" that Baxter uses); "weeping and gnashing the teeth" (a phrase from Jesus' sayings in Matthew, for example Matthew 8:12); "rivers of pleasure" (compare Psalm 36:8; the NET reads "the river of your delicacies"); "your broken bones" (compare Psalm 51:8; NET "the bones you crushed"). These are our suggestions and are not given by Baxter.
- **commutative justice:** kept as Baxter's term, meaning the justice of an exchange between equals, where one party owes the other an equal return. His point is that creature owes God everything already, so no work of ours earns a debt from him in that sense.
- **property** (Direction 9): Baxter's "propriety," meaning a man's own possessions or what rightly belongs to him.
- **obedient frame:** Baxter's "obediential frame." **accurately / accurate obedience:** kept; Baxter means exact, careful obedience. **burdensome:** his "grievous." **welcome:** his "acceptable."
- **forestalling fear:** Baxter's "preventing fear," in the older sense of a fear that goes before and guards against sin (see GLOSSARY, "prevent").
- **hook** (Direction 5): Baxter's "nook." The word is uncertain; it probably means a fishing hook, though we have not confirmed that. If it meant a net or noose, the sense of the sentence would be much the same.
- **half-blind:** his "purblind." **pickpocket:** his "cut-purse." **kidney stone:** his "stone." **sailors:** his "mariners." **relentless:** his "importunate." **urged to sin:** his "solicited." **imperceptible:** his "insensible." **gladly:** his "fain." **wits:** left as written.
- **fare:** "you shall speed for ever" (Direction 6) means you shall fare, for good or ill, forever; "speed" was a general word for how things turn out.
- **impious** is rendered **ungodly**; **the tempter** is Satan, as Baxter makes plain in Direction 6.
- **More word swaps:** nearer exciting to "nearer stirring up"; "keep still" to "keep ... always" and "still think / remember still" to "keep thinking / remember always" (Baxter's "still" means always); "minded" to "attended to"; "conduct" (Direction 3 heading) kept in Baxter's sense of guidance; "disability" to "inability"; "heart's grief" to "heartache"; "stricken" to "struck"; "play-fellow" to "playmate"; "Methinks" to "I think"; "ofttimes" to "often"; "wilfully" to "willfully"; "fulness" to "fullness"; "but to bring" (Direction 2) to "only to bring."
- **let go of** (Direction 3): Baxter's "lose his hand" and "offer to lose me."
- **diverting** (Direction 7): kept; Baxter means turning aside from one's purpose, not amusing. We have not confirmed that he does not also intend "entertaining."
- **Punctuation:** commas dropped only between subject and verb and before completing clauses, as in CONVENTIONS.md. The row of asterisks in the source is kept as a break.
