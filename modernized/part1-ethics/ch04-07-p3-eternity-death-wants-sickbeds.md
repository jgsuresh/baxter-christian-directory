# Chapter 4: Directions 2-5 against greed (consider eternity, death, your real wants, the dying)

*This range begins at Direction 2 of Baxter's directions against covetousness (greed); the section heading and Direction 1 are in the preceding file.*

**Direction 2. Seriously consider your everlasting state, and how much greater things than riches you have to mind.** Behold by faith the endless joys which you may have with God, and the endless misery which worldlings must undergo in hell. There is no true cure for an earthly mind, but by showing it the far greater matters to be minded: by acquainting it better with its own concerns; and with the greater miseries than poverty or want, which we have to escape; and the greater good than worldly plenty, which we have to seek. It is want of faith that makes men worldlings: they do not see what is in another world: they say their creed, but do not heartily believe the day of judgment, the resurrection of the body, and the life everlasting. There is not a man of them all, but, if he had one sight of heaven and hell, would think less of the world than ever he did before; and would turn his greedy care and toil to a speedy and diligent care of his salvation. If he heard the joyful praises of the saints, and the woeful lamentations of the damned, but one day or hour, he would think ever after that he had greater matters to mind than the scraping together a heap of wealth. Remember, man, that you have another world to live in; and a far longer life to make provision for; and that you must be in heaven or hell forever. This is true, whether you believe it or not: and you have no time but this to make all your preparation in: and as you believe, and live, and labor now, it must go with you to all eternity. These are matters worthy of your care. Can you have time to make such a commotion here in the dust, and care and labor for a thing of nothing, while you have such things as these to care for, and a work of such transcendent consequence to do? [253] Can a man that understands what heaven and hell are find room for any needless matters, or time for so much unnecessary work? The providing for your salvation is a thing that God has made your own work, much more than the providing for the flesh. When he speaks of your body, he says, "Do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink, or about your body, what you will wear... your heavenly Father knows that you need them" (Matthew 6:25, 32). "Do not be anxious about anything" (Philippians 4:6). "Casting all your cares on him because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7). But when he speaks of your salvation, he bids you "continue working out your salvation with awe and reverence" (Philippians 2:12); [254] and "make every effort to be sure of your calling and election" (2 Peter 1:10); and "Exert every effort to enter through the narrow door" (Matthew 7:13; Luke 13:24). "Do not work for the food that disappears, but for the food that remains to eternal life" (John 6:27). That is, "Above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well" (Matthew 6:33). Look up to heaven, man, and remember that there is your home, and there are your hopes, or else you are a man undone forever; and therefore it is for that that you must care and labor. Believe sincerely that you must dwell forever in heaven or hell, as you make your preparation here, and consider this as befits a man, and then be a worldling and greedy if you can: riches will seem dust and chaff to you, if you believe and consider your everlasting state. Write upon the doors of your shop and bedroom, I must be in heaven or hell forever; or, This is the time on which my endless life depends; and it seems to me every time you read it, you should feel your greed stabbed at the heart. O blinded mortals! that love, like worms, to dwell in earth! Would God but give you an eye of faith, to foresee your end, and where you must dwell to all eternity, what a change would it make upon your earthly minds! Either faith or sense will be your guides. Nothing but reason sanctified by faith can govern sense. Remember that you are not a beast, that has no life to live but this: you have a reasonable, immortal soul, that was made by God for higher things, even for God himself, to admire him, love him, serve him, and enjoy him. If an angel were to dwell awhile in flesh, should he turn an earthworm, and forget his higher life of glory? You are like an incarnate angel; and may be equal with the angels, when you are freed from this sinful flesh, Luke 20:36. O beg of God a heavenly light, and a heavenly mind, and look often into the word of God, which tells you where you must be forever; and worldliness will vanish away in shame.

**Direction 3. Remember how short a time you must keep and enjoy the wealth which you have gotten.** How quickly you must be stripped of all! Can you keep it when you have it? [255] Can you make a covenant with death, that it shall not call away your soul? You know beforehand that you are of short continuance, and the world is but your inn or passage; and that a narrow grave for your flesh to rot in is all that you can keep of your largest possessions, except what you lay up in heaven, by laying it out in obedience to God. How short is life! How quickly gone! You are almost dead and gone already! What are a few days or a few years more? And will you make so much of a fuss for so short a life? and so careful a provision for so short a stay? Indeed, how uncertain is your time, as well as short! You cannot say what world you shall be in tomorrow. Remember, man, that You must die! You must die! You must quickly die! You do not know how soon! Breathe yet a few breaths more, and you are gone! And yet can you be greedy, and drown your soul with earthly cares? Do you soberly read your Savior's warning, Luke 12:19-21? Is it not spoken as to you? "You fool! This very night your life will be demanded back from you, but who will get what you have prepared for yourself? So it is with the one who stores up riches for himself, but is not rich toward God." [256] If you are rich today, and are in another world tomorrow, had not poverty been as good? Distracted soul! do you make so great a matter of it, whether you have much or little for so short a time? and take no more care, either where you shall be, or what you shall have to all eternity? Do you say you will cast this care on God? I tell you, he will make you care yourself, and care again before he will save you. And why can you not cast the care of smaller matters on him, when he commands you? Is it any great matter whether you are rich or poor, that are going so fast into another world, where these are things of no significance? Tell me, if you were sure that you must die tomorrow, (yes, or the next month or year,) would you not be more indifferent whether you are rich or poor, and look more after greater things? Then you would be of the apostle's mind, 2 Corinthians 4:18, "We are not looking at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen. For what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal." Our eye of faith should be so fixed on invisible, eternal things, that we should scarcely have leisure or mind to look at or once regard the things that are visible and temporal. A man that is going to execution scarcely looks at all the bustle or business that is done in streets and shops as he passes by; because these little concern him in his departing situation. And how little do the wealth and honors of the world concern a soul that is going into another world, and does not know but it may be this night! Then keep your wealth, or take it with you, if you can.

**Direction 4. Labor to feel your greatest wants, which worldly wealth will not supply.** You have sinned against God, and money will not buy your pardon. [257] You have incurred his displeasure, and money will not reconcile him to you. You are condemned to everlasting misery by the law, and money will not pay your ransom. You are dead in sin, and polluted, and captivated by the flesh, and money will sooner increase your bondage than deliver you. Your conscience is ready to tear your heart for your willful folly and contempt of grace, and money will not bribe it to be quiet. Judas brought back his money, and hanged himself, when conscience was but once awakened. Money will not enlighten a blinded mind, nor soften a hard heart, nor humble a proud heart, nor justify a guilty soul. It will not keep off a fever or consumption, nor ease the gout, or stone, or toothache. It will not keep off ghastly death, but die you must, if you have all the world! Look up to God, and remember that you are wholly in his hands; and think whether he will love or favor you for your wealth. Look to the day of judgment, and think whether money will there get you off, or the rich fare better than the poor.

**Direction 5. Be often with those that are sick and dying, and mark what all their riches will do for them, and what esteem they have then of the world; and mark how it uses all at last.** Then you shall see that it forsakes all men in the hour of their greatest necessity and distress; [258] when they would cry to friends, and wealth, and honor, if they had any hopes, If ever you will help me, let it be now; if ever you will do anything for me, O save me from death, and the wrath of God! But, alas! such cries would be all in vain! Then, oh then! one drop of mercy, one spark of grace, the smallest well-grounded hope of heaven, would be worth more than the empire of Caesar or Alexander! Is not this true, sinner? Do you not know it to be true? And yet will you cheat and betray your soul? Is not that best now, which will be best then? And is not that of little value now, which will be then so little valued? Do you not think that men are wiser then than now? Will you do so much, and pay so dearly for that, which will do you no more good, and which you will value no more when you have it? Does not all the world cry out at last of the deceitfulness of riches, and the emptiness of pleasure and prosperity on earth, and the perniciousness of all worldly cares? And does not your conscience tell you, that when you come to die, you are likely to have the same thoughts yourself? And yet will you not be warned in time? Then all the content and pleasure of your plenty and prosperity will be past: and when it is past it is nothing. And will you venture on everlasting woe, and cast away everlasting joy, for that which is today a dream and shadow, and tomorrow, or very shortly, will be nothing? [259] The poorest then will be equal with you. And will honest poverty, or over-loved wealth, be sweeter at the last? How glad then would you be, to have been without your wealth, if you might have been without the sin and guilt. How glad then would you be to die the death of the poorest saint! Do you think that poverty, or riches, are likelier to make a man unwilling to die? or are usually more troublesome to the conscience of a dying man? O look to the end, and live as you die, and value most that, and seek that now, which you know you shall value most at last when full experience has made you wiser!

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[253] Nullius rei eget qui virtutum dives est: quarum indigentia vere miseros, ac proinde misericordiæ egentissimos facit. Petrarch. Dial. 44. li. 2. (English: He lacks nothing who is rich in virtues; lack of which truly makes people wretched, and therefore most in need of mercy. Petrarch, Dialogue 44, book 2.)

[254] Diis maxime propinquus qui minimis egeat. Socrat. in Laert. (English: He is nearest to the gods who needs the least. Socrates, in Laertius.)

[255] 1 Corinthians 7:31.

[256] Remember Gehazi, Achan, Judas, Ananias and Sapphira, Demetrius, Demas. Jeremiah 6:13; 8:10. Maxime vituperanda est avaritia senilis. Quid enim absurdius quam quo minus vitæ restat, eo plus viatici quærere? Cicero in Cat. Maj. (English: Avarice in old age is most to be condemned. For what is more absurd than, the less of life remains, to seek the more provision for the journey? Cicero, in *Cato Major*.)

[257] Proverbs 11:4, "Wealth does not profit in the day of wrath."

[258] Jeremiah 17:11; James 5:1-3.

[259] Chilon in Laert. p. 43. Damnum potius quam turpe lucrum eligendum; nam id semel tantum dolori esse, hoc semper. (English: Loss is to be chosen rather than shameful gain; for the one is a pain only once, the other always. Chilon, in Laertius, p. 43.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Every quoted verse was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Matthew 6:25, 32 (blend):** Baxter joins verse 25 and verse 32 with "--" between them. We quote the NET for each, joined by an ellipsis. Baxter's "Take no thought" (King James) is the NET's "do not worry"; "thought" there meant anxious care. His "for your Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things" becomes "your heavenly Father knows that you need them" (the NET has "heavenly," and "them" for "all these things"). Baxter sets this against the verses on working out salvation, so the contrast is between anxious care for the body and effort for salvation.
- **Philippians 4:6 (flag):** Baxter's "Be careful for nothing" uses "careful" in the old sense of "full of care, anxious," which is why he then sets "Cast all your care" next to it and plays on "care" throughout the direction. The NET's "Do not be anxious about anything" gives the sense, but the repeated word "care" in Baxter's chain (care, careth) is partly lost.
- **1 Peter 5:7 (flag):** The NET puts this as a participle phrase ("by casting all your cares on him because he cares for you"), continuing the sentence from verse 6, not as a command. We quote only the phrase, dropping "by" and capitalizing its first word. Baxter's "care ... careth" echo survives in "cares ... cares."
- **Philippians 2:12 (flag):** Baxter's "work it out with fear and trembling" is the King James. The NET has "continue working out your salvation with awe and reverence." Baxter leans on "fear" as a motive for diligence, and the NET's "awe and reverence" softens that. The NET also adds "continue."
- **2 Peter 1:10:** Baxter's "give diligence to make your calling and election sure" is the King James; the NET has "make every effort to be sure of your calling and election." The sense of diligent effort is kept.
- **Matthew 7:13; Luke 13:24 (flag):** Baxter's "strive to enter in at the strait gate" blends the two verses. The NET of Luke 13:24 reads "Exert every effort to enter through the narrow door," and Matthew 7:13 has "the narrow gate." We quote Luke's phrase and cite both. "Strait" (narrow) is rendered "narrow" by the NET.
- **John 6:27:** NET wording. Baxter's "meat" is the NET's "food," and "endureth to everlasting life" is "remains to eternal life."
- **Matthew 6:33 (flag):** Baxter's "Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness" is the King James. The NET reads "Above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness," so "Seek first" becomes "Above all pursue" and "the kingdom of God" is "his kingdom" (the antecedent is "your heavenly Father" in verse 32). Baxter's "That is," introduces this as the sum of John 6:27, as he wrote it. Baxter also gives "all these things shall be added to you"; the NET has "will be given to you as well." We capitalize "Above" to begin the quotation.
- **Luke 12:19-21 (flag):** Baxter cites verses 19-21 but quotes only the words of verses 20 and 21. Baxter's "this night thy soul shall be required of thee" is the King James. The NET has "your life will be demanded back from you," so "soul" (a word Baxter leans on elsewhere in the paragraph, "drown thy soul") becomes "life." Baxter's "then whose shall those things be which thou hast provided?" is the NET's "but who will get what you have prepared for yourself?" and his "So is every one that layeth up riches for himself" is "So it is with the one who stores up riches for himself."
- **2 Corinthians 4:18:** NET wording. The NET sentence begins "because we are not looking"; we capitalize "We" to begin Baxter's quotation. Baxter's "We look not at the things which are seen" becomes "We are not looking at what can be seen"; "temporal" is "temporary."
- **Luke 20:36:** cited only, in Baxter's sentence; his "equal with the angels" is not put in quotation marks. The NET has "equal to angels."
- **Proverbs 11:4** (note 257): the footnote's English is Baxter's King James, "Riches profit not in the day of wrath." We give the NET wording, "Wealth does not profit in the day of wrath."
- **Jeremiah 17:11; James 5:1–3; 1 Corinthians 7:31; Jeremiah 6:13; 8:10** (notes 255, 256, 258): cited only. Baxter's reference for 1 Corinthians 7:31 stands alone in note 255; we have not checked the verse against his intended point beyond the reference.
- **Baxter's names in note 256:** Gehazi, Achan, Judas, Ananias and Sapphira, Demetrius, Demas are listed without references. We have added none.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "covenant with death" (compare Isaiah 28:15, 18); "Judas brought back his money, and hanged himself" (compare Matthew 27:3–5); "inn or passage" (a traveler's lodging); "If an angel ... earthworm" (Baxter's image); "The poorest then will be equal with you" (his own point); "this is the time on which my endless life depends" (Baxter's words to be written up).
- **worldlings:** kept as Baxter's word for people whose hearts are set on this world. **covetous / covetousness:** "greedy / greed," as decided in CONVENTIONS.md.
- **consider of:** Baxter's "consider of your everlasting state" is "consider your everlasting state." **pudder** (Baxter's spelling of "pother"): "commotion." **thing of nought:** "thing of nothing." **while** (Can you have while to...): "time." **ado:** "so much of a fuss." **chamber:** "bedroom." **departing case:** "departing situation." **signification:** "significance." **loth:** "unwilling." **speed better:** "fare better." **bring thee off:** "get you off" (acquit you). **set no more by / set so little by / set most by:** "value no more / so little valued / value most." **set lighter by:** "think less of." **methinks:** "it seems to me." **gotten:** left as is (American usage). **scarce:** "scarcely." **pay so dear:** "pay so dearly." **vanity** (of pleasure): "emptiness." **concernments:** "concerns." **favour:** "favor." **Saviour:** "Savior." **Cæsar:** "Caesar." **unfeignedly:** "sincerely." **becometh:** "befits."
- **Other swaps:** **yea** ("Yea, how uncertain"): "Indeed." **wants** (Direction 4, "greatest wants"): kept, in Baxter's sense of "needs." **content** ("all the content and pleasure"): kept, in the old sense of "contentment." **sense** ("faith or sense"): kept, Baxter's word for the bodily appetites and perceptions. **stone** and **consumption** (Direction 4): kept; the stone is a kidney or bladder stone, and consumption is wasting disease.
- **so thou mightst** (Direction 5): "if you might." "So" there means "provided that," and "if" gives that sense with the same word order.
- **Direction 5:** Baxter moves between "you" and "thou" in this Direction; we use "you" throughout.
- **Cicero, Petrarch, Chilon, Socrates, Laertius** (notes 253, 254, 256, 259): the "Laert." in notes 254 and 259 is probably Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of the Philosophers*; "Cat. Maj." is probably Cicero's *Cato Maior* (On Old Age); the Petrarch reference is probably to his *Dialogues* (note 253). We have not confirmed these. Latin is as printed; "Cat. Maj." is Baxter's abbreviation.
- **Note 259, Latin:** rendered loosely ("id ... hoc" taken as "the one ... the other").
