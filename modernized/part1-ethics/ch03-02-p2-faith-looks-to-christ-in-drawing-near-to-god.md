# Chapter 3: Grand Direction II (part 2 of 2)

*This section begins partway through Grand Direction II, in the middle of Baxter's numbered list of the ways faith looks to Christ in drawing near to God (items 7 to 20). The Direction's heading and items 1 to 6 are in the previous file.*

7. Sometimes the soul that would draw near to God is overwhelmed with grief and terror, so that the sense of sin, and danger, and misery even distracts men, and throws them into an agony; so that they say with David, "... I refused to be comforted. I said, 'I will remember God while I groan; I will think about him while my strength leaves me.' (Selah) You held my eyelids open; I was troubled and could not speak" (Psalm 77:2–4). Yes, they think they feel God thrust them from him, and tell them that he has utterly forsaken them. In this case, faith must look to Christ, and remember that he was in an agony when he prayed, and in greater agony than you ever were, so that he sweat even drops of blood; and yet in that agony he prayed more earnestly (Luke 22:44). He himself once cried out upon the cross, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" and yet he was the beloved of the Father, and is now at his right hand in glory: and all this he did that we might not be forsaken. He has removed the enmity: he has reconciled us to God. By grief he passed himself to joy, and he will wipe away his servants' tears, and cause their griefs to end in joy.

8. Sometimes the soul that would draw near to God is harassed with a storm of hideous temptations, and even confounded with a swarm of disordered, perplexed thoughts. Satan assaults it with temptations to despair, temptations to horrid blasphemous thoughts, temptations to entangle, interrupt, corrupt, or pervert the duty which they are about; so that the soul is discouraged, overwhelmed, and broken with the inward assaults, and troubles, and distractions which it undergoes. In this case faith has a Savior suitable to our relief. It can look to him who was tempted in all points just as we are, without sin, and is now such a High Priest as can be touched with the feeling of our weaknesses; and therefore we may come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need (Hebrews 4:14–16). "He had to be made like his brothers and sisters in every respect, so that he could become a merciful and faithful high priest in things relating to God, to make atonement for the sins of the people. For since he himself suffered when he was tempted, he is able to help those who are tempted" (Hebrews 2:17–18). He submitted not only to be tempted by Satan, but tempted in a wilderness, where he had no man to comfort him; and to be tempted to the most horrid blasphemy and wickedness, even to fall down and worship the devil himself: and he allowed the tempter violently to carry him to the pinnacle of the temple (Matthew 4). What should we think of ourselves, if we had been treated thus? Should we not think that God had utterly forsaken us? He allowed himself to be tempted also by men; by the abuses and reproaches of his enemies; by the desertion of his followers; by the carnal counsel of Peter, persuading him to put aside the death which he was to undergo. And he who made all temptations serve to the triumph of his patience, and conquering power, will give the victory also to his grace, in the weakest soul.

9. It would be the greatest attraction to us to draw near to God, and make the thoughts of him pleasant to us, if we could but believe that he dearly loves us, that he is reconciled to us, and takes us for his children, and that he takes pleasure in us, and that he resolves forever to glorify us with his Son; and that the dearest friend that we have in the world does not love us the thousandth part so much as he. And all this in Christ is clearly represented to the eye of faith. All this is procured for believers by him; and all this is given to believers in him: in him God is reconciled to us: he is our Father, and dwells among us, and in us, and walks in us, and is our God (2 Corinthians 6:16–18). Light and heat are not more abundant in the sun than love is in Jesus Christ. To look on Christ, and not perceive the love of God, is as to look on the sun, and not to see and acknowledge its light. Therefore whenever you find your hearts averse to God, and to have no pleasure in him, look then to Jesus, and observe in him the immeasurable love of God: "you will be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and thus to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, so that you will be filled up to all the fullness of God" (Ephesians 3:18–19). Love and goodness are that to the will which delicious sweetness is to the sensory appetite. Draw near then and taste the feast of love which God has prepared and proposed by his Son. Do you not see or feel the love of God? Come near, and look upon God incarnate; upon a crucified Christ; upon the covenant sealed in his blood; upon all the benefits of his redemption; upon all the privileges of the saints; and upon the glory purchased, possessed, and promised by him: put your hand into his wounded side, and be not faithless, but believing; and then you will cry out, "My Lord and my God!"

10. So also, when the soul would gladly perceive in itself the flames of love to God, it is the beholding of Christ by faith that is the striking of fire, and the effectual means of kindling love. And this is the true approach to God, and the true communion and converse with him: so far as we love him, so far we draw near him, and so far we have true communion with him. Oh what would the soul of a believer give, that it could but burn in love to God, as often as in prayer, or meditation, or conversation with others, his name and attributes are mentioned or remembered! For this, there is no such powerful means as believingly to look on Christ, in whom such glorious love appears, as will draw forth the love of all who by a living faith discern it. Behold the love that God has manifested by his Son, and you cannot but love him who is the spring of this transcendent love. In the law God shows his frowning wrath; and therefore it produces the "spirit of slavery leading again to fear": but in Christ God appears to us not only as loving us, but as love itself; and therefore as most lovely to us, giving us the spirit of adoption, or of filial love, by which we flee and cry to him as our Father.

11. The actual unreadiness and inability of the soul for prayer, meditation, and all holy converse with the blessed God is the great hindrance to our walking with him; and against this our relief is all in Christ. He is filled with the Spirit, to share with his members: he can revive us when we are dull: he can give us faith when we are unbelieving: he can give us boldness when we are discouraged: he can pour out upon us the Spirit of supplication, who will help our weaknesses, when we do not know what to pray for as we ought. Beg of him, then, the spirit of prayer: and look to his example, who prayed with strong cries and tears, and continued all the night in prayer, and spoke a parable to this end, that we should always pray, and not grow faint (Luke 18:1). Call to him, and he who is with the Father will reach the hand of his Spirit to you, and will revive your desires, and lift you up.

12. Sometimes the soul is listening to temptations of unbelief, and doubting whether God observes our prayers, or whether there is so much to be got by prayer as we are told. In such a case faith must look to Christ, who has not only commanded it, and encouraged us by his example; but also made us such plentiful promises of acceptance with God, and the grant of our desires. Recourse to these promises will animate us to draw near to God.

13. Sometimes the present sense of our vileness, who are but dust and despicable worms, discourages us, and weakens our expectations from God. Against this, what a wonderful relief is it to the soul, to think of our union with Christ, and of the dignity and glory of our Head! Can God despise the members of his Son? Can he trample upon those who are as his flesh and bone? Will he cut off, or forsake, or cast away the weakest parts of his body?

14. Sometimes the guilt of renewed weaknesses or declines renews distrust, and makes us shrink; and we are like the child in the mother's arms, who fears when he loses his hold, as if his safety were more in his hold of her than in her hold of him. Weak duties have weak expectations of success. In this case, what an excellent remedy has faith, in looking to the perpetual intercession of Christ. Is he praying for us in the heavens, and shall we not be bold to pray, and expect an answer? O remember that he is not weak when we are weak; and that it concerns us that he prays for us: and that we have now an unchangeable Priest, who is able to save them to the uttermost, or to perpetuity, "who come (sincerely) to God through him, because he always lives to intercede for them" (Hebrews 7:24–25). If you heard Christ pray for you, would it not encourage you to pray, and persuade you that God would not reject you? Undoubtedly it would.

15. Sometimes weak Christians, who do not have gifts of memory or utterance, are apt to think that ministers indeed, and able men, are accepted by God, but that he little values such as them. It is here a great encouragement to the soul to think that Jesus, our great High Priest, makes all his children priests to God. They are "a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of his own, so that you may proclaim the virtues of the one who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light: a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices that are acceptable to God through Jesus Christ" (1 Peter 2:5, 9). Even their "broken hearts and contrite spirits are a sacrifice which God will not despise" (Psalm 51:17). He knows the meaning of the Spirit's groans (Romans 8:26–27).

16. The strength of the corruptions that trouble the soul, and are too often struggling with it, and too much prevail, greatly discourages us in our approach to that God who hates all the workers of iniquity. And here faith may find relief in Christ, not only as he pardons us, but as he has conquered the devil and the world himself, and told us to be of good cheer, because he has conquered, and has all power given him in heaven and earth, and can give us victorious grace, in the season and measure which he sees most fitting for us. We can do all things through Christ who strengthens us. Go to him then by faith and prayer, and you will find that his grace is sufficient for you.

17. The thoughts of God are the less delightful to the soul, because death and the grave interpose, and we must pass through them before we can enjoy him: and it is unpleasing to nature to think of a separation of soul and body, and to think that our flesh must rot in darkness. But against this, faith has wonderful relief in Jesus Christ. "Since the children share in flesh and blood, he likewise shared in their humanity, so that through death he could destroy the one who holds the power of death (that is, the devil), and set free those who were held in slavery all their lives by their fear of death" (Hebrews 2:14–15). Oh what an encouragement it is to faith to observe that Christ once died himself, and that he rose from the dead, and reigns with the Father: it being impossible that death should hold him. And having conquered that which seemed to conquer him, it no more has dominion over him, but he has the keys of death and hell. We may now entertain death as a disarmed enemy, and say, "Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting?" Yes, it is sanctified by him to be our friend, even an entrance into our Master's joy: it being best for us to depart and be with Christ (Philippians 1:23). And, therefore, death has become our gain (verse 21). Oh what abundance of strength and sweetness may faith perceive from that promise of Christ, "If anyone wants to serve me, he must follow me, and where I am, my servant will be too" (John 12:26). As he was dead, but now lives forevermore, so he has promised that "because he lives, therefore we shall live also" (John 14:19). But of this, I have written two treatises of death already.

18. The terror of the day of judgment, and of our particular doom at death, makes the thoughts of God less pleasing and delightful to us. And here, what a relief is it for faith to grasp that Jesus Christ must be our Judge! And will he condemn the members of his body? Shall we be afraid to be judged by our dearest Friend?--by him who has justified us himself already, even at the price of his own blood?

19. The very strangeness of the soul to the world unseen, and to the inhabitants and employments there, greatly checks the soul in its desires, and in its delightful approaches unto God. Had we seen the world where God must be enjoyed, the thoughts of it would be more familiar and sweet. But faith can look to Christ, and say, My Head is there: he sees it for me; he knows what he possesses, prepares, and promises to me; and I will quietly rest in his acquaintance with it.

20. Nay, the Godhead itself is so infinitely above us that, in itself, it is inaccessible; and it is ready to amaze and overwhelm us to think of coming to the incomprehensible Majesty: but it emboldens the soul to think of our glorified nature in Christ, and that, even in heaven, God will everlastingly condescend to us in the Mediator. For the mediation of redemption and acquisition shall be ended, (and thus he shall deliver up the kingdom to the Father,) yet it seems that a mediation of fruition shall continue: for Christ said to his Father, "I want those you have given me to be with me where I am, so that they can see my glory" (John 17:24). We shall "rejoice," when "the wedding celebration of the Lamb has come" (Revelation 19:7). "Blessed are those who are invited to the banquet at the wedding celebration of the Lamb!" (verse 9). "The Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple and the light of the new Jerusalem" (Revelation 21:22, 23). Heaven would not be so familiar, or so sweet to my thoughts, if it were not that our glorified Lord is there, in whose love and glory we must live forever.

O Christian, as ever you would walk with God in comfortable communion with him, study and exercise this life of faith, in the daily use and improvement of Christ, who is our life, and hope, and all.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording below was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Starting point.** The range opens at item 7 of a numbered list inside Grand Direction II. We have not invented a heading for it; the Direction's own heading is in the previous file.
- **Psalm 77:2-4 (item 7): the NET changes the sense.** Baxter's King James-style quotation has "I remembered God and was troubled; I complained, and my spirit was overwhelmed," so that remembering God is itself part of the distress, which suits his point about a soul terrified as it draws near to God. In the NET, verse 3 is a resolve ("I said, 'I will remember God while I groan; I will think about him while my strength leaves me'"), with no "troubled" or "complained" there; the "troubled and could not speak" comes in verse 4 ("You held my eyelids open"). Baxter's "my spirit was overwhelmed" has no direct counterpart in the NET. We keep Baxter's extent (verses 2 to 4, skipping the start of verse 2, shown by "..."). The "(Selah)" is the NET's; Baxter's quotation has no counterpart to it. Baxter's "mine eyes waking" is the NET's "held my eyelids open."
- **Luke 22:44** (item 7): Baxter gives only the reference. The fetched NET text of this verse comes back with a closing bracket, which by this project's convention marks a passage the NET brackets as a textual variant (we have not checked the NET's own note on it). Baxter treats it as fully part of the account.
- **Matthew 27:46** (item 7): Baxter quotes the words from the cross with no reference. We use the NET wording of Matthew 27:46 (not in Baxter's text; the same words are also in Mark 15:34, which we did not fetch).
- **Hebrews 4:14-16** (item 8): Baxter's wording (tempted "in all points like as we are, without sin"; "touched with the feeling of our infirmities"; "come boldly to the throne of grace ... find grace to help in time of need") is the King James wording, not in quotation marks; we keep his phrases, modernized in spelling and pronouns only. The NET has "has been tempted in every way just as we are, yet without sin," "incapable of sympathizing with our weaknesses," and "let us confidently approach the throne of grace to receive mercy and find grace whenever we need help." The sense is the same; "infirmities" became "weaknesses" and "boldly" stays.
- **Hebrews 2:17-18** (item 8): NET wording. Baxter's "it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren" is the NET's "he had to be made like his brothers and sisters"; his "make reconciliation" is the NET's "make atonement"; his "succour" is "help." The sense Baxter uses (Christ's own temptation fits him to help the tempted) is unchanged.
- **Matthew 4** (item 8): the temptation in the wilderness; the pinnacle of the temple is in the same chapter. **carnal counsel of Peter** (item 8): an unmarked allusion to Peter's rebuke of Jesus, Matthew 16:22-23 (reference not in Baxter's text). **carnal** kept as Baxter's word (unspiritual; ruled by human thinking).
- **2 Corinthians 6:16-18** (item 9): Baxter gives only the reference after a sentence in his own words ("he is our Father, and dwells among us, and in us, and walks in us, and is our God"). It is a paraphrase of the verses, not in quotation marks, so we keep his wording. The NET has "I will live in them and will walk among them, and I will be their God" (verse 16) and "I will be a father to you" (verse 18).
- **Ephesians 3:18-19** (item 9): NET wording. Baxter's King James order is "breadth, and length, and depth, and height"; the NET has "breadth and length and height and depth." His "passeth knowledge" is the NET's "surpasses knowledge"; "that you may be filled with all the fulness of God" is the NET's "so that you will be filled up to all the fullness of God." The NET's "you will be able" (not "that you may be able") makes the clause a statement of result. Baxter's use does not depend on the difference.
- **Unmarked allusions in item 9** (kept in Baxter's wording): "put your hand into his wounded side, and be not faithless, but believing" is from John 20:27 (the NET has "Put your finger here, and examine my hands. Extend your hand and put it into my side. Do not continue in your unbelief, but believe"); "My Lord and my God!" is Thomas's cry, John 20:28 (NET wording used). Neither reference is in Baxter's text.
- **sensory appetite** (item 9): Baxter's "sensitive appetite," following the decision recorded in CONVENTIONS.md. In his terms, the "will" delights in goodness and the lower "appetite" in physical sweetness.
- **"spirit of bondage unto fear"; "spirit of adoption"** (item 10): Baxter quotes the first and uses the second in his own words, unquoted, from Romans 8:15 (reference not in Baxter's text). NET wording: "the spirit of slavery leading again to fear" and "the Spirit of adoption." The NET has "slavery" for the King James "bondage." The NET also capitalizes "Spirit of adoption" (the Holy Spirit), whereas Baxter writes "spirit of adoption, or of filial love" without quotation marks, and glosses it as a disposition of love. We keep his words and his lowercase, and the capital Spirit appears only here in the note. We have not tried to decide for him which he means.
- **converse; communion; conference** (items 10 and 11): "converse" and "communion" are kept (fellowship; talk with God). Baxter's "conference" is the Puritan word for devout conversation with others, so we have "conversation with others."
- **undisposedness** (item 11): Baxter's word. We render "unreadiness" (lack of inclination or readiness). **quicken / quickens** (item 11): "revive." **Spirit of supplication:** an allusion to Romans 8:26 in the same sentence ("help our infirmities, when we know not what to pray for as we ought"); NET: "the Spirit helps us in our weakness, for we do not know how we should pray." Not in quotation marks in Baxter, so we keep his words with "infirmities" as "weaknesses."
- **Luke 18:1** (item 11): Baxter gives the reference after his own wording ("spake a parable to this end, that we should always pray, and not wax faint"). The NET has "told them a parable to show them they should always pray and not lose heart." We keep his wording ("wax faint" as "grow faint"). The "strong cries and tears" are an unmarked allusion to Hebrews 5:7 (NET: "loud cries and tears"); "all the night in prayer" is from Luke 6:12 (we did not fetch it). Neither reference is in Baxter's text.
- **flesh and bone** (item 13): an unmarked allusion to Ephesians 5:30 as the King James has it ("of his flesh, and of his bones"). The NET text of that verse has only "because we are members of his body"; Baxter's argument rests mainly on the "members" idea he also states in the next sentence.
- **declines** (item 14): Baxter's "decays" (spiritual decline).
- **Hebrews 7:24-25** (item 14): NET wording for the quoted clause, with Baxter's own parenthetical "(sincerely)" kept in place. The NET flags the sense Baxter leans on: Baxter's "to the uttermost, or to perpetuity" is his gloss on the King James "to the uttermost"; the NET has "able to save completely," and does not itself carry the alternative "perpetuity" (forever). His gloss is kept as his; both senses are still his point.
- **1 Peter 2:5, 9** (item 15): Baxter's quotation blends verse 9 and verse 5 (verse 9 first, then verse 5), and the NET text of each differs from what he wrote: the King James "generation" is the NET's "race," and "peculiar people" is "a people of his own"; "showing forth the praises" is "proclaim the virtues." We combine NET wording in his order. His "an holy priesthood" and "to offer up" come from verse 5 (NET: "a holy priesthood and to offer spiritual sacrifices that are acceptable to God through Jesus Christ"; we shortened "and to offer" to "to offer" in joining the quotations, and omit the NET's "you yourselves, as living stones, are built up as a spiritual house to be," which Baxter did not quote). The word "priests" itself is Baxter's.
- **Psalm 51:17** (item 15): Baxter's quotation is a paraphrase of the King James; we keep his wording, modernized. The NET reads "The sacrifice God desires is a humble spirit—O God, a humble and repentant heart you will not reject." His "broken ... contrite" is the King James's wording; the NET has "humble" and "repentant." The sense Baxter needs (God accepts a humbled heart) is the same, though "despise" is stronger than "reject."
- **Romans 8:26-27** (item 15): reference only; we did not paraphrase. NET: "the Spirit himself intercedes for us with inexpressible groanings."
- **Unmarked allusions in item 16** (kept in Baxter's wording): "be of good cheer, because he has conquered" is from John 16:33 (NET: "take courage—I have conquered the world"); "all power given him in heaven and earth" is from Matthew 28:18 (NET: "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me"; Baxter's "power" is kept; the NET has "authority"); "We can do all things through Christ who strengthens us" is Philippians 4:13 (NET: "I am able to do all things through the one who strengthens me"); "his grace is sufficient for you" is 2 Corinthians 12:9 (NET: "My grace is enough for you"). None has a reference in Baxter's text.
- **Hebrews 2:14-15** (item 17): NET wording. Baxter's "we were partakers" is not the King James or the NET ("the children are partakers"; "the children share"); the NET's "the children" is used. His "subject to bondage" is the NET's "held in slavery."
- **Unmarked allusion in item 17:** "he has the keys of death and hell" is from Revelation 1:18 (NET: "I hold the keys of death and of Hades"; reference not in Baxter's text). "No more has dominion over him" is probably Romans 6:9 (we did not fetch it; not confirmed).
- **1 Corinthians 15:55** (item 17): NET wording, used for the quoted words, but Baxter's version ("O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?") differs in order and has "grave" in the second line. The NET has "Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting?" with "death" both times. The reference was not in Baxter's text. The change in order does not affect his point.
- **Our Master's joy** (item 17): an allusion to Matthew 25:21 (NET: "Enter into the joy of your master"); reference not in Baxter's text.
- **Philippians 1:23, 21** (item 17): Baxter writes "it being best for us to depart and be with Christ," which is a paraphrase of verse 23 (NET: "a desire to depart and be with Christ, which is better by far"); we keep his words. "Death is become our gain" paraphrases verse 21 (NET: "living is Christ and dying is gain").
- **John 12:26** (item 17): NET wording; Baxter's "let him follow me, and ... there shall also my servant be" is the King James.
- **John 14:19** (item 17): Baxter quotes in the third person ("because he lives, therefore we shall live also"), where Christ's own words are "because I live, you will live too" (NET). It is his adaptation of the saying, not a quotation, so we keep his wording.
- **two treatises of death** (item 17): Baxter's own earlier writings; we have not identified or confirmed which.
- **interpose; justified** (items 17, 18): left as written. **grasp** (item 18): Baxter's "apprehend." **dearest Friend** capitalized as in the source.
- **condescend; acquisition; fruition** (item 20): "condescend" is kept in its old sense of graciously stooping to us. By "the mediation of redemption and acquisition" Baxter means Christ's work in winning (acquiring) salvation, which he says will end; by "the mediation of fruition" he means a continuing role in our enjoyment (fruition) of God, which he says "seems" to continue. We keep the terms and have not interpreted them further. **(and thus he shall deliver up the kingdom to the Father)** alludes to 1 Corinthians 15:24; reference not in Baxter's text, and we did not fetch it.

The NET: "the Lord God—the All-Powerful—and the Lamb are its temple" (verse 22) and "the glory of God lights it up, and its lamp is the Lamb" (verse 23). "The new Jerusalem" is Baxter's name for the city. His "Almighty" is the NET's "All-Powerful."
