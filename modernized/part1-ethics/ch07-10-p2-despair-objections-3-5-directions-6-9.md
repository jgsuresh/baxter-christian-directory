# Chapter 7: Title 10, Directions against sinful despair (Objections 3-5, Directions 6-9)

*This section begins partway through Title 10, in the middle of the objections and answers that follow Direction 5; it continues from the previous file.*

*Objection 3.* But it is said of Esau, that "later when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for he found no opportunity for repentance, although he sought the blessing with tears" (Hebrews 12:17). It seems then that repentance in this life may be too late.

*Answer.* It is true that Esau's time for the blessing was past as soon as Isaac had given it to Jacob. [370] When he had sold his birthright it was too late to recall it, for the right was made over to his brother; and it was not repentance, and cries, and tears, that could recall the right he had sold, nor recall the words that Isaac had spoken: but this does not prove that our day of grace does not continue till death, or that any man repenting before his death shall be rejected as Esau's repentance was: the apostle neither says nor means any such thing. The sense of his words are only this much: Be careful lest there be any so profane among you, as to set so little store by the blessings of the gospel, even Christ and life eternal, as to part with them for a base lust or passing thing, as Esau, that set more store by a morsel of food than by his birthright: for let them be sure that the time will come (even the time mentioned by Christ, Matthew 25:10–11, when the door is shut and the Lord has come) when they will dearly repent it; and then, as it was with Esau when the blessing was gone, so it will be with them when their blessing is gone, repentance, and cries, and tears will be too late: for the gospel has its justice and terrors as well as the law. This is all in the text, but there is no intimation that our day of grace is as short as Esau's hope of the blessing was.

*Objection 4.* Saul had only his time, which when he lost he was forsaken by God.

*Answer.* Saul's sin provoked God to reject him from being king of Israel, and to appoint another in his stead; but if Saul had repented he would have been saved after that, though not restored to the crown: and it is true, that as God withdrew from him the spirit of government, so many before death, by the greatness of their sins, cause God to forsake them so far as to withhold those stirrings, and convictions, and fears, and disquiet, in sin, which sometimes they had, and to give them over to a "depraved mind," to commit "every kind of impurity with greediness," and glory in it as being "callous" (Romans 1:28; Ephesians 4:18–19). If it is thus with you, you would be no better, you would not be recovered, you think sin is best for you, and hate all that would reform you.

*Objection 5.* It is said in 2 Corinthians 6:2, "Look, now is the acceptable time; look, now is the day of salvation!" And Hebrews 3:7, 12–13, "Oh, that today you would listen as he speaks! Do not harden your hearts ... that none of you may become hardened by sin's deception."

*Answer.* This says no more than that the present time is the best, indeed, the only certain time; and we are not sure that the day of salvation will continue any longer, because death may cut us off: but if it does not, yet sin is a hardening thing, and the longer we sin the more it hardens! indeed, God may withhold the stirrings of his Spirit, and leave us to ourselves, to the hardness of our hearts: and thus he does with thousands of wicked persons, who are left in impenitence and hatred of the truth: but most certainly if those men repented they might be saved, and the very reason why they do not have Christ and life is still because they will not consent.

**Direction 6. Understand by what help and strength it is that the obedience to the gospel must be performed: not merely by your own strength, but by the help of grace, and strength of Christ: if he has only made you willing, he will help you to perform the rest.**

You are not by this covenant to be a savior and sanctifier to yourselves; but to consent that Christ be your Savior, and the Holy Spirit your Sanctifier. You might otherwise despair indeed if you were left to that which you are utterly unable to do. Though you must "continue working out your salvation with awe and reverence, ... the one bringing forth in you both the desire and the effort—for the sake of his good pleasure—is God" (Philippians 2:12–13).

**Direction 7. Understand well the difference between mortal sins and infirmities, that you may not think that every sin is a sign of death or gracelessness; but may know the difference between those sins which should make you think yourselves unjustified, and those sins which only call for particular humiliation, being such as the justified themselves commit.**

Though in the popish sense we take no sin to be venial, that is, which in itself is properly no sin, nor deserves death according to the law of works; yet the distinction between mortal and venial sin is of very great necessity: that is, between sins which prove a man in a state of death, or unjustified, and sins which coexist with a state of grace and justification; between sins which the gospel does not pardon, and those which it pardons, that is, all that stand with true repentance. [371] There are some sins which every one that repents of them does so forsake as to cease committing them; and there are some lesser sins, which they that repent of them do hate indeed, but yet frequently renew, as our defective degrees in the exercise of repentance itself, faith, love, trust, fear, obedience; our empty thoughts and words; some sinful passions, omissions of many duties of thought, affection, word, or deed towards God or man; some minutes of time slip past us; prayer and other duties have a sinful coldness or slackness in them, and such like. Many such sins are rightly called infirmities and venial, because they coexist with life and are forgiven: it is of great use to the peace of our consciences to discern the difference between these two, for one sort require a conversion to another state, and the other require only a particular repentance, and where they are unknown, are forgiven without particular repentance, because our general repentance is virtually, though not actually, particular as to them. One sort are cause of judging ourselves ungodly; and the other sort are only cause of filial humiliation. Any one may see the great need of discerning the difference; but yet it is a matter of very great judgment doctrinally to distinguish them, much more actually to discern them in every instance in yourselves. The way is to know first, what is the condition of the new covenant, and of absolute necessity to salvation or justification; and then every sin that is inconsistent with that condition is mortal, and the rest that are consistent and do coexist with it are venial, or only infirmities. As venial signifies only that sort of sin which is pardonable, and may coexist with true grace, so a venial sin may be in an unsanctified person materially, where it is not pardoned; that is, *e.g.*, his wandering thought or passion is a sin of that sort that in the godly is consistent with true grace: but as venial signifies a sin that is pardoned, or pardonable, without a regeneration, or conversion into a state of life from a state of death, so venial sin is in no unregenerate, unjustified person, but is only the infirmities of the saints; and thus I here speak of it. In a word, that sin which actually coexists with habitual repentance, and with the hatred of it, so far that you would rather be free from it than commit or keep it, and which coexists with a sincere consent to the covenant, that God be your Father, Savior, and Sanctifier, and with the love of God above all, is only an infirmity or venial sin. But to know from the nature of the sin which those are requires a volume by itself to direct you only.

**Direction 8. Understand how necessary a faithful minister of Christ is, in such cases of danger and difficulty, to be a guide to your consciences; and open your case truly to them, and place so much confidence in their judgment of your state as their office, and abilities, and faithfulness require, and do not set up your timid, darkened, perplexed judgments above theirs, in cases where they are fitter to judge.**

Such a guide is necessary, both as appointed by Christ who is the author of his office, and in regard of the greatness, and danger, and difficulty of your case. Do you not feel that you are insufficient for yourselves, and that you have need of help? surely a soul that is tempted to despair may easily feel it. You are very proud, or blindly self-conceited, if you do not. And you may easily know that Christ that appointed them their office requires that they be both used and trusted in their office, as far as reason will allow. And where there is no office, yet ability and faithfulness deserve and require credit of themselves. Why else do you trust physicians and lawyers, and all craftsmen, in their several professions and arts, as far as they are reputed able and faithful? I know no man is to be believed as infallible as God is: but man is to be believed as man; and if you will use and trust your spiritual guide only so far as you use and trust your physician or lawyer, you will find the great benefit, if you choose rightly.

**Direction 9. Remember when you have sinned, how sure, and sufficient, and ready a remedy you have before you, in Jesus Christ and the covenant of grace; and that it is God's design in the way of redemption, not to save any man as innocent, that none may glory, but to save men that were first in sin and misery, and fetch them as from the gates of hell, that love and mercy may be magnified on every one that is saved, and grace may abound more by the occasion of sin's abounding (Romans 5:15, 20).**

Not that any should "continue in sin because grace has abounded: God forbid" (Romans 6:1). But that we may magnify that grace and mercy which has abounded above our sins; and turn the remembrance of our greatest sins to the admiration of that great and wonderful mercy. To magnify mercy when we see the greatness of our sin, and to love much because much is forgiven, [372] this is to please God, and answer the very design and end of our redemption: but to magnify sin, and belittle mercy, and to say, My sin is greater than can be forgiven, this is to please the devil, and to cross God's design in the work of our redemption. Is your disease so great that no other can cure it? It is the fitter for Christ to honor his office upon, and God to honor his love and mercy on. Only "come to him so that you may have life," [373] and you shall find that no greatness of sin past will cause him to refuse you; nor any infirmities which you are willing to be rid of shall cause him to disown you, or cast you out. The prodigal is not so much as reproached with his sins, but finds himself, before he is aware, in his father's arms, clothed with the best robes, the ring and shoes, and joyfully entertained with a feast. [374] Remember that there is enough in Christ and the promise to pardon and heal all sins which you are willing to forsake.

## Translator's notes

- **Title:** Baxter divides Chapter 7 ("Directions for the Government of the Passions") into ten divisions headed "Tit." (Title), meaning a heading or division. This file falls within Title 10, against sinful despair. No Title heading falls inside this range.
- **Where this range starts:** it opens at Baxter's Objection 3 to Direction 5 (the time to which the condition of the gospel extends); Direction 5 and Objections 1-2 are in the previous file, so we have not added a Direction heading.
- **Direction layout:** for Directions 6-9 the source runs heading and body together in one paragraph; following the project layout, we put the first sentence (the Direction itself) in bold on its own line and the rest in the next paragraph. Where the boundary of the heading sentence falls is our judgment; no words were moved.
- **Hebrews 12:17:** NET wording. The King James has "he found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears," where "it" is ambiguous (repentance or the blessing). The NET resolves it as "the blessing" and has "no opportunity for repentance" for "no place of repentance." Baxter's note [370] turns on this very ambiguity: he thinks the "place of repentance" Esau could not find was probably Isaac's change of mind, not Esau's own repentance. The NET's "sought the blessing" fits Baxter's main reading, but the reader should know the King James left it open. Baxter's note keeps his phrase "found no place for" (modernized only in spelling).
- **Matthew 25:10–11:** cited but not quoted; "when the door is shut and the Lord has come" is Baxter's own wording, kept.
- **Romans 1:28; Ephesians 4:18–19:** NET wording. The King James phrases Baxter quotes are "reprobate mind" (NET "depraved mind"), "all uncleanness with greediness" (NET "every kind of impurity with greediness"), and "past feeling" (NET "callous"). "Reprobate" in the King James carried the sense of "rejected," which echoes Baxter's theme here (God rejecting Saul, people given up by God); the NET's "depraved" loses that echo. "Past feeling" (beyond feeling) and "callous" mean much the same.
- **2 Corinthians 6:2:** NET wording ("acceptable time" for the King James "accepted time").
- **Hebrews 3:7, 12–13:** NET wording. Baxter cites verse 7 but his words "harden not your hearts" are from verse 8; we kept his reference (as "3:7, 12–13") and quote NET 3:7–8 and 3:13. The source has a dash ("----") where he skips; we use "...". The King James "To day if ye will hear his voice" is a condition; the NET renders it as a wish ("Oh, that today you would listen as he speaks!"). Baxter's point, that today is the time, is not affected. The NET's "sin's deception" stands for the King James "deceitfulness of sin."
- **Philippians 2:12–13:** NET wording. Baxter cites only 2:13, but his quotation begins in 2:12; we cite 2:12–13. The NET "for" between the two clauses is replaced by "..." to keep Baxter's "Though you must ..., it is he that ..." sentence. Flag: the King James reads "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling: For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure." The NET drops "own" ("your salvation") and has "awe and reverence" for "fear and trembling," and "the desire and the effort" for "to will and to do." Baxter's point is the contrast between our working and God's working in us; "your own" sharpened that contrast in the King James, and the NET's wording softens it slightly.
- **Romans 6:1:** Baxter's quotation is his own paraphrase ("continue in sin because grace hath abounded: God forbid"), kept in his words, modernized. The King James has "Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid." The NET reads "Are we to remain in sin so that grace may increase? Absolutely not!" (Romans 6:1–2). Note that "God forbid" is from verse 2.
- **Romans 5:15, 20:** cited but not quoted; Baxter's own wording kept.
- **John 5:40** (note 373): Baxter's "come to him that you may have life" adapts Christ's words "you are not willing to come to me so that you may have life" (NET). We used the NET wording "so that you may have life" with Baxter's "him" for "me." His reference is in his note, not the text.
- **Unmarked allusions** (Baxter's wording kept): Esau selling his birthright for a meal (Genesis 25:29–34; also Hebrews 12:16, where the NET has "immoral or godless person" for the King James "profane person"); Isaac's blessing given to Jacob (Genesis 27); God's rejection of Saul and the spirit departing from him (1 Samuel 15:23–28; 16:14); "that none may glory" (compare 1 Corinthians 1:29; Ephesians 2:9); "cast you out" (compare John 6:37); "love much because much is forgiven" (Luke 7:47, given in note 372); the prodigal son (Luke 15, given in note 374).
- **mortal; venial** (Direction 7): Baxter's technical terms. He rejects the Roman Catholic sense of "venial" (a sin that in itself does not deserve death under the law) but keeps the distinction in his own sense: "mortal" sins are those that show a person is still unconverted and unjustified; "venial" sins (infirmities) are those that can exist alongside true grace and are forgiven.
- **popish:** Baxter's word for Roman Catholic, kept; it is his polemic.
- **coexist with:** Baxter's "consist with" (be compatible with, exist alongside), used repeatedly in Direction 7. "are consistent and do coexist with it" renders "are consistent and do consist with it."
- **virtually, though not actually; materially; filial humiliation:** kept. "Virtually" means in effect or by implication (our general repentance covers these sins without naming them); "materially" means in the nature of the act itself, as opposed to its standing (pardoned or not); "filial humiliation" is the humbling of a child before a father, not the fear of a condemned person.
- **habitual repentance** (Direction 7): a settled disposition of repentance, as opposed to particular acts of it.
- **Other word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): take heed / be careful; set so light by / set so little store by; set more by / set more store by; transitory / passing; meat / food (his "meat" means food generally); had been saved / would have been saved; motions / stirrings; doth by (thousands) / does with (deals with); disquietments / disquiet; if it be thus / if it is thus; do but (come) / only; yea / indeed; impenitency / impenitence; but (only) / only; else / otherwise; remissness / slackness; overslip / slip past; fitly / rightly; vain (thoughts) / empty; e. g. / *e.g.*; had rather / would rather; unfeigned / sincere; timorous / timid; sure / surely; hath (abounded) / has; artificers / craftsmen; aright / rightly; extenuate / belittle; upbraided / reproached; thou art / you are.
- **Kept as still current or as Baxter's terms:** thus, profane, intimation, convictions, spirit of government, gracelessness, particular humiliation, self-conceited, credit (belief, trust), magnify, cross (thwart), entertained.
- **"you would be no better"** (answer to Objection 4): Baxter means such people do not want to be made better; left as written.
- **"The sense of his words are"** (answer to Objection 3): Baxter's grammar, left as printed.
- **Robert Baron** (note 371): the Latin points to a treatise "Of Mortal and Venial Sin" by "Rob. Baronius." This is probably Robert Baron, a Scottish theologian of the earlier seventeenth century, but we have not confirmed the identification or the work's exact title.
