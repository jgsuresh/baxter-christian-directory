# Chapter 7: Title 5, Directions against sinful hopes (Directions 1-7)

**Title 5. Directions against sinful hopes.**

Hope is nothing but a desirous expectation; therefore the directions given before, against sinful love and desire, may suffice also against sinful hopes, except only for the expecting part. Hope is sinful, 1. When it is placed ultimately upon a forbidden object: as to hope for some evil to yourselves which you mistakenly think is good. To hope for happiness in the creature, or to hope for more from it than it can afford you. To hope for the hurt of other men; for the ruin of your enemies; for the hindering of the gospel, and injury to the church of Christ. [335] 2. When you hope for a good thing by evil means: as to hope to please God, or to come to heaven by persecuting his servants, or by ignorance, or superstition, or schism, or heresy, or any sin. 3. To hope groundlessly for that from God which he never promised. 4. To hope deceitfully for that from God which he has declared he will never give. All these are sinful hopes. But it is not these last that I shall here say much to, because I have said so much already of them in many other writings.

**Direction 1. Hope for nothing from God against faith or without faith; that is, for nothing which he has said he will not give, nor for anything which he has not promised to give, or given you some reason to expect.**

To hope for that which God has told us he will not give, or that which is against the holiness and justice of God to give, this is but to hope that God will prove a liar, or unholy, or unjust, which are wicked and blaspheming hopes. Such are the hopes which a great many ignorant and ungodly persons have; who hope to be saved without regeneration, and without true holiness of heart or life; and hope to be saved in their willful impenitence and beloved sins: who hope that God forgives them those sins which they do not hate, nor will be persuaded to forsake: and hope that the saying over some words of prayer, or doing something which they call a good work, shall save them, though they do not have the Spirit of Christ: or that hope to be saved, though they are unsanctified, because they are not so bad as some others, and do not live in any notorious, disgraceful sin: all these believe the devil who tells them that an unholy person may be saved, and believe that the gospel is false which says, "holiness, for without it no one will see the Lord" (Hebrews 12:14); and they hope that God will prove unholy, unjust, and false to save them, and yet this they call a hoping in God. Hope for that which God has promised, and do not hold back; but not for that which he has said he will not do, yes, solemnly declared cannot be (John 3:3, 5).

**Direction 2. When you hope for any evil to others, or yourself, remember what a monstrous thing it is to make evil the object of your hope, and how those hopes are but your hastening toward chosen misery, and contradict themselves.**

For you hope for it as good; and to be greedy for evil on the supposition that it is good shows your folly, that you will test no better the objects of your hopes: like a sick man that longs and hopes for that which, if he takes it, will be his death. Thus sinners hope for the poisoned bait.

**Direction 3. Understand how much of the root of worldliness consists in your worldly hopes.**

Poor worldlings have little in possession to delight in; but they keep up a hope of more within them. Many a greedy or ambitious wretch, that never reaches that which he desires, yet lives upon the hopes of it: and hope is what sets and keeps men at work in the service of the world, the flesh, and the devil; as divine hope sets and keeps men at work for heaven, for their souls, and for Jesus Christ. And many a hypocrite that loses much on account of his religion yet shows his rottenness by keeping up his worldly hopes, and going no further than will stand with those.

**Direction 4. Has not the world deceived all that have hoped in it to this day?**

Consider what has become of them and of their hopes; what has it done for them, and where has it left them: and will you place your hopes in that which has deceived so many generations of men already?

**Direction 5. Remember that your worldly hopes are a sin so fully condemned by natural demonstration that you are utterly left without excuse.**

You are certain beforehand that you must die; you know how empty the world will be to you then, and how little it can do for you; and yet you are hoping for more of the world!

**Direction 6. Consider that the world declares its emptiness in the very hopes of worldlings.**

In that it is still drawing them by hopes, and never gives them satisfaction and contentment. Almost all the life of a worldling's pleasure is in his hopes. The very thing which he hopes for does not prove so sweet to him in the possession as it was in his hopes. A hoping and still hoping for that which they never shall attain is the worldling's life.

**Direction 7. O turn your souls to those blessed hopes of life eternal, which are sent you from heaven by Jesus Christ, and set before you in the holy Scriptures, and proclaimed to you by the messengers of grace.**

Does God offer you sure, well-grounded hopes of living forever in his joy and glory? And do you neglect them, and lie hoping for that happiness in the world which cannot be attained, and which will give no contentment when you have attained it? This is more foolish than to toil and impoverish yourselves in hope of finding the philosopher's stone, and refuse a kingdom freely offered.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). No Latin or Greek occurs in this note.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** "Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," his name for each of the ten main divisions of Chapter 7 ("Directions for the Government of the Passions"). This file covers Title 5 from its heading through Direction 7; the range ends there.
- **Direction layout:** in the source each Direction's opening sentence runs straight into the rest of its paragraph. Following the project's format, the first sentence is set as the bold Direction line and the following sentences form the body. No words were moved or dropped. In Direction 6 the body therefore begins with Baxter's own sentence fragment "In that it is still drawing them by hopes..."; it is kept as he punctuated it.
- **Hebrews 12:14 (NET flag):** Baxter writes "without holiness none shall see the Lord" (the King James has "no man shall see the Lord"), in which the word "holiness" sits inside the clause he quotes. The NET reads "Pursue peace with everyone, and holiness, for without it no one will see the Lord," with "holiness" carried by the pronoun "it." We give the NET from "holiness" to the end of the verse so that the word Baxter leans on stays in the quotation. The sense he relies on (holiness is necessary to see the Lord) is unchanged.
- **John 3:3, 5:** cited only, not quoted. In the NET, 3:3 reads "born from above" where the King James has "born again"; Baxter's point here (God has declared that salvation without regeneration cannot be) does not depend on that wording.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): save (except); mistakingly (mistakenly); felicity (happiness); hinderance (hindering); ungroundedly (groundlessly); abundance of (a great many); spare not (do not hold back); protested (solemnly declared; in 1673 "protest" meant to affirm solemnly, not to object); unto chosen misery (toward chosen misery); try (test); covetous (greedy); upon the account of (on account of); vanity / vain (emptiness / empty); content (contentment); in hope to find (in hope of finding). "Will stand with those" is kept; it means "is consistent with those (worldly hopes)."
- **the creature:** Baxter's term for created things (the world and anything in it) as distinct from God.
- **worldlings:** kept; people whose hopes and loves are set on this world.
- **religion** (Direction 3): his word for practiced piety, worship and devotion.
- **philosopher's stone** (Direction 7): the substance alchemists sought, believed able to turn base metals into gold; Baxter uses it as the type of a ruinous, hopeless quest.
- **regeneration; unsanctified; impenitence:** theological terms, kept.
- **"I have said so much already of them in many other writings":** Baxter's cross-reference to his other books; we have not identified which he means.
- **Note 335:** the source's "but it would leave" uses an older double-negative idiom after "doubt"; "but" is dropped so the sentence reads in modern English with the same meaning. "Lord Bacon" is Francis Bacon. Baxter cites the essay as "of Lies"; the passage may be from the essay usually titled *Of Truth* (which discusses lies), but we have not confirmed this or checked Baxter's edition, so his title is kept.
- **melancholy** (note 335): kept; see GLOSSARY.md.
