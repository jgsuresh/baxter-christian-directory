# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 9 (part 8 of 13)

*This section begins in the middle of Grand Direction 9, in Baxter's paired run of Temptations and Directions against particular sins, at Temptation 8 (the heading above the range in the source is for a Direction before it). It ends after Temptation 14, whose Direction comes in the next file.*

**Temptation 8.** The tempter is also diligent to keep the end from the sinner's eye, and to persuade him that there is no danger in it, and that it will be as good at last as at first. He cannot endure a thought, a word of death or judgment, unless he can first fortify the sinner by some presumptuous hope, that his sins are pardoned, and his case is good: either he will make them believe him, that there is no such danger to the soul as should deter them; or else he keeps them from thinking of that danger. He is unwilling that a sinner should so much as look into a grave, or go to the house of mourning, and see the end of all the living, lest he should lay it to heart, and thence perceive what worldly pleasure, wealth, and greatness are, by seeing where they leave sinners. If one does but talk of death or judgment, and the life to come, the devil will stir up some scorn, or weariness, or opposition against such talk. If a sinner does but stop to think in secret what will become of him after death, the devil will either lure him, or trouble him, and never let him rest, until he has cast away all such thoughts as tend to his salvation. He cannot endure that when you see the pomp and pleasure of the world, you should think or ask, How long will this last? and what will it prove in the latter end?

*In the margin: Psalm 1; 15; Matthew 25.*

**Direction 8.** Go to the holy Scriptures, and see what they foretell concerning the end of godliness and sin: God knows better than the devil, and is more to be believed. You may see in the word of God what will become of saints and sinners, godly and ungodly, at the last, and what they will think and say when they review their present life; and what Christ will say to them, and how he will judge them, and what will be their reward forever. This is the infallible prediction where you may foresee your endless state. In this mirror continually foresee the end. Never judge of anything by the present taste alone. Ask not only how it tastes, but how it works, and what will be the effects: remember that God's law has inseparably joined holiness and heaven, and sin unrepented of and hell; and seeing that these cannot in fact be separated, let them never be separated from each other in your thoughts. Otherwise you will never understand Christ or Satan. When Christ says, "Will you deny yourself, and take up the cross and follow me?" his meaning is, shall I heal your carnal, worldly heart and life, and bring you by grace to the sight of God in endless glory? You will never understand what prayer, and obedience, and holy living mean, if you do not see the end, even heaven joined to them. When the devil says to the glutton, Eat also of this pleasant dish; and to the drunkard, Take the other cup; and to the fornicator, Take your pleasure in the dark; and to the pleasure-loving, Go to the theater, or the gambling house; come, play at cards or dice; his meaning is, Come, venture upon sin, and do not fear God's threatenings, and refuse his word, and Spirit, and grace, so that I may have your company among the damned, in the fire which shall never be quenched. This is the true English of every temptation. Open your ears then, and whenever the devil or any sinner tempts you to sin, hear him as if he said, I beg you, leap into the flames of hell.

**Temptation 9.** If the tempter cannot quickly draw people to the sin, he will move them at least to weaken their resolution against it, and to deliberate about it, and hear what can be said, and enter into a dispute with Satan or some of his instruments; telling them that it is a sign of falsehood which will not endure the trial, and that we must test all things. And while the sinner is deliberating and disputing, the venom is working itself into his veins, and the senses are secretly undermining and betraying him, and deceiving his mind, bribing his reason, and seducing his will: just as an enemy will negotiate with those who hold a garrison, so that during the negotiation he may send in spies, and find out their weakness, and corrupt the soldiers; so does the devil with the sinner.

*In the margin: Galatians 1:16.*

**Direction 9.** Remember that it is Christ, and not Satan, that you are to listen to. Truth is strong, and can bear the trial before any competent judge; but you are weak, and not so able to judge as you may imagine. Ignorant, unskilled, and unsettled persons are easily deceived, however clear the cause. If it is a cause untried by you, it is not untried by all the godly, nor unknown to him who gave you the holy Scriptures. If it is fit to be called in question and disputed, take the help of able godly teachers or friends, and hear what they can say: matters of endless life or death are not to be rashly ventured on. But if it is a thing past dispute, in which you have already been convinced and resolved, reject the tempter, and tell him that you do not owe him so much service as to dispute with him whether you should care for your salvation. Otherwise there will be no end, until you are betrayed and ruined: innocent Eve is deceived once it comes to a dispute. Do not be like Balaam, who tempted God, and would not be satisfied with his answer.

**Temptation 10.** Also the tempter overcomes very many, by making them presumptuously confident of their own strength: saying, You are not so weak as not to be able to bear a greater temptation than this. Can you not gaze on beauty, or go among empty and tempting company, and yet choose whether you will sin? It is a child indeed who has no more control of themselves. Can your table, your cup, your house, your lands not be pleasing and delightful, without your needing to over-love them, and turn them to sin?

**Direction 10.** O know your own weakness, the treacherous enemy which you still carry about with you, who is ready to open the back door to the devil! Remember that the flesh is on the tempter's side, and how much it can do with you before you are aware. Remember what an unsettled wretch you are, and how many a good purpose has formerly come to nothing, and how often you have sinned by as small a temptation. Remember that without the Spirit of Christ you can do nothing, nor stand against any assault of Satan; and that Christ gives his Spirit and help in his own way, and not to those who tempt him to forsake them, by thrusting themselves into temptations. Shall any mortal man ever presume upon his own strength, after the falls of an Adam, a Noah, a Lot, a David, a Solomon, a Hezekiah, a Josiah, a Peter? and after such ruin of multitudes of professing Christians, as our eyes have seen? "These things happened to them as examples and were written for our instruction, on whom the ends of the ages have come. ... So let the one who thinks he is standing be careful that he does not fall" (1 Corinthians 10:11-12).

**Temptation 11.** It is a great scheme of the devil, and successful with many, to draw them to venture on the sin, by showing them first the effective remedy, the abundant mercy of God, the sufficient satisfaction made by Christ, the full, and free, and universal promise; that these are sufficient to cleanse the soul of any sin, and therefore you need not fear.

*In the margin: Romans 9.*

*In the margin: Hebrews 10:26-29; 2 Thessalonians 1:10.*

**Direction 11.** But God is just, as well as merciful; and there are "objects of wrath," as well as objects of mercy (Romans 9:22-23). Judge how God will use his mercy, and who shall have it, by his own word: for he knows better than you to whom, and how far, to show mercy. Is the tempter himself saved, for all that God is merciful? And the gospel has far more severe punishment than the law for the abusers of grace. Christ is the most dreadful Judge to the wicked, as well as the tenderest Savior to his own. There is enough in his grace to save the penitent: but if you will sin on the presumption that grace will save you, you have little reason to think that you are penitent, or ever will be, without a very merciful change. How many can you name who were ever converted and forgiven, who lived willfully in sin because the remedy was sufficient? I do not doubt that many such have been recalled; but this is not the way to hope: it is a terrible thing to sin deliberately and willfully, because of the greatness of mercy, or the sufficiency of the death of Christ! No man but the penitent convert is saved by Christ; and this is clean contrary to penitence and conversion. Christ does not act as quacks do, who wound a man to show people how quickly their balsams can cure him; or make a man drink a toad, to show the power of their antidotes: but he cures the diseases which he finds, (in believers,) but causes none.

**Temptation 12.** Also the tempter tells the sinner how certain, and easy, and speedy a remedy he has in his own power: it is but repenting, and all sin is pardoned.

*In the margin: James 2:19.*

**Direction 12.** 1. Is it in your power? If so, the greater is your sin, that you sin more when you should repent: if it is easy, what an inexcusable wretch you are, who will not do it, but go on! 2. But repentance is the gift of God, 2 Timothy 2:25-26; and is he likely to give it to those who willfully abuse him in expectation of it? 3. As easy as it is, it is but a few who truly repent and are forgiven, in comparison with those who go on and perish. 4. The easiest repentance is so bitter that it is far easier to refrain from the sin: it is better not to wound yourselves than to have the best salve, if you were sure of it. 5. The repentance which is caused by mere fears of death and hell, without the power of heavenly love to God and holiness, is but the repentance of the damned, and never obtains pardon of sin: the devil has such a repentance, as well as such a faith, which will not save him.

**Temptation 13.** Satan also emboldens the sinner, by telling him how many have repented and fared well, who sinned as badly as this or worse. He tells him of Noah, and Lot, and David, and Peter, and the thief on the cross, and Paul a persecutor, yes, and Manasseh, etc.

**Direction 13.** But consider whether any of those sinned thus because others who had sinned before them had escaped. And think of the millions who never repented, and are condemned, as well as of the few who have repented. Is repentance better than sin? Why then will you sin? Is sin better than repentance? Why then do you intend to repent? Is it not base ingratitude to offend God willfully, because he has pardoned many offenders, and is ready to forgive the penitent? And should a man of reason willfully make work for his own repentance, and do what he knows he will wish with grief that he had never done? If some have been saved who fell into the sea, or who fell from the top of steeples, or who drank poison, or were dangerously wounded, will you therefore throw yourself into the same case, in hope of being saved?

**Temptation 14.** The tempter persuades the sinner that it cannot be that God should make so great a matter of sin, because the thoughts of a man's heart, or his words or deeds, are matters of no great moment, when man himself is so poor a worm; and whatever he does, it is no hurt to God: therefore you need not make such a matter of it.

## Translator's notes

- **Range and position.** The range begins at Baxter's Temptation 8 and ends with Temptation 14; the Direction that answers Temptation 14 falls after the range, in the next file. The most recent heading given before the range ("Direction 7 ... Keep a continual watch upon your thoughts") is not in this range. Baxter's "Tempt. VIII." through "Tempt. XIV." and "Direct. VIII." through "Direct. XIII." are rendered "Temptation 8." etc. and "Direction 8." etc., with Arabic numerals; the numbered sub-points in Direction 12 stay as he has them.
- **Margin notes.** Baxter's margin references are rendered as "In the margin" lines in modern form: Psalms 1 and 15 with Matthew 25 (by Direction 8); Galatians 1:16 (Direction 9); Romans 9, and Hebrews 10:26-29 with 2 Thessalonians 1:10 (Direction 11); James 2:19 (Direction 12). Those were printed as separate margin notes and kept separate. They are references only, with no quoted words, so no NET text is set out for Psalm 1, Psalm 15, Matthew 25, Hebrews 10, 2 Thessalonians 1 or James 2:19. We take the James reference to underlie Baxter's remark at the end of Direction 12 that "the devil has such a repentance, as well as such a faith" (the demons' faith), but this is our reading and Baxter does not say so.
- **Galatians 1:16 (margin, Direction 9).** Baxter gives only the reference. Our guess is that he has in mind Paul's not consulting others (the King James has "I conferred not with flesh and blood"; the NET has "I did not go to ask advice from any human being"). The NET wording is plainer and loses the phrase "flesh and blood," but the point that fits Baxter's Direction (listen to Christ, not to disputers) is not changed. We have not confirmed that this was his meaning.
- **1 Corinthians 10:11-12 (NET).** Baxter quotes the King James: "All these things happened unto them for ensamples, and they are written for our admonition, on whom the ends of the world are come. Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall," giving "1 Cor. x. 11, 12". The NET reads as printed above. Changes: "ensamples" to "examples" (same sense); "admonition" to "instruction" (the NET's wording is milder than "admonition," but Baxter's use of the text as a warning against self-confidence is untouched, since verse 12 carries the warning); "ends of the world" to "ends of the ages" (a change from the King James that Baxter does not lean on). "All these things" is "These things" in the NET. The "..." marks the verse break Baxter runs together, to show that the NET text of verse 11 ends before verse 12.
- **Romans 9:22-23, "vessels of wrath" (Direction 11).** Baxter's sidenote says "Rom. ix." and he puts "vessels of wrath" and "vessels of mercy" in quotation marks, using the King James wording (the second half is not in quotation marks). The NET has "objects of wrath prepared for destruction" and "objects of mercy." I used the NET wording "objects of wrath" and "objects of mercy" for the quotation. **The NET changes some of the imagery Baxter has:** "vessels" recalls the potter and the clay earlier in Romans 9, which "objects" does not show. His argument (God is just as well as merciful, and not everyone receives mercy) does not depend on the word "vessel," so the sense he relies on is kept. The verse reference "Romans 9:22-23" is added by me; Baxter gives only the chapter.
- **Matthew 16:24 (Direction 8) is Baxter's paraphrase.** He has Christ say "Wilt thou deny thyself, and take up the cross and follow me?" in his own question form. He gives no reference. The words resemble Matthew 16:24 (and parallels in Mark 8 and Luke 9), but his wording is a question where the Gospels have a conditional ("If anyone wants to become my follower, he must deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me," NET, Matthew 16:24). I kept his wording, modernized. The reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **Other unmarked allusions** (Baxter's wording kept): "we must prove all things" (Direction 9; the sense of 1 Thessalonians 5:21, and I rendered "prove" as "test," its older sense; we have not confirmed that he means this verse); Balaam "that tempted God" and was not satisfied with his answer (Numbers 22; the NET of 22:19 has Balaam asking "that I may know what more the Lord might say to me," which fits Baxter's point, but the reference is mine, not his); Eve deceived in dispute (Genesis 3); the house of mourning (an echo of Ecclesiastes 7:2, our guess); the fire never quenched (an echo of Mark 9:43-48, our guess); the long list of fallen believers in Direction 10 (Adam, Noah, Lot, David, Solomon, Hezekiah, Josiah, Peter), and the list in Temptation 13 (Noah, Lot, David, Peter, the thief on the cross, Paul as persecutor, Manasseh), which are given by name only. Some of these (Josiah, Hezekiah) are Baxter's choices of fallen examples and not checked by us against each biblical account.
- **2 Timothy 2:25-26 (Direction 12).** Baxter cites this for "repentance is the gift of God." He does not quote it. The NET has "Perhaps God will grant them repentance and then knowledge of the truth, and they will come to their senses and escape the devil's trap," which is a little more tentative ("Perhaps") than Baxter's flat statement, but the point he draws (repentance is God's to give) is kept.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then mine): loth to unwilling; allure to lure; bethink himself to stop to think; gust to taste (his "gust" is "relish"); prognostication to prediction; glass to mirror; conjoined to joined; "indeed" (in "cannot be separated indeed") to "in fact"; play-house to theater; gaming-house to gambling house; the voluptuous to the pleasure-loving (see GLOSSARY, "sensual"); imboldeneth to emboldens; sped well to fared well; treat with (negotiate) to negotiate with, and treaty to negotiation; unskilful to unskilled; mountebanks to quacks; sorer to more severe; professors to professing Christians (his "professors" are those who profess the faith); abate to weaken; "speedy" kept; delectable to delightful; a project (scheme) to a scheme (his "project" is a plan or design); "competent" kept in its old sense of "qualified." "Convinced" in Direction 9 ("in which you have already been convinced and resolved") is kept in the sense of persuaded.
- **Terms kept:** carnal (Direction 8; see GLOSSARY); flesh (Direction 10; see GLOSSARY, "flesh, fleshly"); penitent; presumption and presumptuous (Baxter's word for rash, unfounded confidence); the damned; "over-love" (his own compound, loving something too much). "Pomp" kept.
- **"Sense" and "the senses" (Temptation 9).** Baxter's "sense is secretly undermining and betraying him" is rendered "the senses are secretly undermining and betraying him." He means the bodily senses and appetites working against the reason and will, not "common sense."
- **"Balsams," "antidotes" (Direction 11).** Baxter's picture is of traveling quacks who make a man ill in order to show off their cures; "balsam" is a healing ointment. His point is that Christ does not cause the disease he cures. "(In believers,)" is his own parenthesis and is kept.
- **Persons and gender.** Baxter moves between "he," "they," and "you" for the sinner within a single Temptation; I kept his pronouns; where he writes "themselves" for a child ("a child indeed that hath no more government of themselves"), I kept the plural "themselves." "Man" for a person generally is kept, as in "no man but the penitent convert."
- **Direction 12, item 4.** Baxter's "it is better not wound yourselves" is rendered "it is better not to wound yourselves," supplying only the "to" that his grammar leaves out.
- **Temptation 14.** The range ends with this Temptation; Baxter's answer to it (presumably his Direction 14) falls after the range and is left to the next file. Nothing is omitted.
- **His polemic.** Baxter's strong words for the sinner ("wretch," "base ingratitude") are his own and are kept.
