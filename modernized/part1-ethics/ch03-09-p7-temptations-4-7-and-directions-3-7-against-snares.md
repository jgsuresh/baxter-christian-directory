# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 7 of 13)

*This section continues Grand Direction 9 and breaks off before its end; the next file continues it. It begins at Direction 3 and runs through Direction 7, alternating Baxter's account of the tempter's methods (Temptations 4 to 7) with the Directions against them.*

**Direction 3. Mark but the tendency and the manner of the temptations, and you may perceive the author.** Who else is it that is so much against God, and against your everlasting happiness? Who else is it that would so abuse your reason, to prefer things temporal before things eternal, and the brutish pleasures of a corruptible flesh before the interest of immortal souls? Who else so contradicts all the word of God? Read God's warnings, and he will tell you who it is. Take every temptation then (whoever the messenger may be) as if you saw the devil standing by, and making the proposal to you, and heard him himself urge you to the sin. Suppose you saw him leading you to the whorehouse, the playhouse, the alehouse, and providing you entertainment as the master of the game. How then would you take it? and what would you do? Would you go, and be angry at the strict preacher that would hinder you? and would you take the devil's part? No, nature has possessed you with a fear of him, and an enmity to him: use it for your safety. It cannot be good for you that comes from him. He has a fouler face to appear to you in, than ever yet you saw, when you have done his work, and are where he would have you. O know with whom you have to do.

**Temptation 4.** The tempter is most careful also to hide from people the nature and tendency of the temptation itself; so that they will not know that it is a temptation when they are tempted, but will have nothing in sight but the bait which they desire. The angler does not only hide himself from the fish, but also his rod, and line, and hook, as much as he can. The fowler covers his nets, so that either the fish and bird will not see the snare, or will not know what it is, and what it is laid there for: so when the bait of pleasure, and honor, and wealth is presented by the devil, to the fornicator, gambler, proud person, or greedy person, they will not see what the devil is doing now, and what a game he is playing for their souls! They will not perceive the connection that there is between the pleasure and the sin, and the sin and the threatening, and the threatening and the judgment, and the judgment and the everlasting punishment. When Judas was bargaining with the Pharisees, he did not know that the devil was in him driving on the deal.

**Direction 4.** Be wise and suspicious: blindness or recklessness will lead you into the snare. Be wise, so that you may know the tendency of everything that is presented to your thoughts, and may be able to perceive a danger. Be suspicious and cautious, so that you make a sufficient test, and go on sure grounds, and avoid the very appearance of evil: when it is hell that you fear, do not come too near. Do not play like the fly around the candle: salvation is necessary; but advancement, or wealth, or liberty, or reputation, or life itself are not necessary to you! Test all things. Do not flatter yourselves into the snares by foolish hopes, and by judging of things as the flesh would have them to be, rather than as they are. If no danger appears, turn up all coverings, and search and see that none is hidden. The devil has his gunpowder plots, and mines, which may blow you up before you are aware. Not only lawfulness and indifference, but great good is the pretense for the greatest evil.

**Temptation 5.** It is the tempter's care to bring the tempting object near enough, or draw the sinner near enough to it. The net must come to the fish, or the fish to the net. The distant fire will not burn the wood. The devil's chief confidence is in the sensory appetite, which works most strongly close at hand. If he gets the drunkard into the alehouse, and shows him the cup, he has half conquered him already; but if he is scrupulous and modest, someone will drink a toast, or press him, and put the cup into his hand. The thief, like Achan, will see the bait, and the sight will stir up a greedy desire. The glutton will have the tempting dishes before him, and be at a table which by its variety of delicious food is fitted to become his snare; whereas if he had nothing set before him but the poor man's simple food which has nothing in it fit to tempt him, he might easily have escaped. The fornicator will have his beautiful dirt brought near him, and presented to him in a tempting dress; for at a sufficient distance there would have been little danger. The ambitious person will have advancement offered him, or brought so fair to his hand, that with a little seeking it may be attained. The fearful coward will be threatened with the loss of property or life, and hear the report of the cannons, guns, and drums of Satan. Peter is half conquered when he is got among questioning company in the high priest's hall. Thus David, thus Lot, thus ordinarily sinners are drawn into the snare.

**Direction 5.** As ever you would preserve your innocence and your souls, flee as far from tempting objects as you can: I say, as you can, without distrusting God by neglecting a certain duty. A wife, or a servant, who are bound, cannot flee; nor must we leave undone our certain duty because of an uncertain danger, which may otherwise be avoided; but keep off from the temptation at as great a distance as you can: the safest course is the best when your souls are at stake: if it is not necessary, do not plead the lawfulness of what you do, when it is a temptation to what is unlawful. You say, it is lawful to wear such elaborate ornaments, and to set yourselves out in the neatest dress; but is it lawful to be proud or lustful, or to consume your time unprofitably? If not, do not tempt yourselves or others to it. Keep away from the place where the snare is laid. Look first to the end before you meddle with the beginning. Why should I eat what I know I cannot digest, but must throw up again? And why should I taste what I must not eat? And why should I desire to have that set before me, and to look upon what I must not taste? Do not come near if you would not be caught. What are you doing at the alehouse with a cup before you, if you would not be drawn to excess of drink? If you are prone to excess in eating, do not make your own table your temptation. Flee from the temptation as you would from hell, or from the devil himself. Do not look at the bait of lust, or do not come near, if you are inclined to lust: says Solomon, "Keep yourself far from her, and do not go near the door of her house" (Proverbs 5:8). "but in the end she is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a two-edged sword. Her feet go down to death; her steps lead straight to the grave" (Proverbs 5:4-5). "For she has set her house by death and her paths by the place of the departed spirits. None who go in to her will return, nor will they reach the paths of life" (Proverbs 2:18-19). "Her house is the way to the grave, going down to the chambers of death" (Proverbs 7:27). "Whoever is naive, let him turn in here," ... "To those who lack understanding she has said, 'Stolen waters are sweet, and food obtained in secret is pleasant!' But they do not realize that the dead are there, that her guests are in the depths of the grave" (Proverbs 9:16-18). "Do not lust in your heart for her beauty, and do not let her captivate you with her alluring eyes.... Can a man hold fire against his chest without burning his clothes? Can a man walk on hot coals without scorching his feet?" (Proverbs 6:25, 27-28). Remember that you pray daily, "do not lead us into temptation": and if you will run into it yourselves, are not your prayers hypocritical and an abuse of God? If you would be saved from sin, you must be saved in God's way; and that is, by fleeing from temptations; and not by drawing near, and gazing on forbidden objects, and tempting yourselves: even as God's holy means must be used by all who would come to holiness and heaven; so the devil's must be avoided by him who would escape sin and hell. But if you cannot move far enough from the snare, then double your fear, and watchfulness, and resolution: flee with Joseph (Genesis 39:12) from the sin, if you cannot go out of the house. How carefully should every foot be placed, when we know that every step we take is among snares! Rule your senses if you cannot remove the bait: make Job's covenant with your eyes, that you do not look on what would allure (Job 31:1). Let every sense have a constant watch.

**Temptation 6.** The next great work of the tempter is, to give us the fairest opportunities to sin, and to remove all hindrances, and show people encouraging hopes and invitations. He will show the thief which way he may steal; and show the greedy man which way he may thrive, and deceive, and overreach; and the ambitious man which way he may rise; and the fornicator how he may obtain his desire, and sin unknown; and then he tells them how easy it is; now no one sees you; you may do it without fear or shame. It is the devil's great care to take all things out of the way that would frighten or hinder sinners; so that they may have full opportunity to invite them. Therefore he is very eager that public hindrances should all be removed; especially in a godly magistrate and minister, and that the common disgrace of sinning may be taken off, and if it may be, turned against religion, or fall on those who are the greatest adversaries to sin.

**Direction 6.** It is therefore a principal part of your wisdom and watchfulness, to avoid the opportunities of sinning, and keep out as many hindrances as may be in your own way. It is a most foolish and sinful thing in some men, who think it a fine thing to have power to do harm, though they claim that they hate the doing of it. He who says he hates oppression, yet would have a power to oppress; to have all men at his will and mercy he thinks is fine: so those who would not be gluttonous would have a tempting table still before them, presuming that their own will is a sufficient safeguard against the sin: so those who would not be ensnared by lust, have yet a desire to appear as attractive, and lovely, and desirable as may be, and to be as much loved, so that they may have other affections at command; and also to have opportunity offered them, so that they may sin if they will. And is your will so well established, mortified, and unchangeable, as to be so far trusted? O foolish sinner! who know yourself no better; nor observe your danger; nor perceive that this very desire to have the power to do evil shows a degree of the evil in your heart, and that you are not yet so far from it as you must be, if you would be safe. Maneuver yourself (if you are wise and love yourself) into the greatest difficulty of sinning that you can. Make it impossible, if it may be done. The power is for the act. Do not desire to be able to sin, if you would not sin; not that natural power to do good should be destroyed because it is also a power to do evil, but cast as many blocks in the way of your sinning as you can, till it amounts to a moral impossibility. Desire the strictest laws and governors, and to be still in the eye of others, and arrange it so that you may have no hope of secrecy. Arrange it so that it will be utter shame and loss to you if you sin. If you are tempted to fornication, never be private with her or him who is your snare. If you are tempted to deceive and rob those who trust you, avoid the trust; or if ever you have done it, restore and confess, so that shame may preserve you.

**Temptation 7.** Next the tempter insistently urges our thoughts or imaginations to feed upon the tempting thing: so that the lustful person may be thinking on the objects of his lusts; and the ambitious man thinking on his desired honor; and the greedy man of his desired wealth, his house, or lands, or profitable bargains; and the malicious man be thinking of all the real or imaginary wrongs which kindle malice.

**Direction 7.** Keep a continual watch upon your thoughts. Remember that this is the common entrance of the greatest sins; and if they go no further, the Searcher of hearts will judge you for the adultery, murder, and other sins of your heart. But especially see that your thoughts are so employed on better things, that sin may never find them vacant.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All quotations were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Proverbs 5:5; 2:18; 7:27; 9:18 (important):** Baxter's King James has "hell" in 5:5, 7:27, and 9:18 ("the depths of hell") and "the dead" in 2:18. He calls these the "way to hell" and uses them to warn that lust ends in damnation. The NET reads "the grave" in 5:5, 7:27, and 9:18, and "the place of the departed spirits" in 2:18. The NET wording does not itself say "hell," so the lean toward eternal punishment that Baxter draws ("fly from the temptation as thou wouldst do from hell") rests on his reading, not on the NET wording. Flagged.
- **Proverbs 2:18-19:** Baxter cites "chap. ii. 18, 19" for "Her house inclineth to death, and her paths unto the dead." We kept his reference. The NET also has "nor will they reach the paths of life" for "neither take they hold of the paths of life."
- **Proverbs 9:16-18:** Baxter quotes verse 16 and then jumps to the words of verse 17. The NET prints the two lines of verse 16 as a pair, and we used the first line in quotation marks and the second after "...". The NET has "naive" where the King James has "simple."
- **Proverbs 6:25, 27-28:** Baxter's "eye-lids" is the NET's "alluring eyes," and his "bosom" is the NET's "against his chest." His "--" skip between verse 25 and verse 27 is shown as "...." Baxter's "Can one go upon hot coals" is the NET's "Can a man walk on hot coals."
- **Matthew 6:13 (Lord's Prayer):** Baxter quotes "Lead us not into temptation" with no reference. We quote the NET ("do not lead us into temptation") and add the reference here only; it is not in Baxter's text.
- **Genesis 39:12 and Job 31:1:** Baxter gives these as references for fleeing with Joseph and for Job's "covenant with your eyes," not as quotations. We kept them as references. The NET of Job 31:1 reads "I made a covenant with my eyes; how then could I entertain thoughts against a virgin?"; the King James had "why then should I think upon a maid?"
- **Psalm 101:3 (margin note):** Baxter's margin points to Psalm 101:3, probably for the idea of setting no wicked thing before one's eyes (the King James reads "I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes"). The NET reads "I will not even consider doing what is dishonest," which drops the "before my eyes" image. We kept the reference as a margin note; the sense Baxter likely relies on is weaker in the NET. Flagged, but this is our guess at his use of the verse.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:21-22 (unmarked allusions):** "avoid the very appearance of evil" and "Prove all things" echo the King James ("Abstain from all appearance of evil"; "Prove all things"). The NET reads "Stay away from every form of evil" and "examine all things." Baxter leans on the King James "appearance" ("avoid the very appearance"); the NET has "form" instead, so the word he builds on is not in the NET. Flagged. Not marked in Baxter's text; we kept his words.
- **Other unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Judas bargaining with the Pharisees and Satan entering him (Luke 22:3-6; John 13:27); Achan seeing the bait (Joshua 7:21); Peter among the company in the high priest's hall (Luke 22; Matthew 26); David (2 Samuel 11); Lot (Genesis 19); "Searcher of hearts" (1 Chronicles 28:9; Jeremiah 17:10, our guesses, not confirmed); "adultery, murder ... of your heart" (Matthew 5:21-28). References are our suggestions; none is in Baxter's text here.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): whore-house to whorehouse; play-house to playhouse; ale-house to alehouse; precise (preacher) to strict; cautelous to cautious; gamester to gambler; fool-hardiness to recklessness; trial to test; prove all things to test all things; pretence to pretense; indifferency to indifference; contrive (yourself) to maneuver (yourself); comely to attractive; drink a health to drink a toast; brave (fine thing) to fine; shall (not know, not see) to will;  preferment to advancement (and "offered preferment" to "advancement offered"); credit to reputation; pretend (claim) to claim; gunpowder plots kept; sensitive appetite to sensory appetite; covetous to greedy; estate to property; match (Judas) to deal; innocency to innocence; curious ornaments to elaborate ornaments; fly (verb) to flee; meddle kept; cast it up to throw it up; impediments to hindrances; solicits to insistently urges; fantasies to imaginations; "fall on them" to "fall on those."
- **Polemic and bluntness:** Baxter's blunt words ("whore-house," "beautiful dirt," "fornicator") are kept as he wrote them. "Beautiful dirt" is his contemptuous phrase for the object of lust.
- **"A wife, or a servant, that are bound, cannot fly":** Baxter's point is that those under authority and bound by duty cannot always leave a place of temptation. Rendered as written.
- **"Mortified":** kept as Baxter's term for a will whose sinful desires have been put to death; see the glossary.
- **"affections ... at command" (Direction 6):** "affections" is kept in his technical sense of emotions and desires. His sentence says such people want others' affections within their reach so that they can sin if they like.
- **"The power is for the act":** Baxter's own maxim, left as written. His clarification follows in the next clause (he does not want natural ability destroyed, only moral possibility of sinning removed).
- **Cannons, guns, and drums:** Baxter's war imagery, left as written.
- **Direction numbering:** Baxter's Directions and Temptations are numbered in parallel (Temptation 4 followed by Direction 4, and so on); we have kept that. The file starts at Direction 3; its heading sentence is bolded and the rest of the paragraph follows in the ordinary text.
