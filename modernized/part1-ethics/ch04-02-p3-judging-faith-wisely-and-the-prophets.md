# Chapter 4: Directions 31-36 against unbelief, and the question of how the Jews were bound to believe the prophets

*This range falls in the section Baxter heads "Directions against Unbelief" (part 3 of 3). It begins at the start of Direction 31 and ends in the middle of the Answer to the question about the prophets.*

**Direction 31. Do not judge so great a thing by sudden impressions, or the surprise of a temptation, when you do not have leisure to look up all the evidences of faith, and lay them together, and take a full, deliberate view of all the cause.** It is a mystery so great as requires a clear and vacant mind, delivered from prejudice, abstracted from diverting and deceiving things; which, upon the best assistance and with the greatest diligence, must lay altogether to discern the truth. And if, upon the best assistance and consideration, you have been convinced of the truth, and then will let every sudden thought, or temptation, or difficulty seem enough to question all again, this is unfaithfulness to the truth, and the way to resist the clearest evidences, and never to have done. It is like as if you should answer your adversary in the court, when your witnesses are all dismissed or out of the way, and all your evidences are absent, and perhaps your counselor and advocate too. It is like the casting up of a long and intricate account, which a man has finished by study and time; and when he has done all, one questions this particular, and another that, when his accounts are absent: it is not fit for him to answer all particulars, nor question his own accounts, till he has as full opportunity and help to cast up all again.

**Direction 32.** If the work seems too hard for you, go and consult with the wisest, most experienced Christians; who can easily answer the difficulties which most perplex and tempt you. Modesty will tell you, that the advantage of study and experience may make every one wisest in his own profession; and set others above you, while you have less of these.

**Direction 33.** Remember that Christianity being the surest way to secure your eternal hopes; and the matters of this life, which cause men to forsake it, being such transitory trifles, you can be no losers by it; and therefore if you doubted, yet you might be sure that is the safest way.

**Direction 34.** Do not judge so great a cause in a time of melancholy, when fears and confusions make you unfit. But in such a case as that, as also whenever Satan would disturb your settled faith, or tempt you at his pleasure to be forever newly questioning resolved cases and discerned truths, abhor his suggestions, and give them no entertainment in your thoughts, but cast them back into the tempter's face. There is not one melancholy person of a multitude who is not violently assaulted with temptations to blasphemy and unbelief, when they have but half the use of reason and no calmness of mind to debate such controversies with the devil. It is not fit for them in this incapacity to listen to any of those suggestions, which draw them to dispute the foundations of their faith, but to cast them away with resolute abhorrence; nor should any Christian, who is soundly settled on the true foundation, gratify the devil so much as to dispute with him whenever he provokes us to it, but only try to strengthen our faith, and destroy the remnants of unbelief.

**Direction 35.** Remember that Christ does propagate his religion jointly by his Spirit and his word, and effects himself the faith which he commands. For though there is sufficient evidence of credibility in his word, yet the blinded mind, and corrupt, perverted hearts of men, do need the cure of his medicinal grace, before they will effectually and savingly believe a doctrine which is so holy, high, and heavenly, and does so much control their lusts. See therefore that you distrust your corrupted hearts, and earnestly beg the Spirit of Christ.

**Direction 36.** Labor earnestly for the love of every truth which you believe, and to feel the renewing power of it upon your hearts, and the reforming power on your lives; especially that you may be advanced to the love of God and to a heavenly mind and life. And this will be a most excellent help against all temptations to unbelief; for the heart holds the gospel much faster than the head alone. The seed that is cast into the earth, if it quickens and takes root, is best preserved; and the deeper rooted the surer it abides; but if it dies, it perishes and is gone. When the seed of the holy word has produced the new creature, it is sure and safe; but when it is retained only in the brain as a dead opinion, every temptation can overturn it. It is an excellent advantage that the serious practical Christian has, above all hypocrites and unsanctified men: love will hold faster than dead belief. Love is the grace that abides forever; and that is the enduring faith which works by love. The experienced Christian has felt so much of the power and goodness of the word, that if you puzzle his head with subtle reasonings against it, yet his heart and experience will not allow him to let it go. He has tasted it so sweet that he will not believe it to be bitter, though he cannot answer all that is said against it. If another would persuade you to believe ill of your dearest friend or father, love and experience would better preserve you from his deceit than reasoning would do. The new creature or new nature in believers, and the experience of God's love communicated by Jesus Christ to their souls, are constant witnesses to the word of God: he that believes has the witness in himself; that is, the Holy Spirit which was given him, which is an objective testimony or an evidence, and an effective. Of this see my *Treatise of Infidelity.* Unsanctified men may be more easily turned to unbelief; for they never felt the renewed, quickening work of faith; nor were ever brought by it to the love of God, and a holy and heavenly mind and life. They that never were Christians at the heart, are soonest turned from being Christians in opinion and name.

*Question.* By what reason, evidence, or obligation, were the Jews bound to believe the prophets? Since Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, etc. performed no miracles, and there were false prophets in their days; how then could any man know that indeed they were sent by God, when they nakedly affirmed it?

*Answer.* I mention this objection or case, because in my book of the *Reasons of the Christian Religion* (to which for all the rest I refer the reader) it is forgotten; and because it is one of the hardest questions about our faith.

1. Those that think every book of Scripture now proves itself to be divine *propria luce*, by its own matter, style, and other properties, will accordingly say, that by hearing the prophets then, as well as by reading them now, this intrinsic, satisfactory evidence was discernible. All that I can say of this is, that there are such characters in the prophecies as are a help to faith, as making it the more easily credible that they are of God, but not such as I could have been ascertained by (especially as delivered by parcels then) if there had been no more.

2. Nor do I acquiesce in their answer who say that, Those that have the same spirit know the style of the spirit in the prophets. For, 1. This would suppose none capable of believing them on solid grounds that had not the same spirit; 2. And the spirit of sanctification is not enough to our discerning prophetical inspirations, as reason and experience fully prove. The gift of discerning spirits (1 Corinthians 12:10) was not common to all the sanctified.

3. It is much to be observed that God never sent any prophet to make a law or covenant on which the salvation of the people did depend, without the attestation of unquestionable miracles. Moses performed numerous open miracles, and such as controlled and confuted the opponents' seeming miracles in Egypt; and Christ and his apostles performed more than Moses. So that the laws and covenants by which God would rule and judge the people were all confirmed beyond all such exception.

4. It must be noted that many other prophets also performed miracles to confirm their doctrine, and prove that they were sent by God, as did Elijah and Elisha.

5. It must be noted, that there were schools of prophets, or societies of them, in those times (1 Samuel 10:10; 19:20; 1 Kings 20:35, 41; 22:13; 2 Kings 2:3, 5, 7, 15; 4:1, 38; 5:22; 6:1; 9:1; 1 Corinthians 14:32); who were educated in such a way as fitted them to the reception of prophetical inspirations, when it pleased God to give them. Not that mere education made any one a prophet, nor that the prophets had at all times the present, actual gift of prophecy; but God was pleased so far to acknowledge men's commanded diligence, as to join his blessing to a fitting education, and at such times as he thought fitting, to illuminate such by visions and revelations above all others: and therefore it is spoken of Amos as a thing extraordinary, that he was made a prophet of a herdsman.

6. Therefore a prophet among the Jews was known to be such, usually, before these recorded prophecies of theirs, which we have now in the holy Scriptures: 1. The spirits of the prophets which are subject to the prophets, were judged by those prophets that had indeed the Spirit, and so the people had the testimony of the other prophets concerning them. 2. The Lord's own direction to know a true prophet by (Deuteronomy 18:22) is the coming to pass of that which he foretells. Now it is likely that before they were received into the number of prophets, they had given satisfaction to the societies of the prophets, by the events of things before foretold by them. 3. Or they might have performed miracles before to have satisfied the members of the college of their calling, though these miracles are not all mentioned in the Scripture. 4. Or the other prophets might have some divine testimony concerning them, by visions, revelations, or inspirations of their own. So that the people were not left to the credulity of naked, unproved assertions, of any one that would say that he was sent by God.

7. There were some signs given by some of the prophets to confirm their word. As Isaiah's predictions of Hezekiah's danger, and remedy, and recovery, and of the going back of the shadow on Ahaz's sundial ten degrees, etc.; and more such there might be, which we do not know of.

8. All prophecies were not of equal obligation. The first prophecies of any prophet who brought no attestation by miracles, nor had yet spoken any prophecy that had been fulfilled, might be a merciful revelation from God, which might oblige the hearers to a reverent regard, and an inquiry into the authority of the prophet, and a waiting in suspense till they saw whether it would come to pass; and the fulfilling of it increases their obligation. Some prophecies that foretold but temporal things (captivities or deliverances) might at first (before the prophets produced a divine attestation) be rather a bare prediction than a law; and if men did not believe them, it might not make them guilty of any damning sin at all, but only they refused that warning of a temporal judgment, which might have been of use to them had they received it.

9. But our obligation now to believe the same Scripture prophecies is greater; because we live in the age when most of them are fulfilled, and the rest are attested by Christ and his apostles, who proved their attestations by manifold miracles.

10. When the prophets reproved the known sins of the people, and called men to such duties as the law required, no man could fare badly by obeying such a prophet, because the matter of his prophecies was found in God's own law, which must of necessity be obeyed. And this is the chief part of the recorded prophecies.

11. And any man that spoke against any part of God's law (of natural or supernatural revelation) was not to be believed (Deuteronomy 13; 18); because God cannot speak contrary to himself.

12. But the prophets themselves had another kind of obligation to believe their own visions and inspirations, than any of their hearers had; for God's great extraordinary revelation, was like the light, which immediately revealed itself, and constrained the understanding to know that it was of God: and such were the revelations that came by angelic apparitions and visions. Therefore prophets themselves might be bound to more than their bare word could have bound their hearers to; as, to wound themselves, to go bare, to feed on dung, etc.: and this was Abraham's case in offering Isaac. Yet God did never command a prophet, or any by a prophet, a thing simply evil, but only such things as were of a mutable nature, and which his will could alter, and make to be good. And such was the case of Abraham himself, if well considered.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Baxter gives only references here, with no quotation marks around any verse. The NET texts of the cited verses were fetched from labs.bible.org to check them. Where his wording is an unmarked quotation or allusion, we kept his words, modernized, and give the NET here.
- **Where the range starts and ends:** it begins at the opening of Direction 31 and breaks off inside the Answer (point 12). Baxter's printed "PART II." heading, at the very end of the range, opens the next section and is not rendered here. The bold on Direction 31 covers its first sentence, which we took to be Baxter's heading sentence; the source does not mark where the heading stops.
- **1 Corinthians 12:10 (point 2):** Baxter writes "the gift of discerning spirits." The NET reads "to another discernment of spirits." Same gift; his wording kept.
- **1 Corinthians 14:32 (point 6):** the unmarked quotation "the spirits of the prophets which are subject to the prophets." The NET reads "the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets." The same reference also appears in Baxter's list of verses in point 5, where that verse does not speak of a company of prophets; we kept his list as printed.
- **Point 5 list of references:** converted to modern form. We fetched the NET text of 1 Samuel 10:10, 19:20; 1 Kings 20:35, 41; 22:13; and 2 Kings 2:3, 15; 4:1, 38; 5:22; 6:1; 9:1. Most of them speak of a "company of prophets" or "members of the prophetic guild" (or of "the prophets" as a group). 1 Kings 22:13 has the prophets all agreeing before the king, and 1 Kings 20:41 names one man as "one of the prophets." We did not fetch 2 Kings 2:5, 7.
- **Deuteronomy 18:22 (point 6):** cited, not quoted. The NET says a prediction "not fulfilled" shows the Lord did not speak it. Baxter states the converse (a true prophet is known by the "coming to pass"). His wording kept.
- **Deuteronomy 13; 18 (point 11):** cited only.
- **he that believes has the witness in himself (Direction 36):** an unmarked allusion with no reference in Baxter's text. It resembles 1 John 5:10, which the NET renders "The one who believes in the Son of God has the testimony in himself." (The reference is ours, not Baxter's; we are fairly sure but have not confirmed it is the verse he means.) The NET says "testimony" where Baxter's King James has "witness," and Baxter plays on "witness" ("constant witnesses," "an objective testimony").
- **the enduring faith which works by love (Direction 36):** apparently an unmarked allusion to Galatians 5:6, which the NET renders "faith working through love"; and "the grace that abides forever" resembles 1 Corinthians 13 (the NET at 13:8: "Love never ends"). Neither has a reference in Baxter's text. Kept in his wording.
- **Amos "made a prophet of a herdsman" (point 5):** unmarked allusion, probably to Amos 7:14-15; we did not fetch it.
- **Other unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Moses and the Egyptian magicians (Exodus 7-8, probably); Isaiah's sign for Hezekiah and the shadow on Ahaz's sundial (Isaiah 38 and 2 Kings 20, probably); Abraham and Isaac (Genesis 22); prophets who wounded themselves, went "bare," or ate "dung" (probably 1 Kings 20:35; Isaiah 20:2; Ezekiel 4:12-15, none fetched).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): *apprehensions* to impressions (Direction 31; he means first, hasty ideas); *conjunctly* to jointly; *effecteth* to effects (his word order kept, "effects himself the faith," meaning he himself produces it); *counsellor* to counselor (spelling); *Christians* capitalized throughout; *to be still new questioning* to "to be forever newly questioning" (*still* meaning always); *composedness* to calmness; *hearken* to listen; *endeavour* to try; *easilier* to more easily; *infidelity* to unbelief (our standing rule for *infidel*); *wrought* to performed; *contradicters'* to opponents'; *meet* (fitting) to fitting; *to own* to acknowledge; *groundedly* to "on solid grounds"; *sent of God* to sent by God; *Elias* to Elijah (modern form of the name); *speed ill* to fare badly; *it is like that* to "it is likely that"; *angelical* to angelic; *&c.* to etc.; *dial* to sundial.
- **Words kept, with explanation:** *casting up* (adding up an account); *vacant* mind (empty of distractions); *abstracted* (drawn away); *delivered from* (freed from); *melancholy* (see GLOSSARY.md); *lusts* (desires); *control* (check, hold in); *quicken* (come alive); *objective testimony* (an evidence outside the person) and *effective* (one that produces an effect in the person) are Baxter's pair; *characters* (features, marks); *parcels* (in pieces, in portions); *acquiesce*; *exception* (objection); *apparitions* (appearances); *naked* (bare, without proof); *simply evil* (evil in itself); *the college of their calling* (the company of prophets). *Forgotten* (in the opening of the Answer) means left out.
- **Direction 31, "must lay altogether to discern the truth":** the syntax is odd, probably "must lay all together." Kept as printed.
- **Direction 33:** "if you doubted, yet you might be sure that is the safest way" is kept as written.
- **Direction 36:** his book titles are in italics: *Treatise of Infidelity* (we use his title unchanged) and *Reasons of the Christian Religion*. We have not checked which of his works these are; we have not looked up the cross-references.
- **Latin:** *propria luce* (point 1). Draft English: "by its own light." The translation is a draft prepared for this project and needs a Latinist's check.
- **Point 8, "All prophecies were not of equal obligation":** he means not all were equally binding, as the rest of the point shows.
- **Point 6, numbering:** inside this point Baxter numbers 1-4 again, as printed; we kept it.
- **Punctuation:** the references in points 2, 5, 6 and 11 were put in parentheses in place of Baxter's commas.
- **Polemic:** none against Rome or the sects in this range.
