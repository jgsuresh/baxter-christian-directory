# Chapter 3: Grand Direction VII (part 1 of 2)

**Grand Direction 7. Continue as the covenanted scholars of Christ, the Prophet and Teacher of his church, to learn of him by his Spirit, word, and ministers, the further knowledge of God, and the things that tend to your salvation; and this with an honest, willing mind, in faith, humility, and diligence; in obedience, patience, and peace.**

Though I spoke before of our coming to God by Jesus Christ, as he is the way to the Father, it is fitting that we distinctly speak of our relation and duty to him, as he is our Teacher, our Captain, and our Master, as well as of our making use of him as Mediator directly to God. The necessity of believers, and the office and work of Christ himself, tell us how much of our religion consists in learning of him as his disciples. Acts 7:37, "God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your brothers;" him shall you hear. This was the voice that came out of the cloud on the holy mountain, "This is my one dear Son, in whom I take great delight. Listen to him!" (Matthew 17:5). Therefore the title of disciples is commonly given to believers. And there is a twofold teaching which Christ has sent his ministers to perform; both are mentioned in their commission, Matthew 28:19, 20. The one is to "teach the nations;" that is, to make disciples of them, by persuading them into the school of Christ, which contains the teaching of faith and repentance, and whatever is necessary to their first admission, and to their subjecting themselves to Christ himself as their settled and infallible Guide. The other is teaching them further to know more of God, "and to obey everything I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:20). And this last is what we are now to speak of, and I will add some sub-directions for your help.

**Directions for Learning of Christ, as our Teacher.**

*In the margin: How to learn of Christ.*

**Direction 1. Remember who it is that is your Teacher: that he is the Son of God, who knows his Father's will, and is the most faithful, infallible Pastor of the church.** There is neither ignorance, nor negligence, nor ambition, nor deceit in him, to cause him to conceal the mind of God. There is nothing which we need to know, which he is not both able and willing to acquaint us with.

**Direction 2. Remember what it is that he teaches you, and to what end.** That it is not how to sin and be damned, as the devil, the world, and the flesh would teach you; nor how to satisfy your lusts, or to know, or do, or attain the trifles of the world: but it is how to be renewed to the image of God, and how to do his will and please him, and how to be justified at his bar, and how to escape everlasting fire, and how to attain everlasting joys: consider this well, and you will gladly learn of such a Teacher.

**Direction 3. Let the book which he himself has written by his Spirit be the rule and principal matter of your learning.** The holy Scriptures are of divine inspiration: it is them that we must be judged by, and them that we must be ruled by, and therefore them that we must principally learn. Men's books and teachings are but the means for our learning this infallible word.

**Direction 4. Remember that as it is Christ's work to teach, it is yours to hear, and read, and study, and pray, and practice what you hear.** Do your part, then, if you expect the benefit. You do not come to the school of Christ to be idle. Knowledge does not drop into the sleepy dreamer's mouth. Dig for it as for silver, and search for it in the Scriptures as for a hidden treasure: meditate in them day and night. Leave it to miserable fools to despise the wisdom of the Most High.

**Direction 5. Fix your eye upon himself as your pattern, and study with earnest desire to follow his holy example, and to be made conformable to him.** Not to imitate him in the works which were proper to him as God, or as Mediator; but in his holiness, which he has proposed to his disciples for their imitation. He knew how effective a perfect example would be, where a perfect doctrine alone would be less regarded. Example brings doctrine nearer to our eye and heart; it makes it more noticeable, and tells us with more powerful application, Such you must be, and thus you must do. The eye makes an easier and deeper impression on the imagination and mind than the ear does; therefore Christ's example should be much preached and studied. It will be a very great help to us to have always on our minds the image of the holy life of Christ; that we be affected, as if we always saw him doing the holy actions which he once did. Paul calls the Galatians "foolish," and "bewitched," that "did not obey the truth, when Christ had been set forth as crucified among them evidently before their eyes" (Galatians 3:1). Papists think that images serve well for this purpose: but the records of Scripture, and the living images of Christ whom they persecute and kill, are far more useful. How much more effective example is than doctrine alone, you may perceive by the enemies of Christ, who can bear his holy doctrine, when they cannot bear his holy servants, who practice that doctrine before their eyes. And that which most stirs up their enmity has the advantage for exciting the believer's piety.

Let the image of Christ, in all his holy examples, be always vividly written upon your minds. 1. Let the great ones of the world remember that their Lord was not born of those who ruled, or were in worldly pomp and dignity, but of persons who lived but meanly in the world (though they were of the royal line); how he was not born in a palace, but a stable, and laid in a manger, without the attendants or comforts of the rich.

2. Remember how he subjected himself to his supposed father, and his mother, to teach all children subjection and obedience (Luke 2:51).

3. And how he condescended to labor at a trade, and humble employment in the world; to teach us that our bodies, as well as our minds, must express their obedience, and have their ordinary employment; and to teach men to labor and live in a calling; and to comfort poor laborers, with assurance that God accepts them in the humblest work, and that Christ himself lived so before them, and chose their kind of life, and not the life of princes and nobles, who live in pomp, and ease, and pleasure.

4. Remember how he did not refuse to submit to all the ordinances of God, and to fulfill all righteousness, and to be initiated into the solemn administration of his office by the baptism of John (Matthew 3:15-17), which God approved by sending down upon him the Holy Spirit: to teach us all to expect his Spirit in the use of his ordinances.

5. Remember how he voluntarily began his work with an encounter with the tempter in the wilderness, after his fasting; and allowed the tempter to proceed, until he moved him to the most odious sin, even to worship the devil himself: to teach us that God loves tried servants, and expects that we not be turned from him by temptations; especially those who enter upon a public ministry must be tried men, who have overcome the tempter: and to comfort tempted Christians, who may remember that their Savior himself was most blasphemously tempted to as odious sins as ever they were; and that to be greatly tempted, without consenting or yielding to the sin, is so far from being a sin in itself that it is the greatest honor of our obedience; and that the devil, who troubles and haunts us with his temptations, is a conquered enemy, whom our Lord in person has overcome.

6. Remember how earnestly and constantly he preached; not stories, or jingles, or subtle controversies, but repentance, and faith, and self-denial, and obedience. So great was his love to souls that, when he had hearers, he preached, not only in the temple and synagogues, but on mountains, and in a ship, and any other convenient place; and no fury of the rulers or Pharisees could silence him, until his hour was come, having his Father's commission. And even to particular persons, he was willing, by conversation, to open the mysteries of salvation (John 3 and 4); to teach us to love and attend to the plain and powerful preaching of the gospel, and not to hold back any necessary means for the honor of God, and the saving of souls, because of the enmity or opposition of malicious men, but to work while it is day, seeing "Night is coming when no one can work" (John 9:4).

7. Remember how compassionate he was to men's bodies, as well as to their souls; going up and down with tireless diligence, doing good; healing the blind, and lame, and deaf, and sick, and possessed: and how all his miracles were done in charity, to do good; and none of them to do harm; so that he was only living, walking LOVE and MERCY. To teach us to know God in his love and mercy; and to abound in love and mercy to our brothers; and to hate the spirit of harmfulness, persecution, and uncharitableness; and to spend ourselves in doing good; and to exercise our compassion to the bodies of men, as well as to their souls, according to our power.

8. Remember how his zeal and love endured the reproach, and resisted the opposition of his friends, who went to lay hold on him as if he had been beside himself (Mark 3:20, 21): and how he told Peter, "Get behind me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to me because you are not setting your mind on God's interests, but on man's," when in carnal love and wisdom he rebuked him for resolving to lay down his life, saying, "God forbid, Lord! This must not happen to you!" (Matthew 16:22, 23). To teach us to expect that carnal love and wisdom in our nearest friends will rise up against us in the work of God, to discourage us both from duty and from sufferings; and that all are to be shaken off, and counted as the instruments of Satan, who would tempt us to be unfaithful to our trust and duty, and to favor ourselves by a sinful avoiding of the sufferings which God calls us to undergo.

9. Remember how through all his life he despised the riches of the world, and chose a life of poverty, and was a companion of the humblest, neither possessing nor seeking lavish houses, or great attendants, or spacious lands, or a large estate. He lived in a visible contempt of all the wealth, and splendor, and greatness of the world: to teach us how little these little things are to be valued; and that they are none of the treasure and portion of a saint; and what a folly it is to be fond of such snares, and diversions, and temptations, which make the way to heaven to be to us as a needle's eye.

10. Observe how little he regards the honor and applause of men; Philippians 2:7-9, how he "emptied himself by taking on the form of a slave," refusing to be made king, or to have a "kingdom of this world" (John 6:15). Though he told malignant blasphemers how greatly they sinned in dishonoring him, yet he did not seek the honor of the world: to teach us how little the thoughts or words of ignorant men contribute to our happiness, or are to be counted important; and to turn our eyes from the irrelevant censures of flesh and blood, to the judgment of our Almighty Sovereign, to whom it is that we stand or fall.

11. Remember also how little provision he made for the flesh, and never once tasted any immoderate, sinful pleasure. How far was he from a life of voluptuousness and indulgence in physical pleasure! Though his avoiding the formal fastings of the Pharisees made them slander him as "a glutton and a drunk" (Matthew 11:19), as the sober Christians were called _carnivori_ by those who thought it unlawful to eat flesh; yet so far was he from the guilt of any such sin that never a desire for it was in his heart. You will never find in the gospel that Christ spent half the morning dressing, choosing rather to shorten his time for prayer than not to appear sufficiently spruced up, as our empty, worthless, painted gallants do: nor will you ever read that he wasted his time in idle social visits, or cards, or dice, or in reading romances, or hearing stage-plays: it was another kind of example that our Lord left for his disciples.

12. Notice also how far Christ was from being guilty of any idle, or lascivious, or foolish kind of talk; and how holy and profitable all his speeches were: to teach us also to speak as the oracles of God, such words as tend to edification, and to administer grace to the hearers, and to keep our tongues from all profane, lascivious, empty, idle speeches.

13. Remember that pride and passion are condemned by your pattern. Christ tells you, "Learn from me because I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls" (Matthew 11:28, 29). Therefore he declares that "unless you turn around and become like little children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 18:3). Behold therefore the Lamb of God, and be ashamed of your fierce and ravenous natures.

14. Remember that Christ your Lord and pattern humbled himself to the humblest office of love, even to wash the feet of his disciples: not to teach you to wash a few poor men's feet, as a ceremony once a year, and persecute and murder the servants of Christ the rest of the year, as the Roman Vice-Christ does; but to teach us that if he, their Lord and Master, washed his disciples' feet, we also should stoop as low in any office of love for one another (John 13:14).

15. Remember also that Christ your pattern spent whole nights in prayer to God; [97] so much was he for this holy attendance upon God: to teach us to "always pray and not lose heart" (Luke 18:1). And not to be like the impious God-haters, who do not love any near or serious addresses to God, nor those who use them, but make them the object of their cruelty or scorn.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows each Greek or Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a specialist check them before relying on fine points.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Heading.** The source heading is "_Grand Direct._ VII." This file begins at the top of Grand Direction 7 and ends partway through it, after Direction 5's fifteenth example; the next file continues. Baxter's "_Direct._ I." to "V." are rendered Direction 1 to 5; the fifteen numbered examples under Direction 5 stay numbered as Baxter has them.
- **Acts 7:37 (flag).** Baxter quotes the King James in one run: "A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me, him shall you hear." The NET has only "God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your brothers" (Moses speaking, in Stephen's speech) and lacks "him shall you hear." We quoted the NET for the first part and kept Baxter's last clause in his own words, modernized, outside the quotation marks. Baxter leans on that clause for his point that we are to hear Christ.
- **Matthew 17:5.** NET wording: "my one dear Son, in whom I take great delight. Listen to him!" The King James has "my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased, hear ye him." Sense unchanged.
- **Matthew 28:19-20 (flag).** Baxter's first quotation, "teach the nations," is the King James ("teach all nations"). The NET reads "make disciples of all nations," and the teaching comes only in verse 20 ("teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you"). Baxter's two-fold distinction (first making disciples, then teaching) fits the NET well, but the quoted words "teach the nations" are not in it; we kept his phrase, in quotation marks, and note it. For the second quotation we used the NET and put his connecting "and" outside it.
- **Galatians 3:1 (flag).** Baxter's quotation is the King James, a blend: "foolish," "bewitched," "obeyed not the truth," "set forth as crucified among them evidently before their eyes." The NET reads: "You foolish Galatians! Who has cast a spell on you? Before your eyes Jesus Christ was vividly portrayed as crucified!" The NET has no "obeyed not the truth" (that clause is in the King James but not in the NET text), so Baxter's remark that they "did not obey the truth" has no NET counterpart. We kept his wording, modernized, and give the NET here.
- **Matthew 3:15-17; Mark 3:20-21; Luke 2:51; John 3 and 4; John 13:14** (cited, not quoted; Baxter's own summaries).
- **John 9:4 (partly his paraphrase).** Baxter writes, "work while it is day, seeing the night is coming when none can work." The NET is "We must perform the deeds of the one who sent me as long as it is daytime. Night is coming when no one can work." We kept "work while it is day" as his words and quoted the NET only for the second sentence.
- **Matthew 16:22-23 (flag).** Baxter's "thou art an offence unto me" becomes the NET's "a stumbling block to me"; the sense is close. His "thou savourest not the things of God, but those of men" is the NET's "you are not setting your mind on God's interests, but on man's." Peter's words: King James "Be it far from thee, Lord: this shall not be unto thee," NET "God forbid, Lord! This must not happen to you!" Baxter's "Lord" is placed within the quotation, as in the NET.
- **Philippians 2:7-9 (flag, important).** Baxter writes that Christ "made himself of no reputation, but took upon him the form of a servant," and builds on "reputation" for his point that Christ did not care about the honor and applause of men. The NET reads "emptied himself by taking on the form of a slave." The word "reputation" is gone, so the NET does not itself make Baxter's point; the point must rest on the surrounding verses (the humbling, the obedience to death) and on his other texts. Also "servant" becomes the NET's "slave."
- **John 6:15; "kingdom of this world" (flag, reference).** Baxter gives John 6:15 for both phrases. The NET of John 6:15 reads "Jesus ... knew they were going to come and seize him by force to make him king," so "made a king" is there, but the words "kingdom of this world" are not. They are close to John 18:36, which the NET has as "My kingdom is not from this world." This is probably what Baxter had in mind (we have not confirmed it). We kept his reference and his words, quoting only the second, in quotation marks, and flag it. We did not change his reference.
- **Matthew 11:19.** NET "a glutton and a drunk" for the King James "a gluttonous man, and a winebibber." Baxter's "gluttonous person" and "wine-bibber" are given as "a glutton and a drunk."
- **Matthew 11:28-29.** Baxter cites verses 28 and 29 but quotes only the second half of 29, with a King James "Learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart." NET "gentle and humble in heart." Baxter's "you shall find rest unto your souls" is "you will find rest for your souls."
- **Matthew 18:3 (flag).** Baxter has "except men be converted and become as little children." The NET reads "unless you turn around and become like little children." The King James "be converted" is Baxter's own technical word for conversion; the NET's "turn around" is the same Greek idea but loses the church word. Baxter's "men" is not in the NET (it is "you"); we used the NET's "you." This matters if the passage is used to argue for conversion as a whole change of life, as elsewhere in the *Directory*.
- **Luke 18:1.** King James "pray always and not wax faint" becomes the NET's "always pray and not lose heart." The NET sets this as the evangelist's own introduction to the parable ("Jesus told them a parable to show them they should always pray and not lose heart"); we used its wording.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "Dig for it as for silver, and search for it ... as for a hidden treasure" (compare Proverbs 2:4); "meditate in them day and night" (compare Psalm 1:2); "Behold the Lamb of God" (compare John 1:29); "to speak as the oracles of God" (compare 1 Peter 4:11); "a needle's eye" (compare Matthew 19:24); "that we stand or fall" (compare Romans 14:4); "to administer grace unto the hearers" (compare Ephesians 4:29); "working while it is day" is noted above.
- **Mark 3:21.** Baxter's "beside himself" is kept; the NET reads "He is out of his mind."
- **Pronoun in example 8.** In "when in carnal love and wisdom he rebuked him," the first "he" is Peter; we kept Baxter's pronouns.
- **John 6:15, "made a king."** Baxter puts "made a king" in quotation marks; the NET has "to make him king," and we give it without quotation marks as his summary.
- **Misprint.** The source has "doing, good" (a stray comma) in example 7; we print "doing good."
- **Words changed (Baxter's word, then ours):** "indited" to "written"; "meet" to "fitting"; "improving him" to "making use of him"; "immediately" (to God) to "directly"; "contemn" to "despise"; "notice also" for "mark also"; "neatified" to "spruced up"; "impertinent" to "irrelevant"; "vouchsafed" to "was willing"; "conference" to "conversation" (here meaning talk); "as to make disciples" to "that is, to make disciples"; "stated" (Guide) to "settled"; "maketh it more observable" to "makes it more noticeable"; "serve well for this turn" to "for this purpose"; "mean" (low) to "humble" / "humblest"; "auditors" to "hearers"; "forbear" to "hold back"; "sumptuous" to "lavish"; "still" (always) to "always"; "upon his fasting" to "after his fasting"; "bore rule" to "ruled"; "resolveth" to "declares"; "visitations" to "social visits"; "attendance(s)" to "attendants" and "attendance upon God" kept as "attendance"; "accommodations" to "comforts"; "unwearied" to "tireless"; "hurtfulness" to "harmfulness"; "bid" to "told"; "reputed father" to "supposed father"; "suffered the tempter to proceed" to "allowed."
- **Terms kept:** "papists" (his word for Roman Catholics); "carnal" ("carnal love and wisdom," "carnal" meaning governed by the flesh, in Peter's case by merely human affection); "gallants" (fashionable young men); "romances" (probably idle fictional tales; we have not confirmed what he has in mind); "stage-plays" (theater); "tried" (tested by trial).
- **_carnivori_:** Latin, "flesh-eaters." Baxter says sober Christians were so called by those who thought eating meat unlawful. We have not identified which group he has in mind and make no claim about it.
- **Polemic.** "Papists think that images serve well for this turn," and "the Roman Vice-Christ" (the pope, whom Baxter calls a false substitute for Christ) are Baxter's own polemic, kept as written. His claim that the Roman Church "persecutes and kills" the living images of Christ, and that the pope persecutes Christ's servants after washing a few poor men's feet once a year (probably the Maundy ceremony; we have not confirmed his reference), are kept as his.
- **Direction 5, paragraph on the eye and ear.** Baxter's general claim that the eye makes a deeper impression than the ear is his own; no source is given.
- **"Sub-directions."** The "twofold teaching" and the "sub-directions" are Baxter's own scheme; the Directions that follow are the sub-directions.
- **Sidenote.** The one margin note, "How to learn of Christ," stands above Direction 1.
- **Footnote [97]** is attached in the source to "spent whole nights in prayer to God" and refers to Luke 6:12 (NET: "he spent all night in prayer to God"; the note is only Baxter's remark about the Greek word). Baxter's "importeth that he continued for prayer in it" is kept as "imports that he continued for prayer in it".
- **"Holy Spirit":** Baxter's "Holy Ghost" is modernized to "Holy Spirit," per the glossary.
- **Not checked:** Baxter's "I spoke before of our coming to God by Jesus Christ" refers to an earlier Grand Direction; we have not confirmed which.
