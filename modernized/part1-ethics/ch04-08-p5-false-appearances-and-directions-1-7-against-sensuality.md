# Chapter 4: False appearances of sensuality, and Directions 1 to 7 against it

*This range begins partway through the section's opening discussion, at the numbered item VII ("False appearances of sensuality"), which continues the series of items in the previous file; no section heading is repeated here. It then gives Directions 1 to 7.*

*In the margin: False appearances of sensuality.*

VII. Yet many are wrongfully taken by others (if not by themselves) to be physical and pleasure-loving, by such mistakes as these: 1. Because they do not live as meanly and scantily as the poor, who lack things necessary or helpful to their duty. But by that rule I must not be well, because other men are sick; or I must not go fast, because the lame can go but slowly! If poor men have bad horses, I may ride on the best I can get, to dispatch my business, and redeem my time, provided I prefer not costly, useless ostentation, before true usefulness. 2. Others are accused as pleasure-loving, because the weakness of their bodies requires a more tender treatment, and diet, than healthy men's: some bodies are less fit for duty if they fast; and some are useless through sickness and infirmities, if they are not treated with very great care. And it is as truly a duty to cherish a weak body to enable it for God's service, as to tame an unruly, lustful body, and keep it from offending him. 3. Some melancholy, conscientious persons are still accusing themselves, through mere scrupulosity; questioning almost all they eat, or drink, or wear, or do, whether it is not too much or too pleasing. But it is a cheerful sobriety that God requires, which neither pampers the body, nor yet disables or hinders it from its duty; and not an unprofitable, wrangling scrupulosity.

**Direction 1. The first and grand direction against flesh-pleasing is, that you be sure, by a serious, living faith, to see the better things with God, and to be heartily taken up in heeding, loving, seeking, and securing them.** All the other directions are but subservient to this. For certainly man's soul will not be idle, being a living, active principle: and it is as certain, that it will not act except upon some end, or for some end. And there are no other ends to take us up, but either the things temporal or eternal. And therefore there is no true cure for a physical love of temporal things, but to turn the heart to things eternal. Believingly think first of the certainty, greatness, and eternity of the joys above; and then think that these may more certainly be yours, than any worldly riches or delights, if you do not contemptuously reject them. And then think that this is the time in which you must make sure of them, and win them, if ever you will possess them; and that you are sent into the world on purpose on this business. And then think with yourselves, how fleshly pleasures are the only rivals of the everlasting pleasures; and that, if ever you lose them, it will be by over-loving these transitory things; and that one half of your work for your salvation lies in killing your affections to all below, that they may be alive to God alone. And lastly, think how much higher and sweeter pleasures, even in this life, the godly enjoy than you; and you are losing them while you prefer these sordid pleasures. Do you think that a true believer does not have a more excellent delight in his forethoughts of his immortal blessedness with Christ, and in the assurance of the love of God, and communion with him in his holy service, than you, or any pleasure-seeker, has in fleshly pleasures? Sober and serious meditation on these things will turn the mind to the true delights.

**Direction 2. Be acquainted with the range of physical desires, and follow them, and watch them in all their extravagances.** Otherwise, while you are stopping one gap, they will be running out at many more. I have given you many instances in my *Treatise of Self-denial*. I will here briefly set some before your eyes.

1. Watch your appetites as to food and drink, both quantity and quality. Gluttony is a common, unobserved sin: the flesh no way enslaves men more than by the appetite; as we see in drunkards and gluttons, that can no more refrain than one that thirsts in a burning fever.

2. Take heed of the lust of uncleanness, and all degrees of it, and approaches to it; especially immodest embraces and behavior.

3. Take heed of ribald, filthy talk, and love songs, and of such inflaming snares.

4. Take heed of too much sleep and idleness.

5. Take heed of taking too much delight in your riches, and lands, your buildings, and delightful conveniences.

6. Take heed lest honors, or worldly greatness, or men's applause, become your too great pleasure.

7. And lest you grow to make it your delight, to think on such things when you are alone, or talk idly of them in company with others.

8. And take heed lest the success and prosperity of your affairs please you too much, as him, Luke 12:20.

9. Take not up any inordinate pleasure in your children, relations, or nearest friends.

10. Take heed of a delight in worthless, unprofitable, sinful company.

11. Or in fineness of apparel, to set you out to the eyes of others.

12. Take heed of a delight in romances, play-books, feigned stories, useless news, which corrupt the mind, and waste your time.

13. Take heed of a delight in any recreations which are excessive, needless, devouring time, unsettling the mind, enticing to further sin, hindering any duty, especially our delight in God. They are miserable souls that can delight themselves in no more safe or profitable things, than cards, and dice, and stage-plays, and immodest dancings.

**Direction 3. Next to the universal remedy mentioned in the first direction, see that you have the particular remedies still at hand, which your own particular way of flesh-pleasing most requires.** And do not let the love of your worthless pleasures prejudice you against a just information, but impartially consider the disease and the remedy. Of the particulars soon.

**Direction 4. Remember still that God would give you more pleasure, and not less; and that he will give you as much of the delights of sense as is truly good for you, if you will take them in their place, in subordination to your heavenly delights.** And is not this to increase and multiply your pleasure? Are not health, and friends, and food, and convenient habitation, much sweeter as the fruit of the love of God, and the foretastes of everlasting mercies, and as our helps to heaven, and as the means to spiritual comfort, than of themselves alone? All your mercies are from God: he would take none from you, but sanctify them, and give you more.

**Direction 5. See that reason keep up its authority, as the governor of sense and appetite.** And so take an account, whatever the appetite would have, of the ends and reasons of the thing, and to what it leads. Take nothing and do nothing merely because the sense or appetite would have it; but because you have reason so to do, and to gratify the appetite. Else you will deal as brutes, if reason is laid by (in human acts).

**Direction 6. Go to the grave, and see there the end of fleshly pleasure, and what is all that it will do for you at the last.** One would think it should cure the mad desire of plenty and pleasure, to see where all our wealth, and mirth, and amusement, and pleasure must be buried at last.

**Direction 7. Lastly, be still sensible that flesh is the grand enemy of your souls, and flesh-pleasing the greatest hindrance of your salvation.** The devil's enmity and the world's are both but subordinate to this of the flesh: for its pleasure is the end, and the world's and Satan's temptations are both but the means to attain it. Besides the malignity opened before, consider,

*In the margin: The enmity of the flesh.*

1. How contrary a voluptuous life is to the blessed example of our Lord, and of his servant Paul, and all the apostles! Paul tamed his body and brought it into subjection, lest, having preached to others, himself should be a castaway, 1 Corinthians 9:27. And all that are Christ's have crucified the flesh, with the affections and lusts thereof, Galatians 5:24. This was signified in the ancient manner of baptizing, (and so is still by baptism itself,) when they went over head in the water and then rose out of it, to signify that they were dead and buried with Christ, Romans 6:3-4, and rose with him to newness of life. This is called our being "baptized into his death" (Romans 6:3); and seems the plain sense of 1 Corinthians 15:29, of being "baptized for the dead" (1 Corinthians 15:29); that is, "for dead" to show that we are dead to the world, and must die in the world, but shall rise again to the kingdom of Christ, both of grace and glory.

2. Physical indulgence shows that there is no true belief of the life to come, and proves, so far as it is powerful, the absence of all grace.

3. It is a homebred, continual traitor to the soul; a continual tempter, and nurse of all sin; the great withdrawer of the heart from God; and the common cause of apostasy itself: it still fights against the Spirit, Galatians 5:17; and is seeking advantage from all our liberties, Galatians 5:13; 2 Peter 2:10.

4. It turns all our outward mercies into sin, and strengthens itself against God by his own benefits.

5. It is the great cause of our afflictions; for God will not spare that idol which is set up against him: flesh rebels, and flesh shall suffer.

6. And when it has brought affliction, it is most impatient under it, and makes it seem intolerable. A flesh-pleaser thinks he is undone, when affliction deprives him of his pleasure.

7. Lastly, it exceedingly unfits men for death; for then flesh must be cast into the dust, and all its pleasure be at an end. Oh doleful day to those that had their good things here, and their portion in this life! when all is gone that ever they valued and sought; and all the true happiness lost, which they brutishly scorned! If you would joyfully then bear the dissolution and ruin of your flesh, oh master it, and mortify it now. Seek not the ease and pleasure of a little walking, breathing clay, when you should be seeking and foretasting the everlasting pleasure. Here lies your danger and your work. Strive more against your own flesh, than against all your enemies in earth and hell: if you are saved from this, you are saved from them all. Christ suffered in the flesh, to tell you that it is not pampering, but suffering, that your flesh must expect, if you will reign with him.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Quoted phrases were fetched from the NET (labs.bible.org).
- **Start of range:** the file begins at item "VII." of an earlier numbered series (the first two items are printed here with the "1." to "3." sub-points). The hint heading ("Object. IV.") is from before the range and is not repeated. Baxter's "Direct. I.-VII." are rendered "Direction 1-7"; the first sentence of each is bold with its label.
- **Romans 6:3:** the NET has "baptized into his death," the same words Baxter quotes. Baxter's reference "Rom. vi. 3, 4" is given as "Romans 6:3-4" and Baxter's own quoted phrase is cited at 6:3. The parenthetical citations after the two quotations were added to follow the convention of citing quoted Scripture; they repeat references Baxter gave in the same sentence.
- **1 Corinthians 15:29:** the NET has "baptized for the dead," matching Baxter's quoted words. Baxter then gives his own reading, "for dead" (that is, as those who are dead to the world); the NET does not say this. His reading is his own and is kept as written.
- **1 Corinthians 9:27:** Baxter's "tamed his body and brought it into subjection, lest, having preached to others, himself should be a castaway" is not in quotation marks and follows the King James wording ("keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest ... I myself should be a castaway"). The NET reads "I subdue my body and make it my slave, so that after preaching to others I myself will not be disqualified." Baxter's "castaway" (reprobate, rejected) carries a stronger sense of final rejection than the NET's "disqualified." His wording is kept as his paraphrase.
- **Galatians 5:24:** Baxter's "crucified the flesh, with the affections and lusts thereof" is the King James wording, not in quotation marks. The NET has "crucified the flesh with its passions and desires." "Affections" is a technical term for Baxter (emotions and desires), so it is kept.
- **Luke 12:20:** "as him" refers to the rich man in Luke 12:16-20, whom Baxter mentions by reference only. Baxter's "Luke xii. 20" is given as "Luke 12:20" and the reference is a reminder of the man's confidence in his prosperity; the NET verse has God saying "You fool!"
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "their good things here, and their portion in this life" (compare Luke 16:25); the references Galatians 5:17, 5:13 and 2 Peter 2:10 are Baxter's own and are not quoted.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): sensual to physical or pleasure-loving; sensualist to pleasure-seeker; "minding" to heeding; competitors to rivals; meat to food; vain to worthless; sports to amusement; incensing to inflaming; discomposing to unsettling; conduce to lead; hinderance to hindrance; despatch to dispatch; contemned to scorned; "apace" to fast; "so I prefer" to "provided I prefer"; "serviceableness" to usefulness; "usage" to treatment; honours to honors; "of purpose" to "on purpose"; "anon" to "soon." "Physical indulgence" stands for Baxter's "sensuality" in Point 2 of Direction 7.
- **Terms kept:** *flesh-pleasing*, *flesh-pleaser* (Baxter's compound for living to satisfy the body's desires); *melancholy* (see the glossary); *affections*; *mortify*; *delights of sense* and *sense and appetite* (the faculty of sensory perception and desire, set against reason in Direction 5); *scrupulosity* (excessive anxiety over small matters of conscience); *malignity* (the harmful, hostile nature of a thing); *voluptuous* (given to pleasure); *redeem my time* (his phrase for making good use of time); *relations* (relatives); *in human acts* (his parenthesis, a technical phrase for deliberate, reasoned choices); *ribald* (coarse, vulgar); *romances* (tales of chivalry and love); *play-books* (printed plays); *uncleanness* (sexual impurity).
- **"opened before"** (Direction 7): "set out earlier"; Baxter refers back to his earlier account of the evil of the sin, which is in a previous file.
- **Treatise of Self-denial:** Baxter's own book title, kept in italics.
- **Direction 2, item 8:** "as him, Luke 12:20" is Baxter's elliptical phrase, kept; it means "as the man did" in that verse.
- **Cross-references:** "Of the particulars anon" (Direction 3) points to later sections of the Directory that are not in this file; we have not located them.
- **Baxter's wording on baptism** (Direction 7, point 1): his remark that baptism was by immersion in the "ancient manner" is his own historical claim and is kept as his; we have not checked it.
