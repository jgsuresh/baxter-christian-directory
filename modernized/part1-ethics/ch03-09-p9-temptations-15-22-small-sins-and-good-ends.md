# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 9 of 13)

*This section begins with Direction 14 of Grand Direction 9 and runs through Direction 22; it breaks off at the start of Temptation 23, whose Direction is in the next file.*

**Direction 14.** If God so much regards us as to make us, and preserve us continually, and to become our Governor, and make a law for us, and judge us, and reward his servants with no less than heaven, then you may easily see that he so much regards us, as to observe whether we obey or break his laws. He who so far cares for a clock or watch, as to make it and wind it up, does care whether it runs true or false. What do these men make of God, who think he does not care what men do? Then he does not care if men beat you, or rob you, or kill you, for none of this hurts God. And the king may say, if any murder your friends, or children, why should I punish him? he did not hurt me. But justice is to keep order in the world, and not only to protect the governor from hurt. God may be wronged, though he is not hurt. And he will make you pay for it, if you hurt others; and smart for it, if you hurt yourself.

**Temptation 15.** The tempter works hard to play down the sin, and make it seem a little one; and if every little sin must be made such a matter of, you will never be quiet.

**Direction 15.** But still remember, 1. There is deadly poison in the very nature of sin, as there is in a serpent, be he never so small. The least sin is worse than the greatest pain that ever man felt; and would you choose it and say, it is little? The least sin is odious to God, and had a hand in the death of Christ, and will damn you if it is not pardoned; and should such a thing be made light of? And many sins counted small may have great aggravations, such as the knowing, deliberate, willful committing of them is. To love a small sin is a great sin; especially to love it so well, that the remembrance of God's will and love, of Christ, and heaven, and hell, will not be enough to resolve you against it. Besides, a small sin is the common way to greater: "when desire conceives, it gives birth to sin, and when sin is full grown, it gives birth to death" (James 1:15). "Think how small a flame sets a huge forest ablaze" (James 3:5). The horrid sins of David and Peter had small beginnings. Mortal sicknesses seem little matters at first. Many a thousand have sinned themselves to hell, who began with what is counted small.

**Temptation 16.** Also the devil draws on the sinner, by promising him that he shall sin but once, or but a very few times, and then do so no more: he tells the thief, and the fornicator, that if they will do it but this once, they shall be quiet.

**Direction 16.** But, O consider, 1. That one stab at the heart may prove incurable. God may deny you time or grace to repent. 2. That it is easier to hold back the first time than the second; for one sin inclines the heart to another. If you cannot refuse the first temptation, how will you refuse the next? When you have lost your strength, and grieved your helper, and strengthened your enemy and your snare, will you then resist better wounded, than now when you are whole?

**Temptation 17.** But when the devil has succeeded for once with the sinner, he makes that an argument for a second: he says to the thief, and drunkard, and fornicator, it is but the same thing that you have done once already; and if once may be pardoned, twice may be pardoned; and if twice, why not three times; and so on.

**Direction 17.** This is to let the devil get in a foot. A spark is easier put out than a flame; but yet remember that the longer the worse: the oftener you sin, the greater is the abuse of the Spirit of God, and the contempt of grace, and the wrong to Christ, and the harder is repentance; and the sharper if you do repent, because the deeper is your wound. Repent therefore quickly, and go no further, unless you would have the devil tell you next, It is now too late.

**Temptation 18.** The tempter makes use of the greater sins of others, to persuade men to venture upon lesser ones. You hear other men curse, and swear, and rail, and do you balk at idle talk? How many in the world are enemies to Christ, and persecute his ministers and servants, and do you make so great a matter of omitting a sermon, or a prayer, or other holy duty?

**Direction 18.** As there are degrees of sin, so there are degrees of punishment: and will you rather choose the easiest place in hell than heaven? However small the matter of sin may be, your willfulness, and sinning against conscience, and mercies, and warnings, may make it great to you. Are great sinners so happy in your eyes, that you would be as like them as you dare?

**Temptation 19.** Also he would embolden the sinner, because of the commonness of the sin, and the multitude that commit either that or worse, as if it were not, therefore, so bad or dangerous.

**Direction 19.** But remember, that the more examples you have to take warning by, the more inexcusable is your fall. It was not the number of angels that fell, that could keep them from being devils and damned for their sin: God will do justice on many as well as on one. The sin is the greater, and therefore the punishment shall not be the less. Make the case your own: will you think it a good reason for anyone to abuse you, beat you, rob you, because many have done so before? He should rather think, that you are abused too much already, and therefore he should not add to your wrongs. If when many had spit in Christ's face or struck him, some one should have given him another spit or blow, as if he had not had enough before, would you not have taken him to be the worst and cruelest of them all? If you do as the most, you will fare as the most.

**Temptation 20.** It is a dangerous temptation when the devil proposes some very good end, and makes sin seem the fittest, or the necessary means to accomplish it: when he blinds men so far as to think that it is necessary to their salvation, or to other men's, or to the welfare of the church, or progress of the gospel, or the pleasing of God, then sin will be committed without regret, and continued in without repentance; on this account it is that heresy, and will-worship, and superstition are kept up: Colossians 2:18, 21-23, "Even though they have the appearance of wisdom with their self-imposed worship and humility achieved by an unsparing treatment of the body" (Colossians 2:23). It is for God that much of the wickedness of the world is done against God: it is for the church and truth that papists have murdered and persecuted so many.

**Direction 20.** Remember that God needs no sinful means to attain his ends: he will not be beholden to the devil to do his work; he would not have forbidden it, if he would have had you do it. He is never at such a loss, but he can find right means enough to perform his work by: it is a great part of our wisdom, on which our salvation depends, to choose and use right means, when we are resolved on a right end. It is a horrible injury against God to attribute sin to him, and make it seem necessary to his ends and honor. Good ends will not justify evil actions. What sin is so odious that has not had good ends claimed for it? Even Christ was murdered as a criminal for good ends, at least claimed ones, even to defend God's honor from blasphemy, and Caesar from injury, and the nation from calamity. And his disciples were killed so that God might be served by it, and pestilent troublers of the world taken away (John 16:2; Acts 24:5; 17:6).

**Temptation 21.** He would make us presume because we are God's children, and special grace cannot be wholly lost, and we have found that once we had grace, therefore we may venture as being safe.

**Direction 21.** But many thousands shall be damned, who once thought they had the truth of grace. It is a hard controversy among learned and godly men, whether some in a state of saving grace do not fall from it and perish; but it is beyond controversy, that they shall perish who live and die impenitently in willful sin. To plead truth of grace for encouragement in sin, is so much against the nature and use of grace, as may make you question the truth of it. You can be no surer that you have true grace, than you are sure that you hate all known sin, and desire to be free from it. Christ teaches you how to answer such a horrid temptation, Matthew 4:6-7, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down. For it is written, 'He will command his angels concerning you' ..." -- "You are not to put the Lord your God to the test." Sonship, and promises, and truth of grace, are unfitting arguments to draw you to sin, and heinous aggravations of sin so committed.

**Temptation 22.** The devil often, most dangerously, imitates the Holy Spirit, and comes in the shape of an angel of light: he will be for knowledge in the gnostics; for unity and government in the papists; for mortification in the friars; for free grace and tenderness of our brethren's consciences in the libertines; for peace and mutual forbearance in the Socinians; for zeal, self-denial, and fearlessness of men, and claimed revelations and spirituality, in the Quakers. He will be against heresy, schism, error, disobedience, hypocrisy, in pretense, in haters and persecutors of holiness and reformation; and when he will seem religious, he will be superstitious, and seem to outgo Christ himself.

**Direction 22.** Keep close to Christ, that you may know his voice from the voice of strangers; and get holy wisdom to test the spirits, and to discern between the things that differ: let the whole frame of truth and godliness be in your head and heart, that you may perceive when anyone would make a breach in any part of it. The devil sets up no good but in order to some evil. Therefore, examine where it tends; and not only what it is, but what use he would have you make of it. And love no evil, because of any good that is claimed for it; and dislike or reject no good, because of any evil use that is made of it by others. And whatever doctrine is brought you, test it thus: 1. Receive none that is against the certain nature, attributes, and honor of God. 2. Nor any that is against the light or law of nature. 3. Nor any that is against the Scripture. 4. Nor any that is against holiness of heart and life. 5. Nor any against charity and justice to men. 6. Nor any (about matters to be ordered by men) that is against order; nor any against government and the peace of church and state. 7. Nor any that is against the true unity, peace, and communion of saints. 8. Nor any that is certainly inconsistent with great and certain truths. Thus test the spirits, whether they are of God.

**Temptation 23.** The tempter usually draws men to one extreme, under pretense of avoiding another; causing men to be so fearful of the danger on one side, as to take no heed of that on the other side.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Structure:** Baxter's "_Tempt._ XV." and so on are rendered **Temptation 15.** and so on. His numbering of Directions and Temptations continues from the earlier files of this Grand Direction. The source's last Temptation (23) is cut off at the end of this range; its Direction follows in the next file.
- **James 1:15 (Direction 15):** Baxter cites "James i. 14, 15," but the words he quotes are in verse 15 only, so the reference is given as James 1:15 (verse 14 leads into it). The King James has "lust ... finished"; the NET has "desire ... full grown." The NET's "desire" is milder than "lust," but the progression Baxter relies on, from small beginning to sin to death, is unchanged.
- **James 3:5 (Direction 15):** Baxter's King James words are "how great a matter a little fire kindleth." The NET reads "Think how small a flame sets a huge forest ablaze." The NET puts the contrast in "small flame" against "huge forest," which is the point Baxter wants (a small beginning, great result).
- **Colossians 2:18, 21-23 (Temptation 20):** Baxter's quoted words ("Having a show of wisdom in will-worship, and humility, and neglecting the body") are King James wording of verse 23. The NET reads "the appearance of wisdom with their self-imposed worship and humility achieved by an unsparing treatment of the body." **Flag:** Baxter leans on the King James word "will-worship" (worship of one's own devising) and lists it among the things kept up by good-sounding aims. The NET's "self-imposed worship" keeps the same idea, but the plain label "will-worship" is lost, so we keep Baxter's own term "will-worship" in his running sentence. Also, "neglecting the body" in the King James differs in tone from the NET's "unsparing treatment of the body." The reference "2:18, 21-23" is Baxter's own.
- **Matthew 4:6-7 (Direction 21):** The NET reads "throw yourself down. For it is written, 'He will command his angels concerning you'" and "You are not to put the Lord your God to the test." **Flag:** Baxter's King James has "Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God," and he sets it against the word "temptation" of this whole chapter. The NET's "put ... to the test" is the same sense, but the verbal link with "temptation" is not visible in English. We marked Baxter's skipped words with "..." and the dash he printed.
- **John 16:2; Acts 24:5; 17:6 (Direction 20):** Baxter gives these references only; the quoted idea is his paraphrase ("pestilent troublers of the world"), kept in his words. The King James of Acts 24:5 has "a pestilent fellow"; the NET has "a troublemaker." Acts 17:6 in the NET: "These people who have stirred up trouble throughout the world have come here too." We have not put NET wording in the text since he does not quote.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14); "the voice of strangers" (John 10:5); "try the spirits" (1 John 4:1; NET: "test the spirits to determine if they are from God"); "grieved your helper" (probably the Holy Spirit; compare Ephesians 4:30); "the devil ... get in a foot" (compare Ephesians 4:27, "do not give the devil a foothold"; the match is not certain).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): extenuate / play down; labour / works hard; wilful / willful; pretended / claimed; pretence / pretense; pretendedly / "in pretense" (the sense is "under a show of"); imbolden / embolden; forbad / forbidden; disposeth / inclines; stick at / balk at; malefactor / criminal; entitle him to sin / attribute sin to him; speed / fare; incongruous / unfitting; try the spirits / test the spirits (matching the NET at 1 John 4:1); tendeth / tends; whither it tendeth / "where it tends"; Cæsar / Caesar; quakers / Quakers; Holy Ghost / Holy Spirit; honour / honor; prevailed / succeeded; buffeted / struck.
- **Technical terms kept:** *mortification* (Temptation 22), *will-worship*, *papists* (Baxter's word for Roman Catholics), *gnostics*, *friars*, *libertines*, *Socinians*, *Quakers*. The lists in Temptation 22 are Baxter's polemic against groups of his day, kept exactly as he wrote them; they state his view, not ours.
- **"as there is in a serpent, be he never so small"** (Direction 15): "be he never so small" means "however small he may be"; kept as written.
- **Misc.:** In Direction 15 the "1." begins a list that Baxter never continues; in Direction 16 he gives "1." and "2." We keep his numbering.
