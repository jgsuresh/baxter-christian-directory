# Chapter 3: Grand Direction III (part 1 of 2)

**Grand Direction 3. Understand well what it is to believe in the Holy Spirit; and see that he dwells and operates in you, as the life of your soul, and that you do not resist or quench the Spirit, but thankfully obey him.**

Each person in the Trinity is so believed in by Christians, as that in baptism they enter distinctly into covenant with them: which is, to accept the mercies of, and perform the duties to, each person distinctly. [88] As to take God for our God is more than to believe that there is a God, and to take Christ for our Savior is more than barely to believe that he is the Messiah; so to believe in the Holy Spirit is to take him for Christ's agent or advocate with our souls, and for our Guide, and Sanctifier, and Comforter, and not only to believe that he is the third person in the Trinity. This therefore is a most practical article of our belief.

If the blasphemy against the Holy Spirit is the unpardonable sin, then all sin against the Holy Spirit must needs have a special aggravation by being such. And if the sin against the Holy Spirit is the greatest sin, then our duty toward the Holy Spirit is certainly none of our smallest duties. Therefore the doctrine of the Holy Spirit, and our duty toward him, and sin against him, deserve not the least or last place in teaching, learning, and most serious consideration.

Two sorts do most dangerously sin against or abuse the Holy Spirit. The first is the profane, who through custom and education can say, "I believe in the Holy Spirit," and say that "he sanctifies them and all the elect people of God;" but hate or resist all sanctifying works and motions of the Holy Spirit, and hate all those who are sanctified by him, and make them the objects of their scorn, and deride the very name of sanctification, or at least the thing. [89]

The second sort are the enthusiasts, or true fanatics, who advance, extol, and plead for the Spirit, against the Spirit; covering their greatest sins against the Holy Spirit by loudly praising, and claiming to have, the Holy Spirit. [90] They plead the Spirit in themselves against the Spirit in their brethren, yes, and in almost all the church: they plead the authority of the Spirit in them against the authority of the Spirit in the holy Scriptures; and against particular truths of Scripture; and against several great and needful duties which the Spirit has required in the word; and against the Spirit in their most judicious, godly, faithful teachers. But can it be the Spirit that speaks against the Spirit? Is the Spirit of God against itself? Are we not all baptized "in one Spirit" (and not various or contrary) "into one body?" (1 Corinthians 12:12–13). But it is "no marvel, for Satan to be transformed into an angel of light, or his ministers into the ministers of Christ, and of righteousness, whose end shall be according to their works" (2 Corinthians 11:13–15). The Spirit himself therefore has commanded us that we "do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to determine if they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world" (1 John 4:1). Yes, "the Spirit explicitly says that in the later times some will desert the faith and occupy themselves with deceiving spirits and demonic teachings" (1 Timothy 4:1). Therefore take heed that you neither mistake nor abuse the Holy Spirit.

1. The doctrine concerning the Holy Spirit, to be believed, is briefly this: (1.) That the Holy Spirit, as given since the ascension of Christ, is his agent on earth, or his advocate with people (called by him the Paraclete): instead of his bodily presence, which for a little while he granted to a few, being ascended, he sends the Holy Spirit as better for them, to be his agent continually to the end, and to all, and in all who believe (John 16:7–8). (2.) This Holy Spirit, so sent, infallibly inspired the holy apostles and evangelists, first to preach, and then to write the doctrine of Christ, contained (as dictated by him) in the holy Scriptures; perfectly imprinting therein the holy image of God (John 15:26; 16:13; Galatians 3:1–4; Hebrews 2:3–4). (3.) The same Spirit in them sealed this holy doctrine, and the testimony of these holy men, by many miracles and wonderful gifts, by which they did actually convince the unbelieving world, and plant the churches. (4.) The same Spirit (having first by the apostles given a law or canon to the universal church, establishing its offices and the duty of the officers, and the manner of their entrance) does qualify and dispose men for the regular, ordinary ministerial work (which is to explain and apply the aforesaid Scriptures), and directs those who are to ordain and choose them (they being not wanting on their part); and so he appoints pastors to the church (Ephesians 3:2–4, 8, 13). (5.) The same Spirit assists the ministers (thus sent in their faithful use of the means) to teach and apply the holy Scriptures according to the necessities of the people, the weight of the matter, and the majesty of the word of God. (6.) The same Spirit does by this word (heard or read) renew and sanctify the souls of the elect; illuminating their minds, opening and quickening their hearts, prevailing with, changing, and resolving their wills, thus writing God's word, and imprinting his image by his word upon their hearts, making it powerful to conquer and cast out their strongest, sweetest, dearest sins, and bringing them to the saving knowledge, love, and obedience of God in Jesus Christ (Acts 26:18; John 14:16, 26). (7.) The same Holy Spirit assists the sanctified in the exercise of this grace, to the increase of it, by blessing and concurring with the means appointed by him to that end: and helps them to use those means, perform those duties, conquer temptations, oppositions, and difficulties, and so confirms and preserves them to the end. (8.) The same Spirit helps believers, in the exercise of grace, to feel it, and discern the sincerity of it in themselves, in that measure as they are suited for, and in those seasons when it is fittest for them. (9.) The same Spirit helps them hereupon to conclude that they are justified and reconciled to God, and have right to all the benefits of his covenant. (10.) Also, he assists them actually to rejoice in the discerning of this conclusion. For though reason of itself may do something in these acts, yet so averse is man to all that is holy, and so many are the difficulties and hindrances in the way, that to the effectual performance, the help of the Spirit of God is necessary.

By this enumeration of the Spirit's operations, you may see the errors of many detected, and many common questions answered. 1. You may see their blindness, who claim the Spirit within them against Scripture, ministry, or the use of God's appointed means: when the same Spirit first dictated the Scripture, and makes it the instrument to illuminate and sanctify our souls. God's image is, (1.) Primarily, in Jesus Christ his Son. (2.) Derivatively, by his Spirit, imprinted perfectly in the holy Scriptures. (3.) And by the Scripture, or the holy doctrine of it, instrumentally impressed on the soul. So that the image of God in Christ is the cause of his image in his holy word or doctrine, and his image in his word is the cause of his image on the heart. So a king may have his image, (1.) Naturally, on his son, who is like his father. (2.) Expressively, in his laws, which express his wisdom, clemency, and justice. (3.) And effectively, on his subjects and servants, who are by his laws brought to a conformity to his mind. As a man may first cut his arms or image on his seal, and then by that seal imprint it on the wax; and though it is perfectly cut on the seal, it may be imperfectly printed on the wax; so God's image is naturally perfect in his Son, and regularly or expressively perfect on the seal of his holy doctrine and laws; but imperfectly on his subjects, according to their reception of it in their several degrees.

Therefore it is easy to discern their error, who tell men the light or Spirit within them is their rule, and a perfect rule, yes, and that it is thus in all men in the world; when God's word and experience flatly contradict it, telling us that unbelievers and enemies of God, and all the ungodly, are in darkness, and not in the light; and that all who do not speak according to this word (the law and testimony) have "no light in them;" and therefore no "perfect light to be their rule" (Isaiah 8:20). The ministry is sent to bring them from darkness to light: therefore they had not a sufficient light in them before (Acts 26:17–18). "Beware, ... who turn darkness into light and light into darkness ..." (Isaiah 5:20): telling the children of darkness, and the haters of the light, that they have a perfect light and rule within them, when God says, "They have no light in them." See 1 John 1:4–8. "The one who says he is in the light but still hates his fellow Christian is still in the darkness" (1 John 2:9–11). The light within a wicked man is "darkness" and "blindness," and therefore not his rule (Matthew 6:23; Ephesians 5:8). Even the light that is in godly men is the knowledge of the rule, and not the rule itself at all, nor ever called so by God. Our rule is perfect; our knowledge is imperfect: for Paul himself says, "we know in part ... but when what is perfect comes, the partial will be set aside ... now we see in a mirror indirectly" (1 Corinthians 13:9–10, 12). "The gospel is hidden to those who are lost," being "blinded by Satan" (2 Corinthians 4:3–4).

There is an admirable, unsearchable concurrence of the Spirit, and his appointed means, and the will of man, in the procreation of the new creature, and in all the exercises of grace, as there is of male and female in natural generation; and of the earth, the sun, the rain, the industry of the gardener, and the seminal virtue of life and specification, in the production of plants with their flowers and fruits. And as wise as it would be to say, it is not the male but the female, or not the female but the male that generates; or to say, it is not the earth but the sun, or not the sun but the rain, or not the rain but the seminal virtue, that causes plants with flowers and fruits: so wise is it to say, it is not the Spirit but the word and means, or it is not the word and means but the Spirit, or it is not the reason, and will, and industry of man, but the Spirit: or, if we do not have wisdom enough to assign to each cause its proper share in the effect, that therefore we should separate what God has conjoined, or deny the truth of the causation, because we do not comprehend the manner and influence--this is but to choose to be befooled by pride, rather than confess that God is wiser than we.

(English: God is the effecting principle in creation, the restoring principle in redemption, the perfecting principle in sanctification. [90] Rejectis propheticis et apostolicis scriplis, Manichæi novum evangelium scripserunt: et ut antecellere communi hominum multitudini et semi-dei viderentur, simularunt enthusiasmos seu afflatus, subito in turba se in terram objicientes, et velut attoniti diu tacentes; deinde tanquam redeuntes ex specu Trophonio et plorantes, multa vaticinati sunt; prorsus ut Anabaptistæ recens fecerunt in seditione monasteriensi. Etsi autem in quibusdam manifesta simulatio fuit, tamen aliquibus reipsa à diabolis furores immissos esse certum est. Carion. Chron. 1. 3. p. 54. (English: After rejecting the writings of the prophets and apostles, the Manichaeans wrote a new gospel; and in order to seem to rise above the common crowd of men and to be demigods, they pretended to enthusiasms or inspirations, suddenly throwing themselves on the ground in the crowd, and staying silent a long time as if stunned; then, as if returning from the cave of Trophonius, and weeping, they uttered many prophecies; exactly as the Anabaptists did recently in the Münster sedition. Although in some there was plain pretense, yet it is certain that in some, madness was actually sent in by devils. Carion, *Chronicle*, book 3, p. 54. Rendered with some looseness; "scriplis" in the source is a misprint for "scriptis.")

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All verses were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Holy Ghost:** changed to Holy Spirit throughout, including in the creed words ("I believe in the Holy Ghost") and in "sanctifieth them and all the elect people of God." The second looks like a quotation from a catechism or creed-like formula; Baxter gives no source, and we have not identified it.
- **enthusiasts, or true fanatics:** kept as Baxter's terms. Here "enthusiasts" means those who claim direct inspiration by the Spirit and set it against Scripture and ministers. The polemic against them and against the "profane" is Baxter's, left as he wrote it. **brethren:** kept.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): crying up = loudly praising; pretending to = claiming to have; yea = yes; vouchsafed = granted; for a little space = for a little while; indited = dictated; constituting (its offices) = establishing; stated (ordinary ministerial work) = regular; foresaid = aforesaid; hinderances = hindrances; meet for = suited for; infidels = unbelievers; interest (in the effect) = share; reduced (to conformity) = brought; hid = hidden. **christians** capitalized. "Men" is kept where Baxter uses it for people in general.
- **Kept as Baxter wrote them:** Paraclete (his own gloss: advocate); canon (rule); infallibly; the elect; clemency (mercy, leniency); effectively, expressively, regularly (in the seal analogy: "regularly" appears to mean "as a standard or rule," set beside "expressively"); concurrence (working together); **seminal virtue of life and specification** (the generative power in a seed that gives life and fixes its kind); **procreation of the new creature** (new birth); **motions** of the Holy Spirit (his inward movings); **arms** (a coat of arms, on a seal). **resolving** their wills (settling or determining them). **convince** here means persuade by proof, as usual.
- **Numbering:** the paragraph beginning "1. The doctrine" has its own numbered list (1.)-(10.). The following paragraph begins "1. You may see ..." and no "2." appears before the Direction continues in the next part of the chapter; kept as printed.
- **1 Corinthians 12:12–13 (flag):** Baxter's "baptized by one Spirit (and not divers or contrary) into one body" reflects the King James "by." The NET reads "in one Spirit," so we quote that and keep Baxter's parenthesis as his own comment. NET adds "we were all made to drink of the one Spirit," which he does not quote. His point (one Spirit, not many contrary ones) survives either way, but "by one Spirit" (the Spirit as the one who baptizes) differs in sense from "in one Spirit."
- **2 Corinthians 11:13–15:** Baxter's quotation is a condensed blend of verses 13–15 (King James: "no marvel; for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light ... his ministers also be transformed as the ministers of righteousness; whose end shall be according to their works"), so we keep his wording, modernized ("no marvel" is kept). The NET reads: "And no wonder, for even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. Therefore it is not surprising his servants also disguise themselves as servants of righteousness, whose end will correspond to their actions." His "ministers of Christ" matches "apostles of Christ" in verse 13 of the NET ("disguising themselves as apostles of Christ").
- **1 John 4:1; 1 Timothy 4:1:** NET wording. Baxter's "Yea" opens his quotation of 1 Timothy 4:1; the NET opens "Now," so we put "Yes," outside the quotation marks. In 1 Timothy 4:1, Baxter's "doctrines of devils" (King James) is "demonic teachings" in the NET; "seducing spirits" is "deceiving spirits." "Giving heed to" is "occupy themselves with." These shift the tone slightly but not the argument.
- **Isaiah 8:20 (important flag):** Baxter's quoted words "no light in them" are the King James ending of the verse ("if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them"). The NET reads differently: "Then you must recall the Lord's instructions and the prophetic testimony of what would happen. Certainly they say such things because their minds are spiritually darkened." So the NET lacks the exact words "no light in them" and the "law and testimony" he puts in parentheses (NET: "the Lord's instructions and the prophetic testimony"). We keep Baxter's wording, modernized, because the quoted words are not in the NET verse, and "perfect light to be their rule" is his own gloss, not Scripture. His argument (those who disregard God's word are in darkness) still fits the NET's "spiritually darkened," but he leans on the King James phrasing. The later "God says, 'They have no light in them'" repeats the same King James words and is kept as his.
- **Isaiah 5:20 (flag):** Baxter has "Woe to them that put darkness for light, and light for darkness!" The NET has "Beware, those who call evil good and good evil, who turn darkness into light and light into darkness," so "Woe" becomes "Beware" and "put ... for" becomes "turn ... into." "Beware" may read as milder in English than "woe," which Baxter's argument may lean on; we have not checked the Hebrew. The NET verse continues ("who turn bitter into sweet and sweet into bitter"), so our quotation ends with an ellipsis; Baxter's exclamation mark is his own punctuation, not the NET's.
- **1 John 2:9–11:** NET "the one who says he is in the light but still hates his fellow Christian is still in the darkness"; Baxter's King James has "his brother" and "even till now." We quote verse 9 and keep his reference to verses 9-11. "Fellow Christian" in the NET for the King James "brother" may narrow the sense; we do not assert what Baxter meant by it.
- **Matthew 6:23; Ephesians 5:8:** Baxter's quotation marks around "darkness" and "blindness" are his own words applied to a wicked man's inward light; the NET of Matthew 6:23 has "the light in you is darkness," and neither verse has the word "blindness." We keep his words as printed and give the references as he did.
- **1 Corinthians 13:9–10, 12:** NET wording. "Now we see through a glass darkly" (King James) becomes "now we see in a mirror indirectly" in the NET; Baxter's point (our knowledge is partial) is unaffected. "That which is perfect" becomes "what is perfect" and "that which is in part shall be done away" becomes "the partial will be set aside." The ellipses mark where we skip text. Baxter has no "For" before "now we see"; the NET has it, so we begin the quotation at "now."
- **2 Corinthians 4:3–4 (flag):** Baxter's quotations ("The gospel is hid to them that are lost" and "blinded by Satan") are paraphrase of the King James. The NET reads "But even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled only to those who are perishing, among whom the god of this age has blinded the minds of those who do not believe." Baxter's "blinded by Satan" is his own identification of "the god of this age" (the NET does not use the name Satan in this verse). We keep his wording, modernized ("hid" as "hidden").
- **Acts 26:17–18; Acts 26:18; John 14:16, 26; John 16:7–8; John 15:26; 16:13; Galatians 3:1–4; Hebrews 2:3–4; Ephesians 3:2–4, 8, 13; 1 John 1:4–8:** references only, converted to modern form, with no wording quoted. The NET calls the Spirit "Advocate" in the Gospel of John, close to Baxter's "advocate" (his gloss on "Paraclete"). We have not checked each reference against his claim; Ephesians 3:2–4, 8, 13 seems wide for "he appointeth pastors to the church," but we keep his reference.
- **Unmarked allusions:** "quench the Spirit" in the Direction heading (perhaps 1 Thessalonians 5:19; reference not in Baxter's text); "the blasphemy against the Holy Spirit" (perhaps Matthew 12:31–32 and parallels; reference not in Baxter's text); "the life of your soul" has no reference. Kept in Baxter's wording and given no reference in the text.
- **Latin note [88]:** "1. 5" is probably a misprint for "l. 5" (book 5). The saying is attributed to Bernard (probably Bernard of Clairvaux); we have not checked it.
- **Latin note [89]:** "Joh. Combis" and "comp. Theol." are as printed; we have not identified the author or work and give only a literal rendering of the title.
- **Latin note [90]:** The reference is to a chronicle by "Carion" (probably Johann Carion); we have not confirmed the edition. "Trophonius" appears to refer to a cave associated with a Greek oracle ("the cave of Trophonius"); we have not looked into it further. "Seditione monasteriensi" is presumably the Anabaptist rising at Münster, though the source says only "the Monasterian sedition." The note is part of Baxter's argument against the "enthusiasts," and its claims about the Manichaeans and Anabaptists are the source's and Baxter's, not ours.
