# Chapter 3: Grand Direction VIII (part 5 of 5)

*This section begins partway through Grand Direction 8, at Direction 8 of its first list of directions (on knowing the evil of sin). It then gives a second list of twenty directions, numbered again from 1, "for the executive part" of the cure. It ends with the margin note that opens the next Grand Direction. No new Grand Direction heading is supplied here.*

**Direction 8. Just look at the state and torment of the damned, and consider well the difference between angels and devils, and you may know what sin is.**

Angels are pure; devils are polluted: holiness and sin make the difference. Sin dwells in hell, and holiness in heaven. Remember that every temptation is from the devil, to make you like himself; as every holy impulse is from Christ, to make you like himself. Remember when you sin, that you are learning from and imitating the devil, and are so far like him (John 8:44). And the end of it all is that you may feel his pains. If hell-fire is not good, then sin is not good.

**Direction 9. Look always on sin as one that is ready to die, and consider how all men judge of it at the last.**

What do men in heaven say of it? and what do men in hell say of it? and what do men at death say of it? and what do converted souls, or awakened consciences, say of it? Is it then followed with delight and fearlessness as it is now? is it then applauded? will any of them speak well of it? No, all the world speaks evil of sin in general now, even when they love and commit the individual acts. Will you sin when you are dying?

**Direction 10. Look always on sin and judgment together.**

Remember that you must answer for it before God, and angels, and all the world; and you will know it the better.

**Direction 11. Just look now at sickness, poverty, shame, despair, death, and rottenness in the grave, and it may help you a little to know what sin is.**

These are things within your sight or feeling; you do not need faith to tell you of them. And by such effects you might have some little knowledge of the cause.

**Direction 12. Just look at some eminent, holy persons on earth, and at the mad, profane, malignant world; and the difference may tell you in part what sin is.**

Is there not a loveliness in a holy, blameless person, who lives in love to God and man, and in the joyful hope of eternal life? Is not a beastly drunkard or whoremonger, and a raging swearer, and a malicious persecutor, a very deformed, loathsome creature? Is not the mad, confused, ignorant, ungodly state of the world a very pitiful sight? What then is the sin that all this consists in?

Though the principal part of the cure is in turning the will to the hatred of sin, and is done by this discovery of its malignity; yet I shall add a few more directions for the executive part, supposing that what has been said already has had its effect.

**Direction 1. When you have found out your disease and danger, give yourselves up to Christ as the Savior and Physician of souls, and to the Holy Spirit as your Sanctifier, remembering that he is sufficient and willing to do the work which he has undertaken.**

It is not you who are to be saviors and sanctifiers of yourselves (except as you work under Christ). But he who has undertaken it takes it for his glory to perform it.

**Direction 2. Yet you must be willing and obedient in applying the remedies prescribed to you by Christ, and in observing his directions in order to your cure.**

And you must not be oversensitive, and coy, and dainty, and say, This is too bitter, and that is too sharp; but trust his love, and skill, and care, and take it as he prescribes it, or gives it to you, without any more fuss. Do not say, It is grievous, and I cannot take it: for he commands you nothing but what is safe, and wholesome, and necessary, and if you cannot take it, you must try whether you can bear your sickness, and death, and the fire of hell! Are humiliation, confession, restitution, mortification, and holy diligence worse than hell?

**Direction 3. See that you do not take the part of sin, and do not wrangle or strive against your Physician, or against any who would do you good.**

Excusing sin, and pleading for it and downplaying it, and striving against the Spirit and conscience, and wrangling against ministers and godly friends, and hating reproof, are not the means to be cured and sanctified.

**Direction 4. See that malignity in every one of your particular sins, which you can see and say is in sin in general.**

It is a gross deceit of yourselves, if you will speak a great deal of the evil of sin, and see none of this malignity in your pride, and your worldliness, and your temper and peevishness, and your malice and uncharitableness, and your lying, backbiting, slandering, or sinning against conscience for worldly gain or safety. What self-contradiction it is for a man in prayer to aggravate sin, and when he is reproved for it, to justify or excuse it! for a popish priest to enter sinfully upon his office, by subscribing or swearing the Trent Confession, and then to preach zealously against sin in general, as if he had never committed so horrid a crime! This is like him who will speak against treason, and the enemies of the king, but because the traitors are his friends and kindred, will protect or hide them, and take their part.

**Direction 5. Keep as far as you can from those temptations which feed and strengthen the sins which you would overcome.**

Lay siege to your sins, and starve them out, by keeping away the food and fuel which is their maintenance and life.

**Direction 6. Live in the exercise of those graces and duties which are contrary to the sins which you are most in danger of.**

For grace and duty are contrary to sin, and kill it, and cure us of it, as fire cures us of cold, or health of sickness.

**Direction 7. Do not listen to weakening unbelief and distrust, and do not cast away the comforts of God, which are your cordials and strength.**

It is not a frightened, dejected, despairing frame of mind that is fittest to resist sin; but it is the encouraging sense of the love of God, and a thankful sense of grace received (with a wary fear).

**Direction 8. Be always suspicious of carnal self-love, and watch against it.**

For that is the burrow or fortress of sin; and its common defender; ready to draw you to it, and ready to justify it. We are very prone to be partial in our own cause; as the case of Judah with Tamar, and of David when Nathan reproved him in a parable, show. Our own passions, our own pride, our own censures, or backbitings, or injurious dealings, our own neglects of duty, seem small, excusable, if not justifiable things to us; whereas we could easily see the faultiness of all these in another, especially in an enemy: when yet we should be best acquainted with ourselves, and we should most love ourselves, and therefore hate our own sins most.

**Direction 9. Give your first and chief labor to killing sin at the root; to cleansing the heart, which is the fountain; for out of the heart come the evils of the life.**

Know which are the master-roots; and bend your greatest care and effort to mortify those: and they are especially these that follow; 1. Ignorance. 2. Unbelief. 3. Thoughtlessness. 4. Selfishness and pride. 5. Fleshliness, in pleasing a brutish appetite, lust, or fancy. 6. Unfeeling hardheartedness and sleepiness in sin.

**Direction 10. Account the world and all its pleasures, wealth, and honors, as no better than they really are, and then Satan will find no bait to catch you.**

Regard all as dung with Paul ("I regard them as dung!" Philippians 3:8); and no man will sin, and sell his soul, for that which he regards as only dung.

**Direction 11. Keep up above in a heavenly way of life, and then your souls will be always in the light, and as in the sight of God, and taken up with those businesses and delights which put them out of taste for the baits of sin.**

**Direction 12. Let Christian watchfulness be your daily work; and cherish a preserving, though not a distracting and discouraging, fear.**

**Direction 13. Beware of the first approaches and beginnings of sin.** Oh how great a matter a little of this fire kindles! And if you fall, rise quickly by sound repentance, whatever it may cost you.

**Direction 14. Make God's word your only rule; and work diligently to understand it.**

**Direction 15. And in doubtful cases, do not easily depart from the unanimous judgment of the generality of the most wise and godly of all ages.**

**Direction 16. In doubtful cases do not be emotional or rash, but proceed deliberately, and test things well, before you fasten on them.**

**Direction 17. Be acquainted with your bodily temperament, and what sin it most inclines you to, and what sin your calling or company also lays you most open to, so that there your watch may be the stricter.** (Of all which I shall speak more fully under the next Grand Direction.)

**Direction 18. Keep in a life of holy order, such as God has appointed you to walk in. For there is no preservation for stragglers who do not keep rank and file, but forsake the order which God commands them.**

And this order lies principally in these points: 1. That you keep in union with the universal church. Do not separate from Christ's body on any pretense whatever. With the church as regenerate, hold spiritual communion, in faith, love, and holiness: with the church as congregate and visible, hold outward communion, in profession and worship. 2. If you are not teachers, live under your particular, faithful pastors, as obedient disciples of Christ. 3. Let the most godly, if possible, be your close companions. 4. Be hard-working in an outward calling.

**Direction 19. Turn all God's providences, whether of prosperity or adversity, against your sins.**

If he gives you health and wealth, remember he thereby obliges you to obedience, and calls for special service from you. If he afflicts you, remember that it is sin that he is offended at, and searches after; and therefore take it as his medicine, and see that you do not hinder, but help on its work, that it may purge away your sin.

**Direction 20. Wait patiently on Christ until he has finished the cure, which will not be until this trying life is finished.**

Persevere in attendance on his Spirit and means; for he will come in season, and will not delay. "So let us search for him! Let us seek to know the Lord! He will come to our rescue as certainly as the appearance of the dawn, as certainly as the winter rain comes, as certainly as the spring rain that waters the land" (Hosea 6:3). Though you have often said, "we are beyond recovery" (Jeremiah 14:19); "he will heal your waywardness, and love you freely" (Hosea 14:4). "For you who respect my name, the sun of vindication will rise with healing wings" (Malachi 4:2): "all who wait for him in faith will be blessed" (Isaiah 30:18).

Thus I have given such directions as may help for humiliation under sin, or hatred of it, and deliverance from it.

## Translator's notes

- **Where this section sits:** The first five paragraphs (Directions 8 to 12 of the first list) finish the directions on knowing what sin is. After a transition paragraph Baxter begins a new list of twenty, "for the executive part" of the cure, and restarts the numbering at 1. We keep his numbers, so "Direction 8" and "Direction 9" through "Direction 12" appear twice in this file, once in each list. The closing margin note belongs to the next section (apparently the next Grand Direction), whose heading falls outside this range.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **John 8:44:** Baxter only cites it (devils as the model of sin); no words are quoted.
- **Philippians 3:8:** NET has "dung" (Baxter's "dung" as well), so the sense holds. Baxter's "Esteem all as dung" is his own paraphrase; the NET words are quoted in parentheses.
- **Hosea 6:3:** Baxter's wording matches the King James Version, as far as we recall (we have not checked) ("Then shall we know, if we follow on to know the Lord: his going forth is prepared as the morning ... as the latter and former rain upon the earth"). The NET reads differently. It has no "Then shall we know, if we follow on" (it has "So let us search for him! Let us seek to know the Lord!"), and "his going forth is prepared as the morning" becomes "He will come to our rescue as certainly as the appearance of the dawn." The NET also has "winter rain" and "spring rain" where the King James has "latter and former rain." The promise of God's coming is the same, but the NET makes it sure ("as certainly as") where the King James reads like a conditional ("if we follow on"). Baxter's call to "persevere" leans on that conditional "follow on," so the NET weakens the exact hook of his argument.
- **Jeremiah 14:19:** Baxter quotes only the phrase "There is no healing" (King James: "there is no healing for us"). The NET has "that we are beyond recovery" (the verse is in the form of a question). We keep to the NET phrase "we are beyond recovery," so the word "healing," which sets up Baxter's contrast with Hosea 14:4 and Malachi 4:2, no longer appears in this verse. The idea (hopelessness answered by God's healing) is still there, but the verbal echo is lost.
- **Hosea 14:4:** Baxter's quotation, "he will heal your backslidings, and love you freely," is not an exact quote: the King James reads "I will heal their backslidings, I will love them freely" (we have not checked this against the King James text). The NET reads "I will heal their waywardness and love them freely." We keep Baxter's pronouns ("he," "your") as his adaptation of the speaker and audience, and use the NET's "waywardness" for "backslidings." In the NET the speaker is God ("I"); Baxter turns it into a statement about Christ. "Backsliding" is the older word for falling back into sin, which "waywardness" may not quite carry.
- **Malachi 4:2:** NET wording. This is a notable change of sense. The King James has "fear his name," "Sun of righteousness," and "healing in his wings." The NET has "respect my name," "the sun of vindication," and "healing wings." "Sun of righteousness" can sound like a title of Christ, which would fit Baxter's medical image of the whole section (Baxter does not say here that he reads it so). "Vindication" and the impersonal "healing wings" may lose that title-like sound, though the sunrise with healing remains. Baxter's "fear" also means reverent awe, which "respect" may understate. Note that the NET text has "my name" (God speaking) where Baxter, quoting the King James, has "his name."
- **Isaiah 30:18:** Baxter's "blessed are all they that wait for him" becomes the NET's "all who wait for him in faith will be blessed." The NET adds "in faith," which Baxter's text lacks. The sense is close.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording, with no reference added in the text): "out of the heart come the evils of the life" (Direction 9; probably Matthew 15:19 and/or Proverbs 4:23; not in Baxter's text); "how great a matter a little of this fire kindles" (Direction 13; probably James 3:5, not in Baxter's text); Judah and Tamar (probably Genesis 38) and David and Nathan's parable (probably 2 Samuel 12), both given by Baxter as examples of partiality in our own cause. These references are suggestions, not in Baxter's text.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): *betwixt* to between; *motion* (every holy motion) to "impulse"; *Nay* to "No"; *extenuating* to "downplaying"; *frightful* to "frightened"; *think well of* to "consider well"; *take heed of* to "beware of"; *look but to* to "just look at"; *Saviour* to Savior; *Holy Ghost* to Holy Spirit; *labour* to labor; *honours* to honors; *amiableness* to loveliness; *hearken not to* to "do not listen to"; *tender* to "oversensitive" (in "you must not be tender"); *fine* to "dainty"; *patron* (of sin) to "defender"; *commodity* to "gain"; *passion* (in your pride, and your passion) to "temper"; *inconsiderateness* to "thoughtlessness"; *senseless hardheartedness* to "unfeeling hardheartedness"; *fantasy* to "fancy"; *conversation* ("heavenly conversation") to "way of life"; *put them out of relish with* to "put them out of taste for"; *converse* (what your calling or converse lays you open to) to "company"; *familiars* to "close companions"; *laborious in* to "hard-working in"; *physic* to "medicine"; *tarry* to "delay"; *passionate* (in Direction 16) to "emotional"; *prove things* to "test things"; *cautelous* to "wary"; *pretence* to pretense. *Bodily temperature* became "bodily temperament": he means a person's physical makeup, which he says inclines a person toward certain sins (we have not checked how he explains the term elsewhere).
- **Technical terms kept:** *malignity* (a strong ill-will or deadly evil in sin, not merely harm); *carnal* (Direction 8; see GLOSSARY.md); *mortify*; *wrangle*; *peevishness* (irritability); *cordials* (restoring, strengthening medicines); *frame of mind*; *regenerate* and *congregate* (the "church as regenerate" is the invisible company of the truly converted; the "church as congregate and visible" is the outward, assembled church); *providences*; *calling* (an occupation or station in life, here an "outward calling" as opposed to an inward call).
- **Direction 7, "a wary fear":** Baxter's "cautelous" means careful or cautious; our "wary" renders it.
- **Polemic:** In Direction 4 Baxter's "a popish priest ... subscribing or swearing the Trent Confession" is his own polemic against the Roman Catholic clergy; we keep his wording. "Trent Confession" is probably the profession of faith associated with the Council of Trent, but we have not confirmed which text he means. He says that a priest who has taken that oath and then preaches against sin is like a man who speaks against treason while protecting traitors who are his kin.
- **Baxter's blunt words kept:** "whoremonger," "beastly," "deformed," "dung."
- **Direction 13 layout:** Direction 13 has the heading sentence followed on the same line by the sentence "Oh how great a matter ...," exactly as Baxter prints it, with no "--" break. Directions 11, 12, 14, 15, 16 and 17 have no commentary after the heading sentence in the source; Direction 17's final parenthesis is Baxter's cross-reference to the next Grand Direction.
- **Direction 18, "life of holy order":** the military image ("rank and file," "stragglers") is Baxter's own. "Pretence whatever" (any excuse) is modernized as "any pretense."
- **Medical imagery:** Baxter's picture of Christ as Physician, sin as disease, and commandments as prescribed medicine runs through the section. We have kept his medical words (disease, Physician, remedies, cure, medicine) without modern substitutes.
- **"men":** "all men judge of it," "what do men in heaven say," and "no man will sin" are kept as Baxter's generic "men," meaning people in general.
- **Whole range covered:** no sentence has been dropped, merged, or split.
