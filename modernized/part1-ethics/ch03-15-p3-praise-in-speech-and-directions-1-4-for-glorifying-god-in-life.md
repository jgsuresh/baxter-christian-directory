# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 15 (part 3 of 4)

*This section begins partway through a Grand Direction, in the middle of its series of Directions on praising God (Directions 5 to 10 of that series). Then, under a new sub-heading, "Directions for glorifying God in our lives," Baxter begins a second series and its numbering starts again at 1. The next file continues that second series.*

**Direction 5. Remember to allow the praises of God their due proportion in all your prayers.**

Do not make a habit of shutting it out, or forgetting it, or cutting it short with two or three words in the conclusion. The Lord's Prayer begins and ends with it: and the first three petitions are for the glorifying of the name of God, and the coming of his kingdom, and the doing of his will, by which he is glorified: and all this before we ask anything directly for ourselves. Practice will help you much in the praise of God.

**Direction 6. Especially let the Lord's Day be principally spent in praises and thanksgivings for the work of our redemption, and the benefits of it.**

This day is set apart by God himself for this holy work; and if you spend it (ordinarily) in other religious duties that do not serve this, you do not spend it as God requires you to. The thankful and praising remembrance of the work of man's redemption is the special work of the day: and the celebrating of the sacrament of the body and blood of Christ (which is therefore called the Eucharist) was part of these exercises of praise, and was used every Lord's Day by the early church. It is not only a holy day, set apart for God's worship in general; but for this eucharistic worship in particular above the rest, as a day of praises and thanksgiving to God: and so all Christians (ordinarily) should use it.

**Direction 7. Let your holy conversation with others be much about the glorious excellences, works, and mercies of the Lord, in the way of praise and admiration.**

This is indeed to speak to edification, and as the "oracles of God" (Ephesians 4:29); "so that in everything God will be glorified" (1 Peter 4:11). Psalm 29:9, "Everyone in his temple says, 'Majestic!'" Psalm 35:28, "Then I will tell others about your justice, and praise you all day long." Psalm 145:6, 11, 21, "They will proclaim the power of your awesome acts. ... They will proclaim the splendor of your kingdom; they will tell about your power, so that mankind might acknowledge your mighty acts and the majestic splendor of your kingdom. ... My mouth will praise the Lord. Let all who live praise his holy name forever." Psalm 105:2, 3, "Tell about all his miraculous deeds. Boast about his holy name."

**Direction 8. Do not speak of God in a light, irreverent, or ordinary way, as if you were talking of ordinary things; but with all possible seriousness, gravity, and reverence, as if you saw the majesty of the Lord.**

An ordinary manner of speech and a holy one are opposites. Only that is holy which is set apart to God from ordinary use. You speak profanely (in the manner, however holy the matter may be) when you speak of God with the careless levity with which you are used to speak of ordinary things. Such speaking of God is dishonorable to him, and hurts the hearers more than silence, by breeding in them a contempt of God, and teaching them to imitate you in slight notions and speech about the Almighty: whereas one who speaks reverently of God, as in his presence, often affects the hearers more deeply with a reverence for his Majesty, with a few words, than irreverent preachers with the most careful sermons, delivered in an ordinary or affected manner. Whenever you speak of God, let the hearers see that your hearts are possessed with his fear and love, and that you put more difference between God and man than between a king and the smallest worm: so when you talk of death or judgment, of heaven or hell, of holiness or sin, or anything that is closely related to God, do it with the gravity and seriousness that the matter requires.

**Direction 9. Do not speak so unskillfully and foolishly of God, or holy things, as may tempt the hearers to turn it into a matter of scorn or laughter.**

Especially understand how your abilities are suited to the company you are in. Among those who are more ignorant, some weak talks may be tolerable and profitable; for they are most affected by what is delivered in their own dialect and manner: but among discerning or fault-finding hearers, unskillful people must be very sparing of their words, lest they do harm while they desire to do good, and make religion seem ridiculous. We may rejoice in the scorn that we undergo for Christ, and that is aimed at his holy laws, or the substance of our duty: but if people are jeered at for speaking ridiculously and foolishly of holy things, they have little reason to take comfort in any of that, except in their honest meanings and intentions; no, they must be humbled for being a dishonor to the name of godliness. But the misery is that few of the ignorant and weak have knowledge and humility enough to see their ignorance and weakness, but they think they speak as wisely as the best, and are offended if their words are not honored accordingly. As a minister should study and labor to gain skill and ability to preach, because it is his work; so every Christian should study to gain skill to talk with wisdom and fitting expressions about holy things, because this is his work. And as unfit expressions and behavior in a minister cause contempt instead of edifying, so they do in conversation.

**Direction 10. Whenever God's holy name or word is blasphemed, or used in levity or jest, or a holy life is made a scorn, or God is notoriously abused or dishonored, be ready to reprove it with gravity where you can; and where you cannot, at least let your detestation of it be suitably shown.**

Among those to whom you may speak freely, lay open the greatness of their sin. Or, if you are unable to give a long or careful talk, at least tell them who has said, "You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain, for the Lord will not hold guiltless anyone who takes his name in vain." And where your speech is improper (as toward some superiors), or is likely to do more harm than good, let your leaving the room, or your looks, or rather your tears, show your dislike. [130]

**Directions for glorifying God in our lives.**

**Direction 1. Our lives glorify God when they are such as his excellences most appear in: and that is when they are most divine or holy; when they are so managed that the world may see that it is God we chiefly respect, and that HOLINESS TO THE LORD is written upon all our faculties and affairs.**

So much of GOD as appears in our lives, so much they are truly worthy of reverence, and raised above the rank of fleshly, worldly lives. [131] God alone is the real glory of every person, and every thing, and every word or action of our lives. And the natural conscience of the world, which, in spite of its atheism, is forced to confess and reverence a Deity, will be forced (even when they are hated and persecuted) to reverence the appearance of God in his holy ones. Let it appear therefore, 1. That God's authority commands you, above all the powers of the earth, and against all the power of fleshly lusts. 2. That it is the glory and interest of God that you live for, and look after principally in the world, and not your own carnal interest and glory: and that it is his work that you are doing, and not your own; and his cause, and not your own, that you are engaged in. [132] 3. That it is his word and law that is your rule. 4. And the example of his Son that is your pattern. 5. And that your hearts and lives are moved and driven in the world by motives drawn from the rewards which he has promised, and the punishments which he has threatened, in the world to come. 6. And that it is a supernatural, powerful principle, sent from God into your hearts, even the Holy Spirit, by which you are inclined and set in motion throughout the course of your lives. 7. And that your daily fellowship is with God, and that people and other creatures are comparatively nothing to you, but are made to stand by, while God is preferred, and honored, and served by you; and that all your business is with him, or for him in the world.

**Direction 2. The more of heaven appears in your lives, the more your lives glorify God.**

Worldly and carnal people are aware that their glory is a vanishing glory, and their pleasure only a passing dream, and that all their honor and wealth will soon leave them in the dust; and therefore they are forced, in spite of their indulgence in physical pleasure, to show some reverence for the life to come. And though they do not have hearts themselves to deny the pleasures and profits of the world, and to spend their days in preparing for eternity, and in laying up a treasure in heaven; yet they are convinced that those who do so are the best and wisest people; and they could wish that they might die the death of the righteous, and that their last end might be like his. As heaven exceeds earth, even in the reverent acknowledgment of the world, though not in their practical esteem and choice; so heavenly Christians have a reverent acknowledgment from them (when malice does not hide their heavenly character with slanders), though they will not be such themselves. Let it appear in your lives that you really seek a higher happiness than this world affords, and that you truly look to live with Christ; and that as honor, and wealth, and pleasure command the lives of the ungodly, so the hope of heaven commands yours. Let it appear that this is your purpose and business in the world, and that your hearts and conduct are above, and that whatever you do or suffer is for this, and not for any lower end; and this is a life by which God is glorified.

**Direction 3. It glorifies God, by showing the excellence of faith, when we despise the riches and honor of the world, and live above the worldling's life; regarding as contemptible what he counts his happiness, and loses his soul for.**

As people despise the toys of children, so a believer must take the passing, empty things of this world for matters so insignificant as not to be worth his regard, except only as they are the matter of his duty to God, or as they relate to him, or the life to come. Paul says, 2 Corinthians 4:18, "we are not looking at what can be seen" (they are not worth our observing or looking at) "but at what cannot be seen. For what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal." The world is under a believer's feet, while his eye is fixed on the heavenly world. He travels through it to his home, and he will be thankful if his way is smooth, and if he has his daily bread: but it is not his home, nor does he make a great matter of whether his treatment in it is kind or unkind, or whether his inn is well adorned or not. He is almost indifferent whether, for so short a time, he is rich or poor, in a high or a low condition, further than as it tends to his Master's service. Let people see that you have a higher birth than they, and higher hopes, and higher hearts, by setting little value on what their hearts are set upon as their happiness. When seeming Christians are as worldly and ambitious as others, and make as great a matter of their gain, and wealth, and honor, it shows that they only cover the base and sordid spirit of worldlings with the mask of the Christian name, to deceive themselves, and bring the faith of Christians into scorn, and dishonor the holy name which they usurp.

**Direction 4. It greatly honors God when his servants can quietly and fearlessly trust in him, in the face of all the dangers and threatenings which devils or men can cast before them; and can joyfully suffer pain or death, in obedience to his commands, and in confidence in his promise of everlasting happiness.**

This shows that we believe indeed that "he exists," and that "he rewards those who seek him" (Hebrews 11:6); and that he is true and just; and that his promises are to be trusted; and that he is able to make them good, in spite of all the malice of his enemies; and that the threats or frowns of sinful worms are contemptible to the one who fears God. Psalm 58:11, "Then observers will say, 'Yes indeed, the godly are rewarded. Yes indeed, there is a God who judges in the earth,'" and who at last will judge the world in righteousness. Paul gloried in the Thessalonians, "for their faith and patience, in all their persecutions and tribulations which they endured; as a manifest token of the righteous judgment of God, that they might be accounted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which they suffered. Seeing it is a righteous thing with God to repay tribulation to those who trouble us, and rest with his saints to those who are troubled" (2 Thessalonians 1:4–7). "If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory, who is the Spirit of God, rests on you: on their part he is spoken ill of, but on your part he is glorified" (1 Peter 4:14). "If you suffer as a Christian, do not be ashamed, but glorify God that you bear such a name" (verse 16). When confidence in God, and assurance of the great reward in heaven (Matthew 5:11–12), cause a believer undauntedly to say as the three witnesses did (Daniel 3), "We do not need to give you a reply concerning this. If our God whom we are serving exists, he is able to rescue us ...": when by faith we can go through the trial of cruel mockings and scourgings, of bonds and imprisonment, to be destitute and afflicted, yes, and tortured, not accepting deliverance (on sinful terms), in this way God is glorified by believers. "They lift their voices," O you afflicted saints, "and shout joyfully; they praise the majesty of the Lord in the west. So in the east extol the Lord, along the seacoasts extol the fame of the Lord God of Israel" (Isaiah 24:14–15). Sing to his praise with Paul and Silas, though your feet are in the stocks. If God calls for your lives, remember that "you are not your own, you were bought at a price. Therefore glorify God with your body [and in your spirits, which are his]" (1 Corinthians 6:19–20). Rejoice in it, if you "bear the marks of Jesus on your bodies" (Galatians 6:17); and if you are "always carrying around in your bodies the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be made visible in your bodies" (2 Corinthians 4:10). And "with complete boldness," see that "Christ [will] be exalted in [your] body, whether [you] live or die" (Philippians 1:20). He dishonors and insults Christ and faith who thinks he is not to be trusted even to the point of death.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording below was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Margin notes:** none in this section.
- **Structure:** Directions 5 to 10 finish a series begun in the previous file. The sub-heading "Directions for the glorifying God in our Lives" is Baxter's own; his numbering restarts at "I." under it. Baxter's heading sentences are bolded here, as in the earlier files.
- **Direction 5, "Use not to shut it out" / "Use will much help you":** Baxter's "use" means habit or practice. Rendered "Do not make a habit of shutting it out" and "Practice will help you much."
- **Direction 6:** "subserve" is "serve." "Eucharist" and "eucharistical" (eucharistic) are kept; the word comes from the Greek for "thanksgiving," which is Baxter's point. "Primitive church" is "early church." "Laudatory exercises" is "exercises of praise." The claim that the early church celebrated the sacrament every Lord's Day is Baxter's, not checked here.
- **Direction 7 (Scripture):**
  - **"oracles of God" / Ephesians 4:29 / 1 Peter 4:11:** Baxter's phrase "oracles of God" is the King James wording of 1 Peter 4:11 ("as the oracles of God"), which he cites after it; the reference he prints just before it, Ephesians 4:29, fits "speak to edification." The NET of 1 Peter 4:11 reads "Whoever speaks, let it be with God's words." We kept Baxter's quoted words "oracles of God" because they are not in the NET verse as such, and we kept his order of references. NET Ephesians 4:29: "...only what is beneficial for the building up of the one in need, that it would give grace to those who hear."
  - **Psalm 29:9:** the King James has "In his temple doth every one speak of his glory." This is the sense Baxter leans on (speaking of God's glory). The NET has "Everyone in his temple says, 'Majestic!'", an exclamation of praise, not an account of his glory. The sense of praise remains, but the "speak of" is gone.
  - **Psalm 35:28:** King James "My tongue shall speak of thy righteousness, and of thy praises all the day long." The NET has "I will tell others about your justice, and praise you all day long": no "tongue," and "justice" for "righteousness."
  - **Psalm 145:6, 11, 21:** Baxter joins verses 6, 11, (part of) 12, and 21 in one quotation; his clause "to make known to the sons of men his mighty acts, and the glorious majesty of his kingdom" is verse 12. The NET renders it "so that mankind might acknowledge your mighty acts and the majestic splendor of your kingdom" ("acknowledge" where the King James has "make known"), and verse 21 reads "Let all who live praise" where the King James has "let all flesh bless." NET verse 6 continues "I will declare your great deeds," which Baxter does not quote. I have used "..." where he skips.
  - **Psalm 105:2, 3:** King James "Talk ye of all his wondrous works: glory ye in his holy name." NET: "Tell about all his miraculous deeds. Boast about his holy name." The NET has also "Sing to him. Make music to him" before "Tell," which he omits.
  - **Holy conversation (heading):** Baxter's "holy conference" is "holy conversation" (talk), not "conduct."
- **Direction 8:** "common" (ordinary) is rendered "ordinary" throughout, since Baxter sets "common" against "holy" (set apart from common use). "Slight conceits" is "slight notions." "Accurate" sermons are "careful" ones; "affected strain" is "affected manner."
- **Direction 9:** "unskilfully" is "unskillfully." "Parts" (a person's abilities) is "abilities." "Captious" is "fault-finding"; "judicious" is "discerning." "Discourses" is "talks"; "meet expressions" is "fitting expressions." "Their honest meanings" is kept in sense ("honest meanings and intentions").
- **Direction 10:** The quotation "Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain..." has no reference in the source. It is Exodus 20:7 (not in Baxter's text); the NET reads "You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain, for the Lord will not hold guiltless anyone who takes his name in vain." "Unmeet" is "improper." "Conveniently manifested" is "suitably shown." "Some superiors" are people of higher rank; Baxter leaves them unnamed.
- **Direction 1 (new series):**
  - **HOLINESS TO THE LORD:** printed in capitals in the source. It is an unmarked allusion to the inscription on the high priest's turban (compare Exodus 28:36; Zechariah 14:20; not given by Baxter, and not checked against the NET here).
  - **"venerable":** rendered "worthy of reverence."
  - **"daily converse is with God":** "converse" is fellowship or dealing, rendered "daily fellowship." **"motives fetched from":** "drawn from." **"actuated":** "set in motion." **"tenor of your lives":** "the course of your lives."
  - **Holy Ghost** is "Holy Spirit" (glossary decision). **Carnal** and **fleshly** are kept as Baxter's terms.
  - **Notes [131] and [132]:** [131] is a quotation attributed to "Paul Scaliger" (p. 728); we have not identified the work or confirmed which Scaliger is meant. [132] is from Acosta, probably the José de Acosta whom Baxter cites elsewhere on the conversion of the native peoples of the Americas (book 4, chapter 18); not confirmed here. Baxter uses it to say that preaching about heaven is not believed when the preacher's conduct contradicts it.
- **Direction 2:**
  - **"sensuality":** "indulgence in physical pleasure" (glossary decision). **"conscious":** "aware." **"convinced":** kept (persuaded). **"conversations are above":** "conduct is above" (compare Philippians 3:20, an unmarked allusion; not checked against the NET).
  - **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "laying up a treasure in heaven" (Matthew 6:20); "die the death of the righteous, and ... my last end ... like his" (Numbers 23:10). We have not fetched or compared the NET for these.
  - **"worms":** "sinful worms" (Direction 4) is Baxter's phrase for human beings; kept.
- **Direction 3:**
  - **2 Corinthians 4:18:** NET "we are not looking at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen. For what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal." The King James has "temporal." Baxter's parenthetical comment sits inside the quotation; I kept it in his place.
  - **Swaps:** "contemn" is "despise"; "the excellency" is "the excellence"; "vanities" is "empty things"; "inconsiderable" is "insignificant"; "felicity" is "happiness"; "visor" is "mask"; "usage" (treatment) is "treatment"; "account" is "count" or "regard." **Kept:** "worldling," "usurp." "Further than as it tends to his Master's service" is kept in sense.
- **Direction 4:**
  - **Hebrews 11:6:** Baxter's "there is a God" and "he is the rewarder of them that diligently seek him" are King James wording. The NET has "believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him." The NET has no "diligently" here, and "exists" for "there is a God." The sense Baxter wants (faith in God and in his rewards) is the same, though "diligently" is lost.
  - **Psalm 58:11:** NET "Then observers will say, 'Yes indeed, the godly are rewarded. Yes indeed, there is a God who judges in the earth.'" King James "Verily there is a reward for the righteous." The NET says the godly "are rewarded"; "righteous" is "godly." Baxter's "and who at last will judge the world in righteousness" is his own addition, outside the quotation.
  - **2 Thessalonians 1:4–7:** Baxter's quotation is the King James text turned into third person ("their faith ... they endured") with some changes, so it is his paraphrase and I kept his wording, modernized. NET: "As a result we ourselves boast about you in the churches of God for your perseverance and faith in all the persecutions and afflictions you are enduring. This is evidence of God's righteous judgment, to make you worthy of the kingdom of God, for which in fact you are suffering. For it is right for God to repay with affliction those who afflict you, and to you who are being afflicted to give rest together with us when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels." Two differences matter: Baxter's "accounted worthy" (King James) against the NET "to make you worthy"; and his "rest with his saints" against the NET's "rest together with us." "Recompense" is "repay" in my rendering.
  - **1 Peter 4:14:** The NET reads "If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory, who is the Spirit of God, rests on you." The final clause that Baxter quotes ("on their part he is evil spoken of, but on your part he is glorified") is in the King James but not in the NET text, so it is not NET wording. I kept his words, modernized ("spoken ill of"), after the NET words. "Happy are ye" is "you are blessed." **1 Peter 4:16:** NET wording ("verse 16" is Baxter's "ver. 16").
  - **Matthew 5:11–12:** a bare reference in Baxter, given by him without a quotation. Converted to modern form.
  - **Daniel 3 ("the three witnesses"):** Baxter gives only the chapter; the words are at verses 16–17. The King James has "We are not careful, O king, to answer thee in this matter: the God whom we serve is able to deliver us." **This changes the sense Baxter leans on:** the NET has "If our God whom we are serving exists, he is able to rescue us from the furnace of blazing fire." The King James takes the God they serve as an established fact; the NET has the "if ... exists" form, which is a conditional. (The NET's own notes may discuss this; we have not read them.) "Not careful ... to answer" means "not anxious to answer." I used "..." where Baxter's quotation stops; the NET continues "from the furnace of blazing fire, and he will rescue us, O king, from your power as well."
  - **Hebrews 11:35–37:** the list "cruel mockings and scourgings, of bonds and imprisonment, to be destitute and afflicted, yes, and tortured, not accepting deliverance" is an unmarked allusion to Hebrews 11:35–37. Not quoted from the NET.
  - **Isaiah 24:14–15:** **This changes the sense Baxter leans on.** The King James reads "Glorify ye the Lord in the fires, even the name of the Lord God of Israel in the isles of the sea." Baxter uses "in the fires" for suffering saints who glorify God in their fires of trial, and he addresses "afflicted saints." The NET has "So in the east extol the Lord, along the seacoasts extol the fame of the Lord God of Israel." The NET has "in the west" and "in the east," not "in the fires." So the NET gives no support to the "fires" reading, and its "They lift their voices" is a statement, not a command ("Lift up your voices"). His interjection "O you afflicted saints" is kept, and it reads awkwardly against the NET's third person. The Hebrew word behind the King James "fires" is uncertain; we have not checked the NET's note.
  - **Paul and Silas, "feet in the stocks":** an unmarked allusion (Acts 16:24–25).
  - **1 Corinthians 6:19–20:** Baxter's "you are not your own, you are bought with a price; therefore glorify God in your bodies and spirits which are his" is a blend of verses 19 and 20 and the King James. The NET has "you are not your own? For you were bought at a price. Therefore glorify God with your body." The NET lacks the King James clause "and in your spirit, which are God's." I used the NET text and put Baxter's extra words in brackets so the added words are visible.
  - **Galatians 6:17:** NET "I bear the marks of Jesus on my body." Baxter has "bear in your bodies the marks of the Lord Jesus," so I changed the person ("bear ... on your bodies") to fit his sentence; the NET has "Jesus," not "the Lord Jesus."
  - **2 Corinthians 4:10:** NET "always carrying around in our body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be made visible in our body." Baxter has "the dying of the Lord Jesus," "manifested," and "in your bodies"; I changed "our" to "your" as he does. The King James "dying" and the NET "death" differ slightly in wording.
  - **Philippians 1:20:** NET "with complete boldness, even now as always, Christ will be exalted in my body, whether I live or die." Baxter's "magnified" is "exalted," and his "in your bodies, whether it be by life or death" is adapted from Paul's first-person words; the bracketed words in the text mark my changes ("will," "your," "you").
  - **Last sentence:** "that thinks he is not to be trusted even unto the death" is kept in sense: someone who thinks Christ cannot be trusted even when it costs his life.
  - **Other swaps:** "threatenings" is kept as "threatenings"; "worldlings" and "usurp" as noted above.
