# Chapter 8: Part 1, Directions 12–22 (seeing God in all things, and guarding the senses)

**Direction 12. First therefore see that you always look upon all things perceptible to the senses as the products of the will of the invisible God, depending on him more than the sunshine does upon the sun; and never see or taste a creature separately from God.**

Will you know what a plant is, and not know that it is the earth that bears and nourishes it? Will you know what a fish is, and yet be ignorant that it lives in the water? Will you know what a branch or fruit is, and yet not know that it grows on the tree? The nature of things cannot be known without the knowledge of their causes and respective parts. It is as good as no knowledge to know incoherent scraps and pieces. To know a hand as no part of the body, or an eye or nose without knowing a head, or a body without knowing its life or soul, is not to know it, for you make it another thing. It is the difference between a wise man and a fool, that *sapiens respicit ad plura, insipiens ad pauciora*: a wise man looks comprehensively to things as they are joined together, and takes all together, and leaves out nothing that is useful to his end; but a fool sees one thing, and overlooks another which is necessary to the true knowledge or use of that which he sees. See God as the cause and life of everything you see. As a carcass is only a ghastly sight without the soul, and quickly decays and stinks when it is separated; so the creature without God is an unlovely sight, and quickly decays and becomes a snare or annoyance to you. God is the beauty of all that is beautiful, and the strength of all that is strong, and the glory of the sun and all that is glorious, and the wisdom of all that is wise, and the goodness of all that is good, as being the only original, total cause of all. You play the brutes when you see the creature, and overlook its Maker, from whom it is, whatever it is. Will you see the sundial, and overlook the sun? Remember it is the use of every creature to show you God, and therefore it is the use of every sense to promote the knowledge of him.

**Direction 13. See God as the Conductor, Orderer, and Disposer of all the creatures, according to their natures, as moved necessarily or freely; and do not behold any of the motions or events of the world without observing the involvement and overruling hand of God.**

Sense reaches only to the effects and events; but reason and faith can see the First Cause and Disposer of all. Again, I tell you that if you look only on the parts of things by sense, and do not see God that sets all together, and does his work by those that never dream of it, you see only the several wheels and pieces of a clock or watch, and do not know him that made and keeps it, that sets on the weight, and winds it up, to fit his ends. Joseph could say, God sent me here, when his brothers sold him into Egypt; and David felt his Father's rod in Shimei's curse.

**Direction 14. See God the End of every creature; how all things are ordered for his service; and be sure you do not stop at any creature without referring it to a higher end:**

else, as I have often told you, you will be only like a child or illiterate person, who opens a book, and admires the workmanship of the printer, and the order and good formation of the letters, but never heeds or understands the subject, sense, or end. Or like one that looks on a beautiful picture, and never heeds either him that made it, or him that is represented by it. Or like one that gazes on the sign at an inn door, and praises the workmanship, but does not know that it is set there to direct him to hospitality and necessities within. And this folly and sin is the greater, because it is the very end of God in all his works of creation and providence to reveal himself by them to the intellectual world; and must God show his power, and wisdom, and goodness so wonderfully in the frame of the creation, and in his daily general and particular providence? and shall man, that daily sees all this, overlook the intended use and end? and so make all his glorious work as nothing, or as lost to him? Sense knows no end but its own delight, and the natural happiness of the sensory creature, such as the things of the senses afford; but reason must take up the work where sense ends its stage, and carry all home to him that is the End of all. "For FROM him and THROUGH him and TO him are all things. To him be glory forever! Amen" (Romans 11:36).

**Direction 15. Besides the general use and ultimate end of every creature, work for a clear acquaintance with the particular use and nearer end of everything which you have to do with, by which it is serviceable to your ultimate end, and suppose always that you saw that special use as serving your highest end, as the title written upon each creature.**

As, suppose upon your Bible it were written, The word of the living God, to acquaint me with himself and his will, that I may please, and glorify, and enjoy him forever. And upon your godly friend suppose you saw this title written, A servant of God, that bears his image, and appointed to accompany and assist me in his service to life everlasting. Upon your food suppose you saw this title written, The provisions of my Father, sent me as from my Savior's hands, not to gratify my love of physical pleasure, and serve my inordinate desires, but to refresh and strengthen my body for his service in my passage to everlasting life. So upon your clothes, your servants, your goods, your cattle, your houses, and everything you have, inscribe thus their proper use and end.

**Direction 16. Know both the final and the intermediate danger of everything that you have to do with; and suppose you always see them written upon everything you see.**

The final danger is hell; the intermediate danger in general is sin; but you must find what sin it is that this creature will be made a temptation to by the devil and the flesh. As, suppose you saw written upon money and riches, The bait of greed and all evil, to pierce me through with many sorrows, and then to damn me. And suppose you saw written upon great buildings, and estates, and honors, and retinues, The great price which the devil would give for souls; and the baits to tempt men to the inordinate love of fleshly pleasures, and to draw their hearts from God and heaven to their damnation. Suppose you always saw written upon beauty, and tempting actions and attire, The bait of lust, by which the devil corrupts the minds of men to their damnation. Suppose you saw written on the playhouse door, The stage of the charlatan of hell, who here cheats men of their precious time, and entices them to empty pursuits, luxury, and damnation, under pretense of instructing them by a shorter and more pleasant way than preachers do. The same I say of gambling, recreations, company: see the particular snare in all.

**Direction 17. To this end be well acquainted with your own particular inclinations and disorders, that you may know what creature is likely to prove most dangerous to you, that there you may keep the strictest watch.**

If you are subject to pride, keep most from the baits of pride, and watch most cautiously against them. If you are subject to greed, watch most against the baits of greed. If you are inclined to lust, away from the sight of alluring objects. The knowledge of your temperament and disease must direct you both in your diet and your medicine.

**Direction 18. Live as in a constant course of obedience; and suppose you saw the law of God also written upon everything you see.**

As when you look on any tempting beauty, suppose you saw this written on the forehead, You shall not lust—Whoremongers and adulterers God will judge—They shall not enter into the kingdom of God. [378] See upon the forbidden dish or cup the prohibition of God, You shall not eat or drink this. See upon money and riches this written, You shall not covet. See upon the face of all the world, "Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him" (1 John 2:15). Thus see the will of God on all things.

**Direction 19. Do not make the objects of sense over-tempting and dangerous to yourselves; but take special care, as much as lies in you, to order all so that you may have as much of the benefit, and as little of the snare of the creature as is possible.**

Would you not be gluttonous pleasers of your appetite? do not choose then too full a table, nor over-pleasant, tempting drinks or dishes, and yet choose those that are most useful to your health. Would you not over-love the world, nor your present house, or lands, or station? Do not be too instrumental yourselves in gilding or sweetening your bait! if you put in the sugar, the devil and the flesh will put in the poison. Will you make all as pleasant and lovely as you can, when you believe that the over-loving of them is the greatest danger to your salvation? Will you be the greatest tempters to yourselves, and then desire God not to lead you into temptation?

**Direction 20. Do not let the tempting object be too near your sense; for nearness enrages the sensory appetite, and gives you an opportunity of sinning.**

Do not come too near the fire if you would not be burned (and yet use prudence in keeping the usefulness of it for warmth, though you avoid the burning). Distance from the snares of pride, and lust, and passion, and other sins, is a most proven remedy, and nearness is their strength.

**Direction 21. Accustom your souls to frequent and familiar exercise about their invisible objects, as well as your senses about theirs.**

And as you are daily and hourly seeing, and tasting, and hearing the creature, so do not be rarely in the humble adoration of him that appears to you in them. Otherwise use will make the creature so familiar to you, and disuse will make God so strange, that by degrees you will wear yourselves out of his acquaintance, and become like carnal, pleasure-loving men, and live all by sense, and forget the holy exercise of the life of faith.

**Direction 22. Do not lose your humble sense of the badness of your hearts, how ready they are as tinder to take the fire of every temptation; and never grow foolhardy and confident of yourselves.**

For your holy fear is necessary to your watchfulness, and your watchfulness is necessary to your escape and safety. Peter's self-confidence betrayed him to deny his Lord. Had Noah, and Lot, and David been more afraid of the sin, they would have been likely to have escaped it. It is a part of the character of the beastly heretics that Jude declaims against, that they were "spots in their love feasts, when they feasted with the church, feeding themselves without fear" (Jude 12). When the knowledge or sense of your weakness and sinful inclination is gone, then fear is gone, and then safety is gone, and your fall is near.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Verses were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where this file begins:** the range opens at the start of Direction 12, in Part 1 of Chapter 8 (general directions for the government of the senses). Direction 12's "First therefore" follows on from the end of Direction 11 in the previous file.
- **creature:** Baxter's word for any created thing (a plant, food, money, a person), not only an animal. Kept throughout.
- **sense / sensible:** "sense" is the bodily senses as a faculty, set against reason and faith. "All things sensible" (Direction 12) is rendered "all things perceptible to the senses," and "such as things sensible afford" (Direction 14) as "such as the things of the senses afford." "Sensitive creature" and "sensitive appetite" become "sensory creature" and "sensory appetite."
- **Direction headings:** each Direction's opening directive is set in bold and the rest in the following paragraph. In Direction 14 the directive sentence runs on with "else, as I have oft told you…"; the bold line ends at Baxter's colon and the sentence continues in the next paragraph without any change to his words.
- ***sapiens respicit ad plura, insipiens ad pauciora*** (Direction 12): kept as printed. (English: the wise man looks to more things, the fool to fewer.) Baxter glosses it himself in the clause that follows. This draft translation was prepared for this project and needs a Latinist's check. We have not identified a source for the saying.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): ever → always (Direction 12); separatedly → separately; parcels → pieces; "as no knowledge" → "as good as no knowledge"; conjunct → joined together; overseeth → overlooks; corrupteth (of a carcass) → decays; whatsoever → whatever; dial → sundial; Conducter → Conductor; interest (God's part in events) → involvement; particles (of things) → parts; poise (a clock's weight) → weight; hither → here; brethren → brothers; oft → often; well-forming → good formation; mindeth → heeds; comely → beautiful; entertainment (food and lodging at an inn) → hospitality; necessaries → necessities; felicity → happiness; labour → work; still (always) → always; subserving → serving; meat (food in general) → food; sensuality → love of physical pleasure; mediate → intermediate; covetousness → greed; attendance (a train of servants) → retinues; mountebank (a quack who sold remedies from a stage) → charlatan; vanity → empty pursuits; nearer (way) → shorter; gaming → gambling; distempers → disorders; like to → likely to; cautelously → cautiously; temper → temperament; physic → medicine; dulcifying → sweetening; as much as in you lieth → as much as lies in you; approved (tested) → proven; they had been like to have escaped → they would have been likely to have escaped; declaimeth → declaims.
- **You play the brutes** (Direction 12): kept; "brutes" means beasts, creatures without reason.
- **intellectual world** (Direction 14): kept; Baxter means rational creatures (humans and angels), who can know God through his works.
- **general and particular providence** (Direction 14): kept as Baxter's technical terms: God's governing of the whole world, and his ordering of each particular event and person.
- **luxury** (Direction 16): kept. In Baxter's day the word often meant lust or self-indulgence more than costly comfort.
- **the playhouse door** (Direction 16): Baxter's attack on the theater is kept as he wrote it; it is his polemic.
- **Whoremongers** (Direction 18): Baxter's blunt word, kept.
- **passion** (Direction 20): kept; strong emotion, especially anger.
- **use … disuse** (Direction 21): "use" means habit or familiarity.
- **inordinate:** kept (excessive, beyond due order).
- **Romans 11:36** (Direction 14): NET wording. Baxter set "OF," "THROUGH," and "TO" in capitals for emphasis; we keep his capitals on the NET's "FROM," "THROUGH," and "TO." The King James "of him" is the NET's "from him"; the sense (God as source) is the same.
- **1 John 2:15** (Direction 18): NET wording. The NET verse ends with a comma; we close the quotation with a period.
- **Jude 12** (Direction 22): Baxter's wording kept, modernized, because the NET reads a different point. The NET has "These men are dangerous reefs at your love feasts, feasting without reverence, feeding only themselves." The King James has "spots in your feasts of charity, when they feast with you, feeding themselves without fear." Baxter's argument in Direction 22 leans on "without fear" (he goes on: "then fear is gone"), and the NET's "without reverence" weakens that link. His "when they feasted with the church" is his own paraphrase of "when they feast with you." "Feasts of charity" is rendered "love feasts," the meal shared by the early church. Baxter's reference was "ver. 12."
- **The inscriptions in Direction 18** are not in quotation marks in the source and are kept in Baxter's wording, modernized ("Thou shalt not lust" → "You shall not lust"; "Thou shalt not covet" → "You shall not covet," which matches the NET of Exodus 20:17). Footnote 378 gives his references. For comparison, the NET has: Romans 7:7, "Do not covet"; Matthew 5:28, "whoever looks at a woman to desire her has already committed adultery with her in his heart"; Ephesians 5:5, "no person who is immoral, impure, or greedy … has any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and God"; Hebrews 13:4, "God will judge sexually immoral people and adulterers." Where Baxter's "Whoremongers and adulterers" echoes the King James of Hebrews 13:4, the NET has "sexually immoral people and adulterers."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording; references not in his text): "God sent me hither," Joseph to his brothers (Genesis 45:5; NET "God sent me ahead of you"); David and Shimei's curse (2 Samuel 16:5–13); "to pierce me through with many sorrows" (1 Timothy 6:10, King James wording; NET "stabbed themselves with many pains"), with "all evil" echoing the same verse; "desire God not to lead you into temptation" (the Lord's Prayer, Matthew 6:13); Peter's denial (Matthew 26:69–75); the falls of Noah (Genesis 9:20–21), Lot (Genesis 19:30–36), and David (2 Samuel 11).
