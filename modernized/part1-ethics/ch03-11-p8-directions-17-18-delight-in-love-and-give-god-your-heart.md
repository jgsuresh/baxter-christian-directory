# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XI, love to God (part 8 of 8)

*This part begins at Direction 17 of Grand Direction 11 (on the love of God) and runs to the end of that Grand Direction. It ends with a one-line margin note that belongs to the next Grand Direction.*

**Direction 17. Think often how delightful a life it would be to you if you could but live in the love of God: and then the delight will provoke desire, and desire will turn your face toward God, until you feel that you love him.**

The love of a friend has its sweetness and delight: and when we love them, we feel such pleasure in our love that we love to love them. How pleasant then would it be to love your God! O blessed, joyful life, if I could but love him as much as I desire to love him! How freely could I leave the ambitious, and the greedy, and the pleasure-loving, and voluptuous, to their doting, delusory, swinish love! How easily could I spare all earthly pleasures! How near should I come to the angelic life! Could I love God as I would love him, it would fill me with continual pleasure, and be the sweetest feast that a soul can have. How easily would it quench all carnal love! How far would it raise me above these transitory things! How much should I despise them, and pity the wretches who know no better, and have their portion in this life! How readily should I obey, and how pleasant would obedience be! How sweet would all my meditations be, when every thought is full of love! How sweet would all my prayers be, when constraining love brought me to God, and dictate and animate every word! How sweet would sacraments be, when my ascending, flaming love should meet that wonderful, descending love which comes from heaven to call me there, and in living bread and spiritual wine is the nourishment and cordial of my soul! How sweet would all my speeches be, when love commanded them, and every word were full of love! How quiet would my conscience be, if it had never any of this accusation against me, to cast in my face, to my shame and confusion, that I am lacking in love to the blessed God! Oh, could I but love God with such a powerful love as his love and goodness should command, I should no more question my sincerity, nor doubt any more of his love to me. How freely then should I acknowledge his grace, and how heartily should I give him thanks for my justification, sanctification, and adoption, which now I mention with doubt and fear! Oh, how it would lift up my soul to his praise, and make it my delight to speak good of his name! What a purifying fire would love be in my breast, to burn up my corruptions! It would endure nothing to enter or stay within me that is contrary to the will and interest of my Lord; but would hate every motion that tends to dishonor and displease him. It would fill my soul with so much of heaven as would make me long to be in heaven, and make death welcome, which is now so terrible. Instead of these withdrawing, shrinking fears, I should desire to depart and to be with Christ, as being best of all. Oh, how easily should I bear any burden of reproach, or loss, or want, when I thus loved God and was assured of his love! How light would the cross be! And how honorable and joyful would it seem, to be imprisoned, reviled, spit upon, and struck for the sake of Christ! How desirable would the flames of martyrdom seem, for the testifying of my love to him who loved me at dearer rates than I can love him! Lord, is there no more of this blessed life of love to be attained here on earth? When all the world reveals your goodness; when your Son has come down to declare your love, in so full and wonderful a manner; when your word has opened us a window into heaven, where afar off we may discern your glory; yet, shall our hearts be clods, and ice? O pity this unloving, unnatural soul! this dead, insensible, unaffectionate soul! Teach me, by your Spirit, the art of love! Love me, not only so as to convince me that I have abundant cause to love you above all, but love me, so as to constrain me to it, by the magnetic, attractive power of your goodness, and the insuperable operations of your omnipotent love.

**Direction 18. In your meditations upon all these incentives of love, preach them over earnestly to your heart, and expostulate and plead with it by way of soliloquy, until you feel the fire begin to burn.**

Do not only think on the arguments of love, but dispute it out with your conscience, and by expostulating, earnest reasonings with your heart, make an effort to move it. There is much more moving force in this earnest talking to ourselves than in mere thinking, that does not break out into mental words. Imitate the most powerful preacher you ever knew: and just as he pleads the case with his hearers, and urges the truth and duty on them, by reason and importunity, so do you in secret with yourself. There is more in this than most Christians are aware of, or are accustomed to practice. It is a great part of a Christian's skill and duty to be a good preacher to himself. This is a lawful and a profitable way of preaching. Nobody here can question your call, or deny you a license, or silence you, if you do not silence yourself. Two or three sermons a week from others is a fair proportion; but two or three sermons a day from yourself is ordinarily too little. Therefore, I have added soliloquies to many of these directions for love, to show you how, by such pleadings with yourselves, to move your hearts, and kindle love.

And oh, that this might be the happy fruit of these directions with you who are now reading or hearing them! that you would but offer up your flaming heart to Jesus Christ our great High Priest, to be presented as an acceptable sacrifice to God! Or, if it does not flame in love as you desire, yet give it up to the Holy Spirit to increase the flames. You little know how much God values a heart. He calls to you himself, "Give me your heart, my son" (Proverbs 23:26). Without it, he does not care for anything that you can give him: he does not care for your fairest words without it: he does not care for your loudest prayers without it: he does not care for your costliest alms or sacrifices, if he does not have your heart. "If you give all your goods to feed the poor, and give your body to be burned, and do not have love, it will profit you nothing. If you speak with the tongue of men and angels, and do not have love, you are but as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal. If you can prophesy and preach to admiration, and understand all mysteries and knowledge, and have faith to do miracles, and do not have love, you are nothing" (1 Corinthians 13:1-3). You have but a shadow, and lack what is the substance and life of all. Come then, and make an agreement with God, and resolve now to offer him your heart. He asks you for nothing that you do not have: it is not for riches or lands that he seeks you; for then the poor might say as Peter did, "I have no silver or gold": give him only what you have, and it is enough. He knows that it is a polluted, sinful heart; but give it to him, and he will make it clean. He knows that it is an unloving heart, that has stood out too long; but give it to him still, and he will pardon and accept it. He knows that it is an unworthy heart; but give it to him, and he will be its worth: only see that you give it to him entirely and unreservedly; for he will not bargain with the devil, or the world, for the dividing of your heart between them. A half-heart and a hollow heart, that is only lent to him until fleshly interest or necessity calls for it again, he will not accept. Only resign it to him, and do but consent that your heart be his, and entirely and absolutely his, and he will take it and use it as his own. It is his own by title: let it also be so by your consent. If God does not have it, who shall have it? Shall the world, or pride, or fleshly lust? Did they make it, or did they purchase it? Will they be better to you in the time of your extremity? Do they bid more for your heart than God will give you? He will give you his Son, and his Spirit, and image, and the forgiveness of all your sins: if the greatest gain, or honor, or pleasure will win it and purchase it, he will have it: if heaven will buy it, he will not break with you for the price. Have the world and sin a greater price than this to give you? And what do you think he will do with your heart? and how will he use it, that you are loath to give it to him? Will he blind it, and deceive it, and corrupt it, and abuse it, and at last torment it, as Satan will do? No; he will more illuminate it, and cleanse it, and bring it to life (Psalm 51:10; Ephesians 2:1; Jeremiah 24:7). He will make it new, and heal and save it (Ezekiel 36:26; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Titus 3:3, 5, and 2:14). He will advance and honor it, with the highest relationships, employments, and delight; for Christ has said, "If anyone wants to serve me, he must follow me, and where I am, my servant will be too. If anyone serves me, the Father will honor him" (John 12:26). He will love it, and govern it, and comfort it, and the heart that is delivered to him shall be kept near to his own: "For the Father himself loves you," says Christ, "because you have loved me" (John 16:27). Whereas if you do not deliver your heart to him, it will feed on the poison of luscious emptiness, which will gripe and tear it when it is down; it will be like a house that nothing lives in but dogs, and flies, and worms, and snakes; it will be like one who is lost in the wilderness, or in the night, who tires himself in seeking the way home, and the longer the worse; despair and restlessness will be its companions forever. Let me now once more in the name of God address your heart. I will not use his commands or threatenings to you now, (though these as backups must be used,) because love must have attractive arguments, and is not raised by mere authority or fear: if there is not love and goodness enough in God to deserve the highest affections of every rational creature, then let him go, and give your heart to one who is better. Hear how God pleads his own cause with an unloving, unthankful people, "Hear the Lord's accusation, you mountains... My people, how have I wronged you? How have I wearied you? Answer me!" (Micah 6:2-3). What is there in him to turn away your heart? Let malice itself say the worst, without notorious impudence, against him: what has he ever done that deserves your disaffection and neglect? What would you have to win a heart that is not in him? For which of his mercies or excellencies is it that you despise and abuse him like this? What do you lack that he cannot, or will not, give you? Does not your tongue speak honorably of his goodness, while your heart contradicts it, and denies all? What have you found that will prove better to you? Is it sin or God that must be your glory, rest, and joy, if you will not be a firebrand of restlessness and misery forever? What do you say yet, sinner? Shall God, or the world and fleshly pleasures, have your heart? Are you not yet convinced which best deserves it, and which will be best to it? Can you be a loser by him? Will he make it worse, and sin make it better? Or will you ever have cause to repent of giving it up to God, as you have of giving it to the world and sin? I tell you, if God does not have your heart, it would be well for you if you had no heart.--I would a thousand times rather have the heart of a dog, or the basest creature, than the heart of that man who follows his fleshly lusts, and is not sincerely delivered up to God, through Christ.

If I have not prevailed with your hearts for God, by all that I have said, your consciences shall yet bear me witness that I showed you God's title, and love, and goodness, and said what ought to have prevailed; and you shall find before long who it is that will have the worst of it: but if you resolve and give them at once to God, he will welcome them, and sanctify, and save them; and this happy day and work will be the angels' joy (Luke 15:7, 10); and it will be my joy, and especially your own everlasting joy.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Last line (margin note):** "To trust in God" is the margin summary for the next Grand Direction (XII, "Trust God with that soul and body..."), whose heading begins on the line after this range. It is kept here because it falls inside the line range; it belongs to the next file.
- **Direction numbers:** Baxter prints "_Direct._ XVII." and "XVIII."; rendered Direction 17 and 18 within Grand Direction 11. The `--` run-in after each Direction heading is dropped; the heading sentence is bold and the body follows as its own paragraph.
- **Proverbs 23:26:** Baxter has "My son, give me thy heart" (King James order). The NET has "Give me your heart, my son"; nothing in his argument depends on the order.
- **1 Corinthians 13:1-3:** Baxter quotes in the order of the King James (verse 3 first, then 1, then 2), and with "give thy body to be burned" and "sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal," "to feed the poor," and "faith to do miracles." The quotation is Baxter's own blend of the three verses, so we keep his wording, modernized, in his order. The NET reads: "If I speak in the tongues of men and of angels, but I do not have love, I am a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. And if I have prophecy, and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith so that I can remove mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing. If I give away everything I own, and if I give over my body in order to boast, but do not have love, I receive no benefit." The NET has "give over my body in order to boast" where the King James has "to be burned" (a difference in the underlying Greek manuscripts, which we have not checked in detail); Baxter's later sentences do not lean on that clause. Baxter's text is in the second person ("If thou speak... thou art but as sounding brass"); the NET is in the first person ("If I speak... I am a noisy gong"). Baxter's words keep the second person, so the address to the reader stays. Baxter's "profit thee nothing" is the NET's "I receive no benefit," and "to admiration" (admirably, so as to be admired) is kept as his phrase. NET FLAG: "to be burned" (King James) versus "in order to boast" (NET) changes the image of martyrdom; Baxter's quotation uses the King James sense, and the NET reading is a different point.
- **Acts 3:6:** Baxter's "Silver and gold have I none" has no reference in the source; the reference given here is not in Baxter's text. We use the NET wording of the phrase, "I have no silver or gold" (the NET continues "but what I do have I give you").
- **Psalm 51:10; Ephesians 2:1; Jeremiah 24:7; Ezekiel 36:26; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Titus 3:3, 5, 2:14:** Baxter gives only the references, as proof texts for his claims (illumine, cleanse, quicken, make new, heal, save). Not quoted. Titus is printed "Tit. iii. 3, 5, and ii. 14."
- **John 12:26; John 16:27:** NET wording; references moved after the quotations (Baxter prints them before). Baxter's "let him follow me" and "there shall also my servant be" become "he must follow me" and "my servant will be too." In John 16:27 the NET continues "and have believed that I came from God"; Baxter stops at "loved me." Baxter's "saith Christ" is an insertion inside the quotation.
- **Micah 6:2-3:** NET has "accusation" where Baxter's King James has "controversy" (a legal dispute, the sense of a lawsuit between God and his people); and "Answer me!" where Baxter has "testify against me." Baxter leans on God as pleading a case, which both readings keep. The "..." marks the NET's poetic second line ("you enduring foundations of the earth") and the lines that follow it in verse 2 ("For the Lord has a case against his people; he has a dispute with Israel!"), which Baxter skips. The NET has no "O" before "my people"; Baxter's "O my people" is the King James.
- **Luke 15:7, 10:** Baxter gives only the references to the angels' joy at a sinner's repentance.
- **Philippians 1:23:** unmarked allusion, "desire to depart and to be with Christ, as being best of all." The NET reads "a desire to depart and be with Christ, which is better by far." We left Baxter's paraphrase in his words.
- **Other unmarked allusions:** "to be with Christ" (see Philippians 1:23 above) and "the angels' joy" (cited at Luke 15). We do not identify other allusions (for example, in "living bread and spiritual wine") with confidence.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): complacency, delight; covetous, greedy; sensual, pleasure-loving; angelical, angelic; magnetical, magnetic; contemn, despise; indite, dictate ("animate" kept); wanting in, lacking in; tendeth, tends; dishonour, dishonor; buffeted, struck; unkind, unloving (he means lacking in love or gratitude); disaffected, unaffectionate (lacking affection); expostulate kept (plead earnestly); bare cogitation, mere thinking; use to practice, are accustomed to practice; gainful, profitable; quicken, bring to life; affect (to move the feelings), move; gripe kept (it means to cause sharp pain); luscious vanity, luscious emptiness; bespeak, address; seconds, backups (supporting arguments); entertain, welcome; presently, at once; ere long, before long; unfeignedly, sincerely; "set by," values; "reasonable creature," rational creature; "break with thee for the price," break with you for the price (to break off dealings over the price); "tireth himself in seeking," tires himself in seeking.
- **Terms kept:** *carnal* (unspiritual); *affections* (emotions and desires, a technical term for Baxter); *doting, delusory, swinish* (his words); *convince* (in "convince me that I have abundant cause" and "not yet convinced," he means show or persuade); *cordial* (a strengthening drink); *importunity* (urgent, persistent pleading); *soliloquy* (here, speaking to oneself); *interest* (in "will and interest of my Lord," what concerns him); *title* (right of ownership; "his own by title"); *disaffection* (lack of love); *unnatural* (against nature, as ingratitude to a Creator); *impudence*. "He will be its worth": left as Baxter wrote; the sense is that God supplies the worth the heart lacks.
- **"call" and "license"** (Direction 18): in Baxter's day a minister needed a call and ordinary authorization to preach; his point is that no one can challenge your right to preach to yourself.
- **"fleshly interest or necessity":** left as written.
- **Pronoun shifts:** Baxter moves between "thou" and "you" in this section; all are rendered "you." The first long paragraph and its prayer are in the first person; kept.
- **Odd syntax:** "Have the world and sin a greater price than this to give you?" kept as a question in Baxter's inverted form. "Let malice itself say the worst against him, without notorious impudence" is kept in his order ("Let malice itself say the worst, without notorious impudence, against him").
- **Paragraphs:** the long paragraph beginning "And oh that this might be..." is one paragraph in the source and is kept whole.
