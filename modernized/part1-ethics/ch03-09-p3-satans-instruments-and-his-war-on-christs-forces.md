# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 3 of 13)

*This section begins partway through Grand Direction 9 ("Spend all your days in a skilful, vigilant ..."), in the middle of Baxter's lettered account of the devil's method; it opens at the numbered point "IV." and continues into "V." Baxter's Roman-numeral points are kept as he has them. The section ends partway through point V, item 8; the next file continues it.*

IV. By what has been said, you may understand what kind of officers and instruments the tempter uses. 1. He commonly uses men who are themselves first deceived and corrupted, as suitable instruments to deceive and corrupt others. These will carry it on with confidence and violence; the work seems natural to them, they are so suited to it: they will be willing to make other men of their mind, and to have the company of others in their way. A drunkard is suited to make a drunkard; and a filthy fornicator to entice another into the sin; and a gambler to make a gambler; and a person given to frivolity and time-wasting to draw another to waste his time in frivolity and foolish amusements: an ambitious or proud person is suited to kindle that fire in others; a swearer is most suited to make a swearer; and so of many other sins.

2. The devil usually chooses for his instruments men who have no great tenderness of conscience, or fear of sinning or of hurting souls. He would have no such cowards in his army as men who fear God are, as far as his ends go: it must be men who will venture upon hell themselves, and do not much fear the loss of their own souls; and therefore they must not be too tender or fearful of destroying others. Butchers and soldiers must not be chosen out of too tender or loving a sort of people; such are not suited to go through his work.

3. He usually chooses instruments who are most deeply engaged in his cause; whose advancement, and honor, and gain, and carnal interest shall be to them what nature is to a dog, or wolf, or fox, or other ravenous creature: who think it a loss, or danger, or suffering to them, if others are not hindered in good, or made as bad as they are. Thus Demetrius and the other craftsmen who lived upon the trade, are the most suitable to plead Diana's cause, and stir up the people against the apostles (Acts 19:24, 38, 39). And the Jews were the most suitable instruments to persecute Christ, who thought that if they "allow him to go on in this way, everyone will believe in him, and the Romans will come and take away our sanctuary and our nation"; and that it was "more to your advantage to have one man die for the people than for the whole nation to perish" (John 11:48-50). And Pilate was the most suitable instrument to condemn him, who feared that he would otherwise be taken to be no friend of Caesar's. And Pharaoh was the most suitable instrument to persecute the Israelites, who was likely to lose by their departure.

4. When he can he chooses such instruments as are much about us, and nearest to us, who have opportunity to be often speaking to us, when others have no opportunity to help us: the fire that is nearest to the wood or thatch is more likely to burn it than one that is far off: nearness and opportunity are very great advantages.

5. If it is possible, he will choose such instruments as have the greatest abilities to do him service: one man of great intelligence, and learning, and eloquence, who is nimble in disputing, and can make almost any cause seem good which he defends, or bad which he opposes, is able to do more service for the devil than a hundred simpletons.

6. If possible, he will choose the rulers of the world to be his instruments; who shall command men, and threaten them with imprisonment, banishment, confiscation, or death, if they will not sin: as the king of Babylon did to the three witnesses and Daniel (Daniel 3 and 6), and as all persecutors have done in all ages against the holy seed. For he knows that (though not with a Job, yet with a carnal person) "Skin for skin! Indeed, a man will give up all that he has to save his life." And therefore, those who have the power of life, and liberty, and estate, have carnal men by the handle, to rule them.

7. He makes the rich his instruments; who, having the wealth of the world, are able to reward and pay evil-doers; and are able to oppress those who will not please them. Landlords and rich men can do the devil more service than many of the poor: they are the Judases who carry the money bag. As the ox will follow the one who carries the hay, and the horse will follow the one who carries the feed, and the dog will follow the one who feeds him, and the crow will be where the carrion is; so carnal persons will follow and obey the one who holds the purse.

8. The devil, if he can, will make those his instruments whom he sees we most esteem and respect; persons whom we think most wise and suitable to be our counselors: we will take from these what we would suspect from others.

9. He will get our relations, and those who have our hearts most, to be his instruments. A husband, or a wife, or a Delilah, can do more than others; and so can a close friend whom we dearly love: when all their interest in our affections is turned over to the devil's service, it may do much. Therefore we see that husbands and wives, if they love completely, usually settle into the same religion, opinion, or way, though when they were first married they differed from each other.

10. As often as he can, the devil makes the multitude his instrument; so that the crowd and noise may carry us on, and make men bold, and put away their fear of punishment.

11. He is very eager to make the ambassadors of Christ his prisoners, and to pay them to speak against their Master's cause: so that in Christ's name they may deceive the helpless flock, "teaching perversions of the truth to draw the disciples away after them" (Acts 20:30). Sometimes by pretense of his authority and commission; making poor people believe that not to hear them, and obey them in their errors, is to be disobedient rejecters of Christ: (and thus the Romish party carry it.) Sometimes by their abilities and plausible, persuasive speeches: and sometimes by their fervor, frightening people into error. And by these two ways most heretics succeed. No one serves Satan so successfully as a false or bribed minister of Christ.

12. He is exceedingly eager to make parents themselves his instruments for their children's sin and ruin; and, alas, how commonly he succeeds! He knows that parents have them under their hands at the most pliable, malleable age; and that they have a combination of almost all advantages: they have the purse and inheritance of their children in their power; they have the interest of love, and respect, and esteem; they are always with them, and can often be urging them; they have the rod, and can compel them. Many thousands are in hell, through the means of their own parents: such cruel monsters will they be to the souls of any others, who are first so to their own. If the devil can get the parents to be cursers, swearers, gamblers, drunkards, worldlings, proud, mockers or railers at a holy life, what a snare is here for the poor children!

V. In the method of Satan, the next thing is to show you how he works to keep off all the forces of Christ, which should resist him and destroy his work, and to frustrate their efforts, and fortify himself. And among many others, these means are notable:

1. He would do what he can to weaken even natural reason, so that men may be dull and incapable of good. And it is lamentable to observe how hard it is to make some people either understand or care. And a brutish kind of upbringing does much to this: and so does habit in lives of indulgence in physical pleasure; even turn men into brutes.

2. He does what he can to hinder parents and masters from doing their part, in the instructing and admonishing of children and servants, and dealing wisely and zealously with them for their salvation: either he will keep parents and masters ignorant and unable, or he will make them wicked and unwilling, and perhaps engage them to oppose their children in all that is good; or he will make them like Eli, lax and negligent, indifferent, formal, cold, and dull; and so keep them from saving their children's or servants' souls.

3. He does all that he possibly can to keep the sinner in security, presumption, and senselessness, even asleep in sin; and to that end to keep him quiet and in the dark, without any light or noise which may awaken him; so that he may live asleep, as without a God, a Christ, a heaven, a soul, or any such thing to think about. His great care is to keep him from considering: and therefore he keeps him always in company, or amusement, or business, and will not let him be often alone, nor retire into a sober conversation with his conscience, or serious thoughts of the life to come.

4. He does his best to keep soul-searching, lively ministers out of the country, or out of that place; and to silence them, if there are any such; and to keep the sinner under some ignorant or dead-hearted minister, who does not himself have that faith, or repentance, or life, or love, or holiness, or zeal, which he should be a means to work in others; and he will do his utmost to draw him to be a leader of men to sin.

5. He does his worst to make ministers weak, to disgrace the cause of Christ, and hinder his work, by their bungling and unsuccessful management, so that there may be none to stand up against sin, but some unlearned or half-witted men, who can scarcely speak sense, or will provoke contempt or laughter in the hearers.

6. He does his worst to make ministers scandalous, so that when they tell men of their sin and duty, they may think such ministers do not mean what they say, and do not believe themselves, or make no great matter of it; but speak for custom, reputation, or for their pay. And so that the people, by the wicked lives of the preachers, may be emboldened to disobey their teaching, and to imitate them, and live without repentance.

7. He will work to load the ablest ministers with reproaches and slanders, which thousands will hear, who never hear the truth in their defense: and so making them hated, the people will receive no more good by their preaching than from a Turk or Jew, until the very truth itself prevails for itself. And to this end especially he does all that he can to stir up continual "divisions in the church"; so that while every party is engaged against the other, the interest of their several causes may make them think it necessary to make the chief who are against them seem hated or contemptible to the people, so that they may be able to do their cause and them no harm: and so they disable them from serving Christ and saving souls, so that they may disable them from hurting themselves, or their faction, or their powerless cause.

8. He does what he can to keep the most holy ministers under persecution; so that they may be like the wounded deer, whom all the rest of the herd will shun; or like a mauled dog, whom the rest will fall upon; or so that the people may be afraid to hear them, lest they suffer with them; or may come to them only as Nicodemus did to Christ, by night.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). The NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **John 11:48-50:** Baxter's quotation follows the King James ("let him alone, all men would believe on him ... take away both our place and nation ... it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not"). The NET has "sanctuary" where the King James has "place," which slightly narrows what the leaders say they fear to lose (the temple, in the NET's reading). The NET also reads "more to your advantage" where the King James has "expedient"; Baxter's point (self-interest drove them) is unchanged. In the NET the second part is Caiaphas's reply to the council, so its "your" is not first-person as Baxter's "for them" was; we kept Baxter's framing ("who thought that if they ...") and the NET words, which leaves the pronouns ("our," "your") as in the NET's direct speech.
- **John 11:48-50, reference:** Baxter printed "John xi. 48, 49," but the words "expedient ... one man die" are in verse 50 in the NET. We show 48-50 in the text.
- **Acts 20:30:** Baxter's "speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them" is the King James. The NET has "teaching perversions of the truth to draw the disciples away after them." Baxter's argument (false ministers lead people astray) is unaffected.
- **"Skin for skin ..." (Job 2:4):** Baxter gives no reference; the NET verse is Job 2:4, which we have added here only (not in Baxter's text). It is Satan's claim about Job. Baxter's words differ from the NET only in wording (King James: "all that a man hath will he give for his life"). Baxter says it does not hold for a Job but does for a carnal person. In the text we quote the full NET sentence ("Indeed," included) rather than using an ellipsis.
- **Acts 19:24, 38, 39; Daniel 3 and 6:** references only, as Baxter gave them (converted to modern form); nothing quoted.
- **"divisions in the church"** (V.7): Baxter puts these words in quotation marks without a reference. We have not identified the verse (1 Corinthians 11:18 is a possible candidate, not confirmed) and have not given a NET wording.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "the Judases that bear the bag" (John 12:6); Delilah (Judges 16); Eli's laxness (1 Samuel 2-3); Nicodemus coming by night (John 3:2; 19:39).
- **Section opening:** this range begins partway through Direction IX, at Baxter's point IV; the numbered items "1." to "12." under IV and "1." to "8." under V stay as he numbered them.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): fit / fittest to suitable / most suitable (or "suited"); wanton time-waster, wantonness, sports to given to frivolity and time-wasting, frivolity, amusements; gamester to gambler; preferment to advancement; elocution to eloquence; wit to intelligence; idiots to simpletons (meaning the untrained or ignorant, not a medical term); bear the bag to carry the money bag; provender to feed; valiant to bold; close in (same religion) to settle into; made over to turned over; bosom friend to close friend; regard (verb) to care; remiss to lax; counsellors to counselors; silly flock to helpless flock (his "silly" has its older sense of helpless or simple); parts to abilities; fervency to fervor; pretence to pretense; ductile, malleable to pliable, malleable; concurrence to combination; estimation to esteem; solicitings to urging; derider to mocker; blockish to dull; beastly education to brutish upbringing; custom in sensual courses to habit in lives of indulgence in physical pleasure; conference to conversation; foment to stir up; worried dog to mauled dog; impotent to powerless; hire (noun and verb) to pay; credit to reputation; odious to hated.
- **Source oddity** (V.1): Baxter's last clause ("even turn men into brutes") has no stated subject; we kept it as printed.
- **carnal:** kept. **have carnal men by the handle:** an old idiom, kept as written; it means they have a handle by which to control them.
- **the three witnesses** (IV.6): Baxter's name for the three men who appear in Daniel 3 (probably those who refused to bow to the image); we have kept his word and not explained why he calls them witnesses.
- **the Romish party:** Baxter's polemical phrase for the Roman Catholic Church and its supporters; kept as his. His charge that they claim Christ's authority to demand obedience in error is his own.
- **a Turk or Jew:** Baxter's words, kept; he means someone outside the Christian faith whom the people would not trust as a preacher. This is his period's language.
- **security** (V.3): Baxter's technical term for careless confidence, kept. **scandalous** (V.6): ministers whose lives give offense or shame, kept as written. **faction:** a party within the church, as Baxter uses it.
- **heretics:** kept as Baxter's word.
- **Pilate; Pharaoh** (IV.3): Baxter gives no references for these two. Possible backgrounds, not in Baxter's text and unconfirmed: John 19:12 (Pilate and "friend of Caesar"); Exodus 14 (Pharaoh pursuing Israel). We have not checked his readings of their motives. Demetrius: Acts 19, as Baxter cites.
