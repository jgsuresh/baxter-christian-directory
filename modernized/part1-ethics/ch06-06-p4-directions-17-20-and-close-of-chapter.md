# Chapter 6: Title 6, Directions 17 (end) to 20, and the close of the chapter

*This file continues Title 6 (directions for young students) from the previous file; it begins partway through Direction 17, at Instance 8, and runs to the end of Chapter 6.*

*Instance 8.* Yes, many would separate God from God, while they would separate God from heaven, and say that we must be content to be shut out of heaven for the love of God; when our heaven is the perfect love of God. And so they say in effect that for the love of God we must be content to be shut out from the love of God.

*Instance 9.* Thus also the common people separate the mercy and the justice of God! As if God did not know better than man to whom his mercy should extend. And as if God is not merciful, if he will be a righteous governor, and unless he will allow all the world to spit in his face and blaspheme him, and let his enemies go all unpunished.

*Instance 10.* Thus many separate threats and promises, fear and love, a perfect law and a pardoning gospel. As if he that is a man, and has both fear and love in his nature, must not make use of both for God and his salvation; and the lawgiver might not fit his laws to work on both. As if hell may not be feared, and heaven loved at once.

*Instance 11.* Thus hypocrites separate and imagine their seeming holiness and devotion to God apart from duties of justice and charity to men. As if they could serve God acceptably, and disobey him willfully! Or as if they could love him whom they never saw, and not love his image in his works and children, whom they daily see. As if they could hate and persecute Christ in his little ones, or at least neglect him, and yet sincerely love him in himself.

*Instance 12.* Thus, by many, Scripture and tradition, divine faith and human faith, are commonly opposed. Because the papists have set tradition in a wrong place, many cast it away because it does not fit that place: when man's tradition and ministerial revelation is necessary to make known and bring down God's revelation to us; and a subservient tradition is no disparagement to Scripture, though a supplemental tradition is; and man must be believed as man, though not as God; and he that will not believe man as man shall scarcely know what he has to believe from God.

*Instance 13.* Thus many separate the sufficiency of the law and rule from the usefulness of an officer, minister, and judge. As if the law must be imperfect, or else need no execution, and no judge for execution. Or as if the judge's execution were a supplement or addition to the law. As if the question, Who shall be the judge? convicted the law of insufficiency; and the promulgation and execution were not presupposed.

*Instance 14.* Thus also many separate the necessity of a public judge from the lawfulness and necessity of a private judgment, or discerning in all the rational subjects. As if God and man governed only brutes; or we could obey a law, and not judge it to be a law, and to be obeyed; and not understand the sense of it, and what it commands us. As if fools and madmen were the only subjects. As if our learning of Christ as his disciples, and meditating day and night in his law, and searching for wisdom in his word, were a disobeying him as our King. As if it were a possible thing for subjects to obey without a private judgment of discretion. Or as if there were any contradiction between my judging what is the king's law, and his judging whether I am punishable for disobeying it. Or as if judging ourselves contradicted our being judged by God.

*Instance 15.* So, many separate between the operation of the word and Spirit, the minister and Christ. As if the Spirit did not usually work by the word; and Christ did not preach to us by his ministers and ambassadors. And as if they might despise his messengers, and not be taken for despisers of himself. Or might throw away the dish and keep the milk.

*Instance 16.* Thus many separate the special love of saints from the common love of man as man. As if they could not love a saint, unless they may hate an enemy, and despise all others, and deny them the love which corresponds to their natural goodness.

*Instance 17.* Thus many separate universal or catholic union and communion from particular. And some understand no communion but the universal, and some none but the particular. Some say we separate from them as to catholic communion, if we do not hold local, particular communion with them; yes, if we do not join with them in every mode. As if I could be personally in ten thousand thousand congregations at once, or else separated from them all. Or, as if I separated from all mankind, if I differed from all men in my face or complexion. Or, as if I cannot be absent from many thousand churches, and yet honor them as true churches of Christ, and hold catholic communion with them in faith, hope, and love; yes, though I dared not join with them personally in worship, for fear of some sinful condition which they impose. Or, as if I need not be a member of any ordered worshipping congregation, because I have a catholic faith and love to all the Christians in the world.

*Instance 18.* Thus are the outward and inward worship separated by many, who think that all which the body performs is against the due spirituality; or that the spirituality is but fancy, and contrary to the form or outward part. As if the heart and the knee may not fittingly bow together; nor decency of order concur with Spirit and truth.

*Instance 19.* Thus many separate faith and obedience; Paul's justification by faith, without the works of the law, from James's justification by works, and not by faith only, and Christ's justification by our words (Matthew 12:37). And thus they separate free grace and justification from any necessary condition, and from the rewardableness of obedience (which the ancients called merit): but of this at length elsewhere.

*Instance 20.* And many separate prudence and zeal, meekness and resolution, the wisdom of the serpent and the innocence of the dove; yielding to no sin, and yet yielding in things lawful; maintaining our Christian liberty, and yet becoming all things to all men, if by any means we may save some. These instances are enough, I will add no more.

**Direction 18. Beware of falling into factions and parties in religion (be the party great or small, high or low, in honor or dishonor); and beware lest you be infected with a factious, censorious, uncharitable, hurting zeal: for these are much contrary to the interest, will, and Spirit of Christ. Therefore among all your readings, deeply suck in the doctrine of charity and peace, and read much, reconciling, moderating authors; such as Dury, Hall, Davenant, Crocius, Bergius, Martinius, Amyraldus, Dallaeus, Testardus, Calixtus, Hottonus, Junius, Paraeus and Burroughs, their Irenicons.**

The reading of such books extinguishes the consuming flame of that infernal, envious zeal described in James 3; and kindles charity, and meekness, and mellowness, and moderation in the heart; and cures those bloodshot eyes, which are unable until cured to discern the truth. It helps us to knowledge, and to that which is more edifying, and keeps knowledge from puffing us up. And experience will tell you in the long run that among ancients and moderns, Greeks and Latins, papists and Protestants, Lutherans and Calvinists, Remonstrants and Contra-Remonstrants, prelatists, Presbyterians, Independents, etc., commonly the moderators are not only the best and most charitable, but the wisest, most judicious men.

**Direction 19. With all your readings always join the reading of the Scriptures, and of the most holy and practical divines; not fantastical, enthusiastic counterfeits, Paracelsian divines; but those that lead you up by the solid doctrine of faith and love to true devotion, and heavenly-mindedness and conduct.**

This must be your bread and drink, your daily and substantial food: without this you may soon be filled with air, that cannot nourish you, and prove in the end as sounding brass and tinkling cymbals. These will breed strength, and peace, and joy, and help you in your communion with God, and hopes of heaven, and so promote the end of all your studies. There is more life and sweetness in these than in things that are more remote from God and heaven.

**Direction 20. Lastly, do all as dying men: do not promise yourselves long life, lest it tempt you to waste your time on things less necessary, and to loiter it away; or lest you lose the enlivening benefit which the sight of death and eternity would yield you in all your studies.**

The nearer you perceive yourselves to be to death and heaven, the greater help you have to be mortified and heavenly. This will make you serious, and keep up right intentions, and keep out wrong ones, and powerfully help you against temptations, that when you have studied to save others, you may not be castaways; nor be cheated by the devil with the shell, and leaves, and flowers, while you go without the saving fruit.

I have spoken the more on this subject of governing the thoughts, because it is so great and excellent a part of the work of man; and God so much regards the heart; and the Spirit of Christ and Satan so much strive for it; and grace is so much employed about it; and our happiness or misery, joy or sorrow, is greatly promoted by our thoughts. And more I would have said, but that in the third chapter, and in my *Treatise of the Divine Life*, there is much said already. And for a method and directions for particular meditations, I have given it at length in the fourth part of the *Saints' Rest*, from where it may easily be taken, and applied to other subjects, as it is there to heaven. It is easy to write and read directions; but I fear lest slothfulness, through the difficulty of practice, will frustrate my directions to most. But if any profit by them, my labor is not lost.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). No verse is quoted in this section, so no NET wording appears; all Scripture here is a bare reference or an unmarked allusion, kept in Baxter's wording.
- **Title:** "Title" is Baxter's word ("Tit.") for one of the six divisions of Chapter 6. This file ends Title 6 and the chapter.
- **Where this file begins:** partway through Direction 17 ("In all your studies be jealous of both extremes"), whose heading and Instances 1 to 7 are in the previous file. Baxter's "Instance" (an example of separating things that belong together) is kept, as in that file.
- **Matthew 12:37 (corrected reference):** the source prints "Matt. xii. 47," but the saying about being justified by our words is Matthew 12:37 (King James: "by thy words thou shalt be justified"). Corrected in the text. Paul's justification by faith "without the works of the law" alludes to Romans 3:28; James's "by works, and not by faith only" to James 2:24. These two are not referenced in Baxter's text.
- **James 3** (Direction 18): cited only; the "envious zeal" is presumably the "bitter jealousy and selfishness" of James 3:14-16 (NET wording).
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): loving "him whom they never saw" while not loving those "whom they daily see" (1 John 4:20); Christ "in his little ones" (Matthew 25:40, 45; 10:42); "meditating day and night in his law" (Psalm 1:2); despising his messengers as despising himself (Luke 10:16); "Spirit and truth" (John 4:24); "the wisdom of the serpent and the innocence of the dove" (Matthew 10:16); "becoming all things to all men, if by any means we may save some" (1 Corinthians 9:22); knowledge "puffing us up" (1 Corinthians 8:1); "sounding brass and tinkling cymbals" (1 Corinthians 13:1, where the NET has "a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal"); "castaways" (1 Corinthians 9:27, where the NET has "disqualified").
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): yea to yes; the vulgar to the common people; suffer to allow; threatenings to threats; conceit (imagine) to imagine, with "from" rendered "apart from" so the verb reads; wilfully to willfully; scarce to scarcely; repugnancy to contradiction; judged of God to judged by God; answerable to corresponds to; visage to face; durst not to dared not; honour to honor; fitly to fittingly; innocency to innocence; at large to at length; Take heed of / take heed lest to Beware of / beware lest; still (always) to always; conversation (conduct) to conduct; quickening (giving life) to enlivening; apprehend yourselves to death to perceive yourselves to be to death; at long running to in the long run; bloodshotten to bloodshot; whence to from where; to the most to to most (for most people); labour to labor.
- **did argue the law of insufficiency** (Instance 13): "argue" here means to accuse or convict (the law of being insufficient); rendered "convicted the law of insufficiency." **were not supposed:** Baxter's "supposed" means presupposed (taken for granted); rendered "presupposed."
- **brutes** (Instance 14): kept; Baxter means animals without reason. **fancy** (Instance 18): kept, in the sense of mere imagination.
- **catholic** (Instance 17): the universal church, not "Roman Catholic." **papists** (Instances 12; Direction 18): Baxter's word for Roman Catholics, kept as his term; the point about tradition set "in a wrong place" is his polemic.
- **subservient / supplemental tradition** (Instance 12): Baxter distinguishes tradition that serves to hand on Scripture (which he accepts) from tradition claimed as an addition to Scripture (which he rejects).
- **rewardableness of obedience (which the ancients called merit)** (Instance 19): kept as written; Baxter says he treats this "at length elsewhere" (not checked).
- **Paraeus and Burroughs, their Irenicons** (Direction 18): Baxter's phrase means "the Irenicons of Paraeus and Burroughs" (an *Irenicon* is a book seeking peace among divided churches). Kept in his order with a comma added. The list names writers Baxter thought peaceable and moderating; we have not checked their identities or works, and spell them as printed except Dallaeus and Paraeus (the source has the ligature æ). Dury is probably John Dury, a known campaigner for Protestant reunion, and Hall probably Bishop Joseph Hall; Amyraldus is probably Moïse Amyraut; we have not confirmed the others.
- **read much, reconciling, moderating authors** (Direction 18): the comma after "much" is Baxter's; it could mean "read much, [especially] reconciling ... authors" or "read many reconciling ... authors." Left as printed.
- **Remonstrants and Contra-Remonstrants; prelatists, Presbyterians, Independents:** parties of Baxter's day (probably the two sides of the Dutch Arminian controversy, and, in English church government, supporters of bishops, of presbyteries, and of independent congregations; not checked against Baxter's other writings). **moderators:** the moderate men in each party, not chairmen of meetings.
- **divines** (Direction 19): theologians or writers on religion; kept. **enthusiastic:** Baxter's technical term for those who claim direct inspiration apart from Scripture (see "enthusiasts" in CONVENTIONS.md); not "eager." **Paracelsian divines:** Baxter's name, apparently after the physician-alchemist Paracelsus, for mystical, speculative writers; kept as written.
- **mortified** (Direction 20): kept (put to death to sin).
- **Baxter's books** (closing paragraph): *Treatise of the Divine Life* and the *Saints' Rest* (fourth part, on meditation); "the third chapter" is Chapter 3 of this Directory. Cross-references not checked.
