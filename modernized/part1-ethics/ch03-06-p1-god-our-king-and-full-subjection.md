# Chapter 3: Grand Direction VI (part 1 of 3)

**Grand Direction 6. Remember that God is your sovereign King, to rule and judge you; and that it is your rectitude and happiness to obey and please him. Labor therefore to bring your souls and bodies into the most absolute subjection to him, and to make it your delight and business sincerely and exactly to obey his will.**

Having surrendered yourselves absolutely to God as your Owner, you are next to subject yourselves absolutely to God as your Governor or King. How much of our religion consists in this, you may see in the nature of the thing, in the design of the law and word of God, in the doctrine and example of Jesus Christ, in the description of the last judgment, and in the common consent of all the world. Though love is the highest work of man, yet it is so far from discharging us from our subjection and obedience, that it compels us to it most powerfully and most sweetly, and must itself be judged by these effects. [96] "If you love me, you will obey my commandments" (John 14:15). "The person who has my commandments and obeys them is the one who loves me" (John 14:21). "If anyone loves me, he will obey my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and take up residence with him. The person who does not love me does not obey my words" (John 14:23–24). "If you obey my commandments, you will remain in my love, just as I have obeyed my Father's commandments and remain in his love. You are my friends if you do what I command you" (John 15:10, 14). "If you understand these things, you will be blessed if you do them" (John 13:17). "For this is the love of God: that we keep his commandments. And his commandments do not weigh us down" (1 John 5:3). "The one who says 'I have come to know God' and yet does not keep his commandments is a liar, and the truth is not in such a person. But whoever obeys his word, truly in this person the love of God has been perfected. By this we know that we are in him. The one who says he resides in God ought himself to walk just as Jesus walked. If you know that he is righteous, you also know that everyone who practices righteousness has been fathered by him" (1 John 2:4–6, 29). "Everyone who resides in him does not sin; everyone who sins has neither seen him nor known him. Little children, let no one deceive you: The one who practices righteousness is righteous, just as Jesus is righteous. The one who practices sin is of the devil because the devil has been sinning from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was revealed: to destroy the works of the devil. Everyone who has been fathered by God does not practice sin because God's seed resides in them, and thus they are not able to sin because they have been fathered by God. By this the children of God and the children of the devil are revealed: Everyone who does not practice righteousness ... is not of God. ... and whatever we ask we receive from him, because we keep his commandments and do the things that are pleasing to him" (1 John 3:6–10, 22). "Blessed are those who wash their robes so they can have access to the tree of life and can enter into the city by the gates" (Revelation 22:14).

I set together these testimonies of Scripture, that the stream of divine authority may carry you to a lively sense of the necessity of obedience.

I shall here first tell you what this full subjection is, and then I shall direct you how to attain it.

*In the margin: Subjection: what it is.*

I. As in God there is first his relation of our King, and then his actual government of us, by his laws and judgment; so in us, there is first our relation of subjects to God, and then our actual obedience. We are subjects by divine obligation, before we consent (as rebels are); but our consent or self-obligation is necessary to our voluntary obedience, and acceptance with God. Subjection is our settled obligation to obedience. This subjection and habit of obedience is then right and full, 1. When the sense of God's authority over us is practical, and not theoretical only. 2. And when it is deep-rooted and fixed, and has become a nature to us: as a man's intention of his end is, who has a long journey to go, which carries him on to the last step: or as a child's subjection to his parents, or a servant's to his master, which is the habit or principle of his daily course of life. 3. When it is lively, and ready to put the soul upon obedience. 4. When it is constant, keeping the soul in a continual attendance on the will of God. 5. When it has universal respect to all his commandments. 6. When it is resolute, powerful, and victorious against temptations to disobedience. 7. When it is superlative, respecting God as our supreme King, and acknowledging no authority against him, nor any but what is subordinate to him. 8. When it is voluntary, pleasant, cheerful, and delightful to us to obey him to the utmost of our power.

*In the margin: How to bring the soul into subjection to God.*

II. To bring the soul to this full subjection and obedience to God is so difficult, and yet so reasonable, so necessary, and so excellently good, that we should not think any diligence too great, by which it is to be attained. The directions that I shall give you are, some of them to accustom the mind to a frame of obedience, and some of them, also, practically to further the exercise of obedience in particular acts.

**Direction 1. Remember the unquestionable, full title that God has to the government of you, and of all the world.**

The sense of this will awe the soul, and help to subject it to him, and to silence all rebellious motions. Should not God rule the creatures which he has made? Should not Christ rule the souls which he has purchased? Should not the Holy Spirit rule the souls which he has regenerated and quickened?

**Direction 2. Remember that God is perfectly fit for the government of you, and all the world.**

You can desire nothing reasonably in a governor which is not in him. He has perfect wisdom, to know what is best: he has perfect goodness, and therefore will be most mindful of his subjects' good, and will put no evil into his laws. He is almighty, to protect his subjects, and see to the execution of his laws. He is most just, and therefore can do no wrong, but all his laws and judgments are fair and impartial. He is infinitely perfect and self-sufficient, and never needed a lie, or a deceit, or unrighteous means to rule the world; nor to oppress his subjects to attain his ends. He is our very end, and interest, and happiness; and therefore has no interest opposite to our good, which should cause him to destroy the innocent. He is our dearest Friend and Father, and loves us better than we love ourselves; and therefore we have reason confidently to trust him, and cheerfully and gladly to obey him, as one who rules us in order to our own happiness.

**Direction 3. Remember how unable and unfit you are to be governors of yourselves.**

So blind and ignorant; so biased by a corrupted will; so turbulent are your passions; so incessant and powerful is the temptation of your senses and appetite; and so unable are you to protect or reward yourselves, that I think you should fear nothing in this world more than to be given up to "your own heart's lusts, to walk in your own (seducing) counsels" (Psalm 81:11–12). The brutish appetite and senses have gained such dominion over the reason of carnal, unrenewed men, that for such to be governed by themselves is for a man to be governed by a swine, or the rider to be ruled by the horse.

**Direction 4. Remember how great a matter God makes of his kingly prerogatives, and of man's obedience.**

The whole tenor of Scripture will tell you this. His precepts, his promises, his threatenings, his vehement exhortations, his sharp reproofs, the sending of his Son and Spirit, the example of Christ and all the saints, the reward prepared for the obedient, and the punishment for the disobedient--all tell you aloud that God is far from being indifferent whether you obey his laws or not. It will teach you to regard that which you find is so regarded by God.

**Direction 5. Consider well the excellence of full obedience, and the present benefits which it brings to yourselves and others.**

Our full subjection and obedience to God is to the world and the soul as health is to the body. When all the humors keep their due temperament, proportions, and place, and every part of the body is placed and used according to the intent of nature, then all is at ease within us: our food is pleasant; our sleep is sweet; our labor is easy; and our liveliness makes life a pleasure to us: we are useful in our places, and helpful to others who are sick and weak. So it is with the soul that is fully obedient: God gives him a reward, before the full reward: he finds that obedience is a reward to itself; and that it is very pleasant to do good. God acknowledges him, and conscience speaks peace and comfort to him; his mercies are sweet to him; his burdens and his work are easy; he has easier access to God than others. Indeed, the world shall find that there is no way to its right order, unity, peace, and happiness, but by a full subjection and obedience to God.

**Direction 6. Remember the sad effects of disobedience, even at present, both in the soul and in the world.**

When we rebel against God, it is the confusion, ruin, and death of the soul, and of the world. When we disobey him, it is the sickness or disordering of the soul, and will make us groan; until our bones are set in joint again, we shall have no ease: God will be displeased, and hide his face; conscience will be unquiet; the soul will lose its peace and joy; its former mercies will grow less sweet; its former rest will turn to weariness; its duty will be unpleasant; its burden heavy. Who would not fear such a state as this?

**Direction 7. Consider that when God does not govern you, you are ruled by the flesh, the world, and the devil.**

And what right or fitness they have to govern you, and what is their work, and final reward, I think you should easily discern. "If you live according to the flesh, you will die" (Romans 8:13). "And ... the person who sows to his own flesh will reap corruption from the flesh" (Galatians 6:8). It will strike you with horror, if, in the hour of temptation, you would but think: I am now going to disobey my God, and to obey the flesh, the world, or the devil, and to prefer their will before his will.

**Direction 8. Turn your eye upon the rebellious nations of the earth, and upon the state of the most malignant and ungodly men; and consider that such madness and misery as you see in them, every willful disobedience to God tends to, and partakes of in its degree.**

To see a swinish drunkard in his vomit; to hear a raging madman curse and swear; or a malignant wretch blaspheme and scorn a holy life: to hear how foolishly they talk against God, and see how maliciously they hate his servants, one would think should turn one's stomach against all sin forever. To think what beasts or incarnate devils many of the ungodly are; to think what confusion and inhumanity possess most of those nations that do not know God, one would think should make the least degree of sin seem odious to us, when the dominion and ripeness of it are so odious.

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

(English: Aristippus was once asked what was exceptional that the philosophers had. "If all laws were to perish," he said, "we would live in the same way [or: evenly, uniformly]." Laertius. ## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Revelation 22:14 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life." He uses it as the climax of his chain of texts on obedience. The NET reads "Blessed are those who wash their robes so they can have access to the tree of life." (The NET footnote may mention the textual variant behind the difference; we have not checked it.) With the NET wording the verse no longer says "do his commandments," so it does not support his point in the same words. Flagged.
- **1 John 3:6, 9 (important):** Baxter's King James has "Whosoever abideth in him sinneth not," "whosoever sinneth," and "doth not commit sin ... he cannot sin." The NET reads "does not sin" in verse 6, but "practices sin" in verses 8 and 9 and "practices righteousness" in verses 7 and 10. The NET wording leans toward settled practice, where the King James reads as a flat statement. Baxter's argument (obedience is the mark of the born-again) holds either way, but readers should know the nuance comes from the NET's rendering. Flagged.
- **1 John 3:10:** the NET includes a clause Baxter does not quote: "the one who does not love his fellow Christian." We marked the omission with "...". We also ran verse 22 on after verse 10 with "...", as Baxter does (his reference is "6-10, 22").
- **1 John 2:29, 3:9:** Baxter's "born of him" and "born of God" are "fathered by him" and "fathered by God" in the NET. The sense (new birth) is the same.
- **John 14:21:** the NET continues "The one who loves me will be loved by my Father ..." We quote only the part Baxter does (through "loves me"). Baxter's "he it is that loveth me" is the NET's "is the one who loves me."
- **John 14:23, 14:24:** Baxter's "make our abode with him" is "take up residence with him"; "keepeth not my sayings" is "does not obey my words." Baxter quotes verse 24 only to its first clause.
- **John 15:10, 15:14:** Baxter cites "chap. xv. 10, 14" and joins the two verses; we do the same, in one quotation. NET wording.
- **John 13:17:** Baxter's "If ye know these things" is "If you understand these things" in the NET. "Know" in the King James could mean knowing about; the NET leans toward comprehension. The point (doing matters, not just knowing) is unchanged.
- **1 John 5:3:** the NET sentence continues past Baxter's quotation; we quote through "weigh us down" (King James "not grievous").
- **Psalm 81:11-12 (paraphrase):** Baxter's "your own heart's lusts, to walk in your own (seducing) counsels" is a blend of the King James (verse 12, "their own hearts' lust ... walked in their own counsels") with his own address to the reader and his added "(seducing)." We kept his wording, modernized, in quotation marks. The NET reads "I gave them over to their stubborn desires; they did what seemed right to them." The NET has no "counsels," and Baxter's "seducing" is his gloss. Flagged. The reference is Baxter's; the NET puts these in verses 11-12 as well.
- **Romans 8:13:** NET wording; the NET sets the phrase in parentheses. Baxter's "ye shall die" is "you will die."
- **Galatians 6:8:** Baxter writes "if ye sow to the flesh, of the flesh ye shall reap corruption." The NET has "the person who sows to his own flesh will reap corruption from the flesh," a statement, not an "if" clause. We quote the NET and mark the dropped opening with "..." The "if ... shall" form Baxter uses is therefore lost. Flagged.
- **Chapter references:** Baxter's "chap. xv. 10, 14" and "chap. xiii. 17" continue the John citations; "chap. ii." and "chap. iii." continue 1 John. We wrote each out in modern form.
- **Numbering misprint:** in the list of marks of full subjection, the source prints the seventh item as "I." (clearly a misprint for 7). We used 7.
- **In the margin:** "Subjection what" is Baxter's brief label; we expanded it slightly to "Subjection: what it is."
- **religion** (first paragraph): kept in Baxter's sense of worship and devotion.
- **rectitude** (heading): uprightness, being in the right.
- **notional only** (point 1): Baxter's word for merely held in thought, not acted on; we use "theoretical only."
- **obediential frame** (section II): "a frame of obedience." Baxter's "habituate" is "accustom."
- **humours/humors** (Direction 5): in the older medicine, the four bodily fluids whose balance was thought to govern health. Kept as "humors."
- **owneth** (Direction 5): "acknowledges" (to claim as one's own, to approve).
- **bones set in joint** (Direction 6): allusion to a dislocated bone being put back in place; Baxter does not mark it.
- **malignant** (Direction 8): Baxter's term for those who are actively hostile to godliness and its people. Kept.
- **bedlam** (Direction 8): his word for a madman (probably from Bedlam, the London hospital for the mentally ill; we have not confirmed this). We use "madman." The tone is Baxter's; we have not softened it.
- **swinish drunkard; beasts or incarnate devils:** Baxter's blunt language, kept.
- **Other word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): resigned (first paragraph) to "surrendered" ("resigned" now suggests giving up); stated (obligation) to "settled"; attendance upon to "attendance on"; acceptation to "acceptance"; owning (authority) to "acknowledging"; regardful to "mindful"; sense (the bodily faculty, Directions 3) to "senses"; vivacity to "liveliness"; excellency to "excellence"; Yea to "Indeed"; discern to "see" (Direction 8, "you see in them"); biassed to "biased"; "scorn at" to "scorn."
- **felicity:** "happiness." **delectable:** "delightful." **plenary:** "full." **methinks:** "I think." **equal** (laws "equal and impartial"): "fair."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "stream of divine authority"; "a child's subjection to his parents, or a servant's to his master".
- **Aristippus; Laertius** (note 96): Diogenes Laertius's *Lives of the Philosophers* is a likely source for the saying about Aristippus, but we have not confirmed this. We make no further claim.
- **Cross-references** to Baxter's earlier discussion of God as Owner (the preceding Grand Direction) have not been checked here.
