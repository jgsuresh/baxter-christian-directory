# Chapter 2: Direction 4

**Direction 4. Do not begin too early with controversies in religion: and when you come to them, let them have but their due proportion of your time and zeal: but live daily upon these certain great essentials, which all Christians are agreed in.**

1. Do not plunge yourselves too soon into controversies: For, (1.) It will be exceedingly to your loss, by diverting your souls from greater and more necessary things: you may get more increase of holiness, and spend your time more pleasingly to God, by drinking more deeply of the essentials of religion, and improving them in your hearts and lives.

(2.) It will corrupt your minds, and instead of humility, love, holiness, and heavenly-mindedness, it will feed your pride, and kindle divisiveness and a dividing zeal, and quench your love, and possess you with a quarreling, contentious spirit, and you will make a religion of these sins and lamentable disorders.

(3.) And it is the way to deceive and corrupt your judgments, and make you mistaken in doctrine or heretical, to your own perdition and the disturbance of the church: for it is two to one that either you immediately go wrong, or else get such an itch after notions and opinions that will lead you to error at last. Because you are not yet ripe and able to judge of those things until your minds are prepared by those truths that are first in order to be received. When you undertake a work that you cannot do, no wonder if it is badly done, and must be all undone again, or worse.

Perhaps you will say that you must not take your religion upon trust, but must "examine all things; hold fast to what is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:21).

*Answer.* Though your religion must not be taken upon trust, there are many controverted smaller opinions that you must take upon trust until you are capable of discerning them in their proper evidence. Until you can reach them yourselves, you must take them on trust, or not at all. Though you must believe all things of common necessity to salvation with a divine faith; yet many subservient truths must be received first by a human faith, or not received at all, until you are more capable of them. Nay, there is a human faith necessarily subservient to the divine faith, about the substance of religion; and the officers of Christ are to be trusted in their office, as helpers of your faith. Nay, let me tell you that while you are young and ignorant, you are not fit for controversies about the fundamentals of religion themselves. You may believe that there is a God long before you are fit to hear an atheist proving that there is no God. You may believe the Scripture to be the word of God, and Christ to be the Savior, and the soul to be immortal, long before you will be fit to manage or study controversies concerning these. For nothing is so false or bad which a playful or wicked wit may not put a plausible gloss upon; and your raw, unfurnished understandings will scarcely be able to see through the pretense, or escape the cheat. When you cannot answer the arguments of seducers, you will find them leave a doubting in your minds; for you do not know how plain the answer to them is to wiser men. And though you must examine all things, you must do it in due order, and as you are able; and wait until your furnished minds are capable of the trial. If you will insist on reading before you know your letters, or pretend to judge of Greek and Hebrew authors before you can read English, you will but become ridiculous in your undertaking.

2. When you do come to smaller controverted points, let them have but their due proportion of your time and zeal. And that will not be one hour in many days, with the majority of private Christians. By the time you have well learned the more necessary truths, and practiced daily the more necessary duties, you will find that there will be but little time to spare for lesser controversies. Opinionists who spend most of their time studying and talking of such points steal that time from greater matters, and therefore from God, and from themselves. Better work is left undone in the meantime. And they that lay out their chief zeal here divert their zeal from things more necessary, and turn their natural heat into a fever.

3. The essential necessary truths of your religion must imprint the image of God upon your hearts, and must dwell there continually, and you must live upon them as your bread, and drink, and daily necessary food: all other points must be studied in subordination to those: all lesser duties must be used as the exercise of the love of God or man, and of a humble heavenly mind. The articles of your creed, and points of catechism, are fountains ever running, affording you matter for the continual exercise of grace: it is both plentiful and solid nourishment of the soul which these great substantial points afford. To know God the Creator, Redeemer, and Sanctifier, the laws and covenant of God, and his judgment, and rewards and punishments, with the parts and method of the Lord's Prayer, which must be the daily exercise of our desires, and love, this is the wisdom of a Christian; and in these must he be continually exercised.

You will say perhaps that the apostle says, "Therefore we must progress beyond the elementary instructions about Christ and move on to maturity, not laying this foundation again: repentance from dead works and faith in God," etc. (Hebrews 6:1).

*Answer.* 1. By "leaving" he does not mean passing over the practice of them as men that have done with them, and are past them; but his leaving at that time to discourse of them, or his supposing them taught already: though he does not lay the foundation again, yet he does not pluck it up. 2. By "principles" he means the first points to be taught, and learned, and practiced: and indeed regeneration and baptism are not to be done again: but the essentials of religion which I am speaking of contain much more: especially to "live in the love of God, which Paul calls the more excellent way" (1 Corinthians 12–13). 3. Going on to perfection is not by ceasing to believe and love God, but by a more distinct knowledge of the mysteries of salvation, to perfect our faith, and love, and obedience.

The points that opinionists call higher, and think to be the principal matter of their growth and advancement in understanding, are usually but some smaller, less necessary truths, if not some uncertain, doubtful questions. Note well 1 Timothy 1:4; 6:4; 2 Timothy 2:23; Titus 3:9, compared with John 17:3; Romans 13:8–10; 1 Corinthians 13; 1 John 3; 1 Corinthians 1:23; 15:1–3; 2:2; Galatians 6:14; James 2; 3:1.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **1 Thessalonians 5:21:** Baxter's "prove all things, and hold fast that which is good" is "examine all things; hold fast to what is good" in the NET. Baxter's reference was not in his text.
- **Hebrews 6:1 (important for Baxter's answer):** Baxter quotes the King James, "Leaving the principles of the doctrine of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works." His three answers each take a word from that verse: "leaving," "principles," and "going on to perfection." The NET reads "we must progress beyond the elementary instructions about Christ and move on to maturity, not laying this foundation again." The NET replaces all three words with others ("progress beyond," "elementary instructions," "maturity"). We quote the NET in the objection and keep Baxter's own words ("leaving," "principles," "perfection") in his answer, since his explanation depends on them. Flagged.
- **1 Corinthians 12:31** (Answer 2): Baxter's "the more excellent way, which Paul calls …" is a paraphrase of "a still more excellent way" (the King James at 12:31). The NET reads "a way that is beyond comparison." We kept Baxter's wording, modernized, and cited 1 Corinthians 12–13.
- **Other references** (the closing list): cited only; converted to modern form.
- **essentials:** Baxter's "substantials." **love:** Baxter's "charity" (in his own prose). **divisiveness:** his "faction." **disorders:** his "distempers." **mistaken in doctrine:** his "erroneous."
- **immediately:** Baxter's "presently." **left undone in the meantime:** his "undone the while." **quarreling:** his "wrangling."
- **a playful or wicked wit** (paragraph 5): Baxter's "a wanton or wicked wit," meaning an unserious or perverse intellect, one that plays with ideas without regard to truth.
- **pretense:** Baxter's "pretence," meaning deception or false claim. **insist on reading:** his "will needs read."
- **opinionists:** left as Baxter's word for people absorbed in opinions and debates. **seducers:** those who lead others astray.
- **human faith; divine faith** (Answer): Baxter's distinction between trusting a human teacher (faith in people) and believing God's word (faith in God). **subservient:** serving a higher truth, subordinate.
- **in subordination to those:** Baxter's "in subserviency to those." **the majority:** his "the generality."
- **Lord's Prayer; creed; catechism:** the traditional basics, as in Direction 3.
- **"one hour in many days"** (point 2): Baxter's rule of proportion: lesser controversies should take very little time.
