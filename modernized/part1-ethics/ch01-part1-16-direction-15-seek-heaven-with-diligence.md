# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 15

**Direction 15. If you have sincerely given yourself up to God, and consented to his covenant, show it by turning the face of your efforts and conduct quite another way, and by seeking heaven more fervently and diligently than ever you sought the world, or fleshly pleasures.**

Holiness does not consist in a mere refraining from a life of bodily indulgence, but principally in living to God. The principle or heart of holiness is within, and consists in the love of God, and of his word, and ways, and servants, and honor, and cause in the world, and in the soul's delight in God, and the word and ways of God, and in its inclination toward him, and desire after him, and care to please him, and reluctance to offend him. The expression of it in our lives consists in the constant, diligent exercise of this internal life, according to the directions of the word of God. If you are a believer, and have subjected yourself to God as your absolute Sovereign, King, and Judge, it will then be your work to obey and please him, as a child his father, or a servant his master (Malachi 1:6). Do you think that God will have servants, and have nothing for them to do? Will one of you commend or reward your servant for doing nothing, and take it at the year's end for a satisfactory answer or account if he says, I have done no harm? God calls you not only to do no harm, but to love and serve him with all your heart, and soul, and might. If you have a better master than you had before, you should do more work than you did before. Will you not serve God more zealously than you served the devil? Will you not labor harder to save your souls than you did to damn them? Will you not be more zealous in good than you were in evil? "So what benefit did you then reap from those things that you are now ashamed of? For the end of those things is death. But now, freed from sin and enslaved to God, you have your benefit leading to sanctification, and the end is eternal life" (Romans 6:21–22). If you are true believers, you have now laid up your hopes in heaven, and therefore will set yourselves to seek it as worldlings set themselves to seek the world. And a sluggish wish, with heartless, lazy, dull efforts, is no fit seeking of eternal joys. A creeping pace does not become a man that is on the way to heaven; especially who went faster on the way to hell. This is not running as for our lives. You may well be diligent and make haste, where you have so great encouragement and help, and where you may expect so good an end, and where you are sure you shall never, in life or death, have cause to repent of any of your just efforts; and where every step of your way is pure, and clean, and delightful, and paved with mercies, and fortified and secured by divine protection; and where Christ is your guide, and so many have fared so well before you, and the wisest and best in the world are your companions. Live then as men that have changed their master, their end, their hopes, their way, and work. Religion does not lay men to sleep, though it is the only way to rest. It awakens the sleepy soul to higher thoughts, and hopes, and labors than it was ever well acquainted with before. "So then, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; what is old has passed away—look, what is new has come!" (2 Corinthians 5:17). You never sought that which would pay for all your cost and diligence until now; you never were in a way that you might make haste in without repenting of your haste, until now. How glad you should be that mercy has brought you into the right way, after the wanderings of such a sinful life! [33] And your gladness and thankfulness should now be shown by your cheerful diligence and zeal. As Christ did not raise up Lazarus from the dead to do nothing, or live to little purpose (though the Scripture does not give us the history of his life); so he did not raise you from the death of sin to live idly, or to be unprofitable in the world. He that gives you his Spirit, to be a principle of heavenly life within you, expects that you stir up the gift that he has given you, and live according to that heavenly principle.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source, except that Baxter's English is modernized.

[33] Acosta says that the Indians are so addicted to their idolatry, and unwearied in it, that he does not know what words can sufficiently declare how totally their minds are transformed into it, no whoremonger having so mad a love for his whore as they for their idols: so that neither in their idleness nor in their business, neither in public nor in private, will they do anything until they have first used their superstition on their idols: they will neither rejoice at weddings, nor mourn at funerals, neither make a feast nor partake of it, nor so much as move a foot out of doors, or a hand to any work, without this heathenish sacrilege: and all this they do with the greatest secrecy, lest the Christians should know it. Book 5, chapter 8, p. 467. See here how nature teaches all men that there is a Deity to be worshipped with all possible love and industry! And shall the worshippers of the true God then think it unnecessary strictness to be as diligent and hearty in his service?

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Romans 6:21–22:** Baxter quotes the King James ("ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life"). The NET reads "you have your benefit leading to sanctification, and the end is eternal life." The NET says "enslaved to God," where the King James has "servants to God." Baxter is arguing from the picture of a new master, and "enslaved" is stronger than Baxter's "servants." We kept the NET and flag it.
- **2 Corinthians 5:17:** the NET reads "he is a new creation" for Baxter's "a new creature." The dash and exclamation are the NET's.
- **Malachi 1:6** (cited only): God's complaint that a son honors his father and a servant his master, but Israel does not honor him.
- **"stir up the gift"** (last sentence): an unmarked allusion to 2 Timothy 1:6. **Lazarus:** John 11. We kept Baxter's wording.
- **a life of bodily indulgence:** Baxter's "a sensual life." **cause in the world:** his "interest in the world," meaning God's cause or standing in the world. **reluctance:** his "lothness." **conduct:** his "conversation." **efforts:** his "endeavours."
- **does not become:** Baxter's "beseemeth not," meaning does not suit or befit. **guide:** his "conductor." **fared so well:** his "sped so well." **delightful:** his "delectable."
- **principle** (opening paragraph and last sentence): kept in the older sense of the source or driving force of a thing. "The principle of holiness is within" means its source is inward.
- **the Indians** (note 33): the native peoples of the Americas, as in Acosta. The description of their religion is Acosta's and Baxter's. Baxter uses it as an argument from nature: if idol-worshippers are so thorough, true worshippers should be more so.
- **worldlings:** left as Baxter's word for people absorbed in worldly concerns.
