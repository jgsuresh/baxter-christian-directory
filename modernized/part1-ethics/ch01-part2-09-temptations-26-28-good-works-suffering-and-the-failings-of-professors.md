# Chapter 1, Part 2 (continued): Temptations 26–28

**Temptation 26.** The devil also tempts them to think that though they sin, yet their good works are a compensation for their bad, and therefore they pray, and do some acts of pharisaical devotion, to make God amends for what they do amiss.

*In the margin: See Proverbs 28:9; Proverbs 15:26, 8; Proverbs 21:27; Isaiah 1:13–14.*

**Direction 26.** Against this consider that however many good works you had, they are all but your duty, and make no satisfaction for your sin. But what good works can you do that shall save a wicked soul? and that God will accept without your hearts? Your hearts must be first cleansed, and yourselves devoted and sanctified to God: for an evil tree will bring forth evil fruit: first make the tree good, and the fruit will be good. It is the love of God, and the hatred of sin, and a holy and heavenly life, which are the good works that God chiefly calls for; and faith, and repentance, and conversion in order to these. And will God take your lip service, or the leavings of your flesh by way of alms, while the world and fleshly pleasure have your hearts? Indeed, you do no work that is truly good. The matter may be good; but you poison it with bad motives and aims. "The outlook of the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to the law of God, nor is it able to do so" (Romans 8:6–7).

**Temptation 27.** Some are tempted to think that God will not condemn them because they are poor and afflicted in this life, and have their sufferings here: and that he that condemns the rich for not showing mercy to the poor will himself show them mercy.

**Direction 27.** Has he not shown you mercy? And is it not mercy which you vilify and refuse? even Christ, and his Spirit, and holy communion with God? or must God show you the mercy of glory without the mercy of grace? which is a contradiction. Strange! that the same men that will not be entreated to accept mercy, nor let it save them, are yet saying that God will be merciful and save them.

*In the margin: See Hebrews 11:6, 7, 9, 10.*

And for your poverty and suffering, is it not against your will? You cannot deny it: and will God save any man for that which is against his will? You would have riches, and honor, and pleasure, and your good things in this life, as well as others, if you knew how: you love the world as well as others, if you could get more of it. And to be carnal and worldly for so poor a pittance, and to love the world when you suffer in it, makes you more inexcusable than the rich. The devils have suffered more than you, and so have many thousand souls in hell; and yet they shall be saved never the more. If you are poor in the world, but rich in faith and holiness, then you may well expect salvation (James 2:5). But if your sufferings make you no more holy, they do but aggravate your sin.

**Temptation 28.** Also the devil blinds sinners by keeping them ignorant of the nature and power of holiness of heart and life. They do not know it by any experience; and he will not let them see it and judge it in the Scripture, where it is to be seen unmixed with its opposites; but he points them only to those who profess holiness, and commonly to the weakest and the worst of them, and to that which is worst in them, and shows them the failings of hypocrites, and the falls of the weaker sort of Christians, and then tells them, This is their godliness and religion; they are all alike.

**Direction 28.** But it is easy to see how these men deceive and condemn themselves. This is as if you should plead that a beast is wiser than a man because some men are drunk, and some are hot-tempered, and some are mad. Drunkenness and fits of temper, which are the disturbances of reason, are no disgrace to reason, but to themselves: nor would they be a disgrace themselves if reason which they hinder were not honorable. So no man's sins are a disgrace to holiness, which condemns them: nor would they be bad themselves if holiness were not good, which they oppose. It is no disgrace to the daylight or sun that there is night and darkness: nor would darkness be bad if light were not good. Will you refuse health because some men are sick? nay, will you rather choose to be dead because the living have infirmities? The devil's reasoning is more foolish than this! Holiness is of absolute necessity to salvation. If many that do more than you are as bad as you imagine, what a case then are you in, who have not nearly so much as they! If they that make it their greatest care to please God, and be saved, are as complete hypocrites as the devil would persuade you, what a hopeless case then are you in, who come far short of them! If so, you must do more than they, and not less, if you will be saved; or else out of your own mouths will you be condemned.

## Baxter's notes

None in these three pairs.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Romans 8:6–7:** Baxter's "The carnal mind is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be; but is enmity to God" is Romans 8:7 in the King James. The NET reads "the outlook of the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to the law of God, nor is it able to do so." The NET's "outlook of the flesh" loses the older "carnal mind," but the sense is the same, as in the note to Direction 9 of Part 1.
- **James 2:5** (Direction 27): cited only. **Hebrews 11:6, 7, 9, 10; Proverbs 28:9; 15:26, 8; 21:27; Isaiah 1:13–14:** margin references, reproduced as printed.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "an evil tree will bring forth evil fruit: first make the tree good, and the fruit will be good" (Matthew 7:17–18; 12:33); "out of your own mouths will you be condemned" (Luke 19:22).
- **pharisaical:** kept; it means like the Pharisees, outwardly religious but inwardly unchanged. **compensation:** left as written.
- **lip service:** Baxter's "lip-labour." **motives and aims:** his "principles and ends." **leavings of your flesh by way of alms:** what is left over after the flesh has been satisfied, given in charity. Kept.
- **the mercy of glory without the mercy of grace** (Direction 27): left as written; Baxter's point is that heaven cannot be given to someone who refuses the gift of grace.
- **if you knew how:** Baxter's "if you could tell how." **pittance:** left as written; a very small amount.
- **those who profess holiness** (Temptation 28): Baxter's "professors of holiness," meaning those who profess religion, not teachers.
- **failings:** Baxter's "miscarriages." **unmixed with its opposites:** his "without any mixed contraries."
- **hot-tempered; fits of temper** (Direction 28): Baxter's "passionate" and "passions," meaning the angry outbursts of the passions.
- **more foolish:** Baxter's "foolisher." **complete hypocrites:** his "very hypocrites."
- **vilify:** left as written, meaning to speak of with contempt.
- **never the more** (Direction 27): "never the more saved," that is, they are no more saved for it.
