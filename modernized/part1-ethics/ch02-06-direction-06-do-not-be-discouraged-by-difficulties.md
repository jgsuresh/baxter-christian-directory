# Chapter 2: Direction 6

**Direction 6. Do not be discouraged at the difficulties and oppositions which will rise up before you when you begin resolutely to walk with God.**

*In the margin: Against discouragements in trials.*

As discouragements keep off multitudes from religion, so they are great temptations to many young beginners to turn back, and, like the Israelites in the wilderness, ready to wish themselves again in Egypt. Three sorts of discouragements arise before them. 1. Some from the nature of the work. 2. Some from God's trials. 3. And some from the malice of the devil and his instruments: or all these.

1. It cannot be expected but that infants and weaklings should think a little burden heavy, and an easy work or journey wearisome. Young beginners are ordinarily puzzled, and at a loss, in every trade, or art, or science. Young scholars have a far harder task than when they are once well entered: learning is wondrously hard and unpleasant to them at first; but when they are once well entered, the knowledge of one thing helps another, and they go on with ease. So a young convert who has been bred up in ignorance, and never used to prayer, or to heavenly talk, nor to hear or join with any that did, will think it strange and hard at first. And those that were used to take their pleasure, and fulfill the desires of the flesh, and perhaps to swear, and talk filthily, or idly, or to lie, will find at first some difficulty to overcome their customs, and live a mortified, holy life: yet grace will do it, and prevail. Especially in point of knowledge, and ability of expression, do not be too hasty in your expectation, but wait with patience, in a faithful, diligent use of means, and that will be easy and delightful to you afterward which before discouraged you with its difficulties.

2. And God himself will have his servants, and his graces, tried and exercised by difficulties. He never intended us the reward for sitting still; nor the crown of victory without a fight; nor a fight without an enemy and opposition. Innocent Adam was unfit for his state of confirmation and reward until he had been tried by temptation. Therefore the martyrs have the most glorious crown, as having undergone the greatest trial. And shall we presume to murmur at the method of God?

3. And Satan, having liberty to tempt and try us, will quickly raise up storms and waves before us as soon as we are set to sea; which often make young beginners fear that they shall never live to reach the haven. He will show you the greatness of your former sins, to persuade you that they shall not be pardoned. He will show you the strength of your passions and corruptions, to make you think they will never be overcome. He will show you the greatness of the opposition and suffering which you are likely to undergo, to make you think you shall never persevere. He will do his worst to meet you with poverty, losses, hardships, injuries, vexations, persecutions, and cruelties, indeed, and unkindness from your dearest friends, as he did by Job, to make you think ill of God, or of his service. If he can, he will make them your enemies that are of your own household. [56] He will stir up your own father, or mother, or husband, or wife, or brother, or sister, or children, against you, to persuade or persecute you from Christ: therefore Christ tells us that if we do not hate all these, that is, cannot forsake them, and treat them as men treat hated things, when they would turn us from him, we cannot be his disciples (Luke 14:26; Matthew 10). Look for the worst that the devil can do against you, if you have once lifted yourself against him, in the army of Christ, and resolve, whatever it costs you, to be saved. Read Hebrews 11. But how little cause you have to be discouraged, though earth and hell should do their worst, you may perceive by these few considerations.

(1.) God is on your side, who has all your enemies in his hand, and can rebuke them, or destroy them in a moment. Oh what is the breath or fury of dust or devils against the Lord Almighty! "If God is for us, who can be against us?" (Romans 8:31). Read that chapter often, Romans 8. In the day when you entered into covenant with God, and he with you, you entered into the most impregnable rock and fortress, and housed yourself in that castle of defense, where you may (modestly) defy all adverse powers of earth or hell. If God cannot save you, he is not God. And if he will not save you, he must break his covenant. Indeed, he may resolve to save you, not from affliction and persecution, but in it, and by it. But in all these sufferings you will "have complete victory through him who loved us" (Romans 8:37): that is, it is far more desirable and excellent to conquer by patience, in suffering for Christ, than to conquer our persecutors in the field by force of arms. O think on the saints' triumphant boasting in their God. "God is our strong refuge; he is truly our helper in times of trouble. For this reason we do not fear when the earth shakes, and the mountains tumble into the depths of the sea" (Psalm 46:1–3). When "many are fighting against me," and "all day long they cause me trouble; they make a habit of plotting my demise," yet he says, "When I am afraid, I trust in you. In God—I boast in his promise—in God I trust; I am not afraid. What can mere men do to me?" (Psalm 56:2–5). Remember Christ's charge: "do not be afraid of those who kill the body, and after that have nothing more they can do. But I will warn you whom you should fear: Fear the one who, after the killing, has authority to throw you into hell. Yes, I tell you, fear him!" (Luke 12:4–5). If all the world were on your side, you might yet have cause to fear; but to have God on your side is infinitely more.

(2.) Jesus Christ is the pioneer of your salvation (Hebrews 2:10), and has gone before you this way himself, and has conquered for you; and now is engaged to make you conqueror: and do you dare not go on where Christ leads the way? He was perfected through suffering himself, and will see that you are not destroyed by it. Can you draw back when you see his steps, and his blood? [57]

(3.) You are not to conquer in your own strength, but by the Spirit of God, and the power of that grace which is sufficient for you, and his strength which appears most in our weakness: "My grace is enough for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9). And as Paul says, "I am able to do all things through the one who strengthens me" (Philippians 4:13). "In the world you have trouble and suffering, but take courage—I have conquered the world" (John 16:33).

(4.) All that are in heaven have gone this way, and overcome such oppositions and difficulties as these: [58] they were tempted, troubled, scorned, opposed, as well as you; and yet they now triumph in glory. "These are the ones who have come out of the great tribulation. They have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb! For this reason they are before the throne of God, and they serve him day and night in his temple, and the one seated on the throne will shelter them" (Revelation 7:14–15). And all that ever come to heaven (of age) are likely to come this way. And does not the company encourage you? and the success of those that have overcome before you? Will you have the end, and yet refuse the way?

(5.) Consider how much greater difficulties ungodly men go through to hell. They have stronger enemies than you have: the devil and wicked men are your enemies; but God himself is theirs, and yet they will go on. Men threaten but death to discourage you, and God threatens damnation to discourage them; and yet they go on, and are not discouraged. And will you be more afraid of man than sinners are of God? and of death or scorn than they are of hell?

(6.) Indeed, you yourselves must cast your souls on these greater evils if by discouragement you turn from the way of godliness. You must run into hell for fear of burning; and upon everlasting death, to escape a temporal death, or less: you will choose God for your enemy to escape the enmity of man; and how wise a course this is, judge for yourselves; when if you do but see that your ways please God, he can "make your enemies be at peace with you," if he sees it for your good (Proverbs 16:7). If you will fear, fear him that can damn the soul.

(7.) Lastly, remember what abundance of mercies you have to sweeten your present life, and to make your burden easy to you: you have all that is good for you in this life, and the promise of everlasting joy; for godliness "is valuable in every way. It holds promise for the present life and for the life to come" (1 Timothy 4:8). What abundance of mercy you have in your bodies, estates, friends, names, or souls, which are the greatest! What promises and experiences to refresh you! What liberty of access to God! A Christ to rejoice in, a heaven to rejoice in! and yet shall a stony or a dirty way discourage you more than these shall comfort you?

The sum of all is, your work will grow easier and sweeter to you as your skill and strength increase. Your enemies are as grasshoppers before you; the power of the Almighty is engaged by love and promise for your help; and do you claim to trust in God, and yet will fear the face of man? "I offered my back to those who attacked, my jaws to those who tore out my beard; I did not hide my face from insults and spitting. But the Sovereign Lord helps me, so I am not humiliated. For that reason I am steadfastly resolved; I know I will not be put to shame. The one who vindicates me is close by. Who dares to argue with me? Let us confront each other! Who is my accuser? Let him challenge me! Look, the Sovereign Lord helps me. Who dares to condemn me? Look, all of them will wear out like clothes; a moth will eat away at them" (Isaiah 50:6–9). "Listen to me, you who know what is right, you people who are aware of my law. Don't be afraid of the insults of men; don't be discouraged because of their abuse. For a moth will eat away at them like clothes; a clothes moth will devour them like wool. But the vindication I provide will be permanent; the deliverance I give will last" (Isaiah 51:7–8). He is no soldier for Christ that will turn back for fear of scorn, or of anything that man can do against him.

And consider whether heaven should be reached more easily. They are things of unspeakable glory that you strive for; and they are unworthily despised if anything is thought too good to part with for them, or any labor, or difficulties, or sufferings too great to undergo to procure them.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Romans 8:37 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "more than conquerors, through Christ that loveth you," and glosses it: "it is far more desirable … to conquer by patience … than to conquer our persecutors in the field by force of arms." That gloss explains "more than conquerors." The NET reads "we have complete victory through him who loved us," which has no "more than," so the gloss reads as a comment on a different phrase. Flagged.
- **Hebrews 2:10:** Baxter's "Captain of thy salvation" is a military image that fits his argument (Christ leads the troops). The NET reads "the pioneer of their salvation." We use the NET; the military sense is lost. Flagged.
- **Psalm 56:2–5:** Baxter's phrases "enemies were many," "wrested his words daily," "fought against him," and "all their thoughts were against him" combine verses 1, 2, and 5 of the King James. The NET renders them as "many are fighting against me" and "all day long they cause me trouble; they make a habit of plotting my demise." The NET loses "wrested his words daily," a detail Baxter picks out. The NET also treats the heading (a prayer of David written when the Philistines captured him in Gath) as part of verse 1, so its verse numbers run one higher than the King James. We cite 56:2–5.
- **Psalm 46:1–3:** NET wording. The NET treats the heading ("For the music director, by the Korahites …") as part of verse 1, which is why the numbering matches Baxter's only for the text.
- **Luke 12:4–5:** NET wording. The NET has "my friends"; Baxter's quote omits it.
- **2 Corinthians 12:9:** Baxter says "grace which is sufficient for thee, and his strength which appeareth most in our weakness." We kept that wording and quoted the NET: "My grace is enough for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness."
- **Philippians 4:13:** Baxter writes "you can do all things through Christ that strengtheneth you," adapting Paul's first person ("I can do"). We quote the NET, first person, and say "as Paul says."
- **John 16:33:** Baxter writes "he hath overcome the world" (third person). The NET is Christ speaking: "take courage—I have conquered the world."
- **Revelation 7:14–15:** NET wording. The NET ends "the one seated on the throne will shelter them," where the King James has "shall dwell among them."
- **Proverbs 16:7:** Baxter's "make your enemies be at peace with you" is the King James. The NET reads "When a person's ways are pleasing to the Lord, he even reconciles his enemies to himself," where "his" is ambiguous in English (the person's enemies, or God's). We kept Baxter's phrase in quotation marks and give the NET here. Flagged.
- **1 Timothy 4:8:** NET wording. Baxter's "profitable to all things" is "valuable in every way."
- **Isaiah 50:6–9; Isaiah 51:7–8:** NET wording. Baxter cites 50:6–10 but quotes through verse 9. In 51:7–8 the NET reads "aware of my law" and "vindication … deliverance" for the King James "in whose heart is my law" and "righteousness … salvation."
- **Luke 14:26; Matthew 10; Romans 8; Hebrews 11** (cited only).
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "the Israelites in the wilderness, ready to wish themselves again in Egypt" (Numbers 14:2–4); Job's trials; "they of his own household" (Matthew 10:36); "grasshoppers" (Numbers 13:33); "the way … stony or dirty" (compare Matthew 7:14).
- **hardships:** Baxter's "crosses." **useless grain:** his "useless corn."
- **mortified** (item 1): see the earlier notes. **innocent Adam … state of confirmation:** Baxter's view that Adam, before the fall, needed to be tested before being confirmed in his state.
- **the saints' triumphant boasting** (paragraph on God's side): left as written.
- **catholic church** (note 57): the universal church, as used in the Latin. Not "Roman Catholic."
- **Huneric; Victor of Vita; Victorianus** (note 57–58): Victor Uticensis (Victor of Vita) wrote about the persecution of Catholic Christians under the Arian Vandal king Huneric in North Africa (A.D. 477–484). The Latin is a confession attributed to Victorianus, a proconsul who was martyred. Baxter uses them as examples of Christians who stood firm.
- **the tongue-cutting miracle** (note 58): Baxter reports Victor's account that those whose tongues were cut at the root still spoke. We have kept it as Baxter and Victor tell it; we make no claim about it ourselves.
- **Solon** (note 56): the Athenian lawgiver, quoted in Diogenes Laertius.
- **Direct. 6 and Luke 14:26** (item 3): Baxter reads "hate" as "forsake … and treat as men treat hated things" when family members would turn a person from Christ.
- **go on with ease; wondrously hard:** left as written.
