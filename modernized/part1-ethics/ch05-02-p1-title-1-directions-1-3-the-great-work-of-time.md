# Chapter 5: Title 1, Directions 1-3 (the greatness of the work, the state of the soul, the gain of redeemed time)

**Title 1. The contemplative directions for redeeming time.**

**Direction 1. Always keep upon your heart, by faith and consideration, the living sense of the greatness and absolute necessity of that work which must command your time; remembering who sets you to work, and on what a work he sets you, and on what terms, and what will be the end.**

It is God that calls you to labor; and will you stand still or be doing other things, when God expects duty from you? Moses must go to Pharaoh when God bids him go; Jonah must go to Nineveh when God bids him go; indeed, Abraham must go to sacrifice his son when God bids him go. And may you go about your fleshly pleasures, when God commands you to his service? He has appointed you a work that is worth your time and all your labor; to know him, and serve him, and obey him, and to seek everlasting life! How diligently should so excellent a work be done! and so blessed and glorious a Master be served! especially considering the unutterable importance of our diligence! we are in the race appointed us by our Maker, and are to run for an immortal crown. It is heaven that must be now won or lost: and have we time to spare in such a race? We are fighting against the enemies of our salvation; the question is now to be resolved, whether the flesh, the world, and the devil, or we, shall win the day, and have the victory. And heaven or hell must be the issue of our warfare: and have we time to spare in the midst of such a fight? when our very loss of time is no small part of the enemy's conquest? Our most wise, omnipotent Creator has been pleased to make this present life to be the trying preparation for another, resolving that it shall go with us all forever, according to our preparations here: and can we play and loiter away our time, that have such a work as this to do? O miserable, senseless souls! do you believe indeed the life everlasting, and that all your lives are given you now, to resolve the question whether you must be in heaven or hell forever? Do you believe this? Again I ask you, do you believe this? I beg you, ask your consciences over and over, whether you do indeed believe it? Can you believe it, and yet have time to spare? What! find time to play away, and game away, and idle and chatter away, and yet believe that this very time is given you to prepare for life eternal? and that salvation or damnation lies on the race which now, even now, you have to run? Is not such a man a monster of stupidity? If you were asleep, or mad, it would be the more excusable to be so senseless: but to do thus awake, and in your wits! Oh where are the brains of those men, and of what metal are their hardened hearts made, that can idle and play away that time, that little time, that only time, which is given them for the everlasting saving of their souls! Truly, sirs, if sin had not turned the ungodly part of the world into a bedlam, where it is no wonder to see a man out of his wits, people would run out with wonder into the streets to see such a monster as this, as they do to see madmen in the country where they are rare; and they would call to one another, Come and see a man that can trifle and play away his time, as he is going to eternity, and is ready to enter into another world! Come and see a man that has but a few days to win or lose his soul forever in, and is playing it away at cards or dice, or wasting it in doing nothing! Come and see a man that has hours to spare, and cast away upon trifles, with heaven and hell before his eyes. For your soul's sake, consider and tell yourself, if your estate in the world depended upon the spending of this day or week, or if your life depended on it, so that you must live or die, or be poor or rich, sick or well, as you spend it, would you then waste it in dressing up, or formalities, or play? and would you find any to spare upon irrelevant trifling? Or rather would you not be up early, and about your business, and turn aside your games, and your diverting company, and disappoint your idle visitors, and let them find that you are not to be spoken with, nor at leisure to do nothing, but will rather seem uncivil and morose than be ruined! And would you do thus for a passing prosperity or life, and does not life eternal require much more? Will your weighty business in the world resolve you to put aside your friends, your playmates and amusements, and to shake off your idleness? and should not the business of your salvation do it? I would desire no more to refute the crazed time-wasters, when they are disputing for their idle amusements and empty pursuits, and asking what harm is in cards, and dice, and stage plays, or tedious feasts or formalities, adorning idleness, than if I could help them to one sight of heaven and hell, and make them well know what greater business they have to do, which is waiting for them while they sleep or play. If I were just now disputing the case with an idle lady, or a pleasure-loving belly-slave or gambler, and he were asking me scornfully, what hurt is in all this? if one did but knock at his door and tell him, the king is at the door and calls for you, it would make him cast away his game and his dispute: or if the house were on fire, or a child fallen into the fire or water, or thieves breaking in upon them, it would make the ladies cast aside the other lace or ribbon? Or if there were but a good bargain or a lordship to be got, they could be up and going, though amusements and game and finery were cast off: and yet the foresight of heaven and hell, though one of them is even at the door, will not do as much with them; because heaven is as nothing to an unbeliever, or a thoughtless, senseless wretch; and as it is nothing to them when it should move them, it shall be nothing to them when they would enjoy it. Do not say, recreation must be used in its season: I know that necessary sharpening is no hindrance: but God and your own conscience shall tell you shortly whether your recreations, feastings, long dressing, and idleness were a necessary sharpening or refreshment of your body, to fit it for that work which you were born and live for; or whether they were the pastimes of a voluptuous, fleshly brute, that lived in these pleasures for the love of pleasure. Truly, if I looked but on this one unreasonable sin of time-wasting, it would help me to understand the meaning of Luke 15:17, Εις ἑαυτον ελθων, that the prodigal is said to come to himself; and that conversion is the bringing a man to his wits.

**Direction 2. Do not be a stranger to the condition of your own soul, but look home until you are acquainted with what state it is in, and what it is in danger of, and what it lacks, and how far you are behindhand in your provisions for immortality: and then be an idle time-waster if you can.**

Could I but go down with you into that dungeon heart of yours, and show you by the light of truth what is there; could I but let in one convincing beam from heaven, which might fully show you what a condition you are in, and what you have to do with your remaining time; I should have no need to argue you out of your childish foolishness, nor to bid you be up and doing for your soul, any more than to bid you stir if a bear were at your back, or the house in a flame about your ears. Alas, our ordinary time-wasters are such as are yet unconverted, carnal wretches, and are all the while in the power of the devil, who is the chief master of the sport, and the greatest gainer. They are such as are utter strangers to the regenerating, sanctifying work of the Holy Spirit; and are yet unjustified, and under the guilt of all their sins, and certain to be with devils in hell forever, if they die thus before they are converted! (This is true, sinner, and you will shortly find it so, by grace or vengeance, though your blind and hardened heart now rises against the mention of it!) And is this a case for a man to sit at cards and dice in, or to play and swagger in? The Lord have mercy on you, and open your eyes before it is too late, or else your conscience will tell you forever in another manner than I am telling you now, that you had need to have made better use of your time, and had greater things to have spent it in. What! for a man in your case, in an unrenewed, unsanctified, unpardoned state, to be thus casting away that little time which all his hopes lie on! and in which, if ever, he must be recovered and saved! O Lord, have mercy on such senseless souls, and bring them to themselves before it is too late! I tell you, man, an enlightened person, that understands what it is, and has escaped it, would not for all the kingdoms of the world be a week or a day in your condition, for fear lest death cut off his hopes and shut him up in hell that very day. He would not dare sleep quietly in your condition a night, lest death should snatch him away to hell; and can you play and amuse yourself in it, and live carelessly in a pleasure-loving course? Oh what a thing it is to be blindfolded in misery, and to be led asleep to hell! Who could persuade men to live thus awake, and go dancing to hell with their eyes open! Oh! if we should imagine a Peter or a Paul, or any of the blessed, to be again brought into such a case as one of these unsanctified sinners, and yet to know what they now know! What would they do? would they feast, and game, and play, and trifle away their time in it? or would they not rather suddenly bewail their former misspent time, and all their sins, and cry day and night to God for mercy, and fly to Christ, and spend all their time in holiness and obedience to God! Alas, poor sinner, do but look into your heart, and see there what you have yet to do (of greater weight than adorning and playing): I almost tremble to think and write what a case you are in, and what you have to do, while you live as if you had time to spare! If you do not know, I will tell you, and the Lord make you know it: you have a hardened heart yet to be softened; and an unbelieving heart to be brought to a living, powerful belief of the word of God and the unseen world: you have an unholy heart and life to be made holy, if ever you will see the face of God (Hebrews 12:14; Matthew 18:3; John 3:3, 5, 6). You have a heart full of sins to be mortified and subdued: and an unreformed life to be reformed; (and what abundance of particulars these generals contain!) You have a pardon to procure through Jesus Christ for all the sins that ever you committed, and all the duties which ever you omitted: you have an offended God to be reconciled to, and for your estranged soul to know as your Father in Jesus Christ! What abundance of Scripture truths you have to learn which you are ignorant of! How many holy duties, as prayer, meditation, holy conversation, etc., to learn which you are unskilled in! and to perform when you have learned them! How many works of justice and charity to men's souls and bodies you have to do! How many needy ones to relieve as you are able! and the sick to visit, and the naked to clothe, and the sad to comfort, and the ignorant to instruct, and the ungodly to exhort! (Hebrews 3:13; 10:25; Ephesians 4:29). What abundance of duty you have to perform in your relations! to parents or children, to husband or wife, as a master or a servant, and the rest! You little know what sufferings you have to prepare for. You have faith, and love, and repentance, and patience, and all God's graces, to get and to exercise daily, and to increase. You have your accounts to prepare, and assurance of salvation to obtain, and death and judgment to prepare for. What does your heart think of all this work? Put it off as lightly as you will, it is God himself that has laid it on you, and it must be done in time, or you must be ruined forever! And yet it must not be your toil, but your delight: this is appointed you for your chief recreation. Look into the Scripture and into your heart, and you will find that all this is to be done. And do you think in your conscience that this is not greater business than your gaudy dressing, your idle visits, or your needless amusements? which is more worthy of your time?

**Direction 3. Remember how profitable the redeeming of time is, and how exceedingly comforting in the review!**

In merchandise, or any trading, in farming, or any gaining course, we usually say of a man that has grown rich by it that he has made use of his time. But when heaven, and communion with God in the way, and a life of holy strength and comfort, and a death full of joy and hope is to be the gain, how cheerfully should time be redeemed for these! If it is pleasant for a man to find himself thrive and prosper in any rising or pleasing employment, how pleasant must it be continually to us to find that in redeeming time the work of God and our souls prosper! Look back now on the time that is past, and tell me which part is sweetest to your thoughts? However it is now, I can tell you, at death, it will be an unspeakable comfort to look back on a well-spent life; and to be able to say in humble sincerity, My time was not cast away on worldliness, ambition, idleness, or fleshly empty pursuits or pleasures; but spent in the sincere and laborious service of my God, and making my calling and election sure, and doing all the good to men's souls and bodies that I could do in the world: it was entirely devoted to God and his church, and the good of others and my soul. What a joy it is when, going out of the world, we can in our place and measure say with our blessed Lord and pattern, "I glorified you on earth by completing the work you gave me to do. And now, Father, glorify me at your side" (John 17:4–5). Or as Paul, "I am already being poured out as an offering, and the time for me to depart is at hand. I have competed well; I have finished the race; I have kept the faith! Finally the crown of righteousness is reserved for me. The Lord, the righteous Judge, will award it to me" (2 Timothy 4:6–8). And, "For our reason for confidence is this: The testimony of our conscience, that with pure motives and sincerity which are from God—not by human wisdom ... we conducted ourselves in the world" (2 Corinthians 1:12). It is a great comfort in sickness to be able to say with Hezekiah, "Please, Lord. Remember how I have served you faithfully and with wholehearted devotion, and how I have carried out your will" (Isaiah 38:3). Oh! time well spent is a precious cordial to a soul that is going to its final sentence, and is making up its last and general accounts: indeed, the reviews of it will be joyful in heaven: which is given, though most freely by the covenant antecedently, yet as a reward by our most righteous Judge, when he comes to sentence men according to that covenant.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org for every verse quoted.
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," meaning a heading or division of the chapter. Chapter 5 has five Titles; this is the first, the "contemplative" directions (things to consider and keep in mind), as distinct from the practical directions of Title 3 (Title 2 gives further contemplative directions).
- **Direction 1, heading:** "Still keep upon thy heart" is rendered "Always keep upon your heart" ("still" in the older sense of continually). "lively sense" is rendered "living sense" (Baxter's "lively" means vivid and living, not energetic); likewise "lively, powerful belief" in Direction 2 is "living, powerful belief." The heading's first sentence is set in bold as in the finished files; the body begins with "It is God that calls you..."
- **John 17:4–5 (flag):** Baxter quotes the King James, "I have glorified thee on earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do: and now, O Father, glorify me with thyself," and stops there. The NET joins the two clauses ("I glorified you on earth by completing the work you gave me to do"), so the separate "I have finished the work," which is the phrase Baxter's point about a finished life rests on, becomes a subordinate "by completing." Baxter's "with thyself" is the NET's "at your side"; we stop the quotation where he stops.
- **2 Timothy 4:6–8 (flag):** Baxter's "I am now ready to be offered" is the NET's "I am already being poured out as an offering" (we drop the NET's opening "For"), and his "I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course" is the NET's "I have competed well; I have finished the race." The NET's athletic image fits Baxter's "race" language earlier in Direction 1, but the soldier's "fight" (which also echoes his "fighting against the enemies of our salvation") is lost. Baxter quotes only to "shall give"; we stop at "will award it to me."
- **2 Corinthians 1:12 (flag):** Baxter cites this for comfort and joy in looking back: the King James "our rejoicing is this." The NET has "our reason for confidence is this," which weakens the note of rejoicing he is leaning on. The King James "simplicity and godly sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom, we have had our conversation in the world" is the NET's "with pure motives and sincerity which are from God—not by human wisdom but by the grace of God—we conducted ourselves in the world." Baxter's quotation skips the King James "but by the grace of God"; we mark the matching NET words ("but by the grace of God") with "...".
- **Isaiah 38:3 (flag):** The King James "how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is good in thy sight" is the NET's "how I have served you faithfully and with wholehearted devotion, and how I have carried out your will." The NET loses the "walking before God" image and "perfect heart," and "done that which is good in thy sight" becomes "carried out your will." The sense Baxter wants (a sincere life to look back on) survives.
- **Luke 15:17 (Greek in the text):** Εις ἑαυτον ελθων is kept as printed (without some accents and breathings). English: "coming to himself." The NET renders it "when he came to his senses," which fits Baxter's point that conversion brings a man "to his wits."
- **Cited only:** Hebrews 12:14; Matthew 18:3; John 3:3, 5, 6; Hebrews 3:13; 10:25; Ephesians 4:29. Baxter's references put in parentheses in modern form; the text around them is his, not a quotation.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Moses sent to Pharaoh (Exodus 3–4); Jonah sent to Nineveh (Jonah 1, 3); Abraham and Isaac (Genesis 22); the race and the immortal crown (1 Corinthians 9:24–25; Hebrews 12:1); "to know him" (compare John 17:3); "making my calling and election sure" (2 Peter 1:10); the sick visited and the naked clothed (Matthew 25:35–36); "redeeming time" (Ephesians 5:16; Colossians 4:5, the text behind the whole chapter).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): prate away to chatter away; sport away to play away; dressings to dressing up / dressing; compliment, complimenting to formalities (Baxter means ceremonious courtesies and visits, not praise); impertinent triflings to irrelevant trifling; betimes to early; turn by, put by, cast by to turn aside, put aside, cast aside; undone to ruined; transitory to passing; play-fellows to playmates; sports to amusements; vanities to empty pursuits; confute to refute; distracted (mad) to crazed; staying for (waiting for) to waiting for; sensual to pleasure-loving; gamester to gambler; riband to ribbon; gaudery to finery; inconsiderate (thoughtless) to thoughtless; wanteth to lacks; fooleries to foolishness; Holy Ghost to Holy Spirit; improved (made profitable use of) to made better use of; durst not to would not dare; dispute (thee out of) to argue (you out of); sport and play to play and amuse yourself; sport and swagger to play and swagger; verily to truly; yea to indeed; beseech to beg; securely to carelessly (Baxter's "security" is careless confidence); hoodwinked (blindfolded) to blindfolded; trimming (adorning oneself) to adorning; holy conference to holy conversation; unskilful to unskilled; chiefest to chief; gainful to profitable; comfortable to comforting; husbandry to farming; "we use to say" to "we usually say."
- **"if thy estate in the world did lie upon ... or if thy life lay on it":** rendered "depended upon ... depended on it."
- **"it would make the ladies cast by the other lace or riband?":** Baxter's sentence ends with a question mark though it is worded as a statement (perhaps a rhetorical question, or a misprint). Kept as printed.
- **"tedious feasts or complimenting, adorning idleness":** the punctuation is ambiguous; "adorning idleness" may be a further item (dressing up and idleness) or a description of the formalities (ways of decorating idleness). We kept Baxter's commas: "tedious feasts or formalities, adorning idleness."
- **"necessary whetting is no letting":** apparently a proverb (sharpening the scythe is no hindrance to mowing); "letting" means hindering. Rendered "necessary sharpening is no hindrance." Baxter means that needful recreation does not delay the work but fits one for it.
- **Kept with a note:** **bedlam** (a madhouse, from the London hospital of Bethlehem); **lordship** (a landed estate with its rights and title); **cordial** (a reviving medicine or drink); **carnal** (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh); **mortified** (put to death, of sins); **behindhand** (behind, in arrears); **voluptuous** (given to pleasure); **antecedently** (beforehand: heaven is given freely by the covenant before any works, and yet given as a reward at the judgment according to that covenant; this is Baxter's own theology of the covenant and is kept as his).
- **belly-slave; an idle lady:** Baxter's blunt words and his examples are kept as written.
- **"metal" of their hearts:** kept as printed.
- **"in disputing the case"** rendered "disputing the case." **"make him to cast away"** rendered "make him cast away."
