# Chapter 8: Part 5, Title 1: against fornication, the greatness of the sin (1–8)

**Part 5.**

**Title 1. Directions against fornication and all uncleanness.**

Though as they are sins against another, adultery and fornication are forbidden in the seventh commandment, and should be handled there, yet as they are sins against our own bodies, which should be members of Christ, and temples of the Holy Spirit, as 1 Corinthians 6:15, 18, 19, so it is here to be handled among the rest of the sins of the senses: and I choose all the more to take it up here, because what I have said in the last two titles, against gluttony and drunkenness, serves also for this. The same arguments, and convincing questions, and directions, will almost all serve, if you only change the name of the sin: and as the reader does not love needless tediousness, so I am glad of this means to avoid the too frequent naming of such an odious, filthy sin: yet something most proper to it must be spoken. And, 1. I show the greatness of the sin; and, 2. Give directions for the cure.

1. There is no sin so odious, but love for it, and frequent use of it, will do much to reconcile the very judgment to it; either to think it lawful, or tolerable and venial: to think it no sin, or only a little sin, and easily forgiven. And so with some brutish persons it does in this. But, 1. It is reason enough against any sin, that it is forbidden by the most wise, infallible, universal King of all the world. Your Maker's will is enough to condemn it, and shall be enough to condemn those that are servants of it. [448] He has said, "You shall not commit adultery." "Do not be deceived! The sexually immoral, idolaters, adulterers, passive homosexual partners, practicing homosexuals ... will not inherit the kingdom of God" (1 Corinthians 6:9–10). "Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ? Should I take the members of Christ and make them members of a prostitute? Never! Or do you not know that anyone who is united with a prostitute is one body with her? For it is said, 'The two will become one flesh.' But the one united with the Lord is one spirit with him. Flee sexual immorality! 'Every sin a person commits is outside of the body'—but the immoral person sins against his own body. Or do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you?" (1 Corinthians 6:15–19). (Note that he does not speak this to fornicators; for their bodies are not temples of the Holy Spirit; but to those that by filthy heretics in those times were tempted to think fornication no great sin.) So, "But among you there must not be either sexual immorality, impurity of any kind, or greed, as these are not fitting for the saints. Neither should there be vulgar speech, foolish talk, or coarse jesting ... For you can be confident of this one thing: that no person who is immoral, impure, or greedy (such a person is an idolater) has any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and God. Let nobody deceive you with empty words, for because of these things God's wrath comes on the sons of disobedience. Therefore do not be sharers with them" (Ephesians 5:3–6). "Now the works of the flesh are obvious: sexual immorality, impurity, depravity ... I am warning you, as I had warned you before: Those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom of God!" (Galatians 5:19). "For this is God's will: that you become holy, that you keep away from sexual immorality, that each of you know how to possess his own body in holiness and honor, not in lustful passion like the Gentiles who do not know God" (1 Thessalonians 4:3). See also Colossians 3:5, 6. "Marriage must be honored among all and the marriage bed kept undefiled, for God will judge sexually immoral people and adulterers" (Hebrews 13:4). "... detestable persons ... the sexually immoral ... their place will be in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur" (Revelation 21:8). "Outside are the dogs and the sorcerers and the sexually immoral, and the murderers" (Revelation 22:15). "So also Sodom and Gomorrah and the neighboring towns, since they indulged in sexual immorality and pursued unnatural desire in a way similar to these angels, are now displayed as an example by suffering the punishment of eternal fire" (Jude 7). I shall add no more lest I be tedious.

2. Besides Scripture, God has planted in nature a special sense of shame and modesty to restrain this sin; and they that commit it violate the law of nature, and sin against a witness and condemner that is within them. And scarcely any one of them ever commits it boldly, quietly, and fearlessly, till first they have hardened their hearts, and seared their consciences, and overcome the light of nature, by frequent, willful sinning. [449] Nature hides the obscene parts, and teaches man to blush at the mention of anything that is beyond the bounds of modesty. Do not say that it is mere custom, for the corrupted nature of man is not so over-precise, nor the villainy of the world so rare and modest, but before this day it would have quite banished all restraints of this sin, above most others, if they could have done it, and if God had not written the law which condemns it very deep in nature, with almost indelible characters. So that in spite of the horrid wickedness of the earth, though mankind is almost universally inclined to lust, yet there are universally laws and customs restraining it; so that except a very few savages and cannibals like beasts, there is no nation on the earth where filthiness is not a shame, and modesty does not lay some rebukes upon uncleanness. Ask no further then for a law, when your nature itself is a law against it. And the better any man is, the more does he abhor the lusts of uncleanness. So that "among saints," says the apostle, it is "not to be named" (that is, not without need and detestation), Ephesians 5:3; and verse 12, "For the things they do in secret are shameful even to mention." And when drunkenness had uncovered the shame of Noah, his son Ham is cursed for beholding it, and the other sons blessed for their modesty and reverent covering of him.

3. And that God has not put this law into man's nature without very great cause, although the implicit belief and submission due to him should satisfy us, though we did not know the causes particularly, yet much of them is well known to common observation: as that if God had not restrained lust by laws, it would have made the female sex most contemptible and miserable, and used worse by men than dogs are. For, first, rapes and violence would deflower them, because they are too weak to make resistance: and if that had been restrained, yet the lust of men would have been unsatisfied, and most would have grown weary of the same woman whom they had abused, and take another; at least when she grew old, they would choose a younger, and so the aged women would be the most calamitous creatures upon earth. Besides that lust is addicted to variety, and grows weary of the same; the fallings out between men and women, and the sicknesses that make their persons less pleasing, and age, and other accidents, would expose them almost all to utter misery. And men would be law-makers, and therefore would make no laws for their relief, but what was consistent with their lusts and ends. So that half the world would have been ruined, had it not been for the laws of matrimony, and such others as restrain the lusts of men.

4. Also there would be a confused mixture in procreation, and no men would well know what children are their own: which is worse than not to know their lands or houses.

5. By this all natural affection would be diminished or extinguished: as the love of husband and wife, so the love between fathers and children would be diminished.

6. And consequently the due education of children would be hindered, or utterly overthrown. The mothers that should first take care of them would be disabled and turned away, that fresh harlots might be received, who would hate the offspring of the former. So that by this means the world and all societies, and civility, would be ruined, and men would be made worse than brutes, whom nature has either better taught, or else made for them some other supply. Learning, religion, and civility would be all in a manner extinct, as we see they are among those few savage cannibals that are under no restraint. For how much all these depend upon education, experience tells us. In a word, this confusion in procreation would introduce such confusion in men's hearts, and families, and all societies, by corrupting and destroying necessary affection and education, that it would be the greatest plague imaginable to mankind, and make the world so base and beastly, that to destroy mankind from off the earth would seem much more desirable. Judge then whether God should have left men's lusts unrestrained.

*Objection.* But (you will say) there might have been some moderate restraint to a certain number, as it is with the Muslims, without so much strictness as Christ uses.

*Answer.* That this strictness is necessary, and is an excellence in God's law, appears thus. 1. By the greatness of the mischief which otherwise would follow: to be remiss in preventing such a confusion in the world would be an enmity to the world. 2. In that man's nature is so violently inclined to break over, that if the hedge were not close, there would be no sufficient restraining them; they would quickly run out at a little gap. 3. The wiser and the better any nation or persons are, even among the heathens, the more fully do they consent to the strictness of God's laws. 4. The cleanest sort of brutes themselves are taught by nature to be as strict in their copulations: though it is otherwise with the mere terrestrial beasts and birds, yet the aerial go by couples: those that are called the fowls of the heavens, that fly in the air, are commonly taught this chastity by nature; as if God would not have lust come near to heaven. 5. The families of the Muslims that have more wives than one show the mischief of it in the effects, in the hatred and disagreement of their wives, and the great slavery that women are kept in; making them like slaves that they may keep them quiet. And when women are thus enslaved, who have so great a part in the education of children, by which all virtue and civility are maintained in the world, it must necessarily tend to the debasing and brutifying of mankind.

7. Children being the most precious of all our treasure, it is necessary that the strictest laws be made for the securing of their good education and their welfare. If it shall be treason to debase or counterfeit the king's coin, and if men must be hanged for robbing you of your goods or money, and the laws are not thought too strict that are made to secure your estates; how much more is it necessary that the laws be strict against the corrupting of mankind, and against the debasement of your image on your children, and against that which tends to the rooting out of all virtue, and the ruin of all societies and souls!

8. God will have a holy seed in the world, that shall bear his image of holiness, and therefore he will have all means fitted to it. Brutish, promiscuous generation tends to the production of a brutish seed. And though the word preached is the means of sanctifying those that remain unsanctified from their youth; yet a holy marriage, and holy dedication of children to God, and holy education of them, are the earlier means, which God would not have neglected or corrupted, and to which he promises his blessing: as you may see, 1 Corinthians 7:14; Malachi 2:15, "Did he not make one? Yet he had the residue of the Spirit. And why one? That he might seek a godly seed. Therefore take heed to your spirit, and let none deal treacherously against the wife of his youth: for the Lord hates putting away."

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Every verse quoted as NET was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Labels:** this file begins Part 5 of Chapter 8. The source heads Part 5 only with "PART V." and gives no separate sub-heading for the Part; the first division is headed "_Tit._ 1." "Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," that is, a heading or division within a Part (as in Part 4, Titles 1 and 2, against gluttony and drunkenness, which Baxter calls "the last two titles"). Title 1 continues in the next file.
- **Numbering:** Baxter announces two heads ("1. I show the greatness of the sin; and, 2. Give directions for the cure"). The greatness of the sin then runs as a numbered list 1–8 (continuing to 9 in the next file); inside item 1 he also begins "But, 1. It is reason enough...", and the Answer to the Objection has its own list 1–5. All kept as printed.
- **References placed after the quotations:** Baxter gives most references before the verse (for example "1 Cor. vi. 9, 10, 'Be not deceived...'"); following our practice they are moved into parentheses after the quotation. In item 8 his "as you may see, 1 Cor. vii. 14; Mal. ii. 15, '...'" is left in his order because the references are part of his sentence.
- **"You shall not commit adultery":** Exodus 20:14 (NET); the reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **1 Corinthians 6:9–10:** NET wording, with "..." for the part Baxter skips (the NET's "thieves, the greedy, drunkards, the verbally abusive, and swindlers"). The King James had "fornicators ... effeminate ... abusers of themselves with mankind"; the NET has "the sexually immoral ... passive homosexual partners, practicing homosexuals." Baxter's point (the fornicator is excluded from the kingdom) stands, but his word "fornicators" becomes the broader "sexually immoral" here and in most of the verses below (Ephesians 5:3, 5; Galatians 5:19; 1 Thessalonians 4:3; Hebrews 13:4; Revelation 21:8; 22:15; Jude 7), where the King James had "fornication," "whoremonger(s)," or "giving themselves over to fornication."
- **1 Corinthians 6:15–19:** NET wording. Baxter's "(saith he)" in verse 16 corresponds to the NET's "For it is said." Two points of sense: (1) the NET puts "Every sin a person commits is outside of the body" in quotation marks, as a saying of the Corinthians that Paul answers, whereas the King James (as Baxter quotes it, "Every sin that a man doth is without the body") reads it as Paul's own statement; Baxter quotes it in the King James sense. (2) Baxter's quotation stops at "which is in you," so the NET verse is cut at "who is in you."
- **Ephesians 5:3–6:** NET wording, with "..." where Baxter skips (the end of verse 4). Baxter's quotation ends with "be not ye therefore partakers with them," which is verse 7, though he cites 3–6; we keep his reference. The King James verse 3 reads "let it not be once named among you"; the NET reads "among you there must not be." Baxter leans on "named" later, in item 2 ("among saints ... not to be named"); there we keep his King James words, since the NET does not have "named." In verse 5 Baxter's text has "the kingdom of God"; the NET has "the kingdom of Christ and God."
- **Ephesians 5:12:** NET wording.
- **Galatians 5:19:** Baxter cites verse 19, but his quotation runs to verse 21; NET wording, with "..." where he skips. The King James list began "Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness"; the NET has "sexual immorality, impurity, depravity," with no separate "adultery" (the NET follows a Greek text without that word). Baxter's argument does not depend on the extra word.
- **1 Thessalonians 4:3:** Baxter cites verse 3, but his quotation runs to verse 5; NET wording. The King James "possess his vessel in sanctification and honour" becomes the NET's "possess his own body in holiness and honor," and "the lust of concupiscence" becomes "lustful passion."
- **Colossians 3:5, 6:** the source prints "Col. v. 5, 6," but Colossians has four chapters; the verses on fornication are 3:5–6, so we have corrected the reference.
- **Hebrews 13:4:** NET wording. The King James was a statement ("Marriage is honourable"); the NET reads it as a command ("Marriage must be honored"). Baxter's use (God will judge the sexually immoral) is unaffected.
- **Revelation 21:8; 22:15:** NET wording, with "..." where Baxter skips. "Brimstone" becomes "sulfur." Baxter's "For without are dogs" has no "For" in the NET.
- **Jude 7:** NET wording of the whole verse. The King James "in like manner, giving themselves over to fornication, and going after strange flesh" becomes "indulged in sexual immorality and pursued unnatural desire in a way similar to these angels"; the NET makes explicit a comparison with the angels of the previous verse.
- **Malachi 2:15 (with 2:16):** kept in Baxter's King James wording (modernized only in spelling and verb forms), because the NET reads the verse very differently and does not contain the words Baxter builds on. NET: "No one who has even a small portion of the Spirit in him does this. What did our ancestor do when seeking a child from God? Be attentive, then, to your own spirit, for one should not be disloyal to the wife he took in his youth." His last clause, "for the Lord hates putting away," is from verse 16 in the King James; the NET has "'I hate divorce,' says the Lord God of Israel." Baxter's argument rests on "make one" and "a godly seed" (God made one wife for one husband to raise holy children); the NET's "What did our ancestor do when seeking a child from God?" does not carry that reading. **putting away** (kept): divorce.
- **1 Corinthians 7:14:** cited, not quoted (the children of a believing parent are called holy).
- **Unmarked allusion:** Noah's drunkenness and Ham (Genesis 9:20–27). Baxter says "his son Ham is cursed"; in the text of Genesis (9:25) the curse is pronounced on Canaan, Ham's son. Kept as Baxter wrote it.
- **"members of Christ, and temples of the Holy Spirit, as 1 Corinthians 6:15, 18, 19":** Baxter's paraphrase of those verses, not a quotation.
- **venial** (kept): in Baxter's usage, a sin that is pardonable or slight, as opposed to mortal.
- **brutish, brutes** (kept): like or belonging to the beasts.
- **sense of shame:** Baxter's "pudor," the Latin word for shame or modesty, used in English in his day.
- **savages and cannibals:** Baxter's words and his view of the peoples outside the civilized and Christian world, kept as written.
- **Muslims:** Baxter's "Mahometans," the term of his day. His claims about Muslim families and the treatment of women are his own, kept as written.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): the rather to all the more; too often naming to too frequent naming; do but to only; vitiated to corrupted; despite to spite; scarce to scarcely; albeit to although; notorious (widely known) to well known; consisted with to was consistent with; hereby to by this; else to otherwise; excellency to excellence; must needs to must necessarily; preciousest to most precious; vitiating to corrupting; extirpation to rooting out; thereunto to to it; former (earlier, first) to earlier; wherefore to why; hateth to hates.
- **Kept words:** deflower; calamitous; fallings out (quarrels); accidents (chance events, misfortunes); civility (civilized order and manners); in a manner (so to speak); break over (break through bounds); fowls of the heavens; brutifying (making brutish); generation (begetting, procreation); seed (offspring); harlot; the word preached (preaching).
- **"the debasement of your image on your children":** Baxter's figure, carrying on from "the king's coin": as a coin bears the king's image, children bear their father's. Kept as written.
- **Note 448:** a list of Scripture references, converted to modern form as printed. We have not checked whether each reference (for example Proverbs 6:13–15) is exactly what Baxter intended.
- **Note 449:** Boniface (Winfrid) is the English missionary to the Germans; Ethelbald, as Baxter says, was a Mercian king. "Perefrid" is as printed; we have not identified the recipient or confirmed the letter number. "Salvagus, bishop of Sarzana," "Paul V," and "Giraldi, *Apology for the Venetian Senate*" are as Baxter cites them; we have not checked the passage. The Latin "Episcopus-Pauli 5. Jussu" is printed with a hyphen joining "Episcopus" and "Pauli"; we read it as "bishop, by order of Paul V." The "Roman law, 12. tab." is Baxter's citation of the Twelve Tables; we have not confirmed that this wording is from them. Baxter's claim about the clergy is his polemic against Rome, kept as written.
- No passage in this range has been omitted.
