# Chapter 1, Part 2 (continued): Temptations 29–30

**Temptation 29.** Another way of the tempter is by drawing them desperately to risk their souls; come what may, they will put it to the risk rather than live so strict a life.

**Direction 29.** But, O man, consider what you do, and who will bear the loss of it! and how quickly it may be too late to recall your gamble! What should put you on so mad a resolution? Is sin so good? is hell so easy? is your soul so contemptible? is heaven such a trifle? is God so hard a master? is his work so grievous, and his way so bad? does he require anything unreasonable of you? has God set you such a grievous task that it is better to risk damnation than perform it? You cannot believe this if you believe him to be God. Come near, and think more deliberately on it, and you will find you might better run from your food, your friend, your life, than from your God, and from a holy life, when you run but into sin and hell.

**Temptation 30.** Another great temptation is making them believe that their sins are but such common infirmities as the best have: they cannot deny that they have their faults; but are not all men sinners? They hope that these are not reigning, unpardoned sins.

**Direction 30.** But, oh how great a difference there is between a converted and an unconverted sinner! between the failings of a child and the contempt of a rebel! between a sinner that has no gross or mortal sin, and hates, bewails, and strives against his infirmities; and a sinner that loves his sin, and is loath to leave it, and makes light of it, and does not love a holy life. God will one day show you a difference between these two, when you see that there are sinners that are justified and saved, and sinners that are condemned.

\* \* \* \* \*

*(This ends Baxter's first series of thirty Temptations. A second series of nine subordinate Temptations, on the particular excuse "they are but infirmities," follows; Baxter restarts the numbering at 1.)*

## Baxter's notes

None in these two pairs.

## Translator's notes

- **No Scripture is quoted or cited in these two pairs.**
- **risk** (Temptation 29): Baxter's "venture." **gamble:** his "adventure" (a venture one has risked).
- **who will bear the loss of it:** Baxter's "who will have the loss of it."
- **"you might better run from your food, your friend, your life, than from your God"** (Direction 29): you would do better to run from anything else, even from the necessities of life, than from God and a holy life.
- **infirmities; reigning, unpardoned sins** (Temptation 30): Baxter's terms for the difference between ordinary weakness (infirmities, which every Christian has) and dominant, unrepented sin (reigning sins). See the notes to Direction 2 of Part 1 on "mortal" and "venial." **gross or mortal sin:** left in Baxter's sense (a sin that rules the person and is inconsistent with holiness).
- **the failings of a child and the contempt of a rebel** (Direction 30): the contrast Baxter wants to make: a child who loves his father and falls short, versus a rebel who despises him.
- **loath:** Baxter's "loth." **bewails:** left as written.
- **the divider** (end of the first series): Baxter's own row of asterisks, reproduced.
