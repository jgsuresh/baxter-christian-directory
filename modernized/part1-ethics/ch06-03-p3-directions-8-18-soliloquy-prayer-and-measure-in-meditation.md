# Chapter 6: Title 3, Directions 8-18 about the work itself

*This file continues Title 3 (making good thoughts effectual) and its "Directions about the work itself" from the previous file; it begins at a new Direction.*

**Direction 8. Whenever you are called to meditate on any smaller truth or thing, see that you do not take it as separated from the greater, but always view it as connected to them, and planted and growing in them, and receiving their life and beauty from them; so that you may always preserve the life and interest of the greatest matters in your hearts, and may not mortify the least, and turn it into a deceit or idol.**

We are to climb upwards, and not to descend downwards: and therefore we begin at the body of the tree, and so pass up to the few and greatest boughs; and from there to the smaller numerous branches, which as they are hard to discern, number, and remember, so they are not all strong enough to bear us; but are fitted rather to be looked on, than trodden and rested on. But if you do not take them as growing from the greater boughs, but cut them off, they lose their life, and beauty, and fruitfulness. If all the controversies in the church had been managed with due honor and preservation of holiness, charity, unity, peace, and greater truths; and if all the circumstantial matters in religion had been ordered with a safeguard, and due regard, and proper subordination to the power and spirituality of holy worship, the Christian world would have had more life, and strength, and fruitfulness, and less imagery, unholy, ludicrous formality, and hypocrisy.

**Direction 9. Let the end and order of your meditations be first for the settling of your judgments, and next for the resolving and settling of your wills, and thirdly, for the reforming and bettering of your lives; and, only in the fourth place, after all these, for the raising of your holy passions or lively feeling; which must have only its proper room and place.**

But indeed where some of these are done already, they may be assumed, and we may proceed to that which is yet to be done. For instance, if you know what is sin and duty, but do not do it, your meditation must be, not to make you know what you did not know, but first to consider well what you know, and set the powerful truth before you; and then labor by this to bring your wills to a fixed resolution of obedience. But if it is a truth whose principal use is on the will and affections (as to draw up the heart to the love of God, by meditating on his attractive excellencies), then the most pains must be taken there. On which see Chapter 3, Direction 11.

**Direction 10. Turn your thoughts often into soliloquies; methodically and earnestly preaching to your own hearts, as you would do on that subject to others if it were to save their souls.**

[308]

As this will keep you in order, from rambling and running off, and will also supply you with continual matter (for method is a wonderful help both to invention, memory, and delight), so it will bring things soonest to your affections: and earnest pleading of convincing reasons with our own hearts is a powerful way to make the fire burn, and to kindle desire, fear, love, hatred, repentance, shame, sorrow, joy, resolution, or any good effect. Convictions, reproaches, remonstrances, rebukes, and self-persuasions may be very powerful; whereas a dull way of bare thinking is only like a dull way of preaching, without any lively application, which stirs the hearers little. Learn purposely from the liveliest books you read, and from the best and liveliest preachers you hear, to preach to your hearts, and use it in an orderly way, and you will find it a most powerful way of meditating.

**Direction 11. Turn your meditations often into brief prayers and addresses to God; for that will keep you reverent, serious, and awake, and make all the more powerful, because the more divine.**

When you meditate on sin, turn sometimes to God, by penitent lamentation, and say, Lord, what a wretch and rebel was I to entertain such an enemy of yours in my heart! and for nothing to offend you and violate your laws! O pardon, O cleanse me, O strengthen me! Conquer and cast out this odious enemy of you and me. So when you are seeking to stir up or exercise any grace, send up a fervent request to God to show his love and power upon your dead and sluggish heart, and to be the principal agent in a work which is so much his own. Prayer is a most holy duty, in which the soul deals so nearly with God, that if there is any holy seriousness in the heart, it will thus be stirred up: a dull and wandering mind will bear some reverence to God; and therefore involve him in all.

**Direction 12. Let every meditation be undertaken in a humble sense of your own insufficiency, with a believing dependence on your Head and Savior, to guide and enliven you by his Holy Spirit, and to cover the infirmities of your holiest thoughts.**

Whatever good is written upon our hearts must be "written ... by the Spirit of the living God": and this "confidence" we must have "in God through Christ": "Not that we are adequate in ourselves to consider anything as if it were coming from ourselves, but our adequacy is from God" (2 Corinthians 3:3–5). How heavily will all go on, or rather how certainly shall we labor in vain, and cast off all, if Christ casts us off, and leaves us to ourselves! Do not think that your life and strength are rooted in yourselves: go to him by renewed acts of faith, by whom you must be enlivened.

**Direction 13. Do not let your holy thoughts be so rare as to keep you unfamiliar with the matter of your meditations, nor so short as to be gone before you have made anything of it.**

Now and then a cursory thought will not acquaint the soul with God, nor bring it to a habit and temperament of holiness. Whereas that which you think on frequently and seriously, as your business and delight, will become the nourishment and nature of your souls; as the air which we daily breathe in, and the food which we daily live upon, do to our bodies. And you will find that as practice will breed skill and strength, so it will cause such acquaintance and familiarity as will very much tend to the fruit and comfort of the work. Whereas those that only cast now and then a look at God and holiness, or are rare and short in holy thoughts, lose so quickly the little which they get, that it makes no great change in them.

**Direction 14. Yet do not overdo it as to intensity or length; but carry on the work sincerely according to the abilities of your minds and bodies; lest going beyond your strength, you damage your brains, and unsettle your minds, and make yourselves unable to do anything at all.**

Though we cannot love God too much in our esteem, yet it is possible to think of him with too much passion, or too long at once; because it may be more than the spirits and brain can bear; and if once they are overstrained, if they do not break, like a lute-string screwed too high, they will be like a leg that is out of joint, that can pain you, but not bear you. While the soul rides on so lame or dull a horse as the body is, it must not go the pace which it desires, but which the body can bear; or else it may quickly be dismounted, or like one that rides on a tired horse. It is not the horse that goes at first with chafing heat and violence which will travel best; but you must press on at the pace that you are able to keep up. You little know how lamentable and distressed a case you will be in, or how great an advantage the tempter has, if once he but tires you by overdoing!

**Direction 15. Do not choose unnecessarily or ordinarily the bitterest or most unpleasant subjects for your meditation, lest you make it grow a burden to you; but dwell most on the sweet, delightful thoughts of the infinite love of God revealed by Christ, and the eternal glory purchased by him, and the wonderful helps and mercies in the way.**

As it is the gospel which Christ's ministers must preach to others, so it is the gospel which in your meditations you must preach most to yourselves. It is love and pleasure which you must principally strive to stir up: and you must do it by contemplating lovableness and happiness, the objects of love and pleasure. For the thoughts of terror, and wrath, and misery are unfit to stir up these: though to the unconverted, dull, secure, presumptuous, or pleasure-loving sinner, such thoughts are very necessary to awake him, and prepare him for the thoughts of love and peace. It is the principal part of this art to keep off loathing and aversion, and to keep up readiness and delight.

**Direction 16. When you are in company, let out the fruit of your secret meditations in holy, edifying conversation.**

Do not gather for yourselves only, but that you may communicate to others. The good "expert in the law who has been trained for the kingdom of heaven" must "bring out of his treasure what is new and old" (Matthew 13:52). That is good which does good. God is communicative; and the best men are most like him: indeed, fluent talk sometimes is a great instructor to ourselves, and brings those things into our minds with clearness which long meditation would not have done. For one thing brings in another; and in a warm conversation the spirits are stirred up, and the understanding and memory are engaged to a close attention; so that just in the speaking, we often have such a sudden appearance of some truth, which before we took no notice of, that we find it is no small addition to our knowledge which comes in this way. As some find that vocal prayer stirs them up more, and keeps the mind from wandering, than mere mental prayer does; so free conversation is only a vocal meditation. And what man's thoughts are not more guilty of disorder, wanderings, and interruptions than his talk is?

**Direction 17. Obey all that God reveals to you in your meditations, and turn them all into faithful practice; and do not make thinking the end of thinking.**

Otherwise you will only do as the ungodly and disobedient in their prayers, who offer to God "a sacrifice like fools, for they do not realize that they are doing wrong" (Ecclesiastes 5:1–2). Away with the sin, and do the duty, on which you think.

**Direction 18. Do not think that the same measure of contemplation and striving with their own affections is necessary to all; but that an obedient, active life may be as acceptable to God, when he calls men to it, as a more contemplative life.**

This leads me necessarily to give you some directions about the difference of these ways.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** "Title" is Baxter's word for a heading or division of the chapter ("_Tit._" in the source). This file sits within Title 3, in its second series of numbered Directions ("Directions about the work itself"), which Baxter numbers from 1; this file runs from Direction 8 to Direction 18. The section closes by leading into Title 4 (the contemplative and the active life), which is not in this file.
- **Bold headings:** as in the neighboring files, the first sentence of each Direction is set in bold as its heading.
- **2 Corinthians 3:3–5 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James: "written ... with the Spirit of the living God" and "such trust have we through Christ to God-ward: not that we are sufficient of ourselves to think any thing as of ourselves; but our sufficiency is of God," adapting it as "this trust we must have." We use the NET: "written not with ink but by the Spirit of the living God" (with "..." for the skipped words), "we have such confidence in God through Christ," and "Not that we are adequate in ourselves to consider anything as if it were coming from ourselves, but our adequacy is from God." Baxter's point leans on "sufficient ... to think": in a chapter on the government of the thoughts, the King James makes the verse say we cannot even *think* anything good of ourselves. The NET's "consider" keeps some of that, but "adequate ... adequacy" for "sufficient ... sufficiency" loses the echo of Baxter's own "insufficiency" in the Direction's heading. To keep his "this ... we must have" we split the NET phrase: "this 'confidence' we must have 'in God through Christ'" (Baxter's order was "through Christ to God-ward"). Flagged.
- **Matthew 13:52 (important):** Baxter writes "the good scribe instructed to the kingdom of God" must "bring forth out of his treasure things new and old." The King James has "every scribe which is instructed unto the kingdom of heaven" and "bringeth forth out of his treasure things new and old"; "good" and "of God" are Baxter's own wording. The NET reads "every expert in the law who has been trained for the kingdom of heaven is like the owner of a house who brings out of his treasure what is new and old." We quote the NET phrases, with "brings" changed to "bring" to follow Baxter's "must," and keep Baxter's own word "good" outside the quotation marks ("The good 'expert in the law ...'"). His "of God" for "of heaven" is not kept, since the NET phrase is quoted. Baxter applies the "scribe" to any Christian who shares what he has gathered in meditation; the NET's "expert in the law" makes that application harder to see. Flagged.
- **Ecclesiastes 5:1–2:** Baxter's "sacrifice of fools, and consider not that they do evil" is the King James. The NET reads "to offer a sacrifice like fools, for they do not realize that they are doing wrong"; the sense Baxter uses (worship offered without obedience) is kept, though "evil" becomes "wrong." The NET's notes number this verse 4:17 in the Hebrew text; we keep Baxter's English numbering.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "your Head and Savior" (compare Ephesians 4:15; 5:23); "the tempter" (Matthew 4:3); "That is good which does good" is Baxter's own saying, not a quotation.
- **Chapter 3, Direction 11** (Direction 9): Baxter's cross-reference ("chap. iii. direct. xi."); we have not checked it against that section.
- **mortify the least** (Direction 8): "mortify" kept (see CONVENTIONS.md); here it means to deaden, to make the smaller truth lifeless by cutting it off from the greater. **always:** Baxter's "still" (in its older sense, continually), twice. **view it as connected:** his "behold it as connexed." **from there:** his "thence."
- **circumstantial matters** (Direction 8): his "circumstantials," the secondary or outward matters of worship (times, places, gestures, ceremonies) as distinct from its substance; one word added. **with a safeguard:** his "with a salvo," a reservation that protects the main thing. **proper subordination:** his "just subserviency." **imagery:** kept; in Baxter's usage probably the outward show (or image-like ceremony) put in place of the reality of worship; we have not settled the exact sense. **formality:** his "compliment," meaning empty ceremonial courtesy, not praise.
- **only** (Direction 9): his "but" (in the sense of "only"), twice. **assumed:** his "supposed." **For instance, if:** his "As if," which here means "as, if" (introducing an example), not "as though." **by this:** his "hereby."
- **thoughts** (Direction 10): his "cogitations." **soliloquies:** kept; speeches made to oneself. **running off:** his "running out" (wandering from the subject). **supply you with:** his "find you" (provide you with). **invention:** kept, in its older sense of finding matter to say. **repentance:** his "repentings." **reproaches; remonstrances; rebukes:** his "upbraidings; expostulations; reprehensions." **whereas:** his "when" (in its older sense, "whereas"). **from:** his "of" (learn of). **in an orderly way:** his "orderly."
- **brief prayers** (Direction 11): his "ejaculatory prayers," that is, short prayers sent up suddenly in the middle of other thoughts or work; no sexual sense. **stir up:** his "excite" (also Directions 15 and 16). **the soul deals so nearly with God:** his "the sold hath so nearly to do with God"; "sold" is a misprint for "soul" in the source, which we have corrected. **involve him in all:** his "interest him in all" (bring God into every part of it). The clause "a dull and wandering mind will bear some reverence to God" is kept as printed; the sense seems to be that even a dull mind will show some reverence when it speaks directly to God.
- **enliven / enlivened** (Direction 12): his "quicken / quickened" (give life to). **rooted in yourselves:** his "radically in yourselves" (having their root in yourselves). **Holy Spirit:** kept as in the source ("Holy Spirit").
- **so rare; rare** (Direction 13): his "so seldom; seldom." **unfamiliar with:** his "strange to." **nourishment:** his "nutriment." **practice:** his "use." **change in them:** his "alteration on them."
- **as to intensity** (Direction 14): his "in point of violence." **damage your brains:** his "craze your brains" ("craze" in its older sense of crack or impair). **unsettle:** his "discompose." **make yourselves unable:** his "disable yourselves." **love God too much in our esteem:** his "estimatively love God too much," that is, value God too highly; Baxter's point is that the judgment cannot overvalue God, though the feelings can be strained too far. **the spirits:** kept; in the physiology of Baxter's day, the fine "animal spirits" thought to carry the work of the brain and nerves. This is his period's medicine, not ours. **press on at the pace ... keep up:** his "put on in the pace ... hold out."
- **strive** (Direction 15): his "endeavour" (verb). **lovableness; happiness:** his "amiableness; felicity" (per CONVENTIONS.md). **secure:** kept as Baxter's term for careless confidence (see CONVENTIONS.md). **pleasure-loving:** his "sensual" (see GLOSSARY.md). **aversion:** his "averseness."
- **conversation; talk** (Direction 16): his "discourse" (per GLOSSARY.md). **communicate / communicative:** kept; to impart or share good things with others, and of God, that he gives himself and his goodness; not "talkative." **most like him:** his "likest to him." **indeed:** his "nay." **brings in another:** his "leadeth in another." **often:** his "oftentimes." **vocal prayer; mental prayer:** kept; prayer spoken aloud and prayer made silently in the mind. **only:** his "but." **wanderings:** his "vagaries" (in its older sense, wanderings of the mind).
- **Otherwise** (Direction 17): his "Else."
- **obedient, active life** (Direction 18): his "obediential, active life"; the following Title 4 heading in the source has "obedient, active Life."
- **affections** (Directions 9, 10, 18): kept; emotions and desires (see GLOSSARY.md).
- ***Saints' Rest*** (note 308): Baxter's own book, probably *The Saints' Everlasting Rest*, whose fourth part (as we understand it, not confirmed here) treats heavenly meditation; we have not checked the passage he points to. "Of this see" is rendered "On this see."
