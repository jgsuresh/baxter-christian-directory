# Chapter 1, Part 2: Second Series, Temptation 7

**Temptation 7 (second series).** But, says the tempter, it cannot be a mortal, reigning sin, because it is not committed with the whole heart, nor without some struggling and resistance: do you not feel the Spirit striving against the flesh? and so it is with the regenerate (Galatians 5:17; Romans 7:20–23). The good which you do not do, you would do; and the evil which you do, you would not do; so then it is no more you that do it, but sin that dwells in you. In a self-indulgent, unregenerate person there is but one party, there is nothing but flesh; but you feel the combat between the flesh and the Spirit within you.

*In the margin: What resistance of sin may be in the ungodly.*

**Direction 7.** This is a snare so subtle and dangerous that you have need of eyes in your head to escape it. Understand therefore that as to the two texts of Scripture, much abused by the tempter, they speak not at all of mortal reigning sin, but of the unwilling infirmities of such as had subdued all such sin, and walked not after the flesh, but after the Spirit; and whose wills were habitually bent to good; and would gladly have been perfect, and not have been guilty of an idle thought, or word, or of any imperfection in their holiest service, but lived up to all that the law requires: but this they could not do, because the flesh did cast many hindrances in the way of the will in the performance. But this is nothing to the case of one that lives in gross sin, and an ungodly life, and has strivings and convictions, and ineffective wishes to be better and to turn, but never does it. This is but sinning against conscience, and resisting the Spirit that would convert you; and it makes you worthy of many blows, as being rebellious against the persistent pleadings of grace. Sin may be resisted where it is never conquered; it may reign nevertheless despite some contradiction. Everyone that resists the king does not depose him from his throne. It is a dangerous deceit to think that every good desire that contradicts sin conquers it, and is a sign of saving grace. It must be a desire after a state of godliness, and an effective desire too. There are degrees of power: some may have a less and limited power, and yet be rulers. As the evil spirits that possessed men's bodies were a legion in one, and but one in others, yet both were possessed; so it is here. Grace is not without resistance in a holy soul; there are some remnants of corruption in the will itself, resisting the good; and yet it does not follow that grace does not rule. So it is in the sin of the unregenerate. No man in this life is so good as he will be in heaven, or so bad as he will be in hell; therefore none is void of all moral good. And the least good will resist evil, in its degree, as light does darkness. As in these cases:

1. There is in the unregenerate a remnant of natural knowledge and conscience. Some disclosures of God and his will there are in his works: God has not left himself without witness. See Acts 14:17; 17:27; Romans 1:19–20; 2:7–9. This light and law of nature governed the heathens; and this in its measure resists sin, and assists conscience.

2. When supernatural, external revelation in the Scripture is added to the light and law of nature, and the ungodly have all the same law as the best, it may do more.

3. Moreover, an ungodly man may live under a most powerful preacher, who will never let him alone in his sins, and may stir up much fear in him, and many good purposes, and almost persuade him to be a true Christian; and not only to have some ineffective wishings and strivings against sin, but to do many things as the preacher directs, as Herod did after John, and to escape the common pollutions of the world (2 Peter 2:20).

4. Some sharp affliction, added to the rest, may make him seem to others a true penitent: when he is stopped in his course of sin, as Balaam was by the angel with a drawn sword, and sees that he cannot go on but in danger of his life; and that God is still meeting him with some hardship, and hedging up his way with thorns (for such mercy he shows to some of the ungodly); this may breed not only resistance of sin, but some reformation. When the Babylonians were planted in Samaria they did not fear God, and he sent lions among them; and then they feared him, and sent up some kind of service to him, performed by a base sort of priests; "they were worshiping the Lord and at the same time serving their own gods," thinking it was safest to please all (2 Kings 17:25, 32–33). Affliction makes bad men most like the good.

5. Good education and company may do very much: it may help them to much knowledge, and make them strict in professing religion; and constant companions with those that fear sin, and avoid it; and therefore they must necessarily go far then, as Joash did all the days of Jehoiada (2 Chronicles 24:2). As plants and fruits change with the soil by transplantation, and as the climate makes some dark-skinned and some light-skinned; so education and association have so great a power on the mind that they come next to grace, and are often the means of it.

6. And God gives to many, internally, some grace of the Spirit, which is not proper to those that are saved, but common or preparatory only. And this may make much resistance against sin, though it does not mortify it. One that should live but under the convictions that Judas had when he hanged himself, I guarantee, would have strivings and combats against sin in him, though he were unsanctified.

7. Indeed, the influence and power of one sin may resist another: as greed may make much resistance against indulgence in physical pleasure and pride of life, and pride may resist all disgraceful sin.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **Romans 7:19–20 (the tempter's argument):** the sentence "The good which you do not do, you would do; and the evil which you do, you would not do; so then it is no more you that do it, but sin that dwells in you" is Baxter's adaptation, in the second person, of Paul's words in the first person. We kept his wording, modernized. The NET reads "For I do not do the good I want, but I do the very evil I do not want! Now if I do what I do not want, it is no longer me doing it but sin that lives in me."
- **2 Kings 17:25, 32–33:** Baxter's "they feared the Lord, and served their own gods" is the King James at verse 33. The NET reads "They were worshiping the Lord and at the same time serving their own gods." We quote the NET. In the NET the settlers "did not worship the Lord" at first (verse 25); Baxter's "did not fear God" is the King James wording.
- **Galatians 5:17; Romans 7:20–23; Acts 14:17; 17:27; Romans 1:19–20; 2:7–9; 2 Peter 2:20; 2 Chronicles 24:2** are cited only.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "Everyone that resists the king" (compare Romans 13:2); "a legion in one, and but one in others" (the Gerasene demoniac, Mark 5:9; Luke 8:30, and other possessions); Herod and John the Baptist (Mark 6:20); Balaam and the angel with a drawn sword (Numbers 22:23); "hedging up his way with thorns" (Hosea 2:6); Judas's remorse (Matthew 27:3–5); Joash and Jehoiada (2 Chronicles 24:2, cited).
- **climate makes some dark-skinned and some light-skinned** (item 5): Baxter's "the climate maketh some blackmoors and some white." "Blackmoor" was an old English word for a dark-skinned person. Baxter's comparison reflects an older belief that climate shaped skin color, which modern science does not hold in the same way. We have rendered his words in modern terms and note the belief.
- **self-indulgent** (Temptation 7): Baxter's "sensual." **mortal, reigning sin; mortify:** kept; see the earlier notes.
- **blows:** Baxter's "stripes." **persistent pleadings:** his "importunities." **hindrances:** his "stops." **effective, ineffective:** his "effectual, uneffectual."
- **the Spirit striving against the flesh** (Temptation 7): from Galatians 5:17. The tempter's argument is that internal conflict proves one is regenerate. Baxter answers that the same struggle can be felt by an unregenerate person, for six reasons, listed as items 1–6, and one more in item 7.
- **disclosures:** Baxter's "discoveries." **external:** his "extrinsic."
- **base sort of priests** (item 4): left as written; in 2 Kings 17:32 the settlers made priests of the lowest of the people.
- **go far then** (item 5): Baxter means they can go a long way in outward religion.
- **association:** Baxter's "converse." **I guarantee:** his "I warrant him."
- **the interest and power of one sin** (item 7): Baxter's "interest," meaning stake or influence. **greed:** his "covetousness." **indulgence in physical pleasure:** his "sensuality." **pride of life:** left as written, from 1 John 2:16.
- **do many things as the preacher directs** (item 3): Baxter's "do many things after the preacher."
