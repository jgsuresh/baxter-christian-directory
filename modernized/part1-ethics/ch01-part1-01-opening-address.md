# Chapter 1, Part 1

**Directions to Unconverted, Graceless Sinners, for the Attaining of True Saving Grace** [10]

If ungodly, miserable sinners were as few as the devil and their self-love would make themselves believe, [11] I might omit this part of my work as needless. For the healthy do not need the physician, but the sick. If you go into twenty families, and ask them all whether any of them are in an unsanctified state, unrenewed and unpardoned, and under the wrath and curse of God? you will meet with few that will not tell you they hope it is better with them than that; and though they are sinners, as all are, yet that they are repenting, pardoned sinners. Nay, there is scarcely one of many of the most wicked and notoriously ungodly but hopes they are in a penitent, pardoned state. Even the haters of God will say they love him; and the scorners at godliness will say that they are not ungodly; and that it is but hypocrisy and peculiarity that they deride: and it would be well for them, if saying so would pass for proof, and he that will be their Judge would take their words. But God will not be deceived, though foolish men are wise enough to deceive themselves. Wickedness will be wickedness when it has clothed itself with the fairest names: God will condemn it when it has found out the most plausible pretenses and excuses. Though the ungodly think to brazen it out in pride and scorn, and expect to be saved by their hypocritical lip-service as readily as the most holy worshippers of the Lord, yet they are "like wind-driven chaff"; "the wicked cannot withstand judgment, nor can sinners join the assembly of the godly" (Psalm 1:4–6). And if God knows better than foolish men, then certainly the flock is little to whom the Father is "well pleased to give" the kingdom (Luke 12:32). And "the gate is wide and the way is spacious that leads to destruction, and there are many who enter through it. How narrow is the gate and difficult the way that leads to life, and there are few who find it!" (Matthew 7:13–14). When Christ was asked, "Lord, will only a few be saved?" he answered, "Exert every effort to enter through the narrow door because many, I tell you, will try to enter and will not be able to" (Luke 13:23–24). But, alas! we need no other information than common experience to tell us whether the majority of men are holy, and heavenly, and self-denying; that seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and love God above all, and will forsake all they have for the sake of Christ: and undoubtedly none but such are saved; as you may see Hebrews 12:14; Matthew 6:20–21, 33; Luke 14:33.

Seeing then the godly are so few and the ungodly so many; and that God will accept nothing as holiness that is not such indeed; and seeing it is so terrible a thing to any man that has his wits about him to live one day in an unconverted state, because he that dies so is lost forever; it seems to me it should be our wisdom to be suspicious of ourselves, and careful lest we be deceived in so great a business, and diligent in searching and examining our hearts, whether they are truly sanctified or not; because it can be no harm to make sure work for our salvation; whereas presumption, carelessness, and negligence may betray us to incurable misery and despair.

I do not here suppose the reader to have any such acquaintance with his heart, or care for his salvation, or obedient willingness to be taught and ruled by Jesus Christ as is proper to those that are truly sanctified; for it is ungodly persons that now I am speaking to. And yet, if I should not suppose them to have some capacity and disposition to make use of the directions that I give them, I might as well pass them by and spare my labor. I tell you therefore, reader, what it is that I presuppose in you, and expect from you, and I think you will not judge me unreasonable in my suppositions and expectations.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced exactly as in the source. The Latin is not modernized; a draft English translation follows each passage in parentheses. The translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition; have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[10] Leg. Danielis Episcop. Epist. ad Bonif. Mogunt. inter Epist. Bonif. 67. de Methodo convertendi Paganos. (Read the letter of Daniel, bishop, to Boniface of Mainz, among the letters of Boniface, no. 67, on the method of converting pagans.)

[11] Hæsit tam desperati insulæ excidii, insperatique mentio auxilii, memoriæ eorum qui utriusque miraculi testes extitere: et ob hoc reges, publici, privati, sacerdotes, ecclesiastici, suum quique ordinem servarunt. (The memory of so desperate a ruin of the island, and of unhoped-for help, remained fixed in those who had been witnesses of both wonders; and for this reason kings, public officials, private persons, priests, and clergy each kept to their own order.) At illis decedentibus, cum successisset ætas tempestatis illius nescia, et præsentis tantum serenitatis expers, ita cuncta veritatis ac justitiæ moderamina concussa ac subversa sunt, ut earum non dicam vestigium, sed ne monumentum quidem in supra dictis propemodum ordinibus appareat; exceptis paucis, et valde paucis, (But when they passed away, and a generation succeeded that knew nothing of that storm and knew only the present calm, all the restraints of truth and justice were so shaken and overturned that not a trace of them, I will not say, but not even a monument appears in almost all the orders mentioned above; except a few, a very few,) qui ob amissionem tantæ multitudinis, quæ quotidie prona ruit ad tartara, tam brevis numeri habentur, ut eos quodammodo venerabilis mater ecclesia in sinu suo recumbentes non videat, quos solos veros filios habeat. (who, because of the loss of so great a multitude, which daily rushes headlong to hell, are reckoned of so small a number that venerable mother church, in a way, does not see them resting in her bosom, though they alone are her true sons.) Quorum nequis me egregiam vitam omnibus admirabilem, Deoque amabilem carpere putet; si qua liberius de his, immo lugubrius, cumulo malorum compulsus, qui serviunt non solum ventri, sed et diabolo potius quam Christo. Gildas p. (mihi) 514. (Let no one think that I am carping at their excellent life, admirable to all and dear to God, if I speak somewhat too freely, or rather mournfully, driven by the heap of evils, about those who serve not only the belly but the devil rather than Christ. Gildas, p. 514 in my copy.) It was Pythagoras's saying, (which Ambrose says he has from the Jews,) Communem atque usitatam populo viam, non esse terendam. (The common and customary road of the people is not to be trodden.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** quoted Scripture in this project is from the NET Bible®. See [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). Baxter quotes the King James wording, often loosely. Where he quotes a verse we use the NET wording of the same verse, keeping the extent of what he quotes. The NET reads "narrow door" in Luke 13:24 where the King James has "strait gate."
- **Hebrews 12:14:** the 1846 edition prints "Heb. xxi. 14." Hebrews has only 13 chapters, and "follow holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord" is 12:14, so we corrected it. Please check it against the original.
- **the healthy** (paragraph 1): Baxter's "the whole," as in the older translation of Matthew 9:12, "They that be whole need not a physician."
- **peculiarity** (paragraph 1): Baxter's "singularity," meaning being odd or standing out, here an accusation by the ungodly that the godly are affectedly different.
- **brazen it out** (paragraph 1): Baxter's "bear it out," meaning to put on a bold face.
- **expect to be saved … as readily as** (paragraph 1): Baxter's "think to be saved … as soon as."
- **incurable** (paragraph 2): Baxter's "remediless," meaning "without remedy."
- **unsanctified, unrenewed, unpardoned, unconverted, ungodly:** left as Baxter's terms. They name states of the soul and recur throughout the Directory.
