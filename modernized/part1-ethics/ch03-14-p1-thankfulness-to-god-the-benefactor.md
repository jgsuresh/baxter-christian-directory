# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XIV (part 1 of 2)

**Grand Direction 14. Let thankfulness to God your Creator, Redeemer, and Regenerator, be the very temperament of your soul, and faithfully expressed by your tongue and life.**

Though our thankfulness is no benefit to God, yet he is pleased with it, as that which is suitable to our condition, and shows the candor and honesty of the heart. An unthankful person is but a devourer of mercies, and a grave to bury them in, and one who does not have the sense and honesty to know and acknowledge the hand that gives them; but the thankful person looks above himself, and returns all, as he is able, to him from whom they flow.

True thankfulness to God is told apart from the counterfeit by these qualifications: 1. True thankfulness, having a just estimate of mercies by comparison, prefers spiritual and everlasting mercies to those that are merely bodily and temporary. But carnal thankfulness chiefly values carnal mercies, though in theory it may confess that the spiritual are the greater. 2. True thankfulness inclines the soul to a spiritual rejoicing in God, and to a desire for more of his spiritual mercies: but carnal thankfulness is only a delight in the prosperity of the flesh, or the delusion and carnal security of the mind, inclining men to carnal, empty mirth, and to a desire for more of the same fleshly pleasure, plenty, or contentment: as a beast that is well fed will skip, and play, and show that he is pleased with his condition; or if he has ease, he would not be disturbed. 3. True thankfulness kindles in the heart a love for the giver above the gift, or at least a love for God above our carnal prosperity and pleasure, and brings the heart ever nearer to God by all his mercies. But carnal thankfulness springs from carnal self-love, or love of fleshly prosperity; and is moved by it, and serves it, and loves God, and thanks him, but only so far as he gratifies or satisfies the flesh. A childlike thankfulness makes us love our Father more than his gift, and desire to be with him in his arms; but a dog loves you and is thankful to you only for feeding him: he loves you in subordination to his appetite and his bones. 4. True thankfulness inclines us to obey and please him who binds us by his benefits. But carnal thankfulness puts God off with the hypocritical, merely ceremonial thanks of the lips, and spends the mercy on pleasing the flesh, and makes it only the fuel of lust and sin. 5. True thankfulness to God is necessarily transcendent, as his mercies are transcendent. The saving of our souls from hell, and the promising us eternal life, besides the giving us our very beings and all that we have, bind us to be totally and absolutely his who is so transcendent a Benefactor to us, and cause the thankful person to devote and resign himself and all that he has to God, to answer so great an obligation. But carnal thankfulness falls short of this absolute and total dedication, and still leaves the sinner in the power of self-love, devoting himself (really) to himself, and using all that he is, or has, to please his fleshly mind, and giving God only the tithes or leavings of the flesh, or so much as it can spare, lest he should stop the streams of his kindness, and deprive the flesh of its prosperity and contentments.

**Directions for Thankfulness to God, our Benefactor.**

**Direction 1. Understand well how great this duty is, in the nature of the thing, but especially how the very design and tenor of the gospel, and the way of our salvation by a Redeemer, calls for it as the very character of the soul, and of every duty.**

*In the margin: Gratitude is to the promise, much what obedience is to the law.*

A creature that is wholly his Creator's, and is preserved every moment by him, and daily fed and maintained by his bounty, and is put into a capacity for eternal life, must needs be obliged to incessant gratitude. And unthankfulness among men is justly taken for an unnatural, monstrous vice, which forfeits the benefits of friendship and society: in 2 Timothy 3:2 the "ungrateful" are numbered with the "unholy," etc. as part of the monsters who should come in the last times (and whom we have lived to see, exactly answering that large description of them). But the design of God in the work of redemption is purposely laid for the raising of the highest thankfulness in man: and the covenant of grace contains such abundant, wondrous mercies as might compel the souls of men to gratitude, or leave them utterly without excuse. It is a great truth, and much to be considered, that gratitude is that general duty of the gospel which contains and animates all the rest, as being essential to all that is properly evangelical. A law, as a law, requires obedience as the general duty: and this obedience is to be exercised and found in every particular duty which it requires. And the covenant with the Jews was called The Law because the regulating part was most prominent: and so obedience was the thing that was chiefly required by the law, though their measure of mercy obliged them also to thankfulness. But the gospel or new covenant is most prominently a history of mercy, and an offer and promise of the most unmatchable benefits that ever were heard of by the ears of man: so that the gift of mercy is the predominant or leading part in the gospel or new covenant: and though God is still our Governor, and the new covenant also has its precepts, and is a law, yet that is, in a sort, only the subordinate part. And what obedience is to a law, that thankfulness is to a benefit, even the formal answering of its obligation: so that though we are called to as exact obedience as ever, yet it is now only a thankful obedience that we are called to. And just as law and promises or gifts are joined in the new covenant, just so should obedience and thankfulness be joined in our hearts and lives; one to God as our Ruler, and the other to him as our Benefactor: and these two must animate every act of heart and life. We must repent of sin; but it must be a thankful repenting, as becomes those who have a free pardon of all their sins procured by the blood of Christ, and offered to them in the gospel: leave out this gratitude, and it is no evangelical repentance. And what is our saving faith in Christ, but the assent to the truth of the gospel, with a thankful acceptance of the good which it offers us, even Christ as our Savior, with the benefits of his redemption. The love to God that is required there is the thankful love of his redeemed ones: and the love to our very enemies, and the forgiving of wrongs, and all the love to one another, and all the works of charity required there, are the exercises of gratitude, and are all to be done on this account, because Christ has loved us, and forgiven us, and that we may show our thankful love to him. Preaching, and praying, and sacraments, and public praises, and communion of saints, and obedience, are all to be animated with gratitude; and they are performed in an evangelical way only so far as thankfulness is the very life and character of them all. The dark and defective explanation of this by preachers gave occasion to the antinomians to run into the opposite extreme, and to take too much away from God's law and our obedience; but if we obscure the doctrine of evangelical gratitude, we do as badly or worse than they. Obedience to our Ruler, and thankfulness to our Benefactor, joined and working together like the head and heart in the natural body, make a Christian indeed. Understand this well, and it will much incline your hearts to thankfulness.

**Direction 2. Let the greatness of the manifold mercies of God be continually before your eyes.**

Thankfulness is caused by the due understanding of the greatness of mercies. If you either do not know them to be mercies, or do not know that they are mercies to you, or do not believe what is said and promised in the gospel, or forget them, or do not think of them, or make light of them through the corruption of your minds, you cannot be thankful for them. I have before spoken of mercy in order to the kindling of love, and therefore shall now only recite these following, to be always in our memories. 1. The love of God in giving you a Redeemer, and the love of Christ in giving his life for us, and in all the parts of our redemption. 2. The covenant of grace, the pardon of all our sins: the justification of our persons: our adoption, and title to eternal life. 3. The fitness of means for calling us to Christ: the gracious and wise arrangements of Providence to that end: the gifts and compassion of our instructors: the care of parents: and the helps and examples of the servants of Christ. 4. The effectiveness of all these means: the giving us to will and to do, and the opening of our hearts, and giving us repentance unto life, and the Spirit of Christ to mortify our sins, and purify our nature, and dwell within us. 5. A standing in his church, under the care of faithful pastors: the liberty, comfort, and frequent benefit of his word and sacraments, and the public communion of his saints. 6. The company of those who fear the Lord, and their faithful admonitions, reproofs, and encouragements: the kindness they have shown us for body, or for soul. 7. The mercies of our relations, or homes, our property, and the notable changes and turning points of our lives. 8. The manifold preservations and deliverances of our souls, from errors and those who lead us astray; from terrors and distress; from dangerous temptations, and many a soul-wounding sin; and that we are not left to the errors and desires of our hearts, to seared consciences, as forsaken of God. 9. The manifold deliverances of our bodies from enemies, hurts, distresses, sicknesses, and death. 10. The mercies of adversity, in wholesome, necessary chastisements, or honorable sufferings for his sake, and support or comfort under all. 11. The communion which our souls have had with God, in the course of our private and public duties, in prayer, sacraments, and meditation. 12. The use which he has made of us for the good of others; that our time has not been wholly lost, and we have not lived as burdens of the world. 13. The mercies of all our friends and his servants, which were to us as our own; and our interest in the mercies and public welfare of his church, which are more than our own. 14. His patience and forbearance with us under our constant uselessness and provocations, and his renewed mercies notwithstanding our abuse: our perseverance until now. 15. Our hopes of everlasting rest and glory, when this sinful life is at an end. Magnify these mercies in your more extended meditations, and they will surely constrain you to cry out, "Praise the Lord, O my soul. With all that is within me, praise his holy name. Praise the Lord, O my soul. Do not forget all his kind deeds. He is the one who forgives all your sins, who heals all your diseases, who delivers your life from the Pit, who crowns you with his loyal love and compassion" (Psalm 103:1–4). "Enter his gates with thanksgiving and his courts with praise. Give him thanks. Praise his name. For the Lord is good. His loyal love endures, and he is faithful through all generations" (Psalm 100:4–5). "The Lord is compassionate and merciful; he is patient and demonstrates great loyal love. For as the skies are high above the earth, so his loyal love towers over his faithful followers" (Psalm 103:8, 11). "Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good, for his loyal love endures" (Psalm 136:1), etc. "Give thanks to the Lord. Call on his name. Make known his accomplishments among the nations. Sing to him. Make music to him. Tell about all his miraculous deeds. Boast about his holy name. Let the hearts of those who seek the Lord rejoice" (Psalm 105:1–3).

**Direction 3. Be well acquainted with the greatness of your sins, and aware of them as they heighten the greatness of God's mercies to you.**

This is the main end why God will humble those whom he will save; not to drive them to despair of mercy, nor that he takes pleasure in their sorrows for themselves; but to work the heart to a due esteem of saving mercy, and to a serious desire for it, that they may thankfully receive it, and carefully retain it, and faithfully use it. An unhumbled soul treats Christ, and grace, and glory lightly: it has no taste for any spiritual mercy: it cannot be thankful for that which it finds no great need of. But true humiliation restores our appetite, and teaches us to value mercy as it is. Think therefore what sin is (as I have explained to you in Direction 8), and think of your many grievous sins: and then think how great those mercies are that are bestowed on such great, unworthy sinners! Then mercy will melt your humbled hearts, when you confess that you are "unworthy to be called sons" (Luke 15); and that you are "not worthy to look up to heaven" (Luke 18:13); and that you are "not worthy of the least of all the mercies of God" (Genesis 32:10). The humble soul is the thankful soul, and therefore is so greatly valued by the Lord.

**Direction 4. Understand what misery you were delivered from, and estimate the greatness of the mercy by the greatness of the punishment which you had deserved.**

Misery as well as sin must tell us the greatness of our mercies. This is explained earlier, in Chapter 1, Direction 9.

**Direction 5. Suppose you saw the damned souls, or suppose you had been one day in hell yourselves; think then how thankful you would have been for Christ and mercy.**

And you were condemned to it by the law of God, and if death had brought you to execution you would have been there, and then mercy would have been more esteemed. If a preacher were sent to those miserable souls to offer them a pardon and eternal life on the terms on which they are offered to us, do you think they would make as light of it as we do?

**Direction 6. Do not neglect to keep clear the evidences of your title to those special mercies for which you should be most thankful: and do not listen to Satan when he would tempt you to think that they are none of yours, so that he might make you deny God the thanks for them which he expects.**

Of this I have spoken in the directions for love.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org, not quoted from memory.
- **Heading of the Grand Direction:** it is Baxter's own heading for this section of Chapter 3; "Creator, Redeemer, and Regenerator" is kept as written. This file ends partway through the Grand Direction (after Direction 6); the next file continues it.
- **Margin note position:** in the source the margin note ("Gratitude is to the promise, much what obedience is to the law") comes just above Direction 1; we place it below the Direction's heading. "much what" means "much the same as."
- **2 Timothy 3:2:** Baxter's "unthankful" and "unholy" are the King James words; the NET has "ungrateful" and "unholy" in a longer list (verses 2-4). The quotation marks in our text enclose the NET words. In 2 Timothy 3:1 the NET has "last days" where Baxter says "last times"; he is paraphrasing around the quotation, so we kept his prose. His point (that unthankfulness ranks with the worst vices) is unchanged. The parenthesis "and which we have lived to see" is Baxter's own comment on his times.
- **Psalm 103:1-4:** Baxter's quotation is the King James ("Bless the Lord, O my soul ... who forgiveth all thine iniquities; ... who redeemeth thy life from destruction; who crowneth thee with loving-kindness and tender mercies"). The NET has "Praise" for "Bless," "the Pit" for "destruction," and "loyal love and compassion" for "loving-kindness and tender mercies." The NET (Psalm 103:1) also carries the superscription "By David," which we omitted as it is not part of Baxter's quotation. The sense Baxter uses (an inventory of benefits) is not changed.
- **Psalm 100:4-5:** the NET has "Give him thanks. Praise his name." where the King James has "be thankful unto him, and bless his name." Baxter's emphasis on thankfulness is kept in "thanksgiving" and "Give him thanks." "Everlasting" (his "mercy is everlasting") is not in the NET, which says "His loyal love endures"; the sense is close.
- **Psalm 103:8, 11:** Baxter's quotation of verse 8 ("slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy") is rendered in the NET as "patient and demonstrates great loyal love," and "them that fear him" as "his faithful followers." The NET's "loyal love" renders the Hebrew word that the King James translates "mercy"; we kept the NET wording, so the repeated word "mercy" of Baxter's version does not appear here.
- **Psalm 136:1:** Baxter's quotation ends "his mercy endureth for ever." The NET reads "for his loyal love endures"; the NET text for this verse does not include "for ever," but the refrain is the same. Baxter adds "&c." for the rest of the refrain; we kept it as "etc."
- **Psalm 105:1-3:** the NET omits Baxter's "among the people" (it has "among the nations") and reads "Boast about his holy name" for "Glory ye in his holy name." The verse numbering matches.
- **Luke 15 (the prodigal son):** Baxter gives only "Luke xv." for "unworthy to be called sons." This is his paraphrase of the prodigal's words in Luke 15:19 and 21, where the NET has "no longer worthy to be called your son." We kept his wording, in his quotation marks, and his reference. **Luke 18:13:** Baxter's "not worthy to look up to heaven" is his paraphrase of the tax collector, who "would not even look up to heaven" in the NET; the NET says nothing about worthiness there.
- **Genesis 32:10:** Baxter's quotation, "not worthy of the least of all the mercies of God," is the King James ("I am not worthy of the least of all the mercies"). The NET reads "I am not worthy of all the faithful love you have shown your servant." The NET does not have "least," and "of God" is Baxter's addition. We kept Baxter's wording because the force of his quotation lies in "the least of all," and flag it here.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording, no reference added in the text): "the giving us to will and to do" (probably Philippians 2:13; the NET there has "the desire and the effort" where the King James has "to will and to do"); "repentance unto life" (probably Acts 11:18); "seared consciences" (probably 1 Timothy 4:2); "left to the errors and desires of our hearts" (we have not identified a single verse). We have not checked these against the NET.
- **Cross-references:** "direct. viii." (Direction 3) is rendered "Direction 8" with no chapter given in the source; we have not confirmed which chapter's Direction 8 Baxter means. "chap. i. direct, ix." (Direction 4) is rendered "Chapter 1, Direction 9"; we have not verified which passage this is. "The directions for love" (Direction 6) is Baxter's reference to an earlier Grand Direction.
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** ingenuity (in its older sense of open candor and honesty) to *candor*; corporal to *bodily*; transitory to *temporary*; notionally to *in theory*; complimental to *merely ceremonial*; benignity to *kindness*; bereave to *deprive*; contents (in "prosperity and contents") to *contentments*; molested to *disturbed*; gratify and subservient, modernized to *gratifies* and *serves*; obligeth to *binds*; bespeaketh to *calls for*; complexion (of the soul) to *character*, meaning its settled makeup (in the opening heading Baxter's "temperament" is kept); eminent/eminently to *prominent/prominently* or *leading*; tender (an offer) to *offer*; derogate to *take away*; conjoined to *joined*; subservient (Direction 1, the new covenant's precepts) to *subordinate*; apprehension to *understanding*; aptness to *fitness*; disposals of Providence to *arrangements of Providence*; efficacy to *effectiveness*; habitations to *homes*; estates to *property*; passages (of our lives) to *turning points*; seducers to *those who lead us astray*; unprofitableness to *uselessness*; aggravate (Direction 2) to *magnify*; aggravation (Direction 3) to *heighten the greatness of*; aggravated sins to *grievous sins*; sets light by to *treats lightly*; relisheth to *has no taste for*; recovereth our appetite to *restores our appetite*; sensible of to *aware of*; Saviour to *Savior*; honourable to *honorable*; christian to *Christian*.
- **Technical terms kept:** *carnal* (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh; see GLOSSARY.md), *mortify* (put to death), *evangelical* (belonging to the gospel), *transcendent* (surpassing all measure), *Regenerator* (the one who gives new birth).
- **antinomians** (Direction 1): those who held that the gospel frees believers from obeying God's law. Baxter's point is that careless preaching on gratitude pushed some to that extreme. This is his own explanation.
- **"in a sort"** (Direction 1): "in a way."
- **Direction 5, "And you were condemned to it":** kept as Baxter wrote it; it is a short statement that the law of God condemned them to hell.
- **Pronouns:** Baxter moves between "thou" and "you" in the headings; the Grand Direction and Direction 6 headings use "thy/thou," which we render "your/you." Direction 6's second clause ("hearken not to Satan") is rendered "do not listen to Satan."
