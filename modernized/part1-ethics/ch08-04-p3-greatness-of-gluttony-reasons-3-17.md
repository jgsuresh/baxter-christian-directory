# Chapter 8: Part 4, Title 1, the greatness of the sin of gluttony (reasons 3-17)

*This file begins partway through Baxter's numbered list under "III. The Greatness of the Sin of Gluttony" (Part 4, Title 1), at reason 3; reasons 1-2 are in the previous file, and the list continues in the next file.*

3. Gluttony is also a deadly enemy to the mind, and to all the noble employments of reason, both religious, civil, and technical. [407] It unfits men for any close and serious studies, and therefore tends to nourish ignorance, and keep men fools. It greatly unfits men for hearing God's word, or reading, or praying, or meditating, or any holy work, and makes them have more desire to sleep; or so indisposes and dulls them that they have no life or fitness for their duty; but a clear head, not troubled with their drowsy vapors, will do more and get more in an hour than a full-bellied beast will do in many. So that gluttony is as much an enemy to all religious and manly studies as drunkenness is an enemy to a garrison, where the drunken soldiers are disabled from resisting the enemy.

4. Gluttony is also an enemy to diligence, in every honest trade and calling; for it dulls the body as well as the mind. It makes men heavy, and drowsy, and slothful, and go about their business as if they carried a coat of lead, and were in fetters; they have no vivacity and alacrity, and are fitter to sleep or play than work. [408]

5. Gluttony is the immediate symptom of a carnal mind, and of the damnable sin of flesh-pleasing, described before; and a carnal mind is the very sum of iniquity, and the proper name of an unregenerate state; it is "hostile to God, for it does not submit to the law of God, nor is it able to do so:" so that those who are thus "in the flesh cannot please God; and those who walk after the flesh shall die" (Romans 8:6-8, 13). The filthiest sins of lechers, and misers, and thieves, are but to please the flesh: and who serves it more than the glutton does?

6. Gluttony is the breeder and feeder of all other lusts: *sine Cerere et Baccho friget Venus*: it pampers the flesh to feed it, and make it a sacrifice for lust. As dunging the ground makes it fruitful, especially of weeds; so does gluttony fill the mind with the weeds and vermin of filthy thoughts, and filthy desires, and words, and deeds. [409]

7. Gluttony is a base and beastly kind of sin. For a man to place his happiness in the pleasure of a swine, and to make his reason serve his throat, or sink into his guts; as if he were but a hogshead to be filled and emptied, or a drain for liquor to run through into the gutter; or as if he were made only to carry food from the table to the dunghill; how base a kind of life this is! Yes, many beasts will not eat and drink excessively as the gluttonous epicure will do. [410]

8. Gluttony is a prodigal consumer and devourer of the creatures of God. What is he worthy of, who would take food and drink and cast it away into the gutter? [411] No, who would go to a great deal of cost and pains to get the pleasantest food he could procure, to cast away? The glutton does worse. It would be better of the two to throw all his excesses into the drain or ditch, for then they would not first hurt his body. And are the creatures of God of no more worth? Are they given you to do worse than cast them away? Would you have your children use their provisions thus?

9. Gluttony is a most unthankful sin, that takes God's mercies, and spews them as it were in his face; [412] and carries his provisions over to his enemy, even to the strengthening of fleshly lusts; and turns them all against himself! You could not have a bit but from his liberality and blessing; and will you use it to provoke him and dishonor him?

10. Gluttony is a sin which turns your own mercies, and wealth, and food, into your snare, and to your deadly ruin. You please your throat, and poison your soul. [413] It would be better for you a thousand times that you had lived on scraps, and in the poorest manner, than thus to have turned your plenty to your damnable sin. "You eat your fill, ... be careful not to forget the Lord" (Deuteronomy 6:11-12). "Feed me with my allotted portion of bread, lest I become satisfied and act deceptively and say, 'Who is the Lord?'" (Proverbs 30:9). "They ate until they were beyond full; he gave them what they desired. They were not yet filled up" (Psalm 78:29-30).

11. Gluttony is a great time-wasting sin. What a great deal of time is spent in getting the money that is laid out to please the throat! and then by servants in preparing for it; and then in long sitting at meals and feastings; and not a little in taking medicine to carry it away again, or to ease or cure the diseases which it causes; besides all the time which is lost in languishing sickness, or cut off by untimely death. Thus they live to eat, and eat to frustrate and to shorten life.

12. It is a thief that robs you of your estates, and devours that which is given you for better uses, and for which you must give account to God. It is a costly sin, and consumes more than would serve many better purposes. How great a part of the riches of most kingdoms are spent in luxury and excess! [414]

13. It is a sin that is a great enemy to the common good: princes and commonwealths have reason to hate it, and restrain it as the enemy of their safety. Men do not have money to defray the public expenses, necessary to the safety of the land, because they consume it on their guts: armies and navies must be unpaid, and fortifications neglected, and all that tends to the glory of a people must be opposed as against their personal interest, because all is too little for the throat. No great works can be done to the honor of the nation or the public good; no schools or almshouses built or endowed, no colleges erected, no hospitals, nor any excellent work, because the guts devour it all. If it were known how much of the treasure of the land is thrown down the drain by epicures of all ranks, this sin would be frowned into more disgrace.

14. Gluttony and excess is a sin greatly aggravated by the necessities of the poor. What an incongruity it is, that one member of Christ (as he would be thought) should be feeding himself deliciously every day, and abounding with abused superfluities, while another is starving and pining in a cottage, or begging at the door! and that some families should do worse than cast their delicacies and abundance to the dogs, while thousands at that time are ready to famish, and are forced to feed on such unwholesome food as kills them as soon as luxury kills the epicure! Do these men believe that they will be judged according to their feeding of the poor? [415] Or do they take themselves to be members of the same body with those whose sufferings they so little feel? 1 Corinthians 12:26. It may be you will say, I do relieve many of the poor. But are there not more yet to be relieved? As long as there are any in distress, it is the greater sin for you to be luxurious. Deuteronomy 15:7-8, "If a fellow Israelite from one of your villages in the land ... should be poor, you must not harden your heart or be insensitive to his impoverished condition. Instead, you must be sure to open your hand to him," etc. No, how often are the poor oppressed to satisfy luxurious appetites! Great numbers must have hard bargains and hard treatment, and toil like horses, and scarcely be able to get bread for their families, that they may bring in all to belly-god landlords, who consume the fruit of other men's labor upon their devouring flesh.

15. And it is the more heinous sin because of the common calamities of the church and servants of Christ throughout the world. One part of the church is oppressed by the Turk, and another by the pope, and many countries wasted by the cruelties of armies, and persecuted by proud, impious enemies; and is it fit then for others to be wallowing in physical indulgence and gluttony? Amos 6:1, 3-6, "Woe to those who live in ease in Zion ... You refuse to believe a day of disaster will come, but you establish a reign of violence. They lie around on beds decorated with ivory and sprawl out on their couches. They eat lambs from the flock and calves from the middle of the pen. They sing to the tune of stringed instruments ... They drink wine from sacrificial bowls and pour the very best oils on themselves. Yet they are not concerned over the ruin of Joseph." It is a time of great humiliation, and are you now given up to fleshly luxury? Read Isaiah 22:12-14, "At that time the Sovereign Lord of Heaven's Armies called for weeping and mourning, for shaved heads and sackcloth. But look, there is outright celebration! You say, 'Kill the ox and slaughter the sheep, eat meat and drink wine. Eat and drink, for tomorrow we die!' ... 'Certainly this sin will not be forgiven as long as you live,' says the Sovereign Lord of Heaven's Armies."

16. Luxury is a sin most unseemly for men in so great misery, and incongruous to the state of the gluttonous themselves. O man, if you had but a true sight of your sin and misery, of death and judgment, and of the dreadful God whom you offend, you would perceive that fasting, and prayer, and tears befit one in your condition much better than glutting your devouring flesh. What! a man unpardoned, unsanctified, in the power of Satan, ready to be damned if you die thus, (for so I must suppose of a glutton,) for such a man to be taking his fleshly pleasure! For a Dives to be faring sumptuously every day, who must shortly lack a drop of water to cool his tongue, is as foolish as for a thief to feast before he goes to hanging: yes, and much more. For you might yet prevent your misery; and another posture better suits you to that end: "Fasting" and "crying mightily to God" is more fitting to your state. See Jonah 3:8; Joel 1:14; 2:15.

17. Gluttony is a sin so much the greater, by how much the more will and delight you have in committing it. The sweetest, most voluntary and beloved sin is (*cæteris paribus*) the greatest; and few are more pleasant and beloved than this.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[407] As smoke drives away the bees from their hive, says Basil *de Junin*; so gluttony expels all spiritual gifts, and excellent endowments of mind.

[408] Says Basil, A ship heavily laden is unfit to sail: so a full belly to any duty.

[409] Semper saturitati juncta est lascivia. Hieron. (Lust is always joined to fullness. Jerome.)

[410] Ventri obedientes animalium numero computantur non hominum. Senec. (Those who obey the belly are counted in the number of animals, not of men. Seneca.)

[411] It is Chrysostom's saying in Hebr. Hom. 29. (Chrysostom, Homily 29 on Hebrews.)

[412] Jeremiah 5:7.

[413] Magna pars libertatis est bene moratus venter. Senec. (A well-behaved belly is a great part of freedom. Seneca.)

[414] When a friend of Socrates complained to him, What a costly place this is! Wine will cost so much, and honey so much, and purple so much: Socrates took him to the meal-hall, Look, says he, you may buy here half a sextare of good meal for a halfpenny (which boiled in water was his food); God be thanked the market is very cheap. Then he took him to an oil-shop, where a measure (chœnix) was sold for two brass dodkins. Then he led him to a broker's shop, where a man might buy a suit of clothes for ten drachmas. You see, said he, that the pennyworths are reasonable, and things good, cheap throughout the city. Plutarch, *De Tranquillitate Animi*, page 153.

[415] Matthew 25.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording below was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where this file sits:** Chapter 8, Part 4 ("taste and appetite"), Title 1 (against gluttony), section III, "The Greatness of the Sin of Gluttony." "Title" (Baxter's "*Tit.*") is his word for a heading or division within a Part. Section IV, "The Directions or Helps against it," begins after the next file.
- **Romans 8:6-8, 13 (reason 5):** Baxter's "It is enmity against God, and neither is nor can be subject to his law" is the King James of 8:7 ("the carnal mind is enmity against God: for it is not subject to the law of God, neither indeed can be"); we use the NET of 8:7, where the subject is "the outlook of the flesh" (the King James "carnal mind," which Baxter's argument rests on). "In the flesh cannot please God" is the NET of 8:8. The last clause, "those who walk after the flesh shall die," is Baxter's own blend (8:13 in the King James reads "if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die"); we kept his wording, modernized. The NET of 8:13 reads "if you live according to the flesh, you will die."
- **Deuteronomy 6:11-12 (reason 10):** NET wording. The King James Baxter used, "When thou shalt have eaten and be full, then beware lest thou forget the Lord," puts the warning more directly after fullness; the NET sense is the same.
- **Proverbs 30:9 (reason 10):** Baxter cites verse 9, but the first clause ("Feed me ...") is verse 8; we keep his reference. Flagged: the King James "lest I be full and deny thee" is in the NET "lest I become satisfied and act deceptively," so the NET loses "deny thee," which is the danger of fullness that Baxter is pointing at (though "Who is the Lord?" still carries it). The NET also has "my allotted portion of bread" for "food convenient for me."
- **Psalm 78:29-30 (reason 10):** the source prints "Psal. lxxv. 29, 30," but Psalm 75 has only ten verses; the words are Psalm 78:29-30, and we correct the reference. Flagged: Baxter's King James ends "they were not estranged from their lust" (they still craved after being fed), which is his point about gluttony. The NET reads "They were not yet filled up; their food was still in their mouths," which keeps the sense of unsatisfied appetite but not the word "lust."
- **1 Corinthians 12:26; Jonah 3:8; Joel 1:14; 2:15** (reasons 14 and 16): cited. In reason 16, "Fasting" and "crying mightily to God" are Baxter's words in quotation marks, adapted from the King James (Jonah 3:8 "cry mightily unto God"; Joel 1:14 and 2:15 "sanctify a fast"). We kept them, modernized. The NET reads "cry earnestly to God" (Jonah 3:8) and "Announce a holy fast" (Joel 1:14; 2:15).
- **Deuteronomy 15:7-8 (reason 14):** NET wording, with Baxter's skip marked "...". Flagged lightly: the King James "nor shut thy hand against thy poor brother, but thou shalt open thy hand wide unto him" becomes in the NET "or be insensitive to his impoverished condition. Instead, you must be sure to open your hand to him," and the NET goes on "and generously lend him whatever he needs" (not quoted by Baxter). The image of the shut hand is lost.
- **Amos 6:1, 3-6 (reason 15):** NET wording, with Baxter's skips (the rest of verse 1, verse 2, and the end of verse 5) marked "...". The King James "put far away the evil day, and cause the seat of violence to come near" is the NET "refuse to believe a day of disaster will come, but you establish a reign of violence." Baxter's closing line, "they are not grieved for the affliction of Joseph," is the NET "they are not concerned over the ruin of Joseph"; the King James "grieved," which fits his argument (they feel nothing for the suffering church), becomes "concerned." Flagged.
- **Isaiah 22:12-14 (reason 15):** NET wording, with Baxter's skip in verse 14 marked "...". The King James "joy and gladness" is the NET "outright celebration," and "this iniquity shall not be purged from you till ye die" is "this sin will not be forgiven as long as you live." The sense Baxter uses (God called for mourning, and they feasted) is the same.
- **Unmarked allusion (reason 16):** "a Dives ... faring sumptuously every day, who must shortly lack a drop of water to cool his tongue" is the rich man of Luke 16:19-24 (King James "fared sumptuously every day"; NET "feasted sumptuously every day"). "Dives" is Latin for "rich man," the traditional name given to him; kept. "Faring" (living, eating) kept from the King James phrase.
- **Latin in the body:** *sine Cerere et Baccho friget Venus* (reason 6): "without Ceres and Bacchus, Venus grows cold," that is, without food and wine, lust cools. The line is probably from Terence's *Eunuchus*, but we have not confirmed it or checked Baxter's source. *cæteris paribus* (reason 17): "other things being equal."
- **Basil *de Junin*** (note 407): kept as printed. It is probably a misprint for *de Jejunio* (Basil's homilies on fasting), but we have not confirmed it.
- **Plutarch note** (note 414): "sextare" is a Roman measure (*sextarius*), roughly a pint; "chœnix" a Greek dry measure; "dodkins" small, low-value coins; "broker's shop" a dealer in second-hand goods; "things good, cheap" is kept with the source's comma; it may stand for the older idiom "good cheap" (cheap, a good bargain), or mean "good and cheap." "Purple" is purple-dyed cloth. Page number kept as printed.
- **Seneca and Jerome** (notes 409, 410, 413): kept as Baxter attributes them; we have not checked the exact sources.
- **technical:** Baxter's "artificial" (to do with the arts and crafts), reason 3. **have more desire to sleep:** his "have more mind to sleep." **indisposes:** his "undisposeth." **vapors:** in the physiology of Baxter's day, fumes thought to rise from the stomach to the head and dull it; kept.
- **calling** (reason 4): a person's occupation or vocation; kept.
- **carnal** (reason 5): unspiritual, ruled by the flesh; kept. **flesh-pleasing, described before:** Baxter's cross-reference to his earlier treatment; we have not checked where.
- **hogshead:** a large cask. **drain; gutter:** Baxter's "sink" (a drain or cesspool) and "channel" (the street gutter), reasons 7, 8 and 13. Where "sink" is a verb in reason 7 ("sink into his guts") it is kept. **food:** Baxter's "meat," which meant food in general (reasons 7, 8; "meat" kept in the Isaiah quotation, where the NET has it). **meals:** his "meat" in "long sitting at meat" (reason 11).
- **epicure:** one devoted to the pleasures of eating; kept. **luxury, luxurious:** in Baxter, self-indulgence in food and drink (and comfort), not only expensive living; kept.
- **creatures of God** (reason 8): created things, here food and drink; kept. **cost and pains:** Baxter's "cost and curiosity" (fussy, elaborate care).
- **medicine:** his "physic" (reason 11). **estates:** property, possessions (reason 12). **public expenses:** his "public charges." **all ranks:** his "all degrees." **almshouses:** houses endowed to lodge the poor.
- **delicacies:** his "delicates." **forced:** his "fain" (obliged). **Great numbers:** his "Abundance" (many people), reason 14. **hard treatment:** his "hard usage."
- **the Turk; the pope** (reason 15): Baxter's terms for the Ottoman Empire and the Roman papacy; this is his polemic, kept as he wrote it. **physical indulgence:** his "sensuality" (see the glossary).
- **befit:** his "become" (suit). **lack:** his "want." **better suits:** his "doth better beseem." **more fitting:** his "fitter."
- **"if you die thus" (reason 16):** Baxter shifts from "a man" to "thou" and back to "such a man" inside one sentence; we kept the shift.
- **"Gluttony and excess is"** (reason 14): Baxter's singular verb kept.
