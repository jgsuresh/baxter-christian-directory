# Chapter 1, Part 1: Directions 18 and 19

**Direction 18. If you would prove true converts, come over to God, as your Father and happiness, with desire and delight, and embrace Christ as your only Savior, with thankfulness and joy; and set out on the way of godliness with pleasure and eagerness, as your exceeding privilege, and the only way of profit, honor, and contentment: and do it not as against your wills, as those that would rather do otherwise if they dared, and consider the service of God an unsuitable and unpleasant thing.**

You are never truly changed until your hearts are changed; and the heart is not changed until the will or love is changed. Fear is not the real man; but usually is mixed with unwillingness and dislike, and so is contrary to that which is indeed the man. Though fear may do much for you, it will not do enough: it is often more strongly felt than love, even in the best, as being more passionate and violent; but yet there is no more acceptability in all than there is will or love. [37] God did not send soldiers, or inquisitors, or persecutors to convert the world by working upon their fear, and driving them upon that which they take to be harmful to them: but he sent poor preachers, who had nothing of worldly fears or hopes to move their hearers with; but had authority from Christ to offer them eternal life; and who were to convert the world by proposing to them the best and most desirable condition, and showing them where the true happiness is, and proving the certainty and excellence of it to them, and working upon their love, desire, and hope: God will not be your God against your wills, while you regard him as the devil, who is only terrible and hurtful to you, and take his service for slavery, and would rather be away from him, and serve the world and the flesh, if it were not for fear of being damned. He will be feared as great, and holy, and just; but he will also be loved as good, and holy, and merciful, and every way suited to be the happiness and rest of souls. If you do not take God to be better than the creature (and better to you), and heaven to be better for you than earth, and holiness than sin, you are not converted; but if you do, then show it by your willingness, eagerness, and delight. Serve him with gladness and cheerfulness of heart, as one that has found the way of life, and never had cause of gladness until now. If you see your servant do all his work with groans, and tears, and lamentations, you will not think he is well pleased with his master and his work. Come to God willingly with your hearts, or you do not come to him at all. You must either make him and his service your delight, or at least your desire; as recognizing him as most fit to be your delight, so far as you enjoy him.

**Direction 19. Remember always that conversion is the turning from your carnal selves to God; and therefore that it engages you in a perpetual opposition to your own corrupt notions and wills, to mortify and annihilate them, and take them captive wholly to the holy word and will of God.**

Do not think that your conversion dispatches all that is to be done in order to your salvation. No, it is but the beginning of your work, that is, of your delight and happiness; you are but engaged by it to that which must be performed throughout all your lives; it enters you into the right way, not to sit down there, but to go on until you come to the desired end. It enters you into Christ's army, that afterwards you may there win the crown of life; and the great enemy that you engage against is yourselves. There will still be a law in your members, rebelling against the law that the Holy Spirit has put into your minds: your own notions and your own wills are the great rebels against Christ, and enemies of your sanctification. Therefore it must be your resolved daily work to mortify them, and bring them completely over to the mind and will of God, which is their rule and end. If you feel any notions arising in you that are contrary to the Scripture, and quarrel with the word of God, suppress them as rebellious, and do not give them liberty to cavil with your Maker, and impudently dispute with your Governor and Judge; but silence them, and force them reverently to submit. If you feel any will in you contrary to your Creator's will, and that there is something which you would have or do which God is against, and has forbidden you, remember now how great a part of your work it is to fly for help to the Spirit of grace, and to destroy all such rebellious desires. Do not think it enough that you can bear the denial of those desires; but destroy the desires themselves at once. For if you leave the desires alone, they may at last lay hold upon their prey before you are aware: or if you should be guilty of nothing but the desires themselves, it is no small iniquity; being the corruption of the heart, and the rebellion and adultery of the principal faculty, which should be kept loyal and chaste to God. The contrariness of your will to the will of God is the sum of all the impiety and evil of the soul; and the subjection and conformity of your will to his is the heart of the new creature, and of your uprightness and sanctification. Do not therefore favor any self-conceitedness or self-willedness, nor any rebelliousness against the mind and will of God, any more than you would put up with the disjointing of your bones, which will be little for your ease or use until they are set back in their proper place.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced exactly as in the source. A draft English translation follows the Latin passage in parentheses; it was prepared for this project and is not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check it before relying on fine points.

[37] Passibilis timor est irrationabilis, et ad irrationabilia constitutus, sed eum præcipit qui cum disciplina et recta ratione consistit, cujus proprium est reverentia. Qui enim propter Christum et doctrinam ejus Deum timet, cum reverentia ei subjectus est; cum ille qui per verbera aliaque tormenta timet Deum, passibilem timorem habete viderur. Dydimus Alex. in Pet. 1. (A fear that is mere passion is irrational and fixed on irrational things; but he commands the fear that rests on discipline and right reason, whose mark is reverence. For he who fears God because of Christ and his teaching is subject to him with reverence; but he who fears God through blows and other torments seems to have a fear of mere passion. Didymus of Alexandria, on 1 Peter. The source prints "habete viderur," apparently for "habere videtur.")

## Translator's notes

- **No Scripture is quoted in quotation marks in these two Directions.** Baxter alludes without marking them to: "a law in your members, rebelling against the law" (Romans 7:23); "the crown of life" (Revelation 2:10; James 1:12); and the law "put into your minds" (Hebrews 8:10). We kept his wording, modernized. Scripture quotations elsewhere are from the NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).
- **happiness:** Baxter's "felicity." **embrace:** his "close with." **eagerness:** his "alacrity."
- **"Fear is not the real man"** (Direction 18): Baxter means fear is not the true self; it is mixed with unwillingness and so is not what the person really is. Left in his wording.
- **acceptability** (Direction 18): Baxter's "acceptableness," meaning how acceptable something is to God.
- **inquisitors** (Direction 18): the officers of the Inquisition. Baxter contrasts Christ's method (poor preachers who persuade) with Rome's (force and fear), as in the note to Direction 7.
- **notions:** Baxter's "conceits." **impudently:** his "malapertly." **contrariness:** his "crossness." **uprightness:** his "rectitude."
- **silence them** (Direction 19): Baxter writes "silence it, and force it," shifting from "conceits" (plural) to "it." We kept the plural.
- **dispatches** (Direction 19): "despatcheth," meaning finishes or settles. **mortify:** kept in its theological sense.
- **members** (Direction 19): kept in Baxter's quotation of Romans 7:23, where it means the parts of the body.
- **Didymus** (note 37): Didymus of Alexandria ("the Blind"), whom Baxter cites on 1 Peter. The Latin names the fear of reverence over the fear of torment.
