# Chapter 5: Title 5, on whom the duty of redeeming time is principally incumbent (Sorts 1-8 and conclusion)

**Title 5. On whom this duty of redeeming time is principally incumbent.**

Though the redeeming of time is a duty of grand importance and necessity to all, yet all these sorts following have special obligations to it.

**Sort 1.** Those that are in the youth and vigor of their time. Nature is not yet so much corrupted in you as in old accustomed sinners; your hearts are not so much hardened; sin is not so deeply rooted and confirmed; Satan has not triumphed in so many victories; you are not yet plunged so deep as others into worldly encumbrances and cares; your understanding, memory, and strength are in their vigor and do not yet fail you: and who should go fastest, or work hardest, but he that has the greatest strength? You may now get more by diligence in a day than hereafter you can get in many. How few prove good scholars, or wise men, that do not begin to learn till they are old! "Keep away from youthful passions," therefore (2 Timothy 2:22). "Remember your Creator in the days of your youth" (Ecclesiastes 12:1). If you are now trained up in the way you should go, you will not depart from it when you are old (Proverbs 22:6). Oh that you could but know what an unspeakable advantage, and benefit, and comfort it is to come to a ripe age with the provisions and equipment of that wisdom, and holiness, and acquaintance with God, which should be attained in your youth! and what a misery it is to have then to learn that which you should have been many years before practicing, and to have then to begin to live when you must make an end! much more to be cast to hell, if death should find you unready in your youth! or to be forsaken by God to a hardened age! Happy are they that, with Timothy and Obadiah, learn the Scripture and fear God in their childhood, and from their youth (1 Kings 18:12; 2 Timothy 3:15).

**Sort 2.** Necessity makes it incumbent on the weak, and sick, and aged, in a special manner to redeem their time. If they will not make much of it that are sure to have but a little; and if they will trifle and loiter it away, that know they are near their journey's end, and ready to give up their accounts, they are inexcusable above all others. A thief or murderer will pray and speak good words when he is going out of the world. Well may it be said to you, as Paul does (Romans 13:11–12), "it is already the hour for us to awake from sleep," when your salvation or damnation is so near! It is high time for that man to look about him, and prepare his soul, and lose no time, who is so speedily to appear before the most holy God, and be treated forever as he has lived here.

**Sort 3.** It is especially incumbent on those to redeem the time who have loitered and misspent much time already. If conscience tells you that you have lost your youth in ignorance and empty pursuits, and much of your age in negligence and worldliness, it is a double crime in you if you do not diligently redeem the time that is left. [299] The just care of your salvation requires it, unless you are willing to be damned. Decency and duty to God require it; unless you will defy him, and resolve to abuse and despise him to the utmost, and spend all the time against him which he shall give you. The nature of true repentance requires it; unless you will know none but the repentance of the damned; and begin to repent the misspending of your time when it is gone, and all is too late.

**Sort 4.** It is especially their duty to redeem the time who are short of time through poverty, service, or restraint. If poor people that must labor all the day will not redeem the Lord's day, and those few hours which they have, they will then have no time at all for things spiritual: servants that are not masters of their time, and are held close to their work, need to be very diligent in redeeming those few hours which are allowed them for higher things.

**Sort 5.** Those that enjoy any special helps either public or private must be especially careful to improve them and redeem the time. Do you live under a convicting, powerful ministry? O improve it and redeem the time; for you do not know how soon they may be taken from you, or you from them. Do you live with godly relations, parents, husband, wife, masters in a godly family, or with godly fellow-servants, friends, or neighbors? Redeem the time: get something from them every day: you do not know how short this season will be. Do you live where you have books and leisure? Redeem the time: this also may not be long. Would not Joshua have been horribly inexcusable if he had loitered when God made the sun stand still while he pursued his enemies? O do not you loiter, while the sun of mercy, patience, means, and helps do all attend you.

**Sort 6.** Those must especially redeem the time who are ignorant, or graceless, or weak in grace, and have strong corruptions, and little or no assurance of salvation, and are unready to die, and have yet all or most of their work to do: [300] if these loiter, they are doubly to blame. Surely the time past of your lives may suffice to have loitered and done evil (1 Peter 4:3). Has not the devil had too much already? Will you stand "all day without work" (Matthew 20:6). Look home and see what you have yet to do; how much you lack toward a safe and comfortable death! "Sow righteousness for yourselves, reap unfailing love. Break up the unplowed ground for yourselves, for it is time to seek the Lord, until he comes and showers deliverance on you" (Hosea 10:12).

**Sort 7.** It much concerns those to redeem the time who are in any office, or have any opportunity of doing any special or public good; especially magistrates and ministers of Christ. Your life will not be long: your office will not be long: O rouse yourselves against sin and Satan, and for Christ and holiness, while you may: God will test you only for a time. Let Obadiah hide and feed the prophets when he is called to it, and while he may, that God may hide him, and not think to push off duty, and save himself for a better time. Mordecai says to Esther, "Don't imagine that because you are part of the king's household you will be the one Jew who will escape. If you keep quiet at this time, liberation and protection for the Jews will appear from another source, while you and your father's household perish. It may very well be that you have achieved royal status for such a time as this!" (Esther 4:13–14). Are you ministers? O preach the gospel while you may: redeem the time: all times are your season: so great a work, and the worth of souls, commands you to do it "whether it is convenient or not" (2 Timothy 4:2). A man that is to save many others from drowning, or to quench a fire in the city, is inexcusable above all men if he does not redeem time by his greatest diligence and speed.

**Sort 8.** Lastly, it is especially incumbent on those to redeem the time who, being recovered from sickness, or saved from any danger, are under the obligation both of special mercy and special promises of their own; who have promised God in the time of sickness or distress that if he would but spare them and try them once again, they would amend their lives, and live more holily, and spend their time more carefully and diligently for their souls, and show all about them the truth of their repentance by the greatness of their change, and an exemplary life. Oh it is a most dangerous, terrible thing to return to security, sloth, and sin, and break such promises to God! Such are often given over to woeful hard-heartedness or despair; for God will not be mocked with deceptive words.

Thus I have explained this great duty of redeeming time the more largely because it is of unspeakable importance; and my soul is frequently amazed with astonishment that the sluggish world can so unfeelingly and impenitently go on in wasting precious time, so near eternity, and in so needy and dangerous a case. Though, I bless my God, I have not wholly lost my time, but have long lived in a sense of the odiousness of that sin, yet I wonder at myself that such overpowering motives do not compel me to make continual haste, and to be always at work with all my might, in a case of everlasting consequence.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All quoted verses were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," that is, a heading or main division of the chapter. Chapter 5 has five such Titles; this is the last. **incumbent:** kept (a duty "lies on" someone).
- **Sort:** Baxter numbers his eight classes of people "Sort I." to "Sort VIII."; we keep the word and give the numbers in Arabic figures.
- **2 Timothy 2:22 (flag):** Baxter quotes the King James "Flee youthful lusts." The NET has "keep away from youthful passions." "Flee" is more urgent than "keep away from," and "passions" is broader than "lusts"; the urging to haste that Baxter wants is a little weaker in the NET.
- **Ecclesiastes 12:1:** NET wording; it matches the King James for the phrase Baxter quotes.
- **Proverbs 22:6:** not in quotation marks in the source; Baxter paraphrases the King James ("Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it"). We keep his wording. The NET reads "Train a child in the way that he should go, and when he is old he will not turn from it."
- **1 Kings 18:12; 2 Timothy 3:15** (cited only): Baxter's "fear God ... from their youth" echoes Obadiah's words in the King James ("I thy servant fear the Lord from my youth"); the NET has "your servant has been a loyal follower of the Lord from my youth." For Timothy the NET has "from infancy you have known the holy writings."
- **Romans 13:11–12 (flag):** Baxter quotes the King James, "Now it is high time to awake out of sleep," and then picks up "high time" in his next sentence ("It is high time for that man to look about him"). The NET reads "it is already the hour for us to awake from sleep," so the echo of "high time" in Baxter's own sentence is lost. Baxter cites verses 11–12 but quotes only from verse 11. We moved the reference into parentheses after "as Paul does," where Baxter put it.
- **1 Peter 4:3:** not in quotation marks; Baxter's paraphrase of the King James "the time past of our life may suffice us to have wrought the will of the Gentiles." Kept in his wording. The NET reads "For the time that has passed was sufficient for you to do what the non-Christians desire."
- **Matthew 20:6 (flag):** the King James has "Why stand ye here all the day idle?"; the NET has "Why are you standing here all day without work?" The NET's "without work" suggests lack of employment, where Baxter's point is idleness, loitering. The source ends this question with a period after the reference; we keep Baxter's punctuation.
- **Hosea 10:12 (flag):** NET wording. Baxter's King James has "reap in mercy" and "rain righteousness upon you"; the NET has "reap unfailing love" and "showers deliverance on you." The NET also has "unplowed ground" for "fallow ground."
- **Esther 4:13–14 (corrected reference; flag):** the source prints "Esther ix. 13, 14," but the words are Mordecai's message in Esther 4:13–14; we corrected the reference. Baxter's King James ends with a question ("who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"); the NET makes it a statement ("It may very well be that you have achieved royal status for such a time as this!"). The NET also has "liberation and protection" for "enlargement and deliverance." Baxter's "Saith Mordecai to Esther" is rendered "Mordecai says to Esther."
- **2 Timothy 4:2 (important flag):** Baxter builds on the King James "in season and out of season": "all times are your season ... to do it in season and out of season." The NET reads "whether it is convenient or not," which loses the word "season" that ties the quotation to his sentence and to the whole chapter's theme of seasons and opportunity.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Joshua and the sun standing still (Joshua 10:12–14); Obadiah hiding and feeding the prophets (1 Kings 18:4, 13); "God will not be mocked" (Galatians 6:7).
- **Footnote [300] (Ephesians 2:2):** Baxter gives only the reference. In the NET the verse speaks of former life "according to this world's present path, according to the ruler of the domain of the air"; how Baxter means it to apply here is not stated.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): vigour, vigor; provisions and furniture, provisions and equipment; unexcusable, inexcusable; mispent, misspent; vanity, empty pursuits; Ingenuity, Decency (in Baxter's day "ingenuity" meant an honest, right-minded, generous disposition, what we might call common decency or gratitude; "Decency ... require it" keeps his plural sense of "Ingenuity and duty"); scanted of time, short of time; had need to be, need to be; convincing (ministry), convicting (one that convicts the conscience); get somewhat by them, get something from them; want (lack), lack; bestir you, rouse yourselves; try you but a time, test you only for a time; shift off duty, push off duty; delusory, deceptive; opened, explained; admiration, astonishment (the older sense of "admiration" was wonder); insensibly, unfeelingly; still at work, always at work ("still" meaning continually).
- **"to be then to learn ... and to be then to begin to live"** (Sort 1): rendered "to have then to learn ... and to have then to begin to live." **"in practising"** rendered "practicing."
- **"Had not Joshua been horribly unexcusable if he would have loitered"** (Sort 5): rendered "Would not Joshua have been horribly inexcusable if he had loitered," a change of verb forms only.
- **"O loiter not you"** rendered "O do not you loiter," keeping Baxter's emphatic "you" (you, as against Joshua) and his emphatic "do all attend you."
- **security** (Sort 8): Baxter's term for careless, false confidence about one's spiritual state, not safety. Kept.
- **means** (Sort 5): the means of grace (preaching, sacraments, prayer, and the like). Kept.
- **"be used for ever"** (Sort 2): rendered "be treated forever."
- **"masters in a godly family"** (Sort 5): masters of a household in Baxter's sense, heads of family to whom servants answered. Kept.
