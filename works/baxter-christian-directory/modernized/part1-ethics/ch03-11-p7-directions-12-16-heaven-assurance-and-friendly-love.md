# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 11 (part 7 of 8)

**Direction 12. It is a powerful means to kindle the love of God in a believer, to foresee by faith the glory of heaven, and what God will be there to his saints for ever. [117]**

And thus to behold God in his glory is the use of grace. Though the manner of knowing him thus by faith is far short of what we there expect, yet it is the same God and glory that we now believe, which then we must more openly behold. And therefore, as that apprehension of love will inconceivably excel the highest which can be attained here; so the forethoughts of that excel all other arguments and means to affect us here; and will raise us as high as means can raise us. The greatest things, and the greatest interest of our souls, being there, will greatly raise us to the love of God, if anything will do it: to foresee how near him we shall be before long; and what a glorious proof we shall have of his good will; and how our souls will be ravished everlastingly with his love! To think what hearts the blessed have that see his glory, and live with Christ! how full of love they are! and what a delight it is to them thus to love! must needs affect the heart of a believer. Lift up your head, poor drowsy sinner! look up to heaven, and think where you must live for ever! Think what the holy ones of God are doing! Do they love God, or do they not? Must it not then be your life and work for ever? And can you forbear to love him now, who is bringing you to such a world of love? You would love him more who would give you security to possess a kingdom you never saw, than him who gives you only some toy in the hand. And do not let it seem too distant to affect you: the time is as nothing until you will be there: you do not know but you may be there tonight. There you will see the Maker of the worlds, and know the mysteries of his wondrous works. There you will see your blessed Lord, and feel that love which you read of in the gospel, and enjoy the fruits of it for ever. There you will see him who suffered for you, and rose again, whom angels see and worship in his glory. You will see there a more desirable sight than those who saw him heal the blind, and lame, and sick, and raise the dead; or those who saw him in his transfiguration; or those who saw him on the cross, or after his resurrection; or than Stephen saw when he was stoned; or Paul when he was converted; yes, more than it is likely he saw when he was in his rapture, in the third heavens! O who can think believingly on the life which we must shortly live there, the glory which we must see, the love which we must receive, and the love which we must exercise, and not feel the fire begin to flame, and the mirror in which we see the Lord become a burning glass to our affections! Christ and heaven are the books which we must be often reading; the mirrors in which we must daily gaze, if ever we will be good proficients and practitioners in the art of holy love.

**Direction 13. Exercise your souls so frequently and diligently in this way of love, that the method of it may be familiar to you, and the means and motives still at hand, and you may be able at once to fall into the way, as one who is well acquainted with it, and may not be distracted and lost in generalities, as not knowing where to fix your thoughts.**

I know no methods alone will serve to raise the dead, and cause a carnal, senseless heart to love the Lord. But I know that many honest hearts, who have the spirit of love within them, have great need to be warned that they not quench the Spirit; and great need to be directed how to stir up the grace which is given them: and that many live a duller, or more distracted, uncomfortable life than they would do if they did not lack skill and diligence. The soul is most backward to this highest work, and therefore has the greater need of helps: and the best have so much need that it is well if all will serve to keep up loving and grateful thoughts of God in their minds. And when every trade, and art, and science requires diligence, exercise, and experience, and all are bunglers at it at first, can we reasonably think that we are likely to attain any high degrees with slight, and short, and seldom thoughts?

**Direction 14. Yet do not let weak-headed or melancholy persons set themselves to those methods or lengths of meditation which their heads cannot bear; lest the tempter get advantage of them, and abate their love by making religion seem a torment to them; but let such take up shorter, obvious meditations, and exercise their love in an active, obedient way of living.**

That is the best medicine which is fitted to the patient's strength and case: and that is the best shoe which is most suitable for the foot, and not that which is the biggest or the finest. It is a great design of Satan to make all duties grievous and burdensome to us; and thereby to cast us into continual pain, and fear, and trouble, and so destroy our delight in God, and consequently our love. Therefore do not claim inability because of carnal unwillingness and laziness of mind; but yet do not spoil everything by grasping at more than you are able to bear. Take on yourself as you are able, and increase your work if God increases your strength. If a melancholy person cracks his brain with immoderate, ill-timed efforts, he will only disable himself for everything.

**Direction 15. Keep clear, and hold fast the evidences of your sincerity, that you may perceive your share in the love of God, and resist the temptations which would hide his love to you, and cause you to doubt it, or deny it.**

Satan does not have his end when he has troubled you, and robbed you of your peace and comfort; it is worse that he is seeking to effect by this: his malice is more against God than against you; and more against God and you in this point of love than in any other grace or duty. He knows that God esteems this most; and he knows if he could kill your love, he kills your soul. And he knows how natural it is to man to love those who love him, and hate those who hate him, be they never so excellent in themselves. And therefore, if he can persuade you into despair, and to think that God hates you, and is resolved to damn you, he will not despair of drawing you to hate God. Or if he only brings you to fear that he does not love you, he will think accordingly to abate your love. I know that a truly gracious soul keeps up its love when it loses its assurance; and mourns, and longs, and seeks in love, when it cannot triumph and rejoice in love: but yet there are some prints left on the heart of its former apprehensions of the love of God: and such souls exceedingly disadvantage themselves as to the exercises of love, and make it a work of wondrous difficulty. Oh! it will exceedingly kindle love when we can see God's surest love-tokens in our hearts, and look to the promises, and say, They are all mine; and think of heaven as that which shall certainly be our own: and can say with Thomas, "My Lord and my God!" and with Paul, "So the life I now live in the body, I live because of the faithfulness of the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me." Denial of our grace may seem to be humility, but it tends to extinguish love and gratitude.

But, you will say, I must avoid soul-delusion and pharisaical ostentation on the other side; and few reach assurance; how then should we keep up the love of God?

*In the margin: Signs of the love of God.*

*Answer.* 1. Though I have not come to the point of trying and discerning grace, I shall give you this much help on the way, because it is so useful to the exercises of love. (1.) If you do not have an enjoying, delighting love, yet try whether you do not have a desiring, seeking love. Love appears as truly in desiring and seeking good as in delighting in it. Poor men show their love of the world by desiring and seeking it, as much as rich men do in delighting in it. What is it that you most desire and seek? (2.) Or if this is so weak that you scarcely discern it, do you not find a mourning and lamenting love? You show that you loved your money by mourning when you lose it; and that you loved your friend by grieving for his death, as well as by delighting in him while he lived. If you heartily lament it as your greatest unhappiness and loss when you think that God casts you off, and that you are void of grace, and cannot serve and honor him as you would, this shows you are not void of love. (3.) If you do not feel that you love him, do you feel that you would gladly love him, and that you love to love him? If you do so, it is a sign that you do love him? When you not only desire to find such an evidence of salvation in you, but when you desire love itself, and love to love God. Would you not rather have a heart to love him perfectly than to have all the riches in the world? Would you not rather live in the love of God, if you could reach it, than to live in any earthly pleasure? If so, be sure he has your hearts. The will is the love, and the heart; if God has your will he has your heart and love. (4.) What has your hearts if he does not have them? Is there anything that you prefer and seek before him, and that you would rather have than him? Can you be content without him, and let him go in exchange for any earthly pleasure? If not, it is a sign he has your hearts. You love him savingly if you set more by nothing else than by him. (5.) Do you love his holy image in his word? Do you delight and meditate in his law? (Psalm 1:2.) Is it in your hearts? (Psalm 40:8.) Or do you pray, "Give me a desire for your rules..." (Psalm 119:36)? If you love God's image in his word (the wisdom and holiness of it), you love God. (6.) Do you love his image on his children? If you love them for their heavenly wisdom and holiness, you so far love God. He who loves the candle for its light does love the light itself and the sun: he who loves the wise and holy, for their wisdom and holiness, does love wisdom and holiness themselves. The word and the saints being more within the reach of our perceptible apprehensions than God himself is, we ordinarily feel our love to them more perceptibly than our love to God; when in fact it is God, in his word and servants, whom we love (1 John 3:14; Psalm 15:4). (7.) Though for lack of assurance you do not feel the delights of love, do you not have a heart that would delight in it more than in all the riches of the world if you could only get assurance of your share in it? Would it not comfort you more than anything if you could be sure he loves you, and could perfectly love him and obey him? If so, it is not for lack of love that you do not delight in him, but for lack of assurance. So that if God has your heart, either in a delighting love, or a seeking and desiring, or a lamenting, mourning love, he will not despise it or reject it. "The Lord is near the brokenhearted" (Psalm 34:18). "...a humble and repentant heart you will not reject" (Psalm 51:17). "May the Lord, who is good, forgive everyone who has determined to follow God... even if he is not ceremonially clean according to the standards of the temple" (2 Chronicles 30:18-19). By these evidences you may discern the sincerity of love in small degrees: and so you may make love the occasion of more love, by discerning that goodness of God which is shown to you in the least.

2. But suppose you cannot yet attain assurance; do not neglect to make good use of that goodness and mercy of God which he reveals to you in the state that you are in. Love him, but as Infinite Goodness should be loved, who "...loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life" (John 3:16). Love him as the most blessed and merciful God, who made you and all things, and has given to the world a universal pardon, on condition of their penitent acceptance, and offers them everlasting life, and all this purchased by the blood of Christ. Love him as one who offers you reconciliation, and entreats you to be saved: and as one who delights not in the death of the wicked, but rather that they turn and live: and as one who would have all men to be saved, and come to the knowledge of the truth, though he will save none but the penitent, who do acknowledge the truth. And when you love him sincerely on these accounts, you will have the evidence of his special love to you.

**Direction 16. Use your sense of natural and friendly love to raise you to the love of God.**

When you see or feel what love a parent has for children, and a husband for a wife, or a wife for a husband, or faithful friends for one another; think then, What love do I owe to God! Oh how insignificant is the loveliness of a child, a wife, a friend, the best of creatures, in comparison with the loveliness of God! Unworthy soul! can you love a drop of goodness in your friend; and can you not love the ocean of goodness in your God? Is a spark in the creature more lovable than the fire that kindled it? You can love your friend for all his blemishes, his ignorance, his passions, and manifold imperfections: and can you not love your God, who has none of these, nor anything to discourage or damp your love? You love, and deservedly love, your friend, because he loves you, and deals in a friendly way with you: but oh how much greater is the love of God! Did ever a friend love you as he has loved you? Did ever a friend do for you as he has done? He gave you your being, your daily safety, and all the mercies of your life! He gave you his Son, his Spirit, and his grace! He pardoned your sins, and took you into his favor, and adopted you as his son, and an heir of heaven! He will glorify you with angels in the presence of his glory! How should such a friend as this be loved! How far above all mortal friends! Their love and friendship is but a token and message of his love. Because he loves you, he sends you kindness and mercy by your friend: and when their kindness ceases, or can do you no good, his kindness will continue, and comfort you for ever. Love them therefore as the messengers of his love; but love him in them, and love them for him, and love him much more.

## Translator's notes

- **Structure:** this file begins at the start of Direction 12 and runs through Direction 16 of Grand Direction 11; the Grand Direction continues in the next file. Direction 15 contains a long Answer with numbered sub-points, kept as Baxter has them.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Galatians 2:20 (important):** Baxter's quotation is the King James, "by the faith of the Son of God, that loved me." The NET reads "because of the faithfulness of the Son of God, who loved me." The King James most naturally reads as Paul's own faith in the Son; the NET reads the Son's faithfulness as the ground of his life. Baxter uses it to show how assurance kindles love; the NET shifts the emphasis from the believer's faith to Christ's. Flagged. Baxter gives no reference; Galatians 2:20 is added here in the notes only, not in his text.
- **Thomas, "My Lord and my God!":** no reference in Baxter; the words are in John 20:28 (added here, not Baxter's). The NET has "My Lord and my God!"
- **Psalm 119:36 (important):** Baxter quotes "Incline my heart unto thy testimonies" as a prayer for love of God's word. The NET reads "Give me a desire for your rules, rather than for wealth gained unjustly," so the thought of a heart inclined (turned) toward God's word becomes a desire for it. Close in sense, but the verb "incline" is lost. We quote only the first clause, as Baxter's extent is a short phrase; the NET's second clause is not Baxter's.
- **Psalm 40:8:** Baxter paraphrases "Is it in your hearts?" from the King James, "thy law is within my heart." The NET reads "Your law dominates my thoughts," which is a stronger, mental image and loses the "within the heart" wording he leans on. We keep his question and cite the verse without quoting it. Psalm 1:2 is likewise his paraphrase ("delight and meditate in his law"); the NET has "he finds pleasure in obeying the Lord's commands; he meditates on his commands day and night."
- **Psalm 34:18:** Baxter's "nigh to them that be of a broken heart" is rendered with the NET's "near the brokenhearted." The NET's second line says "he delivers those who are discouraged"; Baxter does not quote it.
- **Psalm 51:17 (important):** Baxter has "A broken and contrite heart is his sacrifice, which he will not despise." The NET reads "The sacrifice God desires is a humble spirit... a humble and repentant heart you will not reject." The King James words "broken and contrite" are replaced by "humble and repentant". We quote only the second half, which matches his closing clause. Flagged.
- **2 Chronicles 30:18-19 (important):** Baxter's "good Lord will have mercy on every one that prepareth their hearts to seek him, though they do it not according to the preparation of the sanctuary" is from the King James. The NET is a prayer: "May the Lord, who is good, forgive everyone who has determined to follow God... even if he is not ceremonially clean according to the standards of the temple." The NET has "forgive" for "have mercy," "determined to follow" for "prepareth their hearts to seek," and "ceremonially clean" for "preparation of the sanctuary." Baxter's point (God accepts a sincere but imperfect heart) is kept, but "prepare the heart" is lost. The NET also has verse 19 beginning mid-sentence; the ellipsis marks the clause we skip ("the Lord God of his ancestors").
- **John 3:16 (important):** Baxter's King James wording is "so loved the world as to give his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life." The NET reads "For this is the way God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life." The NET takes "so" as manner ("this is the way"), not degree ("so greatly"). Baxter's use (God's love is great and offered to all the world) leans on both degree and "world"; the degree sense is lost. Because of the way the sentence is built, we quote from "loved" on.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording, no reference in his text): "quench not the Spirit" (1 Thessalonians 5:19); "delighteth not in the death of the wicked, but rather that they turn and live" (probably Ezekiel 33:11); "would have all men to be saved, and come to the knowledge of the truth" (probably 1 Timothy 2:4); Stephen stoned (Acts 7); Paul "when he was converted" (Acts 9); Paul "in the third heavens" (2 Corinthians 12:2). These references are our identification, not Baxter's.
- **1 John 3:14; Psalm 15:4:** cited by Baxter only as references for loving the saints, not quoted; we have not quoted them.
- **Word swaps:** "glass" (mirror) to mirror, and "burning-glass" to burning glass (a lens that focuses the sun's rays to set things alight); "physic" to medicine; "wanted not skill" to "did not lack skill"; "meetest" to "most suitable"; "mar" to spoil; "endeavours" to efforts; "inconsiderable" to insignificant; "amiable" to lovable; "fain" to gladly; "sensible apprehensions" to perceptible apprehensions (those we feel through the senses); "presently" to at once; "interest in the love of God" to "share in the love of God"; "toy" kept.
- **Kept as Baxter's terms:** carnal; assurance; melancholy (a long-lasting low or fearful mood, tied in his day to bodily "humors"); affections (emotions); proficients and practitioners; "weak-headed" (of weak or easily disturbed mind); "pharisaical"; "soul-delusion."
- **"be they never so excellent in themselves":** "never so" means "however." Kept.
- **"Answer. 1. ... (3.)":** the sentence "If you do so, it is a sign that you do love him?" ends with a question mark in the source, apparently a misprint for a period. Kept as printed. The following "When you not only desire..." is a fragment in the source; kept.
- **"Though I have not come to the point of trying and discerning grace":** Baxter probably means he has not yet reached, in this book, the part that treats of testing and discerning grace. (A later footnote, [120], at a marker outside this range, says only "Of hope and assurance I have spoken afterward"; we have not checked where.)
- **Direction 14, "obediential":** modernized to "obedient."
- **Direction 12, "thou mayst be there this night":** "you may be there tonight." Baxter's point is that death may come at any time.
- **Footnote [117]:** the printed Latin is kept; "Toletan." is as printed (Julian of Toledo is our identification; hedged, as we have not checked the work).
