# Chapter 7: Introduction and Title 1, Directions 1-20 (against all sinful passions in general)

**Chapter 7. Directions for the Government of the Passions.**

The passions are to be considered, 1. As in themselves, and the sin of them as respecting God and ourselves only: and so I am to speak of them here. 2. As they are a wrong to others, and a breach of the commandments which require love and duty toward our neighbor: and so I shall speak of them later.

Passions are not sinful in themselves, for God has given them to us for his service; and there is none of them but may be sanctified and used for him. But they are sinful, 1. When they are misguided and placed on wrong objects. 2. When they darken reason, and delude the mind, and keep out truth, and seduce to error. 3. When they rebel against the government of the will, and trouble it, and hinder it in its choice or pursuit of good, or urge it violently to follow their brutish inclination. 4. When they are ill-timed. 5. Or immoderate and excessive in degree. 6. Or of too long continuance. 7. And when they tend to evil effects, as to unseemly speeches or actions, or to wrong another.

Passions are holy when they are devoted to God, and exercised upon him or for him. They are good when, 1. They have right objects; 2. And are guided by reason; 3. And are obedient to the well-guided will; 4. And quicken and awaken the reason and the will to do their duty; 5. And tend to good effects, exciting all the other powers to their office; 6. And do not exceed in degree, so as to disturb the brain or body.

**Title 1. Directions against all sinful passions in general.**

**Direction 1. Do not trust to any present actual resistance, without any due, habitual mortification of passions, and fortification of the soul against them. Look most to the holy constitution of your mind and life, and then sinful passions will fall off, like scabs from a healthy body when the blood is purified.**

No wonder if an unholy soul is a slave to passion, when the body is inclined to it: for such a one is under the power of selfishness, carnality, and worldliness; and out from under the government of Christ and his Spirit; and lacks that life of grace by which he should cure and subdue the corruptions of nature. The way for such a one to master passion is not to strive by natural, selfish principles and reasons, which are partial, poor, and weak; but to look first to the main thing, and to seek with speed and earnestness for a new and sanctified heart, and get God's image, and his Spirit, and renewing, quickening grace: this is the only effectual conqueror of nature. A dull and gentle disposition may seem without this to conquer that which never much assaulted it: (the trial of such persons being some other way); but none conquers Satan indeed but the Spirit of Christ. And if you should be free from passion, and not be free from an unholy, carnal, worldly heart, you must perish at last, if you seemed the calmest persons upon earth. Begin therefore at the foundation, and see that the body of sin is mortified, and that the whole tree is rooted up which bears these evil, bitter fruits; and that the holy, victorious new nature is within you; and then you will resist sin with light and life, which others still resist but as in their sleep.

**Direction 2. More particularly, let your souls be always possessed with the fear of God, and live as in his family, under his eye and government, that his authority may be more powerful than temptations, and your holy fellowship with him may make him still more regarded by you than men or any creatures.**

And then this sun will put out the lesser lights, and the thunder of his voice will drown the whisperers that would provoke you, and the humming of those wasps which make you so impatient. God would make the creature nothing, and then it would do nothing to disturb you, or carry you into sin.

**Direction 3. Dwell in the delightful love of God, and in the sweet contemplation of his love in Christ, and roll over his tender mercies in your thoughts, and let your way of life be with the holy ones in heaven, and your work be thanksgivings and praise to God: and this will habituate your souls to such a sweetness, and mellowness, and stability, as will resist sinful passion even as heat resists cold.**

**Direction 4. Keep your consciences continually tender, and then they will check the first appearance of sinful passions, and will smart more with the sin than your emotional natures do with the provocation.**

A seared conscience, and a hardened, senseless heart, are to every sin as a man that is fast asleep is to thieves; they may come in and do what they will, as long as they do not wake him. But a tender conscience is always awake.

**Direction 5. Labor after wisdom, strength of reason, and a solid judgment; for passion is cherished by folly.**

Children are easily overthrown, and leaves are easily shaken with every little wind; while men keep their way, and rocks and mountains are not shaken. Women and children, and old, and weak, and sick people are usually most emotional. If a wise man should have an emotional nature, he has that which can do much to control it: while folly is a weathercock at the wind's command.

**Direction 6. See that the will is confirmed and resolute, and then it will soon command down passion.**

Men can do much against passion if they will. Nature has set the will on the throne of the soul; it is the sinful connivance and negligence of the will which is the guilty cause of all the rebellion; as the connivance of the commanders is the common cause of mutinies in an army. [323] The will either consents, or is remiss in its office, and in forbidding and repressing the rage of passion. When I say you can do it if you will, you think this is not true, because you are willing, and yet passion does not yield to your will's command; but I do not mean that every kind of willingness will serve; it is not a sluggish wish that will do it; but if the will were resolute without any compliance, or connivance, or negligence in its proper office, no sinful passion could remain; for it is no further sin than it is voluntary, either by the will's compliance, or omission and neglect. Therefore let most of your labor be to waken and confirm the will; and then it will command down passion.

**Direction 7. Labor after holy fortitude, courage, and magnanimity.**

Great minds are above all troubles, desires, or commotions about little things. A poor, base, low, and childish mind is never quiet longer than it is rocked asleep or flattered.

**Direction 8. Especially see that you do not lack self-denial, and that worldliness and fleshly-mindedness are thoroughly mortified; for sinful passion is the very breath and pulse of a selfish, fleshly, worldly mind.**

It is not more natural for dogs to fight about a bone than for such to snarl and quarrel, or be in some disordered passion, about their selfish, carnal interest. Greed will not let the mind be quiet. It is as natural for a selfish man to be under the power of sinful passions as for a man to shake that has a fever, or to fear that is melancholy. Fleshly men have a canine appetite and feverish thirst continually upon them, after some flesh-pleasing trifle or other.

**Direction 9. Keep a court of justice in your souls, and call yourselves daily to account, and let no passion escape without such a censure as is due.**

If reason and conscience thus exercise and maintain their authority, and passion is every day soundly rebuked, it will wither like a plant that is cropped as fast as it springs up.

**Direction 10. Deliberate and foresee the end; examine whether passion tends to that which will be approvable when it is past.**

Looking to the end shames all sinful passions: they are blind, and moved only by things present; they cannot endure the sight of the time to come, nor to be examined where they go, or where is their home.

**Direction 11. Keep a continual awareness of the danger and odiousness of sinful passions, by knowing how full they are of the spawn of many other sins.**

See the evil of them in the effects. Notice what passion does in others and yourselves; what abundance of evil thoughts, and words, and deeds come from sinful passions!

**Direction 12. Observe the immediate troublesome effects, and the disorders of your soul, and so turn the fruit of passions against themselves.**

Notice how they discompose you, and disturb your reason, and make your minds like muddied waters, and breed a diseased unquietness in you, unfitting you for your works, and breaking your peace; so that you can neither know, nor use, nor enjoy yourselves.

**Direction 13. Let death look your passions frequently in the face.**

It has a mortifying power; and as it shows us the emptiness of the creature, so it takes down those passions which creature interest and deceit have caused. It rouses reason, and restores it to its dominion, and silences the rebellion of the senses. A man that is to die tomorrow, and knows it, would more easily repel today a temptation to lust, or greed, or drunkenness, or revenge, than at another time he could have done. One look into eternity will powerfully rebuke all carnal passions.

**Direction 14. Remember always that God is present.**

Will you behave yourselves emotionally before him, when the presence of your prince would calm you? Shall God and his holy angels see you like a madman lay aside your reason and misbehave yourself?

**Direction 15. Have always some pertinent scripture ready to rebuke your passions; that you may say as Christ to Satan, "Thus it is written."**

Speak to it in the name and word of God; though the bare words will not charm these evil spirits, yet the authority will curb them. For this "word ... is living and active ... it is able to judge the desires and thoughts" (Hebrews 4:12). "Made powerful by God for tearing down strongholds. We tear down arguments and every arrogant obstacle that is raised up against the knowledge of God, and we take every thought captive to make it obey Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:4–5).

**Direction 16. Set Christ continually before you as your pattern, who calls you to learn from him to be meek and lowly (Matthew 11:29): who did not desire the wealth or glory of the world; who loved his own that were in the world, but did not love the things of the world; who never was lifted up, or sinfully cast down; who never despised or envied man, nor ever feared man; who never was overly merry or overly sad; who being reviled, did not revile in return; but was silent as a lamb before the shearers.**

[324]

**Direction 17. Keep as far from all occasions of your passions as other duties will allow you; and arrange your affairs and occasions into as great an opposition as may be to the temptation.**

Do not run into temptation, if you would be delivered from evil. Much might be done by a willing, prudent man, by the very ordering of his affairs. God and Satan work by means; let the means then be regarded.

**Direction 18. Have a due care of your bodies, that no disorder is cherished in them which causes the disorder of the soul.**

Passions have a real dependence on the temperament of the body; and much of the cure of them lies (when it is possible) in the body's improvement.

**Direction 19. Turn all your passions into the right channel, and make them all holy, using them for God upon the greatest thing.**

This is the true cure; the bare restraint of them is but a palliative cure, like the easing of pain by a dose of opium. Cure the fear of man by the fear of God; and the love of the creature by the love of God; and the cares for the body by caring for the soul; and earthly, fleshly desires and delights by spiritual desires and delights; and worldly sorrow by profitable, godly sorrow.

**Direction 20. Control the effects, and deprive your passions of what they would have; and that will before long destroy the cause.**

Deny yourselves the things which carnal love and desire would have; refrain from the things which carnal mirth or anger would provoke you to, and the fire will go out for lack of fuel. (Of which more in the particulars.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Quoted verses were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," meaning a heading or division of the chapter. Chapter 7 has ten Titles; this file has the chapter's introduction and Title 1, the general directions against all sinful passions. Titles 2-10 deal with particular passions.
- **passions:** kept as Baxter's word for the emotions and affections (love, fear, anger, sorrow, desire, and the like), not a modern psychological term. **passionate** (Directions 4, 5, 14): rendered *emotional*, following GLOSSARY.md ("passionately" becomes "emotionally"); Baxter means swayed or carried away by passion, often hot-tempered.
- **melancholy** (Direction 8): kept; see GLOSSARY.md.
- **Introduction:** the second consideration (passions as a wrong to others) Baxter says he will treat "after," that is, later in the *Directory* under duties to one's neighbor; we have not checked the exact place.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): "prosecution" (of good) to *pursuit*; "unseasonable" to *ill-timed*; "awake" (verb) to *awaken*; "wanteth" to *lacks*; "want not" to *do not lack*; "the main" to *the main thing* (one word added for sense); "from under" to *out from under*; "converse" (with God) to *fellowship*; "conversation" (Direction 3) to *way of life*; "so they do not waken him" to *as long as they do not wake him*; "when" (meaning "whereas," Direction 5) to *while*; "weather-cock" to *weathercock*; "distempered" and "distemper" to *disordered* and *disorder*; "covetousness" to *greed*; "ague" (a fever with fits of shaking and chills) to *fever*; "toy" (a trifle) to *trifle*; "springeth" to *springs up*; "whither" to *where*; "apprehension" (awareness, not anxiety) to *awareness*; "mark" to *notice*; "virtue" (power) to *power*; "vanity" to *emptiness*; "exciteth" to *rouses*; "easilier" to *more easily*; "still" (meaning always) to *always* in Directions 2, 14, and 15 (kept as "still" where it can mean "yet more," as in "still more regarded"); "bedlam" (a lunatic, from Bethlem, the London hospital for the insane) to *madman*; "lay by" to *lay aside*; "over merry or over sad" to *overly merry or overly sad*; "reviled not again" to *did not revile in return*; "dumb" (unable or unwilling to speak) to *silent*; "contrive" to *arrange*; "a very dependence" to *a real dependence*; "emendation" to *improvement*; "palliate" to *palliative* (a cure that eases symptoms without removing the cause); "frustrate your passions of" to *deprive your passions of*; "ere long" to *before long*; "Cross yourselves of" (deny or thwart yourselves) to *Deny yourselves*, since "cross yourselves" now suggests making the sign of the cross; "forbear" to *refrain from*; "for want of" to *for lack of*.
- **Kept as written:** "brutish" (animal-like, irrational); "quicken" (stir to life); "mortification," "mortified," "mortifying" (putting sin to death); "carnal" (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh); "the creature" (created things, as distinct from God); "creature interest" (the interest of created things, worldly interest); "remiss"; "magnanimity" (greatness of soul); "smart"; "censure"; "approvable"; "odiousness"; "discompose"; "charm" (as by a spell); "canine appetite" (an old medical name for ravenous, insatiable hunger).
- **"Labor after"** (Directions 5, 7): strive for.
- **"God would make the creature nothing"** (Direction 2): kept as printed. The sense seems to be that, where the fear of God rules, God would make creatures as nothing to us, and then they could not disturb us; the syntax is compressed.
- **"as for a man to shake that has a fever, or to fear that is melancholy"** (Direction 8): Baxter's word order kept; it means "as for a man who has a fever to shake, or a man who is melancholy to fear."
- **"Women and children, and old, and weak, and sick people are usually most emotional"** (Direction 5): Baxter's generalization, translated faithfully; it reflects the assumptions of his time.
- **"like a madman"** (Direction 14): reflects period attitudes to mental illness; kept in substance.
- **Passions and the body** (Direction 18): Baxter's claim that passions depend on the temperament of the body reflects the medicine of his day (the bodily "humors"); kept as his.
- **"Thus it is written"** (Direction 15): Baxter's wording, kept, with no reference in his text. Jesus answers Satan with "It is written" in Matthew 4:4, 7, 10 (NET: "It is written," "Once again it is written," "For it is written"). The reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **Hebrews 4:12** (Direction 15): Baxter quotes the King James "word is quick and powerful, ... a discerner of the thoughts." The NET has "the word of God is living and active ... it is able to judge the desires and thoughts of the heart." We give the NET wording to the extent Baxter quotes. Baxter's "quick" (living) and "powerful" are the words he leans on, that the word has force to curb passion; the NET's "living and active" keeps that sense. "Discerner" becomes "able to judge."
- **2 Corinthians 10:4–5** (Direction 15): NET wording, which runs as two sentences ("... tearing down strongholds. We tear down arguments ..."). Baxter quotes the King James: "Mighty through God, to the pulling down of strong holds, casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringeth into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ." **NET flag:** Baxter applies the verse to the passions; the King James "casting down imaginations" fits his point about disordered thoughts and fancies, while the NET's "arguments" points to reasonings raised against God. Likewise "every high thing" becomes "every arrogant obstacle." Also, in Paul the subject is "the weapons of our warfare," while Baxter applies the words to the word of God; that application is his.
- **Matthew 11:29** (Direction 16): not quoted but given in Baxter's words ("learn of him to be meek and lowly"), kept. The NET has "learn from me because I am gentle and humble in heart." Baxter's "meek and lowly" is the King James wording.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording; references not in his text): "the body of sin" (Romans 6:6); "the whole tree ... which bears these evil, bitter fruits" (compare Matthew 7:17–18); "let your way of life be with the holy ones in heaven" (compare Philippians 3:20, King James "our conversation is in heaven," NET "our citizenship is in heaven"); "a seared conscience" (1 Timothy 4:2); "who loved his own that were in the world" (John 13:1); "loved not the things of the world" (compare 1 John 2:15); "being reviled, reviled not again" (1 Peter 2:23, NET "When he was maligned, he did not answer back"); "silent as a lamb before the shearers" (Isaiah 53:7, NET "like a sheep silent before her shearers"); "Do not run into temptation, if you would be delivered from evil" (compare Matthew 6:13, NET "do not lead us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one"); "godly sorrow" and "worldly sorrow" (2 Corinthians 7:10). Baxter's note 324 gives 1 Peter 2:21–24 and Isaiah 53 for Christ as pattern.
- **Plutarch** (note 323): Plutarch's moral essay usually called *On Tranquillity of Mind* (Latin *De tranquillitate animi*). Baxter gives no passage; we have not checked which passage he has in mind for the comparison of the will to commanders who connive at mutiny.
- **Heading of Direction 1:** Baxter's Direction 1 has a heading of two sentences followed by a body paragraph, so it is set as a bold heading line with the body below. Directions 2-20 are each a single paragraph in Baxter; following the other Chapter 7 files, the opening directive sentence is set in bold with the label and the rest of the paragraph follows below (Directions 3 and 16 are a single sentence each and are wholly bold). No sentences were split or merged.
- **Numbering:** Baxter numbers the first Direction "1." and the rest in Roman numerals (II-XX); rendered as Arabic numerals throughout.
- No Latin or Greek in this section.
