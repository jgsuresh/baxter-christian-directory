# Chapter 10: Title 3, Directions 1–2 (false zeal and true zeal)

**Title 3. Directions against Sloth and Laziness in Things Spiritual: and for Zeal and Diligence.**

Zeal in things spiritual is contrary to sloth, and coldness, and remissness; and diligence is contrary to idleness. Zeal is the fervor or earnestness of the soul: [582] its first subject is the will and affections, excited by the judgment; and from there it appears in the practice. It is not a distinct grace or affection, but the vigor and liveliness of every grace, and their fervent operations.

*In the margin: The kinds of false zeal.*

**Direction 1. Be sure that you understand the nature and use of zeal and diligence, and do not mistake a carnal, degenerate sort of zeal for that which is spiritual and genuine.**

1. There is a zeal and activity merely natural, which is the effect of an active temperament of body. 2. There is an affected zeal, which is hypocritical, about things that are good; when men speak, and make an outward stir, as if they were truly zealous, when it is not so. 3. There is a selfish zeal: when a proud and selfish person is fervent in any matter that concerns himself; for his own opinions, his own honor, his own estate, or friends, or interest, or anything that is his own. 4. There is a partial, factious zeal: [583] when error, or pride, or worldliness has engaged men in a party, and they think it is their duty or interest at least to side with the sect or faction which they have chosen, they will be zealous for all the opinions and ways of their espoused party. 5. There is a superstitious, childish, carnal zeal, for small, indifferent, inconsiderable things: like that of the Pharisees (and all such hypocrites) for their washings, and fastings, and other ceremonial observances. 6. There is an envious, malicious zeal, against those that have precedence, and cross your desires, or cloud your honor in the world, or that contradict you in your notions and ways: such is that described at length (James 3). 7. There is a peevish, contentious, wrangling zeal, which is assaulting every man who is not squared exactly to your notions. 8. There is a malignant zeal, against the cause and servants of the Lord, which carries men to persecute them. See that you do not take any of these, or any such like, for holy zeal.

*In the margin: The harms of false zeal.*

If you should so mistake, these harms would follow: 1. Sinful zeal makes men doubly sinful: as holy zeal is the fervency of our grace, so sinful zeal is the intensity and fervency of sin. 2. It is an honoring of sin and Satan: as if sin were a work, and Satan a master, worthy to be fervently and diligently followed. 3. It is the most effectual, violent way of sinning, making men do much evil in a little time; and making them more destructive and hurtful to others than other sinners are. 4. It blinds the judgment, and makes men take truth for falsehood, and good for evil, and disables reason from doing its office. 5. It is the violent resister of all God's means; and teaches men to rage against the truth that should convince them: it stops men's ears, and turns away their hearts from the counsel which would do them good. 6. It is the most furious and bloody persecutor of the saints, and church of Jesus Christ: [584] it made Paul once exceedingly mad against them (Acts 26:10, 11), and "shut them up in prison, and punish them in the synagogues, and compel them to blaspheme, and persecute them even to foreign cities, and vote for their death." Thus "concerning zeal he persecuted the church" (Philippians 3:6). 7. It is the turbulent disturber of all societies; a destroyer of love; a breeder and fomenter of contention; and an enemy to order, peace, and quietness. 8. It highly dishonors God, by presuming to put his name to sin and error, and to credit him with all the wickedness it does. Such zealous sinners commit their sin as in the name of God, and fight against him ignorantly by his own (claimed or abused) authority. [585] 9. It is an impenitent way of sinning: the zealous sinner justifies his sin, and pleads reason or Scripture for it, and thinking that he does well, indeed, that he is serving God when he is murdering his servants (John 16:2). 10. It is a multiplying sin, and makes men exceedingly desirous to have all others of the sinner's mind: the zealous sinner makes as many sin with him as he can.

Indeed, if it is but a zeal for small and useless things, or about small controversies or opinions in religion; 1. It shows a mind that is lamentably foreign to the tenor of the gospel, and the mind of Christ, and the practice of the great substantial things. 2. It destroys charity and peace, and breeds censuring and abusing others. 3. It dishonors holy zeal by accident, making the profane think that all zeal is no better than the foolish passion of deceived men. 4. And it disables the persons that have it from doing good; even when they are zealous for holy truth and duty, the people will think it is but of the same nature with their erroneous zeal, and so will disregard them.

*In the margin: The signs of holy zeal.*

The signs of holy zeal are these: 1. It is guided by a right judgment. It is a zeal for truth and good, and not for falsehood and evil (Romans 10:2). 2. It is for God, and his church or cause, and not only for ourselves. It is consistent with meekness, and self-denial, and patience, as to our own concerns, and causes us to prefer the interest of God before our own (Numbers 12:3; Exodus 32:19; Galatians 4:12; Acts 13:9, 12). 3. It is always more careful of the substance than the circumstances; it prefers great things before small; it does not contend for small controversies to the loss or wrong of greater truths (Matthew 23:22, 23); it extends to every known truth and duty, but in due proportion, being hottest in the greatest things, and coolest in the least; it makes men rather zealous of good works than of their controverted opinions (Titus 2:14). 4. Holy zeal is always charitable; it is not cruel and bloody, nor of a hurting disposition (Luke 9:55), but is tender and merciful, and makes men burn with a desire to win and save men's souls, rather than to hurt their bodies (1 Corinthians 13). [586] Zeal against the sin is joined with love and pity to the sinner (2 Corinthians 12:21). 5. Yet it excludes that foolish pity which cherishes the sin (Revelation 2:2; 1 Kings 15:13). 6. True zeal is tender of the church's unity and peace; it is not a dividing, tearing zeal: it is first "pure, then peaceable, gentle, accommodating, full of mercy and good fruit" (James 3:17). 7. True zeal is impartial, and is as hot against our own sins, and our children's and other relations' sins, as against another's. [587] 8. True zeal respects all God's commandments, and is not hot for one, and contemptuous of another. It aims at perfection; and does not stint our desires to any lower degree. It makes a man desirous to be like God, even holy as he is holy. It consists principally in the fervor of our love to God; whereas false zeal consists principally in censorious wranglings against other men's actions or opinions: it first works towards good, and then rises up against the hindering evil. 9. It makes a man laborious in holy duty to God, and diligent in all his work; [588] and does not lie only in the heat of the brain, or rigid opinions, or heat of speech. 10. It is not a sudden flash, but a constant resolution of the soul; like the natural heat, and not like a fever (though the feeling part is not always of one degree); therefore it digests and strengthens, whereas false zeal only vexes and consumes. [589]

**Direction 2. When you are thus acquainted with the nature of true zeal, consider next its excellence and singular benefits, that there may be a love for it, and an honor of it in your hearts.**

To that end consider these following commendations of it.

*In the margin: The excellence of zeal and diligence.*

1. Zeal being nothing but the fervor and vigor of every grace, has in it all the beauty and excellence of that grace, and that in a high and excellent degree. If love to God is excellent, then zealous, fervent love is most excellent.

2. The nature of holy objects are such, so great and excellent, so transcendent and of unspeakable consequence, that we cannot be sincere in our estimation and seeking of them without zeal. If it were about riches or honors, a cold desire and a dull pursuit might serve the purpose, and well suit us; but about God, and Christ, and grace, and heaven, such cold desires and efforts are but a contempt. To love God without zeal is not to love him, because it is not a loving him as God. [590] To seek heaven without zeal and diligence is not to seek it, but despise it. To pray for salvation without any zeal is but hypocritically to babble, instead of praying; for no desire of Christ, and holiness, and heaven is saving, but that which prefers them before all the treasures and pleasures of the world; and that which does so has surely some zeal in it; so that some zeal is essential to every grace, as life and heat are to a man.

3. The integrity and honesty of the heart to God consists much in zeal: [591] as he is true to his friend that is zealous for him, and not he that is indifferent and cold. To do his service with zeal is to do it willingly, and heartily, and entirely. To do it without zeal is to do it heartlessly, and by halves, and to leave out the life and kernel of the duty: it is the heart that God first requires.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," meaning a heading or division. Part 1 of Chapter 10 ("Directions about our labor and callings") is divided into Titles; this file opens Title 3. Note that Title 3 is about sloth in *spiritual* things (zeal and diligence in religion), not in one's calling as such.
- **This file** covers the opening of Title 3, all of Direction 1, and the first three numbered points of Direction 2; the next file continues Direction 2.
- **zeal; affections; grace:** kept as Baxter's terms. "Affections" means emotions and desires (see GLOSSARY.md). "Its first subject is the will and affections" uses "subject" in the scholastic sense: the faculty in which a quality resides.
- **remissness:** kept (slackness, negligence).
- **temperament of body:** Baxter's "temperature of body," meaning bodily constitution or temperament (the balance of the "humors" in the older medicine), not heat.
- **affected zeal:** feigned, put on for show; Baxter's word, kept.
- **partial, factious zeal:** "partial" here means partisan. **espoused party:** the party they have adopted.
- **indifferent things:** things neither commanded nor forbidden by God (*adiaphora*), a technical term in the period's debates over ceremonies.
- **ceremonial observances:** Baxter's "ceremonious observances."
- **precedence:** Baxter's "the precedency." **notions:** his "conceits." **squared exactly to:** his "squared just to," meaning made to fit exactly.
- **harms; destructive:** Baxter's "mischiefs" (margin and text) and "mischievous," which in his day meant harmful, not playful. **would follow:** his "would ensue."
- **intensity:** Baxter's "intention," which meant intensity or strain.
- **disturber:** Baxter's "disquieter."
- **credit him with:** Baxter's "entitle him to," meaning attribute to him (not give him a right to).
- **claimed:** Baxter's "pretended" (see GLOSSARY.md).
- **indeed:** Baxter's "yea" (twice).
- **foreign to:** Baxter's "strange to," meaning a stranger to.
- **by accident:** kept; it means incidentally, as a side effect, not by chance (the older philosophical sense: not of its own nature).
- **convince:** kept in Baxter's older sense (prove wrong, refute); see GLOSSARY.md.
- **is consistent with:** Baxter's "consisteth with," meaning can exist together with. **joined with:** his "conjunct with." **concerns:** his "concernments."
- **digests:** Baxter's "concocteth." In the older physiology "concoction" was the body's digesting and ripening of food by natural heat; his image is that true zeal is like healthy natural heat, which nourishes, while false zeal is like a fever, which only wastes the body. **not always:** his "not still."
- **whereas:** Baxter's "when" (twice), used in the sense of "whereas."
- **serve the purpose; suit:** Baxter's "serve the turn" and "beseem." **despise:** his "contemn." **by halves:** his "by the halves."
- **The nature of holy objects are such:** Baxter's grammar (plural verb after "nature") is left as printed.
- **Acts 26:10–11 (paraphrase):** Baxter's quoted words turn Paul's first-person account into a list ("shut them up in prison ... vote for their death"), following the King James wording loosely. We kept his wording, modernized ("strange cities" to "foreign cities"). The NET reads: "Not only did I lock up many of the saints in prisons by the authority I received from the chief priests, but I also cast my vote against them when they were sentenced to death. I punished them often in all the synagogues and tried to force them to blaspheme. Because I was so furiously enraged at them, I went to persecute them even in foreign cities." **exceedingly mad:** Baxter's "exceeding mad" (King James, Acts 26:11), meaning furious; the NET has "furiously enraged."
- **Philippians 3:6 (corrected reference; flagged):** the source prints "Phil. iv. 6," but the words are from Philippians 3:6; we corrected the reference. Baxter adapts the King James "Concerning zeal, persecuting the church" into the third person, so we kept his wording. The NET reads "In my zeal for God I persecuted the church." The NET's "for God" makes explicit that Paul's zeal was religious zeal aimed at God, which fits Baxter's point here (a sinful zeal that takes itself to be serving God).
- **James 3:17:** NET wording. Where the King James has "easy to be entreated," the NET has "accommodating"; the King James "good fruits" is "good fruit." Baxter quotes only the first part of the verse.
- **John 16:2 (cited only):** Baxter's "thinking that he ... is serving God when he is murdering his servants" alludes to it. The NET: "the one who kills you will think he is offering service to God."
- **"holy as he is holy"** (sign 8): an unmarked allusion, compare Leviticus 11:44 and 1 Peter 1:15–16; kept in Baxter's wording.
- **Matthew 23:22, 23:** kept as Baxter cites it; the saying about tithing mint and neglecting "the more important matters of the law" is at 23:23 (and the gnat and camel at 23:24).
- **Psalm 69:10 (note 590):** kept as printed. In the King James and the NET the line "zeal for your house consumes me" is verse 9; Baxter's "10" may follow a numbering that counts the heading, or may be a slip. We have not changed it.
- **Other references** (cited only, not quoted): Romans 10:2; Numbers 12:3; Exodus 32:19; Galatians 4:12; Acts 13:9, 12; Titus 2:14; Luke 9:55; 1 Corinthians 13; 2 Corinthians 12:21; Revelation 2:2; 1 Kings 15:13; James 3.
