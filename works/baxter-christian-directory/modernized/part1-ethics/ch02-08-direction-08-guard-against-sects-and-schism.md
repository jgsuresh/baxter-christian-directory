# Chapter 2: Direction 8

**Direction 8. Keep right apprehensions of the excellency of charity and unity among believers, and receive nothing hastily that is against them; especially be careful lest under the pretense of their authority, their number, their soundness, or their holiness, you devote yourselves too much to any sect or party, to the withdrawing of your special love and rightful communion from other Christians, and turning your zeal to the interest of your party, with a neglect of the common interest of the church; but love a Christian as a Christian, and promote the unity and welfare of them all. [61]**

Make a habit of reading and carefully considering the meaning and reason of those many urgent passages in Scripture, which exhort all Christians to unity and love. [62] Such as John 11:52; 17:11, 21–23; 1 Corinthians 3:10, 17; 12 throughout; 2 Corinthians 11:13; 1 Thessalonians 5:12, 13; Philippians 2:1–3; 1 Peter 3:8; Romans 16:17; 1 Corinthians 1:10; 3:3; 11:18. And John 13:35; Romans 12:9, 10; 13:10; 2 Corinthians 13:11; Galatians 5:6, 13, 22; Colossians 1:4; 1 Thessalonians 4:9; 1 John 3:11, 14, 23; 4:7, 11, 16, 19–21. Surely if the very life of godliness did not lie much in unity and love, we should never have had such words spoken of it, as here you find. Love is to the soul, as our natural heat is to the body: whatever destroys it, destroys life; and therefore cannot be for our good. Be certain that opinion, course, or motion tends to death that tends to abate your love to your brothers, much more which under pretense of zeal provokes you to hate and hurt them. To divide the body is to kill it or to maim it; dividing the essential, necessary parts, is killing it; cutting off any integral part, is maiming it. The first can never be an act of friendship, which is the worst that an enemy can do: the second is never an act of friendship, but when the cutting off a member which may be spared is of absolute necessity to the saving of the whole man, from the worse division between soul and body. By this judge what friends dividers are to the church, and how well they are accepted by God.

He that loves any Christian aright must needs love all that appear to him as Christians. And when malice will not allow men to see Christianity in its profession, and credible appearance in another, this is as well contrary to Christian love, as hating him when you know him to be a true Christian. Censoriousness (not constrained by adequate evidence) is contrary to love, as well as hatred is.

There is a union and communion with Christians as such: this consists in having one God, one Head, one Spirit, one faith, one baptismal covenant, one rule of holy living, and in loving and praying for all, and doing good to as many as we can. This is a union and communion of mind, which we must hold with the catholic church through the world. And there is a bodily local union and communion, which consists in our joining in body, as well as mind, with particular congregations: and this, as we cannot hold it with all, nor with any congregation, but one at once; so we are not bound to hold it with any, that will drive us from it, unless we will commit some sin: [63] habitually we must hold it with the church which regularly we are joined to, and live with; and occasionally we must hold it with all others, where we have a call and opportunity, who in the substance worship God according to his word, and force us not to sin in conformity to them. It is not schism to lament the sins of any church, or of all the churches in the world: the catholic church on earth consists of sinners. It is not schism to refuse to be partaker in any sin of the purest church in the world: obedience to God is not schism. It is not schism that you join not bodily with those congregations where you dwell not, nor have any particular call to join with them; nor that you choose the purest and most edifying society, rather than one that is less pure and profitable to you; *cæteris paribus*, supposing you are at liberty: nor that you hold not bodily communion with that church, that will not allow you to do it, without sinning against God; nor that you join not with the purest churches, when you are called to abide with one less pure.

But it is worse than schism to separate from the universal church: to separate from its faith is apostasy to unbelief. To separate from it in some one or few essential articles, while you claim to hold to Christ the Head, is heresy: to separate from it in Spirit, by refusing holiness, and not loving such as are truly holy, is damning ungodliness or wickedness: to differ from it by any error, of judgment or life, against the law of God, is sin. To magnify any one church or party, so as to deny due love and communion to the rest, is schism. To limit all the church to your party, and deny all or any of the rest to be Christians, and parts of the universal church, is schism by a dangerous breach of charity; and this is the principal schism that I here admonish you to avoid. It is schism also to condemn unjustly any particular church, as no church; and it is schism to withdraw your bodily communion from a church that you were bound to hold that communion with, upon a false supposition that it is no church, or is not lawfully to be communicated with. And it is schism to make divisions or parties in a church, though you divide not from that church. Thus I have (briefly) told you what is schism.

1. One pretense for schism is (usurped) authority, which some one church may claim to command others that owe them no subjection. Thus pride, which is the spirit of hell, having crept into the church of Christ, and animated to usurpations of lordship and dominion, and contending for superiority, has caused the most dangerous schisms in the church, that it was ever infested with. The bishop of Rome (advantaged by the seat and constitution of that empire) having claimed the government of all the Christian world, condemns all the churches that will not be his subjects; and so has made himself the head of a sect, and of the most pernicious schisms that ever did rend the church of Christ: and the bishop of Constantinople, and too many more, have followed the same method in a lower degree, exalting themselves above their brothers, and giving them laws, and then condemning and persecuting them that obey them not. And when they have imposed upon other churches their own usurped authority and laws, they have laid the plot to call all men schismatics and sectaries, that do not acknowledge their tyrannical usurpation, and that will not be schismatics and sectaries with them: and the trick lies in this, that they confuse the churches' unity with their claimed authority, and schism with the refusal of subjection to them. If you will not take them for your lords, they cry out that you divide from the church: as if we could hold communion with no churches, but those whose bishops we obey. Communion with other churches is maintained by faith and charity, and agreement in things necessary, without subjection to them. As we may hold all rightful communion with the churches in Armenia, Arabia, Russia, without subjection to their bishops; so may we with any other church besides that of which we are members. Division or schism is contrary to unity and concord, and not to a usurped government: though disobedience to the pastors which God has set over us is a sin, and dividing from them is a schism. Both the pope and all the lower usurpers should do well first to show their commission from God to be our rulers, before they call it schism to refuse their government. If they had not made better advantage of fire and sword, than of Scripture and argument, the world would but have laughed them to scorn, when they had heard them say, All are schismatics that will not be our subjects: our dominion and will shall be necessary to the unity of the church. The universal church indeed is one, united under one head and governor: but it is only Jesus Christ who is that head, and not any usurping vicar or vice-Christ. The bishops of particular churches are his officers; but he has deputed no vicar to his own office, as the universal head. Above all sects, be careful of this pernicious sect, who claim their usurped authority for their schism, and have no way to promote their sect, but by calling all sectaries that will not be sectaries and subjects to them.

2. Another pretense for schism is the numbers of the party. This is another of the papists' motives; as if it were lawful to divide the church of Christ, if they can but get the greater party. They say, We are the most, and therefore you should yield to us: and so do others, where by the sword they force the most to submit to them. But we answer them, As many as they are, they are too few to be the universal church. The universal church, containing all true, professing Christians, is much more than they. The papists are not a third part, if a fourth, of the whole church. Papists are a corrupted sect of Christians: I will be against dividing the body of Christ into any sects, rather than to be one of that sect or dividing party, which is the greatest.

3. Another pretense for schism is the soundness or orthodoxy of a party. Almost all sects claim that they are wiser and of sounder judgment than all the Christian world besides: indeed, those that most obviously contradict the Scriptures, (as the papists in their half-communion and unintelligible service,) and have no better reason why they so believe or do, but because others have so believed and done already.

But, (1.) the greatest claimants to orthodoxy are not the most orthodox: (2.) and if they were, I can value them for that in which they excel, without abating my due respect to the rest of the church. (3.) For the whole church is orthodox in all the essentials of Christianity, or else they were not Christians: and I must love all that are Christians with that special love that is due to the members of Christ, though I must add besides such esteem for those that are a little wiser or better than others, as they deserve.

4. The fourth pretense for schism, is the holiness of the party that men adhere to. But this must make but a difference of degree, in our esteem and love to some Christians above others: if really they are most holy, I must love them most, and work to be as holy as they; but I must not therefore unjustly deny communion, or due respect, to other Christians that are less holy; nor cling to them as a sect or divided party, whom I esteem most holy. For the holiest are most charitable, and most against the divisions among Christians, and most careful of their unity and peace.

The sum of this direction is: 1. Highly value Christian love and unity. 2. Love those most that are most holy, and be most familiar with them, for your own edification: and if you have your choice, hold local personal communion with the soundest, purest, and best qualified church. 3. But do not hastily entertain any odd opinion of a divided party; or, if you do hold it as an opinion, lay not greater weight on it than there is cause. 4. Acknowledge the best as best, but none as a divided sect; and espouse not their dividing interest. 5. Confine not your special love to a party; especially for agreeing in some opinions with you; but extend it to all the members of Christ. 6. Deny not local communion, when there is occasion for it, to any church that has the substance of true worship, and forces you not to sin. 7. Love them as true Christians and churches, even when they thus drive you from their communion.

It is a most dangerous thing to a young convert, to be ensnared in a sect: it will, before you are aware, possess you with a feverish, sinful zeal for the opinions and interest of that sect; it will make you bold in bitter invectives and censures, against those that differ from them; it will corrupt your church communion, and fill your very prayers with partiality and human passions; it will secretly bring malice, under the name of zeal, into your minds and words: in a word, it is a secret but deadly enemy to Christian love and peace. Let them that are wiser, and more orthodox and godly, than others, show it as the Holy Spirit directs: James 3:13–18, "Who is wise and understanding among you? By his good conduct he should show his works done in the gentleness that wisdom brings. But if you have bitter jealousy (or zeal) and selfishness in your hearts, do not boast and tell lies against the truth. Such wisdom does not come from above but is earthly, natural, demonic. For where there is jealousy and selfishness, there is disorder (or tumult) and every evil practice. But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, accommodating, full of mercy and good fruit, impartial (or wrangling), and not hypocritical. And the fruit that consists of righteousness is planted in peace among those who make peace."

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows the Latin passages in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[61] Against uncharitableness and schism, see more in part 2, chapter 23.

[62] Utrumque imperium, et Mahometicum et pontificium ortum est, ex dissidiis de doctrina--Cum in oriente dilaceratæ essent ecclesiæ--et hæc varietas in multorum animis dubitationes et odium religionis christianæ accenderet, et disciplina laxata esset, &c. Melancth. Ep. Dedic. Chron. Carionis. (English: Both empires, the Mahometan [Muslim] and the papal, arose from disputes over doctrine--When the churches in the East had been torn apart--and this variety kindled doubts in the minds of many, and hatred of the Christian religion, and discipline had been relaxed, etc. Melanchthon, dedicatory epistle to Carion's *Chronicle*. Rendered loosely, since the quotation is cut with dashes.)

[63] Ecclesia vera discreta est à cœtu Cain, qui secesserat a patre, et habuit suos ritus, et suam sectam. Ita statim initio veræ doctrinæ vocem et veram ecclesiam pars humani generis deseruit. Carion Chronic. lib. 1. p. 16. (English: The true church is distinct from the company of Cain, who had withdrawn from his father, and had his own rites and his own sect. So at the very beginning a part of mankind abandoned the voice of true doctrine and the true church. Carion, *Chronicle*, book 1, p. 16.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **James 3:13–18:** NET wording, fetched; Baxter's own parenthetical glosses ("or zeal," "or tumult," "or wrangling") are kept as he printed them, after the nearest NET word; the second "is" in his "where envying and strife is" follows the NET's "where there is." The NET changes the sense Baxter leans on in several places. (1) King James "bitter envying" is "bitter jealousy" in the NET; Baxter's gloss "(or zeal)" offers the other English word for it, and the NET's "jealousy" does not carry "zeal," though his argument that zeal can hide bitterness leans on it. (2) King James "strife" is "selfishness" in the NET (twice). (3) King James "sensual" is "natural" in the NET, and "devilish" is "demonic." This may matter because "sensual" is the word Baxter prints here (we have not confirmed how his readers took it); "natural" does not carry that word. (4) King James "confusion" is "disorder" in the NET; his gloss "tumult" is kept. (5) King James "without partiality" is "impartial" in the NET; Baxter's gloss "(or wrangling)" offers another sense and is not in the NET text; it stands in his text after "impartial" where he had "partiality." (7) King James "easy to be entreated" is "accommodating" in the NET, "good fruits" is "good fruit," "wise man and endued with knowledge" is "wise and understanding," and "sown" is "planted"; none of these changes his point. (6) King James "good conversation" is "good conduct" in the NET, which matches our rule for "conversation." Baxter gives no verse split; we kept his whole run as one quotation.
- **Scripture list (paragraph 2):** the references are a list, not quotations, so no NET text is used; they are converted to modern form. "xii. throughout" is "12 throughout" (1 Corinthians 12). We have not checked each reference against its text. 2 Corinthians 11:13 (which we believe concerns false apostles) does not obviously speak of unity and love, and may be a misprint for another verse; we keep Baxter's reference and note the doubt.
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** *addict yourselves* to "devote yourselves" (in his day "addict" meant devote, with no sense of dependency); *take heed* to "be careful"; *pretence* to "pretense" (a ground or excuse put forward), and *pretend/pretended* to "claim/claimed"; *just communion* to "rightful communion"; *just evidence* to "adequate evidence"; *infidelity* to "unbelief" (following our swap of infidel to unbeliever); *orthodoxness* to "orthodoxy"; *brethren* to "brothers"; *statedly* to "habitually"; *suffer* (allow) to "allow"; *palpably* to "obviously"; *superadd* to "add besides"; *confound* to "confuse"; *own* (acknowledge) to "acknowledge"; *the cheat lieth* to "the trick lies"; *tenderest of* to "most careful of"; *labour* to "work"; *gradual difference* to "difference of degree"; *yea* to "indeed"; *cleave* to "cling"; *insnared* to "ensnared"; *Holy Ghost* to "Holy Spirit"; *vice-christ* to "vice-Christ." *Christians, Christianity, Christian* are capitalized.
- **Technical or period terms kept:** *papists* (Baxter's word for Roman Catholics); *sectaries* and *schismatics* (members of sects; those who cause schism); *catholic church* (the whole church, not the Roman communion); *Censoriousness* (a habit of harsh judging); *integral part* (a part that belongs to the whole but is not essential to its life, in contrast to "essential, necessary parts"); *motion* (an impulse or inward stirring); *interest* (cause or advantage); *half-communion* (as we read Baxter, the withholding of the cup from the laity; not confirmed); *vicar* (a deputy, here the pope as Christ's claimed deputy); *edifying/edification* (building up in faith); *cæteris paribus* (Latin, "other things being equal").
- **Baxter's polemic:** his words on the bishop of Rome, the papists, and the bishop of Constantinople are kept as he wrote them. Claims about the history of Rome's claims, about the proportion of papists in the church ("not a third part, if a fourth"), and about the churches of Armenia, Arabia, and Russia are his own, not confirmed by us.
- **"Let them that are wiser... show it as the Holy Spirit directs":** Baxter takes James 3:13–18 as the Spirit's direction to show wisdom by peace.
- **Numbering:** the items "1." to "4." (the pretenses for schism) are Baxter's; the "(1.) (2.) (3.)" inside item 3's reply and the "1." to "7." in "The sum of this direction" are also his and kept. The source prints "_Direct._ VIII." with no margin note; there are no margin summaries in this range.
- **Unmarked Scripture allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "one God, one Head, one Spirit, one faith, one baptismal covenant" (compare Ephesians 4:4–6); "the spirit of hell" and "head of a sect" are his own phrasing, not quotations.
- **Footnote 61:** cross-reference to Part 2, chapter 23 of the *Directory*; not checked.
- **Footnotes 62 and 63:** Baxter's abbreviations "Melancth." and "Carion" refer to Melanchthon and to Johann Carion's *Chronicle*; this is probably the work Baxter means, but we have not confirmed that the quoted words come from the pages he names. "Mahometicum" is rendered "Mahometan [Muslim]" in the draft translation to show his period term. The source text of note 62 reads "dilaceratæ" and "Chron. Carionis" as printed; no misprint noticed.
- **Cain** (note 63): probably Genesis 4; we have not confirmed how Carion uses it.
