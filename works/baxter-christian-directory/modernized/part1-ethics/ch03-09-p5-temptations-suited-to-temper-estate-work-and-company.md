# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 5 of 13)

*This section begins partway through Grand Direction 9, in the middle of Baxter's paired list of temptations and the directions against them: it opens with Temptation 2 and ends with Temptation 8; Direction 8 follows in the next file. No new Grand Direction heading begins here.*

**Temptation 2.** The tempter also fits his temptations to men's several bodily temperaments, (as I showed, p. 93.) The hot and strong he tempts to lust. The sad and fearful he tempts to discouragement and continual self-vexations; and to the fear of men and devils. Those that have strong appetites, to gluttony and drunkenness. Children, and women, and weak-headed people, to pride of clothing and empty formalities. And masculine, wicked unbelievers, to pride of honor, abilities, and grandeur, and to an ambitious seeking of rule and greatness. The meek and gentle he tempts to give in to the persuasions and will of mistaken and tempting persons. And those that are more stubborn, to a stubborn resistance of all that would do them good. He found it most suitable to tempt a Saul to malice; David, by a surprise, to lust; Absalom to ambition; Peter to fearfulness, and afterward to compliance and pretense, to avoid the offense and displeasure of the weak; Luther to rashness; Melancthon to fearfulness; Carolostadius to unsettledness; Illiricus to excessive zeal; Osiander to self-esteem; (if historians have given them their due.) One shoe does not fit every foot.

**Direction 2.** Let your strictest watch be upon the sins of your temperament. Far greater diligence and resolution is necessary here than against other sins. And withdraw the fuel, and strive against the bodily disorders themselves. Fasting and labor will do much against lust, which idleness and fullness continually feed. And so the rest have their several cures. Know also what good your temperament gives you special advantage for; and let it be turned to that, and still employed in it.

**Temptation 3.** The tempter suits his temptations to your estates, of poverty or riches. The poor he tempts to grumble and be impatient under their wants, and to distress themselves more with griefs and cares; and to think that their sufferings may save them without holiness, and that necessary labor for their bodies may excuse them from much minding the concerns of their souls; and either to criticize and hate the rich through envy, or to flatter them for gain. The rich he tempts to an idle, time-wasting, self-indulgent, fleshly, brutish life; to excess in sleep, and food, and drink, and amusement, and clothing, and costly ways of pride, and idle talk, and visits, and formalities; to love the wealth and honors of the world, and live in continual pleasing of the flesh, to live in luxury every day, and to waste their time in unprofitableness, without a constant calling; and to be unmerciful to the poor, and to tyrannize over their inferiors; Proverbs 30:8-9; Luke 16.

*In the margin: 1 Timothy 6:9.*

**Direction 3.** Here also observe carefully where your danger lies, and there keep a continual watch. Let the poor remember that if they are not rich in grace, it is their own fault; and if they are, they have the chief riches, and have learned to be content in every circumstance: and have great cause to be thankful to God that he thus helps them against the love and pleasures of the world. Let the rich remember that they have no less to do than the poor, because they have more committed to their trust; nor may they ever the more satisfy the excessive desires of the flesh. But they have more to do, and more dangers to fear and watch against, as they have more of their Master's talents to employ, and give account for at the last.

**Temptation 4.** The devil suits his temptations to men's daily work and business. If it is low, to be ashamed of it through pride; if it is high, to be proud of it; if it is hard, to be weary and unfaithful in it, or to make it take up all their minds and time; if it is about worldly things, he tempts them to be tainted by it with a worldly mind; if they labor for themselves, he tempts them to overdo; if for others, he tempts them to deceitful, unfaithful negligence and laziness. If they are ministers, he tempts them to be idle, and unfaithful, and unmoved by the weight of truth, the worth of souls, the brevity of time, so that their sin may be the ruin or the loss of many. If rulers, the devil uses his utmost skill to cause them to take up an interest contrary to the interest of truth and holiness; and to put some quarrel against Christ into their minds, and to persuade them that his interest is against theirs, and that his servants are their enemies.

**Direction 4.** See that your work is lawful, and that God has called you to it, and then take it as the service which he himself assigns to you, and do it as in his sight, and as passing to his judgment, in obedience to his will: and mind not so much whether it is hard or easy, low or high, as whether you are faithful in it. And if it is sanctified to you, by your intending all to the pleasing of God, remember that he loves and rewards the servant that stoops to the lowest work at his command, as much as the one that is employed in the highest. Do all for God, and walk in holiness with him, and keep out selfishness, (the poison of your callings,) and observe the proper danger of your positions, and keep a constant watch against them.

**Temptation 5.** The devil suits his temptations to our several relationships. Parents he tempts to be cold and unconcerned about the great work of a wise and holy education of their children. Children he tempts to be disobedient, ungrateful, void of natural affection, disrespectful dishonorers of their parents. Husbands he tempts to be unloving, unkind, impatient with the weaknesses of their wives; and wives to be irritable, self-willed, proud, loud, passionate, and disobedient. Masters he tempts to use their servants only as their beasts, for their own advantage, without any care of their salvation and God's service; and servants he tempts to be carnal, untrustworthy, false, lazy, eye-servants, that take more care to hide a fault than not to commit it. Ministers and magistrates he tempts to seek themselves, and neglect their charge, and set up their own ends instead of the common good; or to mistake the common good, or the means that lead to it. Subjects and people he tempts to dishonor and grumble against their governors, and to criticize them unjustly, and to disobey them, and rebel; or else to honor, and fear, and serve them more than God, and against God.

**Direction 5.** Here learn well the duties and dangers of your own relationships, and remember that it is much of your work to be faithful and excellent in your relationships. And mind not so much what other men owe to you as what you owe to God and them. Let masters, and ministers, and magistrates first study and carefully practice their own duties, and yet they must next see that their inferiors do their duties, because that is their office: but they must be more desirous that God be served first, and more careful to procure obedience to him, than that they be honored or obeyed themselves. Children, servants, and subjects must be taken up in the well-doing of their proper work; remembering that their good or harm lies far more upon that than upon their superiors' dealings with them, or treatment of them. As it is your own body, and not your superior's, which your soul animates, nourishes, and uses, and which you have the continual awareness and charge of; so it is your own duty, and not your superiors', which you have to do and to answer for, and therefore most to mind and talk of.

**Temptation 6.** The tempter also suits his temptations to our advantages, and hopes of rising or thriving in the world: he sees which is our rising or thriving way; and there he lays his snares, suited to our designs and ends, making some sinful omission or commission seem necessary to them. Either Balaam must prophesy against the people of God, or else God must keep him from honor, by keeping him from sin, Numbers 24:11. If once Judas is set on it, What will you give me? the devil will teach him the way to gain: his way is necessary to such sinful ends.

**Direction 6.** Take heed therefore of overvaluing the world, and being taken with its honor, pleasure, or prosperity; take heed, lest the love of earthly things engage you in eager desires and designs to grow great or rich. For if once your heart has such a design, you are gone from God: the heart is gone, and then all will follow as occasion calls for it. Understand these scriptures: "Do not wear yourself out to become rich" (Proverbs 23:4). "...the one who hastens to gain riches will not go unpunished. ... The stingy person hastens after riches" (Proverbs 28:20, 22). "Those who long to be rich, however, stumble into temptation and a trap and many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction. For the love of money is the root of all evils. ... Now godliness combined with contentment brings great profit" (1 Timothy 6:9, 10, 6). "Are you looking for great things for yourself? Do not look for such things" (Jeremiah 45:5). Be dead to the world: fear the rising way more than the falling way. Love that condition best which fits you for communion with God, or makes you the most profitable servant to him; and hate that most which is your greatest hindrance from these, and would most enslave you to the world.

**Temptation 7.** The tempter suits his temptations to our company: if they have any error or sin, or are engaged in any carnal enterprise, he will make them snares to us, and restless until they have ensnared us. If they do not love us, he will make them continual provocations, and set before us all their wrongs, and provoke us to uncharitableness and revenge. If they love us, he will try to make their love to us the shoehorn or forerunner of their errors and evil ways, to draw us to imitate them. He finds something in all our company to make the matter of some temptation.

**Direction 7.** Converse most with God: let faith make Christ and angels your most regarded and observed company; so that their mind and presence may affect you more than the mind and presence of mortal men. Look not at any man's mind, or will, or actions, without regard to God who governs, and to the rule by which they should all be suited, and to the judgment which will open and reward them as they are. Never see man without seeing God: see man only as a creature dependent on his Maker's will. And then you will lament and not imitate him when he sins; and you will oppose (and Christ says "hate," Luke 14:26) and not be seduced by him, when he would draw you with him to sin and hell: had Adam observed God more than Eve, he would not have been seduced by his helper. Then you will look on the proud, and worldly, and pleasure-loving, as Solomon on the slothful man's vineyard, "Then I scrutinized it. I was putting my mind to it—I saw; I took in a lesson" (Proverbs 24:30-32). You would not long for the plague or leprosy, because it is your friend's disease.

**Temptation 8.** The tempter takes advantage of other men's opinions or speeches about you, or dealings with you; and by every one of them would ensnare you in some sin. If they have low thoughts of you, or speak scornful or dishonoring words of you, he tempts you by it to hate them, or love them less, or to speak contemptuously of them. If they applaud you, he tempts you by it to be proud; if they wrong you, he tempts you to revenge; if they enrich you, or are your benefactors, he would make their benefits a price to hire you to some sin, and make you pay as dearly for them as your salvation is worth. If they scorn you for religion, he would make you ashamed of Christ and his cause; if they admire you, he would draw you by it to hypocrisy. If they threaten you, he would draw you to sin by fear, as he did Peter; if they deal rudely with you, he tempts you to anger, and to repay them in kind, and even to come to dislike religion itself, if men professing religion are against you, or seem to do you any wrong. Thus every man is a danger to his brother.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Baxter's quotations are King James wording; wherever the NET is quoted, the flags below say where it differs.
- **Labels.** Baxter's "_Tempt._ II." to "VIII." are rendered "Temptation 2." to "8." and his "_Direct._" items "Direction 2." to "7."; the numbering is his, restarting inside this Grand Direction. Direction 8 (the answer to Temptation 8) falls in the next file.
- **Cross-reference.** "p. 93" is Baxter's own page reference to an earlier passage in the *Directory*; it has not been checked here.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): temper / temperature to temperament (bodily constitution; "temper" is not meant in the modern sense of anger); trifling compliment to empty formalities, and "compliments" in Temptation 3 to formalities (courtesies and social ceremony); sensual to pleasure-loving; voluptuous to self-indulgent; sports to amusements; apparel to clothing; discourse to talk; concernments to concerns; commodity to advantage; senseless of to unmoved by; "long of themselves" to "their own fault"; fare deliciously to live in luxury (his words probably echo the rich man of Luke 16, which Baxter cites at the end of this sentence); peevish to irritable; clamorous to loud; untrusty to untrustworthy; murmur to grumble; requite to repay; dissimulation to pretense; distaste to come to dislike; shoeing-horn to shoehorn; harbinger to forerunner; hinderance to hindrance; insnare to ensnare; regardfully to carefully; murmuring and censuring to grumbling and criticizing; erroneous to mistaken; yieldingness unto to give in to; stiff to stubborn; inordinate to excessive; distempers to disorders; usage to treatment; sense (awareness) to awareness; espouse to take up; sloth to laziness; accommodated to suited; endeavour to try; mean (thoughts) to low.
- **"Converse"** in Direction 7 is kept in its old sense of keep company, associate ("Converse most with God").
- **Kept as Baxter's words.** "Brutish" (animal-like, lacking reason); "carnal"; "eye-servants" (servants who work only while watched; compare Ephesians 6:6, Colossians 3:22); "talents" (the Master's talents, an allusion to the parable in Matthew 25, which Baxter does not cite); "lust"; "callings" (one's daily occupations); "masculine, wicked unbelievers" (Baxter's phrase; "masculine" probably means strong-minded or forceful, but we have not confirmed this).
- **"Interest."** In Temptation 4, "interest" is used in Baxter's sense of a cause or party one is committed to.
- **"As your salvation comes to."** In Temptation 8 Baxter writes "as dear for them as your salvation cometh to"; rendered "as dearly for them as your salvation is worth." The sense appears to be that the price is as high as the value of salvation itself.
- **Names.** Baxter's spellings "Melancthon," "Carolostadius," "Illiricus," and "Osiander" are kept. These appear to refer to sixteenth-century Reformation figures (Melanchthon, Karlstadt, Flacius Illyricus, Andreas Osiander), but the identifications are our reading, not Baxter's, and have not been confirmed. His "(if historians have given them their due)" is his own hedge about the accuracy of the accounts.
- **Biblical figures** named without references: Saul (malice), David (lust), Absalom (ambition), Peter (fearfulness, and later "compliance and dissimulation"; this may refer to Galatians 2:11-14, but Baxter does not say, and we have not confirmed it), Adam and Eve, Balaam, Judas. "What will you give me?" (Temptation 6) is Baxter's wording of Judas's question; the NET of Matthew 26:15 reads "What will you give me to betray him into your hands?" The allusion is unmarked in his text.
- **Unmarked allusions.** "Have learned in all estates to be content" (Direction 3) echoes Philippians 4:11-12; "rich in grace" echoes the contrast with being rich in this world (compare James 2:5). Neither is cited by Baxter, and we have rendered his words, not the NET's. "Your own body, and not your superior's" is Baxter's argument, not a quotation.
- **Scripture references converted** to modern form: Prov. xxx. 8, 9 to Proverbs 30:8-9; Luke xvi to Luke 16; 1 Tim. vi. 9 (margin) to 1 Timothy 6:9; Numb. xxiv. 11 to Numbers 24:11; Luke xiv. 26 to Luke 14:26; Prov. xxiv. 30-32 to Proverbs 24:30-32. Baxter gives no quotation in Temptation 3, Temptation 6 (Numbers 24:11), or the margin note, so the NET is not quoted there. For Numbers 24:11 the NET reads, in part, "I said that I would greatly honor you, but now the Lord has stood in the way of your honor," which supports Baxter's paraphrase.
- **NET flags (where the NET wording changes the sense Baxter leans on).**
  - *Proverbs 23:4.* Baxter's King James reads "Labour not to be rich." The NET has "Do not wear yourself out to become rich." The NET stresses exhausting effort, where the KJV can sound like a ban on seeking wealth at all; Baxter's use of the verse (do not make riches your plan) fits both, but the NET is a little narrower.
  - *Proverbs 28:20, 22.* The KJV has "He that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent" and "He that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye." The NET has "will not go unpunished" and "The stingy person hastens after riches." Baxter's "not innocent" (guilt) becomes "not unpunished" (consequence), and "evil eye" (a grudging or covetous look) becomes "stingy." The NET verse 22 goes on: "and does not know that poverty will overtake him." Baxter's selection skips verse 21 and ends verse 22 early; we mark the gap with "..." and a leading ellipsis.
  - *1 Timothy 6:9, 10, 6.* Baxter cites "6, 9" but quotes verse 9, part of verse 10, and then verse 6, in that order, with a blending of wording. The KJV reads "they that will be rich" (will, as in resolve) and "foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition"; the NET has "Those who long to be rich" (desire, not resolve) and "senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction." It reads "the root of all evils," where Baxter has "all evil." The NET has "Now godliness combined with contentment brings great profit," against Baxter's "But ... great gain." The NET's plural "evils" may read slightly less absolute than Baxter's "all evil," but we have not checked the NET's own notes on this.
  - *Jeremiah 45:5.* Baxter has "Seek not great matters for yourselves" (his "yourselves" is plural, while the NET's "yourself" is singular). The NET renders it as a question and answer: "Are you looking for great things for yourself? Do not look for such things." The NET also adds a reason in the next lines (disaster coming on all humanity), which Baxter does not quote.
  - *Luke 14:26.* Baxter relies on the word "hate," which the NET keeps ("does not hate his own father and mother ... and even his own life"). No change in sense. The NET quotes more of the verse than Baxter does; we have kept only his one word.
  - *Proverbs 24:30-32.* Baxter's quoted words ("I saw and considered it well, I looked on it, and received instruction") follow the KJV closely; the reference printed is 24:30-32, and the words quoted are from verse 32. The NET of verse 32 reads "Then I scrutinized it. I was putting my mind to it—I saw; I took in a lesson." The NET does not have the phrase "looked on it." Baxter's point (look hard at another's failing and learn from it) holds for both, but the NET is more terse. Baxter's "slothful man's vineyard" is "the vineyard of one who lacks sense" in NET verse 30, with "sluggard" for the field; so "lacks sense" there is a broader charge than sloth.
- **Source oddity.** Baxter's "Direct." III is preceded in the source by a margin note ("1 Tim. vi. 9.") that sits between the end of Temptation 3 and the start of Direction 3; we have kept it in that position, as in the source.
