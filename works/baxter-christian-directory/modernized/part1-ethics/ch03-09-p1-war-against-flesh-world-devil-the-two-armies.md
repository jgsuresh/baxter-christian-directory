# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 1 of 13)

**Grand Direction 9. Spend all your days in a skillful, vigilant, resolute, and valiant war against the flesh, the world, and the devil, as those who have covenanted to follow Christ the Captain of your salvation.**

The flesh is the end of temptation, [104] for all is to please it (Romans 13:14), and therefore it is the greatest enemy; the world is the matter of temptation; and the devil is the first mover, or efficient cause, of it: and this is the trinity of enemies to Christ and us, which we renounce in baptism, and must constantly resist. Of the world and the flesh I shall speak in chapter 4. Here I shall open the methods of the devil. And first I shall prepare your understanding, by opening some presupposed truths.

1. It is presupposed that there is a devil. He who does not believe this proves it to others, by showing how thoroughly the devil can make a fool of him. Apparitions, witchcrafts, and temptations are full proofs of it to the senses; besides what Scripture says.

2. It is supposed that he is the deadly enemy of Christ and us. [105] He was once an angel, and fell from his first estate by sin, and a world of evil spirits with him; and it is probable that his envy against mankind might be the greater, as knowing that we were made to succeed him and his followers in their state of glory: for Christ says that we shall be "equal to angels" (Luke 20:36). He showed his enmity to man in our innocence, and by his temptation caused our fall and misery. But after the fall, God put an enmity into the nature of man against devils, as a merciful safeguard against temptation: so that as the whole nature of man abhors the nature of serpents, so the soul abhors and dreads the diabolical nature. And, therefore, so far as the devil is seen in a temptation now, so far it is frustrated; until the enmity in nature is overcome by his deceits: and this help nature has against temptation, which it seems our nature did not have before the fall, as not knowing the malice of the devil against us.

There is a natural enmity to the devil himself put into all the woman's natural seed: but the moral enmity against his sinful temptations and works is put only into the spiritual seed by the Holy Spirit (except what remnants are in the light of nature). I will be brief about all this and the next, having spoken of them more fully in my *Treatise against Infidelity*, Part 3.

The devil's names tell us what he is. [106] In the Old Testament he is called, 1. The "serpent" (Genesis 3). 2. The Hebrew word, translated "goat demons" in Leviticus 17:7, and "wild goats" in Isaiah 13:21, signifies hairy, as satyrs are described; and sometimes he-goats; because in such shapes he often appears. 3. He is called "Satan" (Zechariah 3:1). 4. "An evil spirit" (1 Samuel 18:10). 5. "A lying spirit" (1 Kings 22:22); for he "is a liar and the father of lies" (John 8:44). 6. His offspring is called "the unclean spirit" (Zechariah 13:2). 7. And he (or his spawn) is called "a spirit of fornication" (Hosea 4:12); that is, idolatry. 8. "A perverse spirit, causing staggering and giddiness as a drunken man" (Isaiah 19:14).

In the New Testament, 1. He is sometimes called simply "a spirit" (Mark 9:20, 26; Luke 9:39; 10:20). 2. Sometimes, πνευματα ακαθαρτα, "unclean spirits" (Luke 6:18); as contrary to the Holy Spirit; and that from their nature and effects. 3. And afterward, δαιμομιον, "demons," a word taken in a good sense in heathen writers, but not in Scripture; because they worshiped devils under that name (unless perhaps Acts 17:18; 1 Timothy 4:1). And, δαιμων with respect to their knowledge, and, as some think, to the knowledge promised to Adam in the temptation. 4. Πειραζων, "the tempter" (Matthew 4). 5. "Satan" (Matthew 4; 1 Peter 5:8). 6. Εχθρος, "an enemy" (Matthew 13:28, 39). 7. "The strong man armed" (Matthew 12). 8. "Angels" (1 Corinthians 6:3; 2 Peter 2:4). "Angels who did not keep their first state" (Jude 6). 9. "A spirit of divination" (Acts 16:16). 10. "A roaring lion" (1 Peter 5:8). 11. "A murderer" (John 8:44). 12. "Belial" (2 Corinthians 6:15). 13. "Beelzebub" (Matthew 12), the "god of flies." 14. "The ruler of this world" (John 12:31), from his power over wicked men. 15. "The god of this age" (2 Corinthians 4:4), because the world obeys him. 16. "The ruler of the domain of the air" (Ephesians 2:2). 17. "The world rulers of this darkness" (Ephesians 6:12). "Principalities and powers." 18. "The father of the wicked" (John 8:44). 19. "The dragon, and the old serpent" (Revelation 12). 20. Διαβολος, "the calumniator," or "false accuser," often. 21. Ὁ πονηρος "the evil one" (Matthew 13:19). 22. "An evil spirit" (Acts 19:15). 23. Απολλυων, "the destroyer," and "Abaddon," the "king of the locusts," and "angel of the abyss" (Revelation 9:11), (unless that speaks of antichrist).

3. He is too strong an enemy for fallen, sinful man to deal with on his own. If he conquered us in innocence, what may he do now? He is dangerous, (1.) By the greatness of his subtlety. (2.) By the greatness of his power. (3.) By the greatness of his malice. And hence, (4.) By his constant diligence, watching when we sleep (Matthew 13:25); and "seeking night and day to devour" (1 Peter 5:8; Revelation 12:4).

4. Therefore Christ has engaged himself in our cause, and has become the "pioneer of their salvation" (Hebrews 2:10). [107] And the world is formed into two armies, which live in continual war: the devil is the prince and general of one, and his angels and wicked men are his armies: Christ is the King and General of the other, and his angels (Hebrews 2:14) and saints are his army. Between these two armies are the greatest conflict in the world.

5. It is supposed also that this war is carried on, on both sides, within us and without us; by inward solicitations, and outward means, which are fitted to it.

6. Both Christ and Satan work by officers, instruments, and means. Christ has his ministers to preach his gospel, and pull down the kingdom of Satan. And Satan has his ministers to preach licentiousness and lies, and to resist the gospel and kingdom of Christ (1 Corinthians 3:5; 4:1; 2 Corinthians 11:15; Acts 13:8-10). Christ has his church, and the devil has his synagogue. Christ's soldiers do every one, in their places, fight for him against the devil. And the devil's soldiers do every one, in their places, fight against Christ. The generals are both unseen to mortals; and the unseen power is theirs; but their agents are visible. The soldiers fight not only against the generals, but against one another; but it is all, or chiefly, for the generals' sakes. It is Christ whom the wicked persecute in his servants (Acts 9:4); and it is the devil whom the godly hate and resist in the wicked.

But yet here are several notable differences. 1. The devil's servants do not do what they do in love to him, but to their own flesh; but Christ's servants do what they do in love to him, as well as to themselves. 2. The devil's army are tricked into arms and war, not knowing what they do; but Christ does all in the open light, and will have no servants but those who deliberately adhere to him, when they know the worst. 3. The devil's servants do not know that he is their general; but Christ's followers all know their Lord. 4. The devil's followers disown their master and their work; they will not admit that they fight against Christ and his kingdom, while they do it: but Christ's followers own their Captain, and his cause, and work; for he is not a master to be ashamed of.

7. Both Christ and Satan work persuasively, by moral means, and neither of them by constraint and force. Christ does not force men against their wills to good, and Satan cannot force them to be bad; but all the effort is to make men willing; and he is the conqueror who gets and keeps our own consent.

8. Their ends are contrary, and therefore their ways are also contrary. The devil's end is to draw man to sin and damnation, and to dishonor God; and Christ's end is to draw man from sin to holiness and salvation, and to honor God. But Christ makes known his end, and Satan conceals his end from his followers.

9. There is something within the good and the bad for the contrary party to work upon; and we are, as it were, divided in ourselves, and have something in us that is on both sides. The wicked have an honorable acknowledgment of God, and of their greatest obligation to him; a hatred of the devil; a love of themselves; a willingness to be happy, and an unwillingness to be miserable; and a conscience which approves of more good than they do, and condemns much of their transgression. This is some advantage for the persuasions of the ministers of Christ to work upon; and they have reason capable of knowing more.

The soldiers of Christ have a fleshly appetite, and the remnants of ignorance and error in their minds, and of earthliness, and carnality, and aversion to God in their wills, with a nearness to this world, and much strangeness to the world to come. And here is too much advantage for Satan to work on by his temptations.

10. But it is the predominant part within us, and the aim of our lives, which shows which of the armies we belong to. And thus we must give up our names and hearts to Christ, and engage under his command against the devil, and conquer to the death, if we will be saved. Not to fight against the bare name of the devil; for so will his own soldiers, and spit at his name, and hang a witch who makes a contract with him: but it is to fight against his cause and work, which is by fighting against the world and the flesh, and for the glory of God.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Hebrews 2:10 (flag):** Baxter's "Captain of our salvation" is the King James wording, and his whole figure of two armies leans on "Captain." The NET reads "pioneer of their salvation," which keeps the idea of a leader going ahead but loses the military sense. We follow the NET inside the quotation in item 4, and keep Baxter's "Captain" in the heading and in later sentences, where he uses it without a verse. Note also that the NET has "their," not "our."
- **Leviticus 17:7 and Isaiah 13:21 (flag):** Baxter quotes the King James "devils" in both. The NET has "goat demons" in Leviticus 17:7, which fits his point about the Hebrew word meaning "hairy" or "he-goats," and "wild goats" in Isaiah 13:21, which has no demonic sense in the NET.
- **Hosea 4:12 (flag):** Baxter's "a spirit of fornication" is the King James "spirit of whoredoms." The NET reads "the wind of prostitution blows them astray; they commit spiritual adultery against their God." The Hebrew word can mean wind or spirit; the NET chooses "wind," so Baxter's argument that this verse names a spirit is weaker in the NET. We keep his wording, modernized.
- **Isaiah 19:14 (flag):** Baxter's "a perverse spirit, causing staggering and giddiness as a drunken man" is not in the NET text, which reads "The Lord has made them undiscerning; ... so that she is like a drunk sliding around in his own vomit." We keep his wording as he wrote it.
- **Zechariah 13:2:** NET "the unclean spirit." Baxter has "a spirit of uncleanness."
- **John 8:44:** NET "he is a liar and the father of lies" (Baxter: "the father of it"). His item 18, "the father of the wicked," is cited to the same verse but is not in the NET, which reads "your father the devil." We keep Baxter's words.
- **Acts 17:18 (flag):** Baxter lists this verse as a possible place where the Greek word for "demons" is used in a good or neutral sense. The NET renders the word there as "foreign gods," which supports his point that pagans used it of their own divinities. **1 Timothy 4:1:** NET "demonic teachings."
- **Matthew 12 ("the strong man armed"):** the phrase "strong man armed" is the King James wording of Luke 11:21; Matthew 12:29 has "a strong man" in the NET. Baxter's reference has no verse number, so we kept it as "Matthew 12."
- **Jude 6 (flag):** Baxter's "Angels which kept not their first state" becomes in the NET "the angels who did not keep within their proper domain but abandoned their own place of residence." The NET sense (a place or domain) is a little different from "state," which fits his phrase "fell from his first estate." We kept his words as a modernized quotation; the NET is given here.
- **Acts 16:16 (flag):** Baxter's "a spirit of divination" is the King James wording. The NET reads "a spirit that enabled her to foretell the future by supernatural means." Same point, longer wording. We kept his phrase.
- **2 Corinthians 6:15:** NET "Beliar," not "Belial"; same figure. We kept Baxter's spelling. **Beelzebub:** the NET has "Beelzebul" (Matthew 12:24). Baxter's gloss "the god of flies" is his own, as is his gloss on the Hebrew in Old Testament item 2.
- **2 Corinthians 4:4:** the NET has "the god of this age," where Baxter has "this world." His comment ("because the world obeys him") still fits.
- **Ephesians 2:2:** NET "the ruler of the domain of the air" (Baxter: "the prince of the power of the air"). **Ephesians 6:12:** NET "the world rulers of this darkness" (Baxter: "the ruler of the darkness of this world"); "Principalities and powers" is the King James wording, kept without a verse number, as Baxter has it.
- **Revelation 9:11:** NET "the angel of the abyss, whose name in Hebrew is Abaddon, and in Greek, Apollyon." The NET verse says the locusts "have as king over them the angel of the abyss"; Baxter's wording "king of the locusts" is a summary of that, kept. **Revelation 12:9:** the NET has "huge dragon—the ancient serpent"; Baxter's "the dragon, and the old serpent" is kept as his wording.
- **1 Peter 5:8 and Revelation 12:4:** Baxter's "seeking night and day to devour" is a blend and paraphrase. The NET of 1 Peter 5:8 reads "is on the prowl looking for someone to devour"; "night and day" is not in it. We keep his wording, modernized.
- **Luke 20:36:** NET "equal to angels" (Baxter: "equal with the angels").
- **Corrected references (printed numbers are probably misprints):** "John xii. 21" corrected to John 12:31 (the verse with "ruler of this world"); "2 Cor. iv. 5" to 2 Corinthians 4:4; "Matt. xxiii. 19" to Matthew 13:19 (the verse with "the evil one"). These are our corrections; Baxter's printed numbers are in the source.
- **References that ran into the item numbers:** in the source the list numbers sit right after the reference ("Gen. iii. 2. The Hebrew word"; "Zech. iii. 1. 4."). We read these as the end of a reference followed by the next item's number, and we give Genesis 3 and Matthew 4 as chapter only, since Baxter gives no verse. Genesis 3:1 has "serpent" in the NET; the "tempter" is at Matthew 4:3 in the NET, but we keep Baxter's chapter-only reference.
- **Hebrews 2:14 ("his angels"):** the NET text of this verse speaks of Christ's sharing human flesh and destroying the devil, and does not mention angels. Hebrews 1:14 in the NET speaks of "ministering spirits," which may be what he had in mind, but we have not confirmed this. We keep his reference.
- **Romans 13:14:** Baxter cites this for "all is to please it" (the flesh). The NET reads "make no provision for the flesh to arouse its desires." It is a reference, not a quotation.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): the woman's seed and the enmity (Genesis 3:15); the church and the devil's "synagogue" (possibly compare Revelation 2:9; not confirmed); "seed" of the woman.
- **Greek as printed:** πνευματα ακαθαρτα ("unclean spirits"); δαιμομιον is printed so in the source, probably a misprint for δαιμόνιον ("demon"); δαιμων ("demon"; Baxter links it to a word for knowing); Πειραζων ("tempter," literally "the one testing"); Εχθρος ("enemy"); Διαβολος ("slanderer, accuser"); Ὁ πονηρος ("the evil one"); Απολλυων ("destroyer," Apollyon). Baxter's own English glosses are kept. These renderings are drafts for this project and should be checked.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word then ours): lapsed sinful man to fallen, sinful man; befool to make a fool of; innocency to innocence; preservative to safeguard; Holy Ghost to Holy Spirit; divers to several; cheated into arms to tricked into arms; endeavour to effort; scope (of our lives) to aim; averseness to aversion; heathen kept; worshipped to worshiped; honourable to honorable; fitted thereunto to fitted to it. **to sense** (proofs "to sense") to "to the senses."
- **Technical terms kept:** *efficient* (the cause that makes a thing happen, as opposed to its aim or "end"); *end* (aim or purpose); *matter* (of temptation: what temptation works with); *presupposed* (assumed as a starting point); *synagogue* (his word for the devil's congregation, set against Christ's church).
- **Baxter's polemic and his views:** "hang a witch," and his appeals to apparitions and witchcrafts as proofs of the devil, are Baxter's own words and views, kept as written; we do not assert anything about them.
- **Cross-references not checked:** "chap. 4" (world and flesh), "chap. i." (note 105), and the *Treatise against Infidelity*, Part 3, are as Baxter has them. In note 106, Baxter's "Pool's Synopsis, Critic" is probably Matthew Poole's *Synopsis Criticorum*; we have not confirmed it. "Levit. i. 77" (Leviticus 1, then a number, probably a page) is as printed in the source and is not a verse. The Latin "Vide" is as printed. Baxter's note 104 gives his titles in quotation marks, which we italicize.
- **"the unseen power is theirs"** and similar: left as Baxter wrote.
