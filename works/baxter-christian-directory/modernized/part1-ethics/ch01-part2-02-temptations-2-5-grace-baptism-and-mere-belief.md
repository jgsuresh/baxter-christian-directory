# Chapter 1, Part 2 (continued): Temptations 2–5

**Temptation 2.** If Satan cannot keep the soul in a sleepy, careless, thoughtless forgetfulness, he would make the unregenerate soul believe that there is no such thing as regenerating grace; but that it is an imagined thing which no man has experience of; and he says, as Nicodemus did, "How can these things be?" (John 3:9). He thinks that natural conscience is enough.

**Direction 2.** But this may easily be refuted by observing that holiness is but the very health and uprightness of the soul; and is supernatural in no other sense than health is to one who is born a leper. It is the uprightness of nature, or its disposition to the use and end that it was made for. Though grace is called supernatural, 1. Because it is not born with us; and 2. Corrupted nature is against it; 3. And the end of it is the God of nature, who is above nature; 4. And the revelation and other means are supernatural (as Christ's incarnation, resurrection, etc.): yet nature, and Scripture, and experience all tell you that man is made for another life, and for such works which he is utterly unfit for until grace has changed and renewed him, as it does for many before your eyes. See 2 Corinthians 5:17; Galatians 6:15; Galatians 4:19; John 3:3, 5, 6; Matthew 18:3; 1 Peter 1:23.

**Temptation 3.** But, says the tempter, if supernatural grace is necessary, yet it may be born in you. Infants have no sin; Christ says that "the kingdom of God belongs to such as these" (Mark 10:14); "Abraham is our father!" ... "We have only one Father, God himself" (John 8:39, 41). You are born of Christian parents.

**Direction 3.** See the full proof of original sin in all infants in my *Treatise of the Divine Life*, part 1, chapters 11 and 12. Grace may indeed be put early into nature, but does not come by nature. [42] "Unless a person is born from above, he cannot see the kingdom of God. ... Unless a person is born of water and spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God" (John 3:3, 5). "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; what is old has passed away—look, what is new has come!" (2 Corinthians 5:17). But how pointless it is for him to boast that he was born holy who finds himself at present unholy! Show that you have a holy, heavenly heart and life, and then you are blessed, whenever it was wrought.

**Temptation 4.** But, says the tempter, baptism is the washing of regeneration: you are baptized, and therefore you are regenerated. The ancients taught that all sins were washed away in baptism, and grace conferred.

**Direction 4. Answer.**

The ancients by baptism meant the internal and external acts joined together, the soul's delivering itself up to God in the covenant, and sealing it by baptism (Matthew 28:19–20): and so it includes conversion, and true repentance, and faith: and all that are thus baptized are pardoned, justified, and holy. But those who have only sacramental regeneration, or the external ordinance, are not for that reason in a state of life; for Christ expressly says that "unless a person is born of water and spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God" (John 3:5–6). And Peter told Simon Magus, after he was baptized, that he was "bitterly envious and in bondage to sin" (Acts 8:23). It is not "the washing off of physical dirt but the pledge of a good conscience to God" (1 Peter 3:21). Christ cleanses his church "with the washing of the water by the word" (Ephesians 5:26). But if you had been cleansed in baptism, if at present you are unclean and unholy, can you be saved so?

**Temptation 5.** When this fails, the tempter would persuade them that godliness is nothing but a matter of mere opinion or belief: to believe all the articles of the faith, and to be no papist or heretic, but of true religion, and to be confident of God's mercy through Christ; for "the one who believes and is baptized will be saved" (Mark 16:16).

**Direction 5.** To this you must answer that it will not save a man that his religion is true unless he is true to it. Read James 2 against such a dead faith. Saving faith is the hearty welcome of Christ as our Lord and Savior, and the delivering up of the soul to him to be sanctified and ruled, as well as pardoned. "Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up" (1 Corinthians 8:1). "That servant who knew his master's will but did not get ready or do what his master asked will receive a severe beating" (Luke 12:47). It is a sorry thing that men should think to be saved by that which will condemn them; by being of a right opinion, and wrong conduct; by believing their duty instead of doing it; and then presuming that Christ forgives them, and that their state is good. Opinion and presumption are not faith.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **John 3:3 (important for this passage):** Baxter quotes "Except you be born again," the usual English rendering. The NET reads "unless a person is born from above," a different (and debated) translation of the Greek word. Baxter's argument in Directions 2–4 is about a second birth, and "born from above" keeps the idea of a new origin but loses "again." John 3:5, which Baxter quotes more fully in Direction 4, is "born of water and spirit" in the NET.
- **John 3:9:** NET wording, which matches Baxter's quote. **John 8:39, 41:** Baxter's "Abraham is your Father; yea, God" is an adaptation of what the Jews say in verses 39 and 41. We quote the NET for what they say.
- **Mark 10:14** (Temptation 3): Baxter's "Of such is the kingdom of God" is the King James wording of Christ's saying about children. The reference is not in Baxter's text. The NET reads "the kingdom of God belongs to such as these."
- **Acts 8:23** (Direction 4): the source prints "Acts viii. 13." The words quoted ("gall of bitterness, and bond of iniquity") are from verse 23, which is what Baxter cites in Direction 2, so we cite 8:23. The NET reads "bitterly envious and in bondage to sin."
- **Mark 16:16** (Temptation 5): the NET prints the ending of Mark (16:9–20) in brackets because it is not in the earliest manuscripts. Baxter treats the verse as Scripture without comment.
- **1 Corinthians 8:1** (Direction 5): Baxter's "charity edifieth" is "love builds up" in the NET. **Luke 12:47:** Baxter's "shall be beaten with many stripes" is "will receive a severe beating."
- **1 Peter 3:21; Ephesians 5:26:** NET wording. The King James "laver" and "washing of water by the word" are unmarked in Baxter's text; we kept his phrases as quotations.
- **the washing of regeneration** (Temptation 4): Baxter's "the laver of regeneration," from Titus 3:5. A laver is a washing basin.
- **uprightness:** Baxter's "rectitude." **leper:** left as written; Baxter's point is that holiness is not something outside nature any more than health is outside a person's nature, even if that person was born ill.
- **put early into nature:** Baxter's "put betimes into nature." **pointless:** his "vain." **blessed:** his "happy."
- **hearty welcome:** Baxter's "hearty entertainment." **conduct:** his "conversation." **a sorry thing:** his "sad."
- **papist:** kept as Baxter's word for a Roman Catholic. **sacramental regeneration:** being regenerate by the sacrament (baptism) alone; see the notes to Direction 2.
- ***Treatise of the Divine Life*:** Baxter's own book, where he argues that every infant is born with original sin.
- **"Answ." in Direction 4:** Baxter writes "Answ." (Answer) after the Direction number; we keep it as part of the label.
