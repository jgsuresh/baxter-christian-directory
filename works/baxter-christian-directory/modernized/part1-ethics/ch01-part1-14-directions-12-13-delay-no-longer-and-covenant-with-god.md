# Chapter 1, Part 1: Directions 12 and 13

**Direction 12. When you have thus considered the condition you are in, consider also whether it is a condition to settle in for even one day.**

If you die unconverted, you are past all hope; for out of hell there is no redemption: [31] and you are certain to die before long; and uncertain whether it will be this night (Luke 12:20). You never lay down with assurance that you would rise again; you never went out of doors with assurance of returning; you never heard a sermon with assurance that you would hear another; you never drew one breath with assurance that you would draw another: a thousand accidents and diseases are ready to stop your breath, and end your time, when God will have it so. And if you die this night in an unregenerate state, there is no more time, or help, or hope. And is this a case then for a wise man to continue in for a day, who can do anything toward his own recovery? Should you delay another day or hour before you fall down at the feet of Christ, and cry for mercy, and return to God, and resolve upon a better course? May I not well say to you, as the angels to Lot, "Get going! ... or else you will be destroyed ... Run for your lives! Don't look behind you" (Genesis 19:15, 17, 22).

**Direction 13. When you are resolved, past your waverings and delays, give yourself entirely and unreservedly to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, as your happiness, your Savior, and your Sanctifier, in a hearty consent to the covenant of grace.**

This is your Christianity; your betrothal to Christ. It is sacramentally done in baptism; but until it is personally owned, and heartily renewed by men of age, they have no reason to be numbered with adult believers, nor to dream of a part in the blessings of the covenant. It is a pity it is not made a more serious, solemn work for men thus to renew their covenant with God. (For which I have written a *Treatise of Confirmation*, but so far in vain.) However, do it seriously yourself: it is the greatest and weightiest action of your life.

To this end, study carefully the covenant of grace which is offered to you in the gospel: understand it well. In it God offers, in spite of your sins, to be your reconciled God and Father in Christ, and to accept you as a son, and an heir of heaven.

The Son offers to be your Savior, to justify you by his blood and grace, and teach you, and govern you as your Head, in order to your everlasting happiness. The Holy Spirit offers to be your Sanctifier, Comforter, and Guide, to overcome all the enmity of the devil, the world, and the flesh, in order to the full accomplishment of your salvation; nothing is expected of you, in order to your title to the benefits of this covenant, but deliberately, sincerely, entirely to consent to it, and to continue that consent, and perform what you consent to perform, and that by the help of the grace which will be given you. See, therefore, that you deliberate well on the matter, but without delays; and count what you shall gain or lose by it. And if you find that you are likely to be a loser in the end, and know of any better way, by all means take it, and boast of it when you have tried the end; but if you are past doubt that there is no way but this, carry it out resolutely and seriously.

And take care of one thing, lest you say, Why, this is no more than everybody knows, and than I have done a hundred times, to give myself up in covenant to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Do you know it, and yet have you not done it? Or have you done it with your lips, and not sincerely with your heart? Lament it as one of your greatest sins that you have thus provokingly dallied with God; and marvel at his mercy that he will yet be willing to enter into covenant with one who has hypocritically profaned his covenant. If you had ever seriously thus covenanted and given yourself up to God, you would not have neglected him by an ungodly life, nor lived afterward to the devil, the world, and the flesh, which were renounced. I tell you, the making of this Christian vow and covenant with God in Christ is the act of greatest consequence of any in all your life, and to be done with the greatest judgment, and reverence, and sincerity, and foresight, and firm resolution of anything that you ever do. And if it were done sincerely by all who do it ignorantly, for show, only with the lips, then all professed Christians would be saved; whereas now, the abusers of that holy name and covenant will have the deepest place in hell. Write it out on your heart, and put your heart and hand to it resolutely, and stand by your consent, and all is your own: conversion is wrought when this is done.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Genesis 19:15, 17, 22** (Direction 12): Baxter's quotation, "Arise, lest thou be consumed: escape for thy life; look not behind thee," is a blend of the angels' words to Lot in verses 15 and 17. We use the NET wording for the two halves, with "..." between them: "Get going! Take your wife and your two daughters who are here, or else you will be destroyed" (v. 15) and "Run for your lives! Don't look behind you or stop anywhere in the valley! Escape to the mountains or you will be destroyed!" (v. 17). Verse 22 is in Baxter's citation but not in his quotation.
- **Luke 12:20** (Direction 12): cited only. It is the parable of the rich fool, where God says, "This very night your life will be demanded back from you" (NET).
- **settle in for even one day** (Direction 12 heading): Baxter's "to be rested in one day," meaning to be content and settle down in.
- **betrothal** (Direction 13): Baxter's "espousals," the engagement stage before marriage. Baxter treats the covenant with Christ as a personal commitment, made first in baptism and then renewed by the person as an adult.
- **men of age** (Direction 13): Baxter's "men at age," meaning grown men, those of adult age. **adult believers:** left as written.
- ***Treatise of Confirmation*** (Direction 13): Baxter's own book, in which he argued for a serious ceremony by which baptized people, once grown, personally take on the covenant. He says here that it has so far had no effect.
- **title** (Direction 13): kept in its legal sense of right or claim. **unfeignedly → sincerely.**
- **marvel at** (Direction 13): Baxter's "admire," meaning marvel at. **be willing:** Baxter's "vouchsafe." **for show:** Baxter's "for fashion."
- **dallied with God:** trifled with him. Left as written.
