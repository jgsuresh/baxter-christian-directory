# Chapter 5: Title 2, contemplative directions for redeeming opportunity (Directions 1-10)

**Title 2. Contemplative directions for redeeming opportunity.**

Opportunity or season is the flower of time. All time is precious; but the season is most precious. The present time is the season for works of present necessity: and for others, they have all their particular seasons, which must not be let slip. [284]

**Direction 1. Remember that it is the great difference between the happy saint and the unhappy world, that one is wise in time, and the other is wise too late.**

The godly know while knowledge will do good: the wicked know when knowledge will only torment them. All those that you see now so exceedingly contrary in their judgment to the godly will be of the very same opinion shortly, when it will do them no good. Bear with their difference and contradiction, for it will be only a very little while. There is not one man who now is the furious enemy of holiness, but will confess before long that holiness was best. Do they now despise it as tedious, fantastical hypocrisy? They will shortly know that it was only the cure of a distracted mind, and the necessary duty to God, which religion and right reason command. Do they now say of sin, What harm is in it? They will shortly know that it is the poison of the soul, and worse than any misery or death. They will think more highly of the worth of Christ, of the necessity of all possible diligence for our souls, of the preciousness of time, of the wisdom of the godly, of the excellencies of heaven, and of the word of God and all holy means, than any of those do who are now reproached by them for being of this mind. But how much the better will they be for this? No more than Adam for knowing good and evil. No more than it will profit a man when he is dead to know of what disease he died. No more than it will profit a man to know what is poison, when he has taken it, and is past remedy. The thief will be wise at the gallows; and the spendthrift prodigal when all is gone. But those who would be safe and happy must be wise in time. The godly know the worth of heaven, before it is lost; and the misery of damnation, before they feel it; and the necessity of a Savior, while he is willing to be a Savior to them; and the evil of sin, before it has undone them; and the preciousness of time, before it is gone; and the worth of mercy, while mercy may be had; and the need of praying, while praying may prevail. They do not sleep till the door is shut, and then knock and cry, Lord, open to us, as the foolish ones (Matthew 25). They are not like the miserable world, which will not believe till they come where devils believe and tremble; nor repent till torment forces them to repent. If ever you would escape the dearly bought experience of fools, be wise in time; and do not leave conscience to answer all your cries, and moans, and fruitless wishes, with this doleful peal, Too late! too late! Only know now by an effectual faith what wicked men will know by feeling and experience, when it is too late, and you will not perish. Only live now as those enemies of holiness will wish that they had lived when it is too late, and you will be happy. Now God may be found: "Seek the Lord while he makes himself available; call to him while he is nearby! The wicked need to abandon their lifestyle and sinful people their plans. They should return to the Lord, and he will show mercy to them, and to their God, for he will freely forgive them" (Isaiah 55:6–7). Only read the doleful lamentation of Christ over Jerusalem (Luke 19:41–42); and then consider what it is to neglect the season of mercy and salvation: "...saw the city, he wept over it, saying, 'If you had only known on this day, even you, the things that make for peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes.'"

**Direction 2. Remember that the neglecting of the season is the frustrating and destroying of the work.**

When the season is past, the work cannot be done. If you do not sow in the time of sowing, it will be in vain at another time. If you do not reap, and do not gather in harvest, it will be too late in winter to hope for fruit. If you wait till the tide is gone, or do not take the wind that serves your purpose, it may be in vain to attempt your voyage. Not all works can be done at all times: Christ himself says, "We must perform the deeds of the one who sent me as long as it is daytime. Night is coming when no one can work" (John 9:4). Do not say then, The next day may serve the purpose: the next day is for another work; and you must do both.

**Direction 3. Consider that even if the work should not be impossible, yet it will be difficult out of season; when in its season it might be done with ease.**

How easily may you swim with the tide; and sail with the wind; and form the iron if you hammer it while it is hot! How easily may many a disease be cured, if it is taken in time, which afterwards is incurable! How easily may you bend a tender twig, and pluck up a plant, which will neither be plucked up nor bent when it is grown up to be a tree! When you complain of difficulties in religion, consider whether your loss of the fittest season, and acquainting yourselves no sooner with God, is not the cause?

**Direction 4. Consider that your work out of season is not so good or acceptable, even if you could do it.**

[285]

"...everything fit beautifully in its appropriate time" (Ecclesiastes 3:11). To speak a "word in season to the weary" is the skill of the faithful messengers of peace (Isaiah 50:4). When out of season good may be turned into evil. Who will thank you for giving medicine, or food, or clothing to the dead? or pitying the poor when it is too late? In time all this may be accepted.

**Direction 5. Remember that if you omit the season, you are left to uncertainties both for time, and means, and grace.**

Lose this time, and for all you know you lose all. Or if you have time, it may be cursed with barrenness, and never again may fruit grow on it. Preachers may be taken from you; and gracious company may be taken from you: helps and means may be turned into hindrances, and opposition, and strong temptations: and then you will find what it was to neglect the season! Or if you have the continuance of all helps and means, how do you know that God will step in by his grace, and bless them to you, and move your hearts? He may resolve that if you resist him now, his Spirit shall strive with you no more. If while it is called today you will harden your hearts, he may resolve to leave you to the hardness of Pharaoh, and to get himself a name upon you, and use you as vessels of wrath, prepared by your neglect and obstinacy for destruction.

**Direction 6. Think how all the creatures keep their proper seasons, in the service which God has appointed them for you.**

[286]

The sun rises and sets in its season, and keeps its daily and annual course, and does not miss a minute. So do the celestial motions. You have day and night, and seedtime and harvest, summer and winter, spring and fall, and all exactly in their seasons. "Even the stork knows when it is time to move on. The turtledove, swallow, and crane recognize the normal times for their migration. But my people pay no attention to what I, the Lord, require of them" (Jeremiah 8:7). Shall only man neglect his season?

**Direction 7. Consider how you know and observe the season for your worldly labors, and should you not much more do so in greater things?**

You will not plow when you should reap; nor do the work of the summer in the winter. You will not lie in bed all day, and go about your business in the night. You will be inquisitive, that you may be skillful in the seasons, for your benefit or safety in the world; and should you not much more be so for a better world? "O you hypocrites!" "You know how to judge correctly the appearance of the sky, but you cannot evaluate the signs of the times" (Matthew 16:3). As at harvest you look for the fruit of your land, so does God in season expect fruit from you (Mark 12:2; Luke 20:10). The "godly" are "like a tree planted by flowing streams; it yields its fruit at the proper time" (Psalm 1:3). Shall worldlings know their season, and shall we not?

**Direction 8. Consider how vigilant the wicked are to know and take their season to do evil.**

And how much more should we be so in doing good! Seducers will take the opportunity to deceive. The thief and the adulterer will take the season of secrecy and darkness. The ambitious and greedy will take the season for profit and promotion. The malicious watch their seasons of revenge. And have we not more need and more encouragement than they? Is it time for them to be building their own houses, and growing great by greed and oppression, and is it not time for you to be honoring God, and providing for endless life? [287] They "cannot sleep unless they cause harm" (Proverbs 4:16); and can you sleep securely while your time passes away, and your work is undone?

**Direction 9. Remember that the devil watches the season of temptation to destroy you.**

He prevails much by taking the time; when he sees you disarmed, forgetting God, in secure prosperity, fittest to listen to his temptations. The same temptations out of season might not prevail. And will you let your enemy outdo you?

**Direction 10. Consider how earnest you are with God in your necessities and distress, not only to relieve and help you, but to do it speedily and in season.**

[288]

You would rather have him come before the season than let it pass. You are impatient till deliverance comes, and can hardly wait for the time till it is ripe. When you are in pain and sickness, you would be delivered speedily: you are ready to cry, "How long, Lord, how long?" [289] And as David, "...it is time ... the appointed time has come" (Psalm 102:13). "O my God, do not delay" (Psalm 40:17). It would not satisfy you if God should say, I will ease you of your pain the next year. Why then should you neglect the time of duty, and use so many delays with God? He gives you all your mercies in their season; why then do you not in season give yourselves up to his love and service? when you have his promise that "in due time we will reap, if we do not give up" (Galatians 6:9).

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," meaning a heading or main division of the chapter. Chapter 5 has five such Titles; this file covers the start of Title 2. Baxter's heading reads "Directions contemplative for redeeming Opportunity"; we put the adjective first. The Direction numbers restart at 1 here, as in the source.
- **Bold heading sentences:** as in the finished files, the first sentence of each Direction is set in bold. In Direction 8 that is only the first sentence; the exclamation "And how much more should we be so in doing good!" is Baxter's second sentence.
- **Isaiah 55:6–7 (flag):** Baxter introduces the quotation with "Now God may be found," leaning on the King James "Seek ye the Lord while he may be found." The NET reads "while he makes himself available," so the echo between his lead-in and the verse is weaker. The NET also has "their God" where the King James has "our God," and "freely forgive" for "abundantly pardon."
- **Luke 19:41–42:** Baxter starts his quotation at "He beheld the city." The NET verse begins "Now when Jesus approached and saw the city," so we begin at "...saw the city." The King James "at least in this thy day" (which suits Baxter's point about the season) is "on this day" in the NET; "the things which belong unto thy peace" is "the things that make for peace."
- **John 9:4 (flag):** Baxter says "Christ himself saith, 'I must work while it is day,'" and his point rests on Christ speaking of himself. The NET follows a reading with "We must perform the deeds of the one who sent me," so the verse no longer reads as Christ speaking only of himself.
- **Ecclesiastes 3:11 (flag):** Baxter's "Every thing is beautiful in its season" becomes the NET "...everything fit beautifully in its appropriate time" (the verse begins "God has made"). The NET's "appropriate time" still carries his point about seasons.
- **Isaiah 50:4 (flag and correction):** the source prints "Isa. 1. 4," almost certainly "Isa. l. 4" (50:4) with the Roman numeral l misread as the digit 1; Isaiah 1:4 has nothing like these words. We cite Isaiah 50:4. Baxter's phrase "word in season to the weary" (King James: "to speak a word in season to him that is weary") is not in the NET, which reads "so that I know how to help the weary." Because the phrase "in season" is exactly what Baxter is using, we kept his wording in his quotation marks.
- **Jeremiah 8:7 (flag):** Baxter's "the stork in the heavens knoweth her appointed time" becomes the NET "Even the stork knows when it is time to move on" ("in the heavens" is not in the NET wording), and "know not the judgment of the Lord" becomes "pay no attention to what I, the Lord, require of them." The NET also prints this as poetry; we added the spaces the API run-together text lacked.
- **Matthew 16:3 (flag):** Baxter's words "O ye hypocrites!" (King James) are not in the NET verse, so we kept them as his, in their own quotation marks, followed by the NET wording. The NET also turns Baxter's question ("can ye not discern...?") into a statement ("you cannot evaluate the signs of the times").
- **Psalm 1:3:** NET wording for the part Baxter quotes. Baxter's "godly" in quotation marks is his own word for the man of Psalm 1; the word is not in verse 3. Baxter's "by the river's side" (King James "rivers of water") is "by flowing streams" in the NET.
- **Proverbs 4:16:** NET wording. "do evil" (King James "done mischief") is "cause harm."
- **Psalm 102:13 (flag):** Baxter's "The time, yea, the set time is come" is rendered from the NET "For it is time to have mercy on her, for the appointed time has come," shortened with ellipses to the extent he quotes. Baxter introduces it "as David"; the NET heading of Psalm 102 calls it "the prayer of an oppressed man" and does not name David. We kept Baxter's attribution as his.
- **Psalm 40:17:** Baxter's "Make no longer tarrying, O my God!" is the NET "O my God, do not delay." Psalm 40's heading does name David.
- **Galatians 6:9 (flag):** Baxter writes "that you shall 'reap in due season if you do not faint,'" turning Paul's "we" into "you." We give the NET wording, "in due time we will reap, if we do not give up," and dropped Baxter's "you shall," which the NET's own verb replaces. "In due season" (Baxter's theme word) is "in due time" in the NET.
- **"How long, Lord, how long?"** (Direction 10): no reference in Baxter's text. It echoes the Psalms, for example Psalm 6:3 and 13:1 (our suggestion, not in Baxter's text). Kept in his wording.
- **Matthew 25** (Direction 1): Baxter's "Lord, open to us" is not set as a quotation in the source, so we kept his wording; the NET of Matthew 25:11 reads "Lord, lord! Let us in!" Mark 12:2 and Luke 20:10 are cited only.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "devils believe and tremble" (James 2:19); "his Spirit shall strive with you no more" (Genesis 6:3); "while it is called today ... harden your hearts" (Hebrews 3:13, 15; Psalm 95:7–8); "the hardness of Pharaoh" and "to get himself a name upon you" (Exodus 9:16; 14:4, 17); "vessels of wrath, prepared ... for destruction" (Romans 9:22); "Adam ... knowing good and evil" (Genesis 3:22); "seedtime and harvest, summer and winter" (Genesis 8:22); "building their own houses" (Haggai 1:4, as his note 287 shows).
- **Word swaps:** "will but torment" to "will only torment" (and similar "but" = only); "ere long" to "before long"; "dear-bought" to "dearly bought"; "As ever you would escape" to "If ever you would escape"; "Do but know ... Do but live ... Read but" to "Only know ... Only live ... Only read"; "bethink you" to "consider" or "think"; "stay till the tide is gone" to "wait till the tide is gone"; "the wind that fits your turn" to "the wind that serves your purpose," and "serve the turn" to "serve the purpose"; "All works cannot be done at all times" to "Not all works can be done at all times" (the modern way to say what Baxter means); "if the work should not be impossible" and "if you could do it" to "even if..." (the sense in both places); "physic" to "medicine"; "for aught thou knowest" to "for all you know"; "set in by his grace" to "step in by his grace"; "diary ... course" to "daily ... course"; "preferment" to "promotion"; "covetous(ness)" to "greedy / greed"; "hearken" to "listen"; "prevent the season" to "come before the season" (prevent in its older sense, go before); "stay the time" to "wait for the time"; "give up yourselves" to "give yourselves up."
- **Kept words:** "fantastical" (fanciful, imaginary); "distracted mind" (in Baxter's day, a deranged or disordered mind, not merely an inattentive one); "doleful" (mournful); "effectual faith" (a faith that takes effect); "religion" (worship and practiced piety; see the glossary); "Seducers" (people who lead others astray, especially into error; not only in the sexual sense); "sleep securely" and "secure prosperity" (security here is Baxter's term for careless, false confidence; see the earlier notes); "get himself a name upon you" (win glory for himself through his dealings with you, as with Pharaoh); "the celestial motions" (the movements of the heavenly bodies).
- **aggravations** (note 284): in Baxter's sense, the circumstances that make a sin worse. The book title is printed here as "Directions for Sound Conversion"; elsewhere in this project it is given as *Directions for a Sound Conversion*. We kept the form printed here.
- **as the foolish ones** (Direction 1): Baxter means the foolish virgins of the parable; left as written.
- **"There is not one man ... but will confess"** (Direction 1): the older construction meaning "who will not confess"; left as written.
