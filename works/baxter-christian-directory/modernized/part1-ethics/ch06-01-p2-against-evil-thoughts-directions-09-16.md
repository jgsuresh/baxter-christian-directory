# Chapter 6: Title 1, Directions 9–16 (against evil and idle thoughts, continued)

**Direction 9. Remember God's continual presence; that all your thoughts are in his sight.**

He sees every filthy thought, and every greedy, and proud, and ambitious thought, and every uncharitable, malicious thought. If you are not atheists, the remembrance of this will somewhat check and control your thoughts, that God sees them. He understands your "motives" "even from far away" (Psalm 139:2). "Won't the one who evaluates hearts discern it?" (Proverbs 24:12). "Why do you respond with evil in your hearts?" says Christ (Matthew 9:4).

**Direction 10. Think seriously what a government you would keep over your thoughts, if they were only written on your foreheads, or seen by all that see you, yes, or only open to some person whom you revere.**

Oh how ashamed would you then be, that men should see your filthy thoughts, your malicious thoughts, your greedy and deceiving thoughts! And is not the eye of God ten thousand times more to be revered and regarded? And is not man your god, if you are awed more by man than by God, and if the eye of man can do more to restrain you?

**Direction 11. Keep your consciences tender, that they may not be regardless or insensible of the smallest sin.**

A tender conscience fears evil and idle thoughts; and will smart in the penitent review of thoughts; but a seared conscience feels nothing, except some grievous, crying sins. A tender conscience obeys that precept, Proverbs 30:32, "If you have done foolishly by exalting yourself or if you have planned evil, put your hand over your mouth!"

**Direction 12. Cast out empty and sinful thoughts in the beginning, before they settle themselves and make a dwelling of your heart.**

They are most easily and most safely resisted at the entrance. Your heart will give them rooting and grow familiar with them, if they make any stay. Besides, it shows the greater sin, because there is the less resistance, and the more consent. If the will were against them, it would not let them alone so long. Yes, and their continuance tends to your ruin; it is like the continuance of poison in your bowels, or fire in your thatch, or a spy in an army: as long as they stay they are working toward your greater harm. If these flies stay long they will lay their eggs and multiply; they will make their nests, and breed their young, and you will quickly have a swarm of sins.

**Direction 13. Be careful that no practical error corrupts your understandings; or that you are not engaged in any evil design: for these will command your thoughts into a course of sinful attendance and service to their ends.**

He that errs and thinks his sin is his virtue or his duty will indulge the thoughts of it without control; yes, he will drive on his mind to such cogitations; and steal from the authority and word of God the motives and incentives of his sin. As false prophets speak against God in the name of God, and against his word as by the alleged authority of his word; so an erring mind will fetch its arguments from God and from the Scripture, for those sinful thoughts which are against God and Scripture. And if evil thoughts will be kept out only with such difficulty when we plead the authority of God and his word against them, and do the best we can to hinder them; how will they prevail when you plead the authority of God and the sacred Scriptures for them, and take it to be your duty to kindle and promote them! For instance; all the sinful thoughts by which the Romish clergy are contriving the support of their kingdom of darkness in the world, and the continuance of their tyranny in the church, are but the products of their error, which tells them that all this should be done, as pleasing to God, and profitable to the church. All the bloody thoughts of persecutors against the church and holy ways of Christ have been cherished by this erroneous thought. John 16:2–3, "A time is coming when the one who kills you will think he is offering service to God. They will do these things because they have not known the Father or me." All Paul's bloody contrivances and practices against the church did come from this. Acts 26:9–10, "Of course, I myself was convinced that it was necessary to do many things hostile to the name of Jesus the Nazarene. And that is what I did." All the scornful and reproachful thoughts and speeches of many of the ungodly against a holy life are from this: 1 Peter 4:4, "So they are astonished when you do not rush with them into the same flood of wickedness, and they vilify you." The empty babbling of hypocrites, who cheat their souls with idle lip-labor, instead of the spiritual service from the heart, and the sacrifice of fools, who offer God some outward thing, while they deny him their hearts and holy obedience, do proceed from this, that "they think that by their many words they will be heard" (Matthew 6:7), "for they do not realize that they are doing wrong" (Ecclesiastes 5:1). All the self-flattery and presumption of the ungodly, and consequently all their ungodly lives, are much from their erroneous thoughts: "For if anyone thinks he is something when he is nothing, he deceives himself" (Galatians 6:3). O come into the light, and forsake your darkness! for sinful thoughts are like hobgoblins and hags, that fly from the light; and like worms and serpents, that creep into holes, and crawl and breed in the dark.

**Direction 14. Remember what an opening of thoughts there will be, when you come into the light, either here by conviction, or at the latest at the day of judgment.**

Then you will be ashamed to see what filth and emptiness you entertained; and with what dross and rubbish you stuffed your minds. When the light comes in, what abundance of things will you see to your astonishment, in the dungeon of your hearts, which now you take no notice of! Remember that all your hidden thoughts must one day be brought into the open light. Do not say that this is a thing impossible, because they are so numerous: for God who sees them all at once, and causes his sun to illuminate so many millions at once, can make you see them all at once, and yet distinctly, and see the shame and filthiness of every one of them.

**Direction 15. When you find that some thoughts of sin and emptiness are following you still, for all that you can do, you must not therefore plunge your souls into so much anxiety, fear, and trouble, as may discourage and distract your mind; but wait on God in the complacential and obediential way of cure.**

It is the tempter's method to keep sinners utterly careless of their thoughts, and unaware of any sin that is in them, as long as he can; and when that hope fails him, he will labor to make a humble, obedient soul so aware of the sin of his thoughts, and so anxious about them, as to confound him, and cast him into melancholy, discouragement, and despair; and then he will have no command of his thoughts at all; but they will be as much ungoverned another way, and feed continually upon terror. The end of this temptation is to distract you and confound you. The pretext of the tempter will be contrary to his end: for while he drives you with terrors to think of nothing else but what you have been or are thinking on, and to make your own thoughts the only or principal matter of your thoughts, he will confound you, and make you indisposed to all good, and unable to govern your thoughts at all. But if you principally study the excellencies of God and godliness, and take the course which tends to make religion pleasant to you, and at the same time keep up a reverent obedience to God, this complacential obedience will best prevail.

**Direction 16. Therefore deliver up your hearts to Christ in love and duty, and consecrate your thoughts entirely to his service, and keep them still exercised on him, or in his work: and this will most effectually cure them of emptiness and sin. [301]**

If you have a friend that you love entirely, you will not feed swine in the room that must entertain him; you will not leave it nasty and unclean; you will not leave it open to every dirty, unsuitable companion, to intrude at pleasure and disturb your friend. So love and pleasure will be readily and calmly careful to keep clean the heart, and shut out empty and filthy thoughts, and say, This room is for a better guest; nothing shall come here which my Lord abhors: is he willing so wonderfully to condescend, as to take up so lowly a dwelling, and shall I cramp him, or offend him, by letting in his loathsome enemies? Will he dwell in my heart, and shall I allow thoughts of pride, or lust, or malice, to dwell with him, or to enter in? Are these fit companions for the Spirit of grace? Do I delight to grieve him? I know as soon as ever they come in, he will either resist them till he drives them out again, or he will go out himself. And shall I drive away so dear a Friend, for the love of a filthy, pernicious enemy? Or do I delight in war? Would I have a continual combat in my heart? Shall I put the Spirit of Christ to fight for his dwelling, against such an ignominious foe? Indeed there is no true cure for sinful, empty, unprofitable thoughts, but by the contrary; by calling up the thoughts to their proper work, and finding them more profitable employment: and this is by consecrating the heart and them entirely to the love and service of him that has by the wonders of his love, and by the astonishing design of his purchase and merits, so well deserved them. Let Christ come in, and deliver him the key, and ask him to keep your heart as his own, and he will cast out buyers and sellers from his temple, and will not allow his house of prayer to be a den of thieves. But if you receive Christ with reserves, and keep up designs for the world and flesh, do not be surprised if Christ will be no partner with them, but leaves all to those guests, which you would not leave for him.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source. A draft English translation follows the Latin in parentheses; the translation was prepared for this project and is not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check it before relying on fine points.

[301] Sicut ignis in aqua durare non potest, ita neque turpis cogitatio in Dei amante: quoniam omnis qui Dei amator est, etiam laboris amans est: cæterum labor voluntarius, naturaliter voluptati inimicus existit. Marcus Erem. (As fire cannot last in water, so neither can a shameful thought last in one who loves God: for everyone who is a lover of God is also a lover of labor; and voluntary labor is by nature an enemy of pleasure. Mark the Hermit.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** Chapter 6 is divided into six "Titles" (Baxter's abbreviation *Tit.*), meaning headed divisions. This file continues Title 1, "Directions against evil and idle thoughts," beginning at the start of Direction 9 and running to the end of Direction 16, the last Direction of Title 1. Title 2 (directions to furnish the mind with good thoughts) follows in the source.
- **Psalm 139:2 (flagged):** Baxter's King James reads "thou understandest my thought afar off," and he turns it to "He understandeth your thoughts afar off," with only "He understandeth" and "thoughts afar off" in quotation marks. We keep his sentence order and quote only the NET's phrases ("motives," "even from far away"); "He understands" is left outside quotation marks because the NET reads "you understand." The NET has "motives" where the King James has "thought"; since Baxter's whole Direction is about thoughts, the NET word narrows the point he leans on.
- **Proverbs 24:12:** NET wording. Baxter's King James is "Doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it?"
- **Matthew 9:4 (flagged):** Baxter's King James reads "Wherefore think ye evil in your hearts?" The NET reads "Why do you respond with evil in your hearts?" The verb "think," which is Baxter's point, becomes "respond"; the NET keeps the idea of thoughts earlier in the verse ("When Jesus perceived their thoughts").
- **Proverbs 30:32 (flagged):** NET wording. The King James "if thou hast thought evil" becomes "if you have planned evil." Baxter cites the verse as a precept about evil thoughts; the NET's "planned" is narrower than "thought."
- **John 16:2–3 (reference corrected):** the source prints "John xvi. 23," which does not contain these words; the quotation is from John 16:2–3. NET wording, beginning partway through verse 2 as Baxter's quotation does. Baxter's text has "whoever killeth you, will think that he doth God service," and the NET keeps "think," so his point about erroneous thoughts stands.
- **Acts 26:9–10:** Baxter's quotation runs into the first words of verse 10 ("which thing I also did"), so we cite 26:9–10 and quote the NET through "And that is what I did," stopping where he stops (the NET goes on "in Jerusalem"). The King James "I verily thought with myself" is the NET "I myself was convinced"; the idea of a mistaken belief, which Baxter needs, survives, though the word "thought" is lost.
- **1 Peter 4:4 (flagged):** NET wording. The King James "they think it strange" is the NET "they are astonished." Baxter places the verse among "scornful and reproachful thoughts"; the NET loses the word "think." The King James "excess of riot" is the NET "the same flood of wickedness."
- **Matthew 6:7; Ecclesiastes 5:1:** NET wording. Baxter runs the two together ("they think to be heard for their much babbling ... and they consider not that they do evil"); we keep each in its own quotation marks and give each its reference in parentheses (in the source the references sit between the two quotations). The NET numbers Ecclesiastes 5:1 also as 4:17 (the Hebrew numbering). The NET of Ecclesiastes 5:1 begins "for they do not realize," which we keep in the quotation; Baxter's King James joins it with "and." His phrase "the sacrifice of fools" earlier in the sentence comes from the same verse (King James wording, kept).
- **Galatians 6:3:** NET wording. The NET begins "For if anyone," where Baxter's King James has "He that thinketh"; we kept the NET opening.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "a seared conscience" (1 Timothy 4:2); "come into the light ... forsake your darkness" (compare John 3:19–21); hidden thoughts "brought into the open light" (compare 1 Corinthians 4:5; Luke 12:2–3); "causes his sun" (Matthew 5:45); "grieve him," the Spirit (Ephesians 4:30); "cast out buyers and sellers from his temple ... his house of prayer ... a den of thieves" (Matthew 21:12–13).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): covetous → greedy; beholdeth → sees; bethink you → think; upon (your thoughts) → over; reverence (verb) → revere; vain/vanity → empty/emptiness; easiliest and safeliest → most easily and most safely; mischief → harm; blow (of flies: lay eggs) → lay their eggs; take heed lest → be careful that ... not; ill design → evil design; pretended → alleged; "so hardly be kept out" → "be kept out only with such difficulty"; hence → from this; gender → breed; at the furthest → at the latest; solicitousness → anxiety; senseless of → unaware of; sensible of → aware of; careful (full of care) → anxious; pretence → pretext; undisposed → indisposed; withal → at the same time; awful (full of awe) → reverent; common → open; composedly → calmly; mean habitation → lowly dwelling; straiten → cramp; noisome → loathsome; suffer → allow; habitation → dwelling; strange (extraordinary) → astonishing; pray him → ask him; marvel not → do not be surprised; partners → partner.
- **government** (Direction 10): kept, in the sense of rule or control, as in the chapter's title, "Directions for the Government of the Thoughts."
- **complacential and obediential** (Direction 15): kept as Baxter's technical terms. "Complacential" here means of delight or loving pleasure in what is good (not "complacent" in the modern sense); "obediential" means of obedience. His "complacential obedience" is obedience that comes from delight in God, as against obedience driven by terror.
- **melancholy** (Direction 15): kept; see GLOSSARY.md. Baxter means a lasting low, fearful, or brooding state; we do not substitute a modern diagnosis.
- **crying sins** (Direction 11): sins that "cry to heaven" for judgment, a set phrase of the period; kept.
- **cogitations; dross; hobgoblins and hags; ignominious; pernicious; smart:** left as written. "Hags" here means witches or evil spirits of the night, not old women.
- **Romish** (Direction 13): Baxter's word for the Roman Catholic clergy, kept as written; the "kingdom of darkness" and "tyranny" passage is his polemic against Rome, reproduced without softening.
- **design** (Directions 13 and 16): a scheme or plan; kept. In Direction 16 "the strange design of his purchase" means Christ's extraordinary plan of redemption ("purchase").
- **Mark the Hermit** (note 301): "Marcus Erem." is probably Mark the Hermit (also called Mark the Ascetic), an early monastic writer; we have not confirmed which work Baxter quotes.
