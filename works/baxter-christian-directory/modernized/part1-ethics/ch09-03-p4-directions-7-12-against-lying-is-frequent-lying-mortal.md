# Chapter 9: Title 3, Directions 7-12 against lying, and whether frequent lying proves a graceless state

**Direction 7. Learn to trust God, if you would not be liars.**

[515]

For lying is the practice of him that thinks he must provide and shift for himself. Even Abraham's and Isaac's equivocation (saying their wives were their sisters) and David's feigning madness proceeded from some distrust in God: they would not have thought it necessary to shift so for their lives, if they had fully trusted God with their lives. Gehazi's greed and lying both proceeded from a lack of confidence in God. If a man were confident of God's protection, and that he had better abide by God's choice in all things than his own, what use could he think he has for lying, or for any sinful shift?

**Direction 8. Do not be too credulous about bad reports, if you would not be liars.**

Malice is so mad, and so unconscionable a sin, and the tongues of men are commonly so careless of what they say, that if you easily believe evil, you only easily believe the devil, and thereby make yourselves his servants in spreading malicious lies. You think because they are spoken by many, and spoken confidently, you may lawfully believe or report what you hear. But this is only to think that the commonness of liars, and their malice and impudence, will warrant you to follow them, even because they are so bad. Will you bark and bite because dogs do so? If a man is stung by an adder, you should help to cure him, and not desire yourselves to sting him: selfish, and self-interested, and malicious, and partial, factious persons are so commonly liars, and impudent in their lies, that it is necessary for you, if you would not be liars yourselves, to beware of reporting anything they say. These spiders will weave a web of the air, or out of their own bowels. [516]

**Direction 9. Do not be rash in speaking things before you have tested them.**

Consider what you say, and know before you speak. Is it not a shame when you have spoken falsely, to come off with saying, I thought it was true? But why will you speak on supposition, and not wait till you better understood the case? If the matter required such haste in speaking, you should have said no more than, I think it is so. "Examine all things," and then "hold fast to what is good," and assert what is true. Says Cicero, *De Natura Deorum*, book 1, *Nihil est temeritate turpius, nec quicquam tam indignum sapientis gravitate aut constantia, quam out falsum sentire, aut quod non satis explorate perceptum sit et cognitum, sine ulla dubitatione defendere*: Nothing is more unseemly than rashness: nor anything so unworthy of the gravity or constancy of a wise man, than either to hold a falsehood, or confidently to defend that which is not received and known on sufficient testing. [517]

**Direction 10. Foresee what is likely to entrap you in a lie, that you may prevent it.**

Do not let the occasion and temptation surprise you unprepared. Foresight will make the temptation easy to overcome, which unforeseen will be too strong for you.

**Direction 11. Get a tender conscience, and walk as in the sight and hearing of God, and as one that is passing to his judgment.**

[518]

A seared conscience dares venture on lies or anything; but the fear of God is the soul's preservative. What makes men lie, but thinking they have to do with none but men? For they think by a lie to deceive a man, and hide the truth; but if they remembered that they have most to do with God, and that he is always present who cannot be deceived, and that his judgment will bring all secret things to light, and expose all their lies before all the world, they would not hire a torn and dirty cloak at so dear a price, for so short a time. No wonder if men are liars that do not fear God, and do not believe the day of judgment.

**Direction 12. To save others from lying as well as yourselves, be sure to watch against it in your children, and wisely help them to see the evil of it.**

For children are very prone to it; and unwise correction frightens them into lies to save themselves, as indulgence and connivance encourage them to it. Make them often read such texts as these: Leviticus 19:11, "You must not steal, you must not tell lies, and you must not deal falsely with your fellow citizen." Psalm 15:2, "... speaks honestly," etc. [519] Isaiah 63:8, "He said, 'Certainly they will be my people, children who are not disloyal.' He became their deliverer." John 8:44, "... the devil ... is a liar and the father of lies." Revelation 21:27; 22:15, "nothing ritually unclean will ever enter into it ... or practices falsehood ... Outside are the dogs ... and everyone who loves and practices falsehood." Psalm 63:11, "the mouths of those who speak lies will be shut up." Psalm 101:7, "Liars will not be welcome in my presence." Proverbs 19:5, 9, "A false witness will not go unpunished, and the one who spouts out lies will not escape punishment" (will perish). Proverbs 29:12, "If a ruler listens to lies, all his ministers will be wicked": so Psalm 31:18; 52:3. Psalm 119:163, "I hate and despise deceit; I love your law." Proverbs 13:5, "The righteous person will reject anything false." Ephesians 4:25, "Therefore, having laid aside falsehood, each one of you speak the truth with his neighbor because we are members of one another": as if to say, A man would not lie to deceive his own members; no more should we to deceive one another. In a word, where the love of God and man prevails, there truth prevails; but where self-love, partiality, and carnal self-interest prevail, there lying is a household servant, and thought a necessary means to these ends.

But because lying is so common and so great a sin, and many cases occur about it daily, though I think what is said offers matter enough to answer them, I shall mention some more of them distinctly, to help the satisfaction of those who cannot fit general answers to all their particular cases.

*Question 1.* Is frequent known lying a certain sign of a graceless state, that is, a mortal sin, proving the sinner to be in a state of damnation?

*Answer.* The difficulty of this case does no more concern lying than any other sin of equal malignity. Therefore I must refer you to those places where I have explained the difference between mortal, reigning sins, and infirmities. At present take this brief solution. 1. It is a thing of too great difficulty to determine just how many acts of a great sin may coexist with a present state of grace (that is, of right by covenant to heaven). 2. All sin which coexists with a habitual, predominant love of God and holiness coexists with a state of life, and no other. 3. He that seldom or never commits such external crimes, and yet does not love God, and heaven, and holiness above all the pleasures and interests of the flesh, is in a state of death. 4. It is certain that this love to God and holiness is not predominant in him whose carnal interest and lust has ordinarily, in the drift and tenor of his life, more power to draw him to the willful committing of known sin than the said love of God, and heaven, and holiness have to keep him from it. For his servants men are, whom they obey, whether it is sin leading to death, or obedience leading to righteousness (Romans 6:16). 5. Therefore the way to know whether sin is mortified, or mortal, is, (1.) By feeling the true bent of the will, whether we love or hate it. (2.) By observing the true bent and tenor of our lives, whether God's interest in us, or the contrary, is predominant when we are ourselves, and are tempted to such sins. 6. He that will sin thus as often as is consistent with saving grace shall never have the assurance of his sincerity, or the peace or comfort of a sound believer, till he repents and leads a better life. 7. He that in his sin retains the spirit of adoption, or the image of God, or habitual divine love, has also habitual and virtual repentance for that very sin before he actually repents; because he has that habitual hatred of it which will cause actual repentance when he is composed to act according to his predominant habits. 8. In the meantime the state of such a sinner is neither to be unregenerate, carnal, unholy, as he was before conversion, and so to lose all his right to life; nor yet to have so full a right as if he had not sinned: but a bar is put in against his claim, which must be removed before his right is full, and such as is ripe for present possession. 9. There are some sins which all men continue in while they live. As defect in the degrees of faith, hope, love, etc.; empty thoughts, words, disorder, passions, etc. And these sins are not totally involuntary; otherwise they would be no sins. Indeed, the evil is powerful in the will against the good, so far as to commit those sins, though not so far as to corrupt the bent of heart or life. 10. There are some sins which none on earth actually repent of, that is, those that they do not know to be sins; and those that they utterly forget; and those faults which they are guilty of just at the time of dying. 11. In these cases, virtual, or implicit, or habitual repentance suffices for the preventing of damnation. As also a will to have lived perfectly suffices in the case of continued imperfections. [520] 12. Things do not work on the will as they are in themselves; but as they are apprehended by the understanding: and that which is apprehended to be either of doubtful evil, or only a little sin and of little danger, will be much less resisted, and more often committed, than sins that are clearly apprehended to be great. Therefore, where any sort of lie is apprehended thus, as of small or doubtful evil, it will be the more often committed. 13. If this apprehension is wrong, and comes from the predominance of a carnal or ungodly heart, which will not allow the understanding to do its office, nor to take that to be evil which he would not leave, then both the judgment and the lie are mortal, and not mortified, pardoned sins. 14. But if this misapprehension of the understanding comes from natural inability, or unavoidable lack of better information, or only from the fault of a vicious inclination, which yet is not predominant, but is the remnant of a vice which is mortified in the main; then neither the error nor the frequent lying is a mortal, but a mortified sin. As, for instance, if false teachers (as the Jesuits) should persuade a justified person that a lie that hurts no man, but is helpful, is only a venial or no sin, it is possible for such a person often to commit it, though he does not err altogether innocently. 15. Though it is true that all good Christians should not indulge the smallest sin, and that true grace will make a man willing to forsake the least, yet certain experience tells us that some constant sinning (named above) does coexist with grace in all that have it on earth; and therefore that lesser sins, as thoughts, passions, are not resisted so much as greater are; and therefore that they are more indulged and favored, or else they would not be committed. No good men rise up with so great and constant watchfulness against an idle thought or word, or a disorder in prayer, etc. as they do against a heinous sin.

He that would have this and all such cases resolved in a word, and not be put to trying the case by all these distinctions, must take another casuist, or rather a deceiver instead of a resolver: for I cannot otherwise resolve him.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All verses quoted here were fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** "Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," a heading or division; Chapter 9 ("Directions for the Government of the Tongue") is divided into six Titles. This file continues Title 3, "Special directions against lying," and begins at the start of Direction 7. Baxter's "Quest." and "Answ." are rendered *Question* and *Answer*, and his Roman numerals as Arabic numbers.
- **Isaiah 63:8 (corrected reference; important):** the source prints "Isa. xxxvi. 8," but the words Baxter quotes ("Surely they are my people; children that will not lie; so he was their Saviour") are Isaiah 63:8; we print 63:8. The King James "children that will not lie" is the whole reason Baxter cites the verse. The NET reads "children who are not disloyal," which has nothing about lying. Flagged.
- **Psalm 101:7 (corrected reference):** the source prints "Psal. ci. 11," but Psalm 101 has eight verses; Baxter's words ("He that speaketh lies shall not tarry in my sight") are verse 7. Corrected.
- **Ephesians 4:25 (corrected reference):** the source prints "Eph. iv. 35"; Ephesians 4 has 32 verses, and the words quoted are verse 25. Corrected. Baxter's "q. d." (Latin *quasi dicat*, "as if to say") is rendered "as if to say."
- **Psalm 119:163; Proverbs 13:5:** Baxter's King James "I hate and abhor lying" and "A righteous man hateth lying" name lying directly. The NET has "deceit" and "will reject anything false." The sense is close, but the word "lying" that Baxter wants children to read is gone. Flagged.
- **Psalm 15:2:** Baxter quotes only "He that speaketh the truth from his heart," etc. The NET's corresponding phrase is "speaks honestly"; "from his heart" is not in the NET wording, so the stress on inward truthfulness is lost. The full NET verse: "Whoever lives a blameless life, does what is right, and speaks honestly."
- **Leviticus 19:11:** NET wording, whole verse as Baxter quotes it. The NET orders the commands "steal ... tell lies ... deal falsely," and ends "with your fellow citizen" where the King James has "one to another."
- **John 8:44:** Baxter's "The devil is a liar, and the father of it" compresses the verse. We give the NET phrases with ellipses ("... the devil ... is a liar and the father of lies"); in the NET the verse begins "You people are from your father the devil" and the words "he is a liar and the father of lies" come at its end.
- **Revelation 21:27; 22:15:** Baxter's dashes mark his skips; we use "...". The NET has "nothing ritually unclean" for the King James "any thing that defileth," and "practices falsehood" for "maketh a lie."
- **Proverbs 19:5, 9:** NET verse 5, with Baxter's parenthesis "(shall perish)" from verse 9 given in the NET wording "(will perish)." The NET has "spouts out lies" for "speaketh lies."
- **Proverbs 29:12:** NET "ministers" for the King James "servants." **Psalm 31:18; 52:3** (cited only).
- **1 Thessalonians 5:21** (Direction 9): Baxter quotes "Prove all things" and "hold that which is good" without a reference (the reference is ours, not in his text). The NET reads "examine all things; hold fast to what is good." "Prove" in the King James means "test," which the NET's "examine" keeps.
- **Romans 6:16** (Question 1, point 4): Baxter paraphrases without quotation marks ("For his servants men are, whom they obey, whether it be sin unto death, or obedience unto righteousness"); we kept his wording, modernized ("unto" to "leading to"). The NET reads "you are slaves of the one you obey, either of sin resulting in death, or obedience resulting in righteousness."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): Abraham's and Isaac's calling their wives their sisters (Genesis 12; 20; 26); David's feigning madness (1 Samuel 21:13); Gehazi's greed and lying (2 Kings 5:20-27); God's judgment bringing secret things to light (compare Ecclesiastes 12:14; Romans 2:16; 1 Corinthians 4:5); the "seared conscience" (1 Timothy 4:2); the "spirit of adoption" (Romans 8:15).
- **Cicero in Direction 9:** the Latin is kept exactly as printed, including "out falsum," an evident misprint for "aut falsum." Baxter gives his own English translation, which we modernized ("temerity" to "rashness"; "upon sufficient trial" to "on sufficient testing"). Baxter's reference "de Nat. Deor. l. 1." is given as *De Natura Deorum*, book 1; we have not checked the passage's exact location.
- **Notes 516 and 517:** the Cicero attributions are Baxter's; we have not confirmed the source of note 516, which he cites only as "Cicer."
- **shift / shift for himself** (Direction 7): kept, in the older sense of using expedients or evasions to get by; "any sinful shift" means any sinful dodge. **feigning madness:** Baxter's "feigning himself mad." **abide by God's choice:** his "stand to God's choice." **lack:** his "want" (here and in point 14).
- **credulous about:** Baxter's "credulous of." **only:** his "but" ("you do but easily believe"; "this is but to think"; "is but a venial"). **spreading:** his "divulging." **self-interested:** his "interested." **it is necessary for you ... to beware of reporting:** his "it behoveth you ... to take heed of reporting." **bowels:** kept (the spider spins from its own insides).
- **speak on supposition** (Direction 9): Baxter's "speak upon thought," meaning on mere opinion without knowing. **wait:** his "stay." **I thought it was true:** his "I thought it had been true."
- **expose:** Baxter's "detect" (Direction 11), in its older sense "uncover." **price:** his "rate." **preservative:** kept.
- **fit general answers to:** Baxter's "accommodate general answers to."
- **coexist with / is consistent with:** Baxter's "consist with" and "stand with," meaning be compatible with (Question 1, points 1, 2, 6, 15). **explained:** his "opened."
- **in him whose** (point 4): Baxter's "is not predominant, whose carnal interest ..."; "in him" was added because modern English needs it.
- **mortal, reigning sins; mortal / venial:** Baxter's technical terms, kept. **mortified** (sin put to death, subdued) and **carnal** (ruled by the flesh): kept, as elsewhere.
- **virtual repentance** (points 7 and 11): kept as Baxter's technical term: repentance present in the disposition and power of the heart, though not yet in an actual act. **composed** (point 7): kept, in the sense of settled, in one's right mind (compare "when we are ourselves," point 5).
- **empty thoughts:** Baxter's "vain thoughts." **powerful:** his "prevalent." **corrupt:** his "vitiate." **that is:** his "viz." **more often:** his "ofter." **predominance:** his "predominancy." **inability:** his "impotency." **allow:** his "suffer."
- **a lie that hurts no man, but is helpful** (point 14): Baxter's "officious," in the older sense of the "officious lie" (Latin *mendacium officiosum*), a lie told to help or oblige someone. The modern sense of "officious" (meddlesome) would mislead. His aside "(as the Jesuits)" is his polemic, kept as written.
- **all good Christians should not indulge the smallest sin** (point 15): left as written; Baxter means that no good Christian should indulge even the smallest sin. **named above:** his "aforenamed."
- **casuist** (last paragraph): kept; one who resolves cases of conscience. **be put to trying:** Baxter's "be put on trying."
- **Reported speech in Direction 9:** the words "I thought it was true" and "I think it is so" are unquoted in the source and left so.
