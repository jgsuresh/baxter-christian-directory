# Chapter 7: Title 8, against sinful fear of the devil (Directions 8-10) and of men (Directions 1-4)

**Direction 8. Consider that if the devil should appear to you, yes, and carry you to the top of a mountain, or the pinnacle of the temple, and talk to you with blasphemous temptations, it would be no other than what your Lord himself submitted to; who was still the dearly beloved of the Father (Matthew 4).**

One sin is more terrible than this.

**Direction 9. Remember that if God should permit him to appear to you, it might turn to your very great advantage; by killing all your unbelief, or doubts, of angels, and spirits, and the unseen world.**

It would tangibly prove to you that there is indeed an unhappy race of spirits, who envy man and seek his ruin; and so would more convince you of the evil of sin, the danger of souls, the need of godliness, and the truth of Christianity. And it is likely this is one cause why the devil no more appears in the world, not only because it is contrary to the ordinary government of God, who will have us live by faith and not by sight; but also because the devil knows how much it would do to destroy his kingdom, by destroying unbelief, atheism, and security, and awakening men to faith, and fear, and godliness. The fowler or the angler must not come in sight, lest he spoil his game by frightening it away.

**Direction 10. If it is the spiritual temptations and harassments only of Satan which you fear, remember that you have more cause to fear yourselves, for he can only tempt you; and if you do not do more against yourselves than all the devils in hell can do, you will never perish.**

And if you are willing to accept and yield to Christ, you need not inordinately fear either Satan or yourselves. For it is in the name and strength of Christ, and under his leadership and protection, that you are to begin and finish your warfare. And the Spirit that is in us is greater and stronger than the spirit that is in the world, and that harasses us (1 John 4:4). And the "Father that gives us to Christ is greater than all, and none can pluck us out of his hands" (John 10:29). And the "God of peace will tread down Satan under our feet" (Romans 16:20). If it were in his power he would harass us daily, and we would never have escaped so far as we have done: our daily experience tells us that we have a Protector.

**Directions against the sinful fear of men, and sufferings by them.**

**Direction 1. Ground your soul and hopes on Christ, and lay up your treasure in heaven; do not be a worldling that lives in hope of happiness in the creature; and then you are so far above the fear of men, as knowing that your treasure is above their reach, and your foundation and fortress safe from their assaults. [344]**

It is a base, hypocritical, worldly heart that makes you immoderately afraid of men! Are you afraid that they should storm and plunder heaven? or that they will cast you into hell? or that they will turn God against you? or that they will bribe or overawe your Judge? No, no! these are none of your fears! No; you are not so much as afraid that they will hinder one of your prayers from prevailing with God; nor that their prison walls and chains should keep out God and his Spirit from you, and force you from your communion with him! You are not afraid that they will forcibly rob you of one degree of grace, or heavenly-mindedness, or hopes of the life to come! (If it is that they will hinder you from these by tempting or frightening you into sin (which is all the hurt they can do your souls), then you are the more bound to cast away the fears of their hurting your bodies, because that is their very temptation to hurt your souls.) No; it is their hurting of your flesh, the diminishing of your estates, the depriving you of your liberty or worldly comforts, or of your lives, which is the thing you fear. And does not this show how much your hearts are yet on earth? and how much unmortified worldliness and fleshliness is still within you? and how much yet your hearts are false to God and heaven? Oh how the discovery should humble you! to find that you are yet no more dead to the things of the world, and that the cross of Christ has yet no more crucified it to you! to find that yet the fleshly interest is so powerful in you, and the interest of Christ and heaven is so low! that God seems not enough for you, and that you cannot take heaven alone for your portion, but are so much afraid of losing earth! O at once search into the bottom of this corruption in your hearts, and lament your worldliness and hypocrisy, and work it out, and set your hearts and hopes above, and be content with God and heaven alone, and then this inordinate fear of man will have nothing left to work upon.

**Direction 2. Set God against man, and his wisdom against their cunning, and his love and mercy against their malice and cruelty, and his power against their impotence, and his truth, and omniscience, and righteousness against their slanders and lies, and his promises against their threatenings; and then if yet you are inordinately afraid of man, you must confess that in that measure you do not believe in God.**

If God is not wise enough, and good enough, and just enough, and powerful enough to save you, so far as it is best for you to be saved, then he is not God: away with atheism, and then do not fear man.

**Direction 3. Remember what man is that you are afraid of.**

He is a bubble raised by Providence, to toss about the world, and for God to honor himself by or upon. He is the mere product of his Maker's will: his breath is in his nostrils! he is hastening to his dust, and in that day his worldly hopes and thoughts perish with him. He is a worm that God can in one moment tread into the earth and hell. He is a dream, a shadow, a dry leaf or a little chaff, that is blown a while about the world. [345] He is just ready, in the height of his pride and fury, to drop into the grave; and that same man, or all those men, whom now you fear, shall one of these days most certainly lie rotting in the dust, and be hidden in darkness, lest their ugly sight and stink be an annoyance to the living. Where now are all the proud ones that made such a bustle in the world but a while ago? In one age they look big, and boast of their power, and rebel, and usurp authority, and are mad to be great and rulers in the world, or persecute the ministers and people of the Lord; and in the next (or in the same) they are viler than the dirt; their carcasses are buried, or their bones scattered abroad, and made the horror and wonder of beholders. And is this a creature to be feared above God, or against God? See Isaiah 51:7, "Listen to me, you who know what is right, you people who are aware of my law. Don't be afraid of the insults of men; don't be discouraged because of their abuse. For a moth will eat away at them like clothes; a clothes moth will devour them like wool. But the vindication I provide will be permanent; the deliverance I give will last." Isaiah 2:22, "Stop trusting in human beings, whose life's breath is in their nostrils. For why should they be given special consideration?" Psalm 146:3-4, "Do not trust in princes, or in human beings, who cannot deliver. Their life's breath departs, they return to the ground. On that day their plans die." When Herod was magnified as a god, he could not save himself from being devoured alive by worms. When Pharaoh was in his pride and glory, he could not save his people from frogs, and flies, and lice. Says God to Sennacherib, "The virgin daughter Zion despises you—she makes fun of you; ... shakes her head after you. Whom have you taunted and hurled insults at? At whom have you shouted and looked so arrogantly?" Oh what a worm is man that you are so afraid of!

**Direction 4. Remember that men as well as devils are chained, and dependent upon God, and have no power but what he gives them, and can do nothing but by his permission.**

And if God will have it done, you have his promise that it shall work for your good (Romans 8:28). And are you afraid that God should do you good by them? If you see the knife or lancet in an enemy's hand only, you might fear it; but if you see it in the surgeon's or in a father's hand, though nature will a little shrink, yet reason will forbid you to make any great matter of it, or inordinately to fear. What if God will permit Joseph's brothers to bind him, and sell him to the Amalekites; and his master's wife to cause him to be imprisoned? Is he not to be trusted in all this, that he will turn it to his good? What if he will permit Shimei to curse David; or the king to cast Daniel into the lions' den; or the three confessors into the furnace of fire? Do you believe that your Father's will is the disposer of all? and yet are you afraid of man? Our Lord told Pilate when he boasted of his power to take away his life or save it, "You would have no authority over me at all, unless it was given to you from above" (John 19:11). [346]

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[344] Omnia Christe tui superant tormenta ferendo. Tollere quæ nequeunt, hæc tollerare queunt. His vita caruisse frui est: posuisse potiri. Et superâsse pati est: et superesse mori. Ad tribunal æternum judicis provocatio salvet est: solet is perperam judicata rescindere. Petrarch. dial. 66. lib. 2. (English, rendered loosely: Your servants, O Christ, overcome all torments by bearing them. What they cannot remove, they are able to endure. For them, to have gone without life is to enjoy it; to have laid it down is to possess it. And to suffer is to have overcome; and to die is to survive. An appeal to the tribunal of the eternal Judge is safe: he is accustomed to overturn things wrongly judged. Petrarch, dialogue 66, book 2.)

[345] Job 13:25; Psalm 1:5-6; 68:2; 73:20; Job 20:8. Victor Uticensis says of Augustine that he died of fear. Nunc illud eloquentiæ, quod ubertim per omnes campos ecclesiæ decurrebat, ipso metu siccatum est flamen: when Gensericus besieged Hippo. (English: Now that [river] of eloquence, which ran abundantly through all the fields of the church, was dried up by fear itself.)

[346] Valentinianus jussus ab Imperatore Juliano immolare idolis, aut militia excedere, sponte discessit. nec mora qui pro nomine Christi amiserat tribunatum, in locum persecutoris sui accepit imperium. Paul. Diaconus, l. 1. p. 1. (English: Valentinian, ordered by the Emperor Julian to sacrifice to idols or leave military service, withdrew of his own accord. And before long he who had lost his tribuneship for the name of Christ received the empire in the place of his persecutor. Paul the Deacon, book 1, page 1.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Where this file falls:** Chapter 7 is "Directions for the Government of the Passions," divided into ten "Titles" ("Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," his name for each main division). This file continues Title 8, "against sinful fear," from the previous file (ch07-08-p1), beginning with Direction 8 of the "Directions against sinful Fear of the Devil," and starts the sub-heading "Directions against the sinful Fear of Men." Numbering restarts at 1 under that sub-heading, as in the source. Following the project's format, each Direction's first sentence is the bold line and the rest is the body. This file ends with Direction 4 against fear of men; the next file continues.
- **Matthew 4** (Direction 8): cited only; the temptation of Christ.
- **1 John 4:4** (Direction 10): Baxter does not set this in quotation marks; his wording is kept. The NET reads "the one who is in you is greater than the one who is in the world."
- **John 10:29 (paraphrase):** Baxter's "Father that giveth us to Christ is greater than all, and none can pluck us out of his hands" adapts the King James ("My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father's hand") into a statement about "us." We kept his adapted wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET reads "My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all, and no one can snatch them from my Father's hand."
- **Romans 16:20 (adapted):** Baxter quotes "God of peace will tread down Satan under our feet," leaving out the King James "shortly" and changing "your" to "our." Because his quotation is adapted, we keep his wording in his quotation marks (as with John 10:29). The NET reads "The God of peace will quickly crush Satan under your feet."
- **Isaiah 51:7-8:** Baxter cites "Isa. li. 7" but quotes verses 7 and 8; we keep his reference and give the NET for both verses. NET "you who know what is right ... aware of my law" for King James "ye that know righteousness, the people in whose heart is my law"; "vindication ... deliverance" for "righteousness ... salvation." The point Baxter uses (the moth will eat the persecutors) is unchanged.
- **Isaiah 2:22 (NET flag, minor):** the King James "Cease ye from man ... for wherein is he to be accounted of?" becomes the NET "Stop trusting in human beings ... For why should they be given special consideration?" The NET makes it explicitly about trusting people rather than simply turning from them; Baxter's point (man is too frail to fear) still stands. Baxter's earlier "his breath is in his nostrils!" (same Direction, not in quotation marks) echoes this verse and is kept in his wording.
- **Psalm 146:3-4 (NET flag, minor):** King James "in whom there is no help" is NET "who cannot deliver"; "his thoughts perish" is "their plans die." Baxter's own phrase "his worldly hopes and thoughts perish with him," earlier in the Direction, echoes the King James wording and is kept.
- **Sennacherib (no reference in Baxter's text):** the quotation is Isaiah 37:22-23 (also 2 Kings 19:21-22, with nearly the same NET wording); the reference is not in Baxter's text. Baxter's "--" skips "the daughter of Jerusalem," shown here as "..." before "shakes her head after you." NET "looked so arrogantly" for King James "lifted up thine eyes on high"; NET "taunted and hurled insults at" for "reproached and blasphemed." We follow the NET's sentence breaks inside the quotation (two questions where the King James has one).
- **John 19:11 (NET flag):** Baxter quotes the King James "Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above." The NET has "authority over me." Baxter's Direction is about men having "no power but what he gives them," and he introduces the verse with Pilate boasting "of his power"; the NET's "authority" is close in sense but loses the verbal echo of "power."
- **Romans 8:28** (Direction 4): cited, not quoted; Baxter's "it shall work unto thy good" is his paraphrase.
- **"sell him to the Amalekites"** (Direction 4): so in the source. In Genesis 37:25-28 Joseph is sold to Ishmaelites (also called Midianites), not Amalekites. Kept as Baxter wrote it.
- **Unmarked allusions** (references not in Baxter's text): "live by faith and not by sight" (2 Corinthians 5:7); "lay up your treasure in heaven" (Matthew 6:19-20); "the cross of Christ has ... crucified it to you" (Galatians 6:14); Herod devoured by worms (Acts 12:21-23); Pharaoh and the frogs, flies, and lice (Exodus 8); Joseph bound, sold, and imprisoned (Genesis 37; 39); Shimei cursing David (2 Samuel 16:5-13); Daniel in the lions' den (Daniel 6); the three in the furnace (Daniel 3); Pilate (John 19:10).
- **Footnote 345 references:** converted to modern form. "Psalm 1:5-6" is as printed; the chaff image is in Psalm 1:4, so the printed verses may be a slip, but we have kept them. "Augustine ... died of fear ... when Gensericus besieged Hippo": this is Baxter's report of "Victor Uticens." (probably Victor of Vita, often called Victor Uticensis). Gensericus is probably the Vandal king usually called Genseric or Gaiseric. We have not checked Victor's text or the historical details. "Flamen" in the Latin is probably a misprint for "flumen" (river); the translation reads it that way, and "[river]" marks the word supplied.
- **Footnote 344:** "salvet est" is probably a misprint (perhaps for "salva est," "is safe"); the translation reads it that way. "tollerare" is an old spelling of "tolerare" (to endure). "Tui" ("your [people]") is rendered "your servants." The source is given as Petrarch, dialogue 66, book 2, presumably his *De remediis utriusque fortunae*; not checked.
- **Footnote 346:** Valentinian (who, by the footnote's own account, later "received the empire") and the Emperor Julian (probably the emperor usually called "the Apostate"; not checked); "Paul. Diaconus" is Paul the Deacon, probably his additions to Eutropius (the *Historia Romana* or *Historia Miscella*); not checked. "nec mora" is rendered "before long."
- **Passions, melancholy:** kept as Baxter's words (see GLOSSARY.md); neither occurs in this range.
- **Security** (Direction 9): kept as Baxter's technical term for a careless, false confidence about one's spiritual state.
- **The creature** (Direction 1, fear of men): kept; Baxter's term for created things as opposed to God.
- **Confessors** (Direction 4): kept; those who confess their faith under persecution (Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego).
- **Interest** (Direction 1): "the fleshly interest ... the interest of Christ and heaven" kept; it means the cause or party of the flesh and of Christ, what each has at stake in the soul.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): thee, thou, thy (you, your); yea (yes); sensibly (tangibly; "sensibly" meant perceptibly to the senses); it is like (it is likely); appeareth, knoweth (appears, knows); infidelity (unbelief); frighting, affrighting (frightening); If it be (If it is); molestations (harassments); molesteth, molest (harasses, harass); he can but tempt (he can only tempt); if you do not more (if you do not do more); conduct (leadership; "conduct" meant guidance or command); giveth (gives); we had never escaped (we would never have escaped); telleth (tells); Bottom thy soul (Ground your soul); be not (do not be); liveth, maketh (lives, makes); lest they should, lest they cast, lest they turn, etc. (that they should, that they will cast, that they will turn: "will" is added where modern English needs it after "afraid that"); engaged (bound, that is, obligated); the diminishing your estates (the diminishing of your estates); accommodations (comforts); doth (does); hath (has); seemeth (seems); presently (at once); policy (cunning; "policy" meant crafty scheming); impotency (impotence); thou believest not (you do not believe); be not (is not); fear not (do not fear); honour (honor); do perish (perish); awhile (a while); be hid (be hidden); hearken (NET "listen"); Saith God (Says God); giveth (gives); unto thy good (for your good); lest God should (that God should); brethren (brothers); thou fearest (you fear); thou art (you are); thou hast (you have).
- **Kept words:** "inordinate(ly)" (out of due order or measure); "worldling"; "lest" (Direction 9, "lest he spoil his game," and Direction 3, "lest their ugly sight and stink," still clear English); "yet" in "if yet you are" (Direction 2: still); "look big" (look important); "mad to be great"; "disposer" (the one who orders and arranges); "make any great matter of it."
- **Punctuation:** in Direction 1 (fear of men), Baxter's parenthesis contains a second parenthesis "(which is all the hurt they can do your souls,)"; both are kept, with the comma before the inner closing parenthesis dropped. In Direction 10, the comma between "yourselves" and "than" and between "us" and "is greater" (subject and verb) are dropped.
