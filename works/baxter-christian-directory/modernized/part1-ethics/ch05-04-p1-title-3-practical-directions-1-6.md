# Chapter 5: Title 3, practical directions for redeeming time (Directions 1-6)

**Title 3. Practical directions for redeeming time.**

**Direction 1. The first point in the art of redeeming time is to dispatch first with greatest care and diligence the greatest works of absolute necessity, which must be done, or else we are undone forever.**

First see that the great work of a sound conversion or sanctification is certainly worked within you. Make sure of your saving interest in Christ: get proof of your adoption and peace with God, and right to everlasting life. Be able to prove to your consciences from the word of God, and from your regenerate, heavenly hearts and lives, that your souls are justified and safe, and may comfortably receive the news of death, whenever it shall be sent to call you away. And then, when you have done only so much of your work, you will incur no such loss of time as will prove the loss of your souls or happiness. Though still there is much more work to do, for yourselves and others, yet when this much is soundly done, you have secured the main thing. If you lose the time in which you should be renewed by the Spirit of Christ, and in which you should lay up your treasure in heaven, you are lost forever. Be sure therefore that you look first to this: and then if you lose only the time in which you might have grown rich or got promotion, your loss is tolerable; you know the worst of it; you may see to the end of it. Yes, if you lose the time in which you should increase in holiness, and edify others, the loss is grievous; but yet it will not cost you heaven. Therefore, as Solomon directs the farmer, "Establish your work outside and get your fields ready; afterward build your house" (Proverbs 24:27); so I advise you to see first that the necessary work is done; when that is done, and well done, you may go quietly and cheerfully about the rest: "above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness"; oh what a great deal is done when this is done!

**Direction 2. Learn to understand well the degrees of duties, which is the greater and which the less, that when two seem to require your time at once, you may know which of them to prefer.**

Not only to know which is simply and in itself the greatest, but which is the greatest for you, and at that season, and as considered in all the circumstances. A great part of the art of redeeming time consists in the wise discerning and performing of this; to give precedence to the greatest duty. He loses his time who is getting a penny when he might get a pound; who is visiting his neighbor when he should be attending his prince; who is weeding his garden when he should be quenching a fire in his house, though he is doing that which in itself is good. So is he losing his time who is preferring his body before his soul; or man before God; or indifferent things before necessary; or private duties before public; or less edifying before the more edifying; or sacrifice before necessary mercy. The order of good works I have shown you before, chapter 3, direction 10, which you may peruse.

**Direction 3. Be acquainted with the season of every duty, and the duty of each season; and take them in their time.**

And thus one duty will help on another; whereas misplacing them and disordering them sets them one against another, and takes up your time with distracting difficulties, and loses you in confusion. As he that takes the morning hour for prayer, or the fittest vacant hour, will do it quietly, without the disturbance of his other affairs; when if the season is omitted, you will scarcely at all perform it, or almost as badly as if you did not do it at all: so it is in matters of conscience, reproof, reading, hearing, meditating, and every duty. A wise and well-skilled Christian should bring his matters into such order that every ordinary duty should know its place, and all should be as the links of one chain which draw on one another; or as the parts of a clock or other machine, which must be all joined together, and each rightly placed. A workman that has all his tools in a heap or out of place spends much of the day in which he should be working in looking for his tools; when he that knows the place of every one can at once take it, and lose no time. If my books are thrown together in a heap, I may spend half the day in looking for them when I should use them; but if they are set in order, and I know their places, it spares me that time. So it is in the right timing of our duties.

**Direction 4. Live continually as under the government of God; and keep conscience tender, and in the performance of its office; and always be ready to render an account to God and conscience of what you do.**

If you live as under the government of God, you will be always doing his work; you will be remembering his judgment; you will be testing your work whether it is such as he approves: this will keep you from all time-wasting empty pursuits. If you keep conscience tender, it will at once check and rebuke you for your sin; and when you lose only a minute of time, it will tell you of the loss: whereas a "seared" conscience is "callous," and will give you over to "indecency" (Ephesians 4:19; 1 Timothy 4:2); and will make only a joke of the loss of time; or at least will not effectually tell you either of the sin or loss. If you keep conscience to its office, it will ask you frequently, what you are doing? and test your works; it will take account of time when it is spent, and ask you, what have you been doing? and how you have spent every day and hour? And (as Seneca could say) "He will be the more careful what he does, and how he spends the day, who expects to be called to a reckoning for it every night." This will make the foreseen day of judgment have such a continual awe upon you, as if you were immediately going to it; while conscience, with respect to it, is continually judging you in advance. Whereas they that have silenced or discarded conscience are like schoolboys that lock their master out of doors, who do it with a design to spend the time in play which they should have spent in learning: but the after-reckoning pays for all.

*In the margin: Rules to know what time must be spent in.*

Here, for the further direction of your consciences, I shall lay down for you a few rules for the right spending of your time. 1. Spend it in nothing (as a deliberate moral act) which is not truly, directly, or remotely an act of obedience to some law of God. (Of mere natural acts, which are no objects of moral choice, I do not speak.) 2. Spend it in nothing which you know must be repented of. 3. Spend it in nothing which you dare not, or may not justifiably pray for a blessing on from God. 4. Spend it in nothing which you would not review at the hour of death, by an awakened, well-informed mind. 5. Spend it in nothing which you would not hear of in the day of judgment. 6. Spend it in nothing which you cannot safely and comfortably be found doing, if death should surprise you in the act. 7. Spend it in nothing which flesh-pleasing persuades you to, against your consciences, or with a secret misgiving or doubting of your consciences. 8. Spend it in nothing which has not some tendency, directly or remotely, to your ultimate end, the pleasing of God, and the enjoying him in love forever. 9. Spend it in nothing which tends to do more hurt than good; that would do a great hurt to yourself or others, under pretense of doing some little good, which perhaps may better be done another way. 10. Lastly, spend it in nothing which is only a smaller good, when a greater should be done.

**Direction 5. Do your best to settle yourselves where there are the greatest helps and smallest hindrances to the redeeming of your time.**

And labor more to adapt your dwelling, condition, and employments to the great ends of your life and time than to your worldly honor, ease, or wealth. Live where there is the best trading for the soul: you may get more by God's ordinary blessing in one year, in a godly family, or in fruitful company, and under an able, godly minister, than in many years in a barren soil, among the ignorant, dead-hearted, or profane, where we must say, as David, "I held my peace even from good, while the wicked was before me" (Psalm 39:1–2). And when we must do all the good we do through much opposition; and meet with great disadvantages and difficulties, which may quickly stop such dull and backward hearts as ours. If you will prefer your profit before your souls in the choice of your condition, and will plunge yourselves into distracting business and company, your time will run in a wrong, unprofitable channel.

**Direction 6. Plan beforehand, with the best of your skill, for the preventing of hindrances, and for the most successful performance of your work.**

If you leave all to the very time of doing, you will have many hindrances rise before you, and make you lose your time, which prudent foresight might have prevented. As for making good use of the Lord's day, if you do not beforehand so order your business that all things may give place to holy duties, you will meet with so many disturbances and temptations as will cost you much of your time and benefit: so for family duties, and secret duties, and meditation, and studies, and the works of your callings; if you do not foresee what hindrance is likely to meet you, that you may prevent it before the time, you must lose much time, and suffer much disappointment.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," meaning a heading or main division of the chapter. Chapter 5 has five such Titles; this file covers the start of Title 3. Baxter's heading reads "Directions practical for redeeming Time"; we put the adjective first. The Direction numbers restart at 1 here, as in the source.
- **Bold heading sentences:** as in the finished files, the first sentence of each Direction is set in bold and the rest follows as body text.
- **Proverbs 24:27:** NET wording. Baxter's King James text ("Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house") says the same in older words. "Husbandman" (farmer) is Baxter's own word for the person Solomon directs; it is not in the verse.
- **Matthew 6:33 (flag):** no reference in Baxter's text (the reference is ours). Baxter's "Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness" is the King James wording; the NET reads "But above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness," which we give from "above all" to the extent he quotes. The NET's "above all" still carries the "first" that Baxter's whole Direction (do the greatest work first) leans on, but "of God" becomes "his," which depends on the verse's context.
- **Ephesians 4:19; 1 Timothy 4:2 (flag):** Baxter strings together three King James phrases: "seared conscience" (1 Timothy 4:2, "having their conscience seared"), "past feeling," and "lasciviousness" (both Ephesians 4:19). In the NET these are "consciences are seared," "callous," and "indecency." We use the NET words in his quotation marks, keeping his sentence ("a 'seared' conscience is 'callous'"). The King James "past feeling," which Baxter sets against a "tender" conscience that feels every lost minute, is sharper for his point than the NET's "callous," though the sense is the same. The NET's "indecency" is narrower than Baxter's general sense of loose living.
- **Psalm 39:1–2 (flag):** Baxter's quotation blends two verses of the King James: "while the wicked is before me" (verse 1) and "I held my peace, even from good" (verse 2). Since it is a blend, we kept his wording in his quotation marks. The NET reads (verse 1) "I will put a muzzle over my mouth while in the presence of an evil person" and (verse 2) "I was stone silent; I held back the urge to speak." The NET does not have "even from good," which is the phrase Baxter leans on (bad company keeps us from doing even good). "The wicked was before me" is kept as Baxter wrote it; it means "a wicked person was before me." The NET heading names David, as Baxter does.
- **Seneca** (Direction 4): Baxter gives the saying in his own English and without a reference. We have not identified or checked the passage; it is kept as his.
- **"chapter 3, direction 10"** (Direction 2): Baxter's cross-reference ("chap. iii. direct. x.") to his earlier treatment of the order of good works. We have not checked the cross-reference against the modernized Chapter 3 files.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "lay up your treasure in heaven" (Matthew 6:20); "sacrifice before necessary mercy" (Matthew 9:13; 12:7; Hosea 6:6).
- **Direction 2's second sentence** ("Not only to know ...") has no main verb in the source; it continues the heading sentence. Kept as Baxter wrote it. Likewise, in Direction 5 "And when we must do all the good we do through much opposition; ..." is an incomplete sentence in the source, continuing the "where we must say" clause before it; kept as printed.
- **Word swaps:** "despatch" to "dispatch" (spelling); "wrought" to "worked"; "hence" to "away"; "done but so much" to "done only so much" (and similar "but" = only, in "lose but," "make but a jest," "is but a smaller good"); "the main" to "the main thing"; "preferment" to "promotion"; "Yea" to "Yes"; "lose you heaven" to "cost you heaven," and "lose you much of your time" to "cost you much of your time"; "husbandman" to "farmer"; "what a deal" to "what a great deal"; "precedency" to "precedence"; "in point of conscience" to "in matters of conscience"; "ill" to "badly"; "engine" to "machine"; "conjunct" to "joined together"; "right placed" to "rightly placed"; "on a heap" to "in a heap"; "presently" to "at once" (and, in "as if you were presently going to it," to "immediately"); "still doing his work" to "always doing his work" (still in its older sense, always); "trying" to "testing"; "vanities" to "empty pursuits" (see the glossary); "reprehend" to "rebuke"; "jest" to "joke"; "looks to be called" to "expects to be called"; "forejudging" to "judging you in advance"; "bolt their master out of doors" to "lock their master out of doors"; "lay you down" to "lay down for you"; "warrantably" to "justifiably" (with good warrant, that is, with justification); "grudge" to "misgiving" (in Baxter's day a "grudge" could be an inward qualm or complaint, not only a lasting resentment); "pretence" to "pretense"; "accommodate ... to" to "adapt ... to"; "habitation" to "dwelling"; "where is best trading" to "where there is the best trading"; "Contrive" to "Plan"; "hinderance(s)" to "hindrance(s)"; "forecast" to "foresight" (noun) and "foresee" (verb); "like to meet you" to "likely to meet you"; "improving of the Lord's day" to "making good use of the Lord's day" (improve in its older sense, make good use of, as in the chapter title).
- **Kept words:** "saving interest in Christ" (a share in Christ that saves); "adoption," "regenerate," "justified" (theological terms); "edify" (build up in faith); "indifferent things" (things neither commanded nor forbidden); "peruse" (read through); "the after-reckoning" (the settling of accounts afterward, here the judgment); "trading for the soul" (Baxter's merchant image: where the soul can make the most gain); "secret duties" (private devotions, such as prayer alone, as distinct from family and public duties); "callings" (occupations, one's work in the world); "preventing" (in Direction 6, forestalling, which is also the modern sense); "suffer much disappointment" (undergo it, not "allow").
- **Rules 1-10:** Baxter's numbered rules run on within one paragraph in the source; we keep them in one paragraph with his numbers.
