# Chapter 4: Directions 7 to 14 against pride, and for humility

**Direction 7. Look to a humbled Christ to humble you.**

Can you be proud while you believe that your Savior was clothed with flesh, and lived in lowliness, and made himself of no reputation, and was despised, and scorned, and spit upon by sinners, and shamefully used, and nailed as a criminal to a cross? The very incarnation of Christ is a condescension and humiliation enough to baffle both men and angels, transcending all belief but such as God himself produces by his supernatural testimony and Spirit. [232] And can pride look a crucified Christ in the face, or stand before him? Did God take upon him the form of a servant, [233] and must you domineer and have the highest place? Had not Christ a place to lay his head on, and must you needs have your adorned, well-furnished rooms? Must you needs show off in the most fantastic fashion, instead of your Savior's seamless coat? Does he pray for his murderers, and must you be revenged for a word or petty wrong? Is he patiently spit upon and buffeted, and are you ready, through proud impatience, to spit upon or buffet others? Surely he that "condemned sin in the flesh," condemned no sin more than pride.

**Direction 8. Look to the examples of the most eminent saints, and you will see they were all most eminent in humility.**

The apostles, before the coming down of the Holy Spirit on them, contended which of them should be the greatest (which Christ permitted that he might most sharply rebuke it, and leave his warning to all his ministers and disciples to the end of the world, that they that would be greatest must be the servants of all, and that they must by conversion become as little children, or never enter into the kingdom of God). But afterward in what humility did these apostles labor, and live, and suffer in the world! Paul "made himself a servant to all, that he might gain the more, though he was free from all men," 1 Corinthians 9:19. They submitted themselves to all the injuries and affronts of men; to be accounted the plagues and troublers of the world, and as the scorn and offscouring of all things, and a gazing-stock to angels and to men. [234] And are you better than they? If you are, you are more humble, and not more proud.

**Direction 9. Look to the holy angels, that condescend to minister for man; and think on the blessed souls with God, how far they are from being proud; and remember, if ever you come to heaven, how far you will be from pride yourself.**

Such a sight as Isaiah's would take down pride: Isaiah 6:1-3, "I saw the Lord seated on a high, elevated throne. The hem of his robe filled the temple. Seraphs stood over him; each one had six wings. With two wings they covered their faces, with two they covered their feet, and they used the remaining two to fly (signifying humility, purity, and obedience). They called out to one another, 'Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of Heaven's Armies! His majestic splendor fills the entire earth!'" So Revelation 4:8, 10, "The elders fall down, and cast their crowns before him that sits on the throne." Look up to heaven, and you will abhor your pride.

**Direction 10. Look upon the great imperfection of your grace and duties.**

Should that man be proud that has so little of the Spirit and image of Jesus Christ? that believes no more, and fears God no more, and loves him no more? and can no better trust in him, nor rest upon his word and love? nor any more delight in him, nor in his holy laws and service? One would think that the lamentable weakness of any one of all these graces, should take down pride and abase you in your own eyes. Is he a Christian that does not even abhor himself, when he perceives how little he loves his God, and how little all his meditations on the love and blood of Christ, and of the infinite goodness of God, and of the heavenly glory, do kindle the fire, and warm his heart? Can we observe the darkness of our minds, and ignorance of God, and strangeness to the life to come, and the woeful weakness of our faith, and not be abased to a loathing of ourselves? Can we choose but even abhor those hearts that can love a friend, and love the trifles and vanities of this life, and yet can love their God no more? that take no more pleasure in his name, and praise, and word, and service, when they can find pleasure in the accommodations of their flesh? Can we choose but loathe those hearts that are so averse to God, so loath to think of him, so loath to pray to him, so weary of prayer, or holy meditation, or any duty, and yet so eager for the business and recreations of the flesh? Can we feel how coldly and unbelievingly we pray, how ignorantly or carnally we talk, how confusedly and emptily we think, and how slothfully we work, and how unprofitably we live, and yet be proud, and not be covered with shame? Oh! for a serious Christian to feel how little of God, of Christ, of heaven is upon his heart, and how little appears in any eminent holiness, and fruitfulness, and heavenliness of life, is so humbling a consideration, that we have much ado to own ourselves, and not lie down as utterly desolate. Should that soul admit a thought of pride, that has so little grace as to be uncertain whether he has any at all in sincerity or not? that cannot with assurance call God, Father, or plead his interest in Christ or in the promises? nor knows if he dies this hour, whether he shall go to heaven or hell? Should he be proud that is no readier to die? and no more assured of the pardon of sin? nor more willing to appear before the Lord? If one pained member will make you groan, and walk dejectedly, though all the rest do feel no pain, a soul that has this universal weakness, a weakness that is so sinful and so dangerous, has cause to be continually humbled to the dust.

**Direction 11. Look upon your great and manifold sins, which dwell in your heart, and have been committed in your life, and there you will see cause for great humiliation.**

If your body were full of toads and serpents, and you could see or feel them crawling in you, would you then be proud? Why, so many sins are ten-thousand-fold worse, and should make you far viler in your own esteem! If you were possessed with devils, and knew it, would you be proud? Why, devils possessing your body are not so bad or hurtful to you, as sin in your soul! The sight of a sin should more take down your pride, than the sight of a devil. Should that man be proud that has lived as you have lived, and sinned as you have sinned, from your childhood until now? that has lost so much time, and abused so much mercy, and neglected so many means, and omitted so many duties to God and man, and been guilty of so many sinful thoughts, and so many false or foolish words, and has broken all the laws of God? Should not he be deeply humbled that has yet so much ignorance, [235] error, unbelief, hypocrisy, indulgence in physical pleasure, worldliness, hardheartedness, security, uncharitableness, lust, envy, malice, impatience, and selfishness, as is in you? Should not your very pride itself be matter of your great humiliation, to think that so odious a sin should yet so much prevail? Look thus on your leprous, defiled soul, and turn your very pride against itself! Know yourself, and you cannot be proud.

**Direction 12. Look also to the desert of all your sins, even to hell itself, and try if that will bring you low.**

Though pride came from hell effectively, yet hell, objectively, may afford you a remedy against it. Think on the worm that never dies, and the fire that never shall be quenched, and consider whether pride becomes that soul, that has deserved these. Will you be proud in the way to your damnation? You might better be proud of your chains and rope, when you are going to the gallows! Think, whether the miserable souls in hell are now minding neat and well set attire, or seeking for dominion, honor, or preferment, or contending who shall be the greatest, or striving for the highest rooms, or setting out themselves to the admiration and applause of men, or quarreling with others for undervaluing or dishonoring them! Do you think there is any place or matter there for such works of pride, when God abases them?

**Direction 13. Look to the day of judgment, when all proud thoughts and looks shall be taken down; and to the endless misery threatened to the proud.**

Think of that world, in which your souls must ere long appear, before the great and holy God, whose presence will abase the proudest sinner. When the tyrants, and gallants, and wantons of the earth, must with trembling and amazement give up their accounts to the most righteous Judge of all the world, then where are their lofty looks and language? then where are their glory, and gallantry, and proud, imperious domineering, and their scornful despising the humble, lowly ones of Christ? Would you then think that this is the same man, that lately could scarcely be seen or spoken with? that looked so big, and swaggered it out in wealth and honor? Is this he that could not endure a scorn, or to be slighted, or undervalued, or plainly reproved? that must needs have the honor and precedence in wit, and greatness, and command? Is this the man that thought he was perfect and had no sin; or that his sins were so small, as not to need the humiliation, renovation, and holy diligence of the saints? Is this the woman that spent half the day in dressing up herself, and house, and furniture for the view of others, and must needs be in the newest or the neatest fashion? that was accustomed to walk in an artificial pace, with a wandering eye, in a wanton garb, as if she were too good to tread on the earth? Oh! then how the case will be altered with such as these! Can you believe, and consider how you must be judged by God, and yet be proud?

**Direction 14. Look to the devils themselves that tempt you to be proud, and see what pride has brought them to; and remember, that a proud man is the image of the devil, and pride is the devil's special sin.**

He that envies your happiness, knows by sad experience the way to misery; and therefore tempts you to be proud, that you may come, by the same way, to the same end that he himself has come to. "...the angels who did not keep within their proper domain but abandoned their own place of residence, he has kept in eternal chains in utter darkness, locked up for the judgment of the great Day," Jude 6.

## Translator's notes

- **Range:** this file begins at Baxter's Direction 7 and ends at the end of Direction 14. Baxter prints his Directions as "Direct. VII." and so on; we render them "Direction 7" and so on, and set his opening sentence of each in bold as its heading. The rest of the Direction follows in ordinary text.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Romans 8:3 (Direction 7):** "condemned sin in the flesh" has no reference in Baxter's text; it matches Romans 8:3 (NET "he condemned sin in the flesh"), the same wording as the King James. The reference is ours, not Baxter's.
- **1 Corinthians 9:19 (Direction 8):** Baxter prints "ix. 10," which is a misprint; the words quoted are in 1 Corinthians 9:19, and we give that reference. His wording is a loose King James quotation with the clauses in a different order. NET reads "since I am free from all I can make myself a slave to all, in order to gain even more people." We keep Baxter's wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET "slave" is stronger than Baxter's "servant" (the King James word); the sense he leans on (Paul's voluntary lowering of himself) is the same.
- **Isaiah 6:1-3 (Direction 9):** NET wording, quoted from the middle of verse 1 through verse 3 as Baxter does. Differences from the King James he used: "train" is the NET "hem of his robe"; "sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up" is "seated on a high, elevated throne"; "seraphims" is "Seraphs"; "Lord of hosts" is "Lord of Heaven's Armies"; "glory" is "majestic splendor." Baxter's "Above it" (the throne) is "over him" in the NET. The parenthesis "(signifying humility, purity, and obedience)" is Baxter's own comment, kept where he put it; the "two wings" gloss is his interpretation, not the text's. The verse 3 words are put in single quotation marks inside Baxter's double marks.
- **Revelation 4:8, 10 (Direction 9):** Baxter's quotation of the elders is a loose King James rendering and perhaps a blend, so we keep his wording, modernized. NET for verse 10 reads: "the twenty-four elders throw themselves to the ground before the one who sits on the throne and worship the one who lives forever and ever, and they offer their crowns before his throne." Verse 8 (NET) has the four living creatures saying "Holy, Holy, Holy is the Lord God, the All-Powerful"; Baxter cites it but quotes only the elders.
- **Jude 6 (Direction 14):** NET wording, with the extent Baxter quotes. In the NET the angels are the object of the verb ("the angels who ... he has kept in eternal chains"), so the quotation starts with "..." and "the angels." The King James "reserved ... under darkness" is NET "kept ... in utter darkness, locked up." "Their first estate" and "their own habitation" are the NET "their proper domain" and "their own place of residence."
- **Footnote [232]:** printed "Matt. ix. 24; v. 40." We give it as "Matthew 9:24; 5:40." These two verses (the girl who is "asleep"; the tunic and coat) do not obviously bear on the incarnation, which is what the note is attached to; the references may be misprinted in the source. We have not tried to correct them.
- **Unmarked allusions (identifications are ours, not Baxter's, and tentative):** "made himself of no reputation" and "the form of a servant" (Philippians 2:7, the latter cited in [233]); "a place to lay his head" (Matthew 8:20; Luke 9:58); "seamless coat" (John 19:23); "pray for his murderers" (Luke 23:34); the apostles contending who was greatest (Luke 22:24; Mark 9:34); "become as little children" (Matthew 18:3); "scorn and offscouring" and "a gazing-stock to angels and to men" (1 Corinthians 4:9, 13); "the worm that never dies, and the fire that never shall be quenched" (Mark 9:48; Isaiah 66:24).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): Holy Ghost, Holy Spirit; Saviour, Savior; meanness, lowliness; brave it out (Direction 7), show off; ten thousand-fold, ten-thousand-fold (hyphenated only); malefactor, criminal; pose, baffle; impatiency, impatience; labour, labor; honour, honor; dishonouring, dishonoring; quarrelling, quarreling; loth, loath; christian, Christian; toys, trifles; discourse (as a verb: "carnally we discourse"), talk; vainly (we think), emptily; forward to (the business of the flesh), eager for; willinger, more willing; sensuality, indulgence in physical pleasure; scarce, scarcely; precedency, precedence; wont, accustomed; thou-forms and -eth/-est endings to modern forms throughout. "Nor knoweth not if he die" became "nor knows if he dies" (the double negative dropped; the sense is the same).
- **Kept as Baxter wrote:** "vanities" (empty, worthless things); "rooms" (positions or seats of honor, as in "the highest rooms"); "ado" (fuss: "much ado to own ourselves," that is, to acknowledge ourselves as ours); "security" (careless confidence); "gallants" (showy, fashionable men) and "wantons" (undisciplined, pleasure-loving people); "preferment" (advancement in rank or office); "wit" (intelligence); "renovation" (being made new by grace); "accommodations of their flesh" (the comforts of the body); "recreations of the flesh"; "offscouring" (filth scraped off, refuse); "gazing-stock" (a spectacle); "conversion" (as in Matthew 18:3); "interest" (here, one's claim or standing); "ere long" (before long); "condescend" (in its older, good sense, to stoop in kindness); "meditations"; "effectively" and "objectively" (scholastic terms: pride came from hell as its cause, yet hell, as an object of thought, may cure it).
- **Direction 12, "become that soul":** "become" is in the older sense "befit"; we changed "become" to "becomes" and kept the sense.
- **Direction 13:** the shift from "he" to "she" ("Is this the woman...") is Baxter's own.
- **Baxter's reference in [235]:** *Treatise of Self-ignorance* is one of Baxter's own works, as he says ("my"); we have not checked it further.
