# Chapter 6: Title 2, Directions 15–20 (our mercies, the judgment, afflictions, callings, ordinances, and the miserable world)

*In the margin: All our particular mercies.*

**Direction 15. Do not overlook that life full of particular mercies which God has bestowed on yourselves, and you will find pleasant and profitable matter for your thoughts.**

To spare me the labor of repeating them, look back to chapter 3, direction 14. Think of that mercy which brought you into the world, and chose your parents, your place, and your condition; which brought you up, and bore with you patiently in all your sins, and closely warned you of every danger: which afflicted you at the right time, and delivered you at the right time, and heard your prayers in many a distress: which has yet kept the worst of you from death and hell; and has regenerated, justified, adopted, and sanctified those that he has fitted for eternal life. How many sins he has forgiven! How many he has in part subdued! How many and suitable helps he has granted you! From how many enemies he has saved you! How often he has delighted you by his word and grace! What comforts you have had in his servants and ordinances, in your relations and callings! His mercies are innumerable, and yet do your meditations lack matter to supply them? If I should but recite the words of David in many thankful psalms, you would think mercy found his thoughts employment.

*In the margin: The account at judgment.*

**Direction 16. Foresee that exact and righteous judgment which shortly you have to undergo; and it will do much to find you employment for your thoughts.**

A man that must give an account to God of all that he has done, both good and evil, and does not know how soon, for all he knows before tomorrow, it seems to me should find himself something better than emptiness to think on! Is it nothing to be ready for so great a day? To have your justification ready? your accounts made up? your consciences cleansed and quieted on good grounds? To know what answer to make for yourselves against the accuser? To be clear and sure that you are indeed regenerate, and have a part in Christ, and are washed in his blood, and reconciled to God, and shall not prove hypocrites and self-deceivers in that trying day! when it is a sentence that must finally decide the question whether we shall be saved or damned; and must determine us to heaven or hell forever; and you have so short and uncertain a time for your preparation: will not this supply matter to your thoughts? If you were going to a trial for your lives, or all your estates, you would think it sufficient to provide you matter for your thoughts on the way. How much more this final, dreadful judgment!

*In the margin: Our afflictions.*

**Direction 17. If all this will not serve the purpose, it is strange if God does not call home your thoughts by sharp afflictions: and it seems to me the good use of them, and the removal of them, should find some employment for your thoughts.**

It is time then to "search and test your ways, and turn again to the Lord" (Lamentations 3:40). To find out the Achan that troubles your peace, and know the voice of the rod, and what God is angry at, and what it is that he calls you to mind! To know what root it is that bears these bitter fruits; and how they may be sanctified to make you conformed to Christ, and "share his holiness" (Hebrews 12:10). Besides the exercise of holy patience and submission, there is a great deal of work to be done in sufferings; to exercise faith, to honor God, and the good cause of our suffering, and to humble ourselves for the evil cause, and to get the benefit. And if you will not meditate on the duty, you shall meditate on the pain, whether you will or not; and say, as Lamentations 3:17–20, "I have forgotten what happiness is. So I said, 'My endurance has expired; I have lost all hope of deliverance from the Lord.' Remember my impoverished and homeless condition, which is a bitter poison. I continually think about this, and I am depressed." Do not put God to remind you by his spur, and help your meditations by so sharp a means! "So he caused them to die unsatisfied and filled with terror. When he struck them down, they sought his favor; they turned back and longed for God. They remembered that God was their protector and that God Most High was their deliverer" (Psalm 78:33–35).

*In the margin: The business of your callings.*

**Direction 18. Be diligent in your callings, and spend no time in idleness, and perform your labors with holy minds, to the glory of God, and in obedience to his commands, and then your thoughts will have the less leisure and liberty for emptiness or idleness.**

Employments of the body will employ the thoughts: they that have much to do have much to think on; for they must do it prudently, and skillfully, and carefully, that they may do it successfully; and therefore must think how to do it. And the urgency and necessity of business will almost force the thoughts, and so carry them on and find them work (though some employments more than others). And let none think that these thoughts are bad or empty because they are about worldly things; for if our labors themselves are not bad or empty, then neither are those thoughts which are needful to the well-doing of our work. Nor let any worldling please himself with this, and say, My thoughts are taken up about my calling; for his calling itself is perverted by him, and made a carnal work to carnal ends, when it should be sanctified. That the thoughts about your labors may be good, 1. Your labors themselves must be good, performed in obedience to God, and for the good of others, and to his glory. 2. Your labors and thoughts must keep their bounds, and the higher things must be always preferred, and sought, and thought on in the first place. And your labors must so far employ your thoughts as is needful to the well-doing of them; but better things must be thought on in such labors as leave a vacancy to the thoughts. But diligence in your calling is a very great help to keep out sinful thoughts, and to furnish us with thoughts which in their place are good.

*In the margin: All ordinances and means of grace.*

**Direction 19. You have all God's spiritual helps and holy ordinances to feed your meditations, and to enliven them, which should be used when your minds grow dull or barren.**

When your minds are empty, and you cannot pump up plentiful matter for holy thoughts, the reading of a timely book, or conversation with a full, experienced Christian, will furnish you with matter: so will the hearing of a profitable sermon: and sometimes prayer will do more than meditation. And weak-headed persons, of small knowledge and shallow memories, must fetch the matter of their meditations thus more frequently from reading and conversation than others need to do: as they can hold but little at a time, so they must go more often; as he that goes to the water with a spoon or a dish must go more often than they that go with a more capacious vessel. Others can carry a storehouse of meditation always about them; but persons of very small knowledge and memory must have their meditations fed by others, as infants by the spoon. Therefore a little and often is the best way, both for their reading or hearing, and for their holy thoughts. How great a mercy is it that weak Christians have such a store of helps; that when their heads are empty, they have books and friends that are not empty, from where they may fetch help as they need it; and that their hearts are not empty of the love of God, which inclines them to do more than their abilities enable them to do.

*In the margin: The miserable sinful world.*

**Direction 20. If all these do not sufficiently furnish your meditations, look through the world, and see what a multitude of miserable souls call for your compassion and daily prayers for their relief.**

Think on the many nations that lie in the darkness of idolatry and unbelief! It is not more than the sixth part of the world that are Christians of any sort. The other five parts are heathens, and Muslims, and some few Jews. And of this sixth part, it is but a small part that are reformed from popery, and such corruptions as the eastern and southern Christians also are too much defiled with. And in the reformed churches, how common are profaneness and worldliness, and how few are acquainted with the power of godliness! What abundance of ignorant and ungodly persons are there, who hate the power and practice of that religion which they profess themselves they hope to be saved by (as if they hoped to be saved for hating, persecuting, and disobeying it). And among those that seem more serious and obedient, how many are hypocrites! And how many are possessed with pride and self-conceitedness, which break forth into unruliness, contentions, and uncharitableness, factions, and divisions in the church! How many Christians are ignorant, emotional, weak, unprofitable, and too many scandalous! And how few are judicious, prudent, heavenly, charitable, peaceable, humble, meek, hardworking, and fruitful, who set themselves wholly to be good and to do good! And of these few, how few are there that are not exercised under heavy afflictions from God, or cruel persecutions from ungodly men! What tyranny is exercised by the Turk without, and the pope within, upon the sincerest followers of Christ! Set all this together, and tell me whether your compassionate thoughts or your prayers need to go out for lack of fuel or matter to feed upon from day to day?

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Every verse in quotation marks as NET was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where this range falls:** it is the last part of Title 2 (furnishing the mind with good thoughts) and begins at a new Direction (15). "Title" is Baxter's word for a main division of Chapter 6. The Direction headings are bolded as the first sentence of each Direction, as in the finished files; the following sentences continue Baxter's same paragraph, which is unbroken in the source.
- **"chapter 3, direction 14"** (Direction 15): Baxter's cross-reference ("chap. iii. direct. xiv."). We have not checked that it points to a passage on particular mercies.
- **Lamentations 3:40** (Direction 17): the source prints "Lam. iii. 4," but Lamentations 3:4 ("He has made my mortal skin waste away") does not contain the words; they are from 3:40, so we corrected the reference. Baxter adapts the verse to "your ways"; we keep his adaptation, modernized ("search and test your ways, and turn again to the Lord"), as his paraphrase. The NET reads: "Let us carefully examine our ways, and let us return to the Lord."
- **Hebrews 12:10:** NET "share his holiness"; the King James, which Baxter quotes, has "partakers of his holiness." The sense is the same.
- **Lamentations 3:17–20:** Baxter quotes from the second half of verse 17 to verse 20; NET wording is used from that point. The NET changes the sense he leans on in two places. (1) King James verse 19 reads "Remembering mine affliction and my misery, the wormwood and the gall," a participle describing the sufferer's own brooding; the NET reads it as a plea addressed to God, "Remember my impoverished and homeless condition, which is a bitter poison." (2) King James verse 20, "My soul hath them still in remembrance, and is humbled in me," is Baxter's main point (affliction forces the mind to dwell on its pain); the NET's "I continually think about this, and I am depressed" keeps the point of continual thinking, but "depressed" is the NET's word and should not be read as a modern diagnosis put in Baxter's mouth. King James verse 17b "I forgat prosperity" is NET "I have forgotten what happiness is"; verse 18 "My strength and my hope is perished from the Lord" is NET "My endurance has expired; I have lost all hope of deliverance from the Lord."
- **Psalm 78:33–35:** NET wording. The NET differs from the King James Baxter quotes: verse 33 King James "Therefore did he consume their days in vanity, and their years in trouble" is NET "So he caused them to die unsatisfied and filled with terror"; verse 34 King James "returned and inquired early after God" is NET "turned back and longed for God"; verse 35 King James "God was their rock, and the high God their Redeemer" is NET "God was their protector and that God Most High was their deliverer." King James verse 34 opens "when he slew them"; the NET has "When he struck them down." Baxter's point (people seek God only when afflicted) is kept in both.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "the Achan that troubles your peace" (Joshua 7:25; 1 Chronicles 2:7); "the voice of the rod" (Micah 6:9); giving an account of what one has done, good and evil (Romans 14:12; 2 Corinthians 5:10); "the accuser" (Revelation 12:10); "washed in his blood" (Revelation 1:5). These references are ours, not in Baxter's text.
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** "overlook not" to "do not overlook"; "seasonably afflicted ... seasonably delivered" to "afflicted at the right time ... delivered at the right time"; "vouchsafed" to "granted"; "oft" to "often"; "want" to "lack" (Directions 15 and 20) and to "need" (Direction 19, "as they want it"); "methinks" to "it seems to me"; "for aught he knows" to "for all he knows"; "vanity" to "emptiness" and "vain" to "empty"; "administer matter" to "supply matter"; "a judgment for your lives" to "a trial for your lives" (a court trial is meant); "by the way" to "on the way"; "serve the turn" to "serve the purpose"; "improvement of them" to "the good use of them" (to "improve" an affliction meant to profit spiritually by it); "try" to "test"; "conformable" to "conformed"; "meditate of" to "meditate on"; "Put not God to remember you" to "Do not put God to remind you" ("remember" here meant "remind"); "necessitate the thoughts" to "force the thoughts"; "still preferred" and "still about them" to "always" ("still" meant always); "quicken" to "enliven"; "seasonable book" to "timely book"; "conference" to "conversation" (talk with another person); "ofter" to "more often"; "from whence" to "from where"; "parts" to "abilities"; "infidelity" to "unbelief"; "Mahometans" to "Muslims"; "passionate" to "emotional" (see GLOSSARY.md); "laborious" to "hardworking"; "thy" to "your."
- **callings** (Directions 15 and 18): one's vocation or daily occupation, kept. **worldling:** a worldly person, kept. **estates** (Direction 16): property and possessions. **"leave a vacancy to the thoughts"** (Direction 18): kept; Baxter means labors that do not take up the whole mind and leave it free for better thoughts. **"a full, experienced Christian"** (Direction 19): "full" means well stocked in knowledge and experience; the source has no comma ("a full experienced christian"), and the comma is ours. **"what it is that he calls you to mind"** (Direction 17): kept; that is, what God is calling you to think about. **scandalous** (Direction 20): giving offense to others, causing them to stumble.
- **Baxter's world estimate** (Direction 20): that Christians of any sort are no more than a sixth of the world, the rest being heathens, Muslims, and a few Jews, is his own 17th-century estimate, kept as he gives it.
- **popery; "the Turk without, and the pope within"** (Direction 20): Baxter's polemic against Rome, kept as he wrote it. "Popery" is his word for Roman Catholicism; "the Turk" is his term, probably for the Ottoman power, persecuting Christians from outside Christendom, as the pope (in his view) did from within it. "The eastern and southern Christians" are probably the Eastern Orthodox and the churches of Africa and the Near East; Baxter does not name them.
