# Chapter 2: Direction 17

**Direction 17. Be careful that you do not receive a doctrine of libertinism as from the gospel; nor think of Christ as an encourager of sin; nor claim free grace as cover for your carnal security or sloth; for this is but to set up another gospel, and another Christ, or rather the doctrine and works of the devil, against Christ and the gospel, and to turn the grace of God into wantonness.**

Because the devil knows that you will not receive his doctrine in his own name, his usual method is to put it forward and preach it in the name of Christ, which he knows you reverence and regard. For if Satan concealed not his own name and hand in every temptation, it would spoil his game; and the more excellent and splendid his claim is, the more powerful the temptation is. [73] Those who gave heed to deceiving spirits and teachings of devils no doubt thought better of the spirits and the doctrines, especially seeming strict, (for the devil has his strictnesses,) as those who "prohibit marriage and require abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving" (1 Timothy 4:1, 3). But the strictnesses of the devil are always intended to make men loose. They shall be strict as the Pharisees in traditions and empty ceremonies, and building the tombs of the prophets, and decorating the sepulchres of the righteous, that they may hate and murder the living saints who worship God in spirit and in truth. Licentiousness is the proper doctrine of the devil, which all his strictness tends to promote. To receive such principles is pernicious; but to attribute them to Christ and the gospel is blasphemous.

The libertines, antinomians, and autonomians of this age have gathered you too many examples. The libertine says, "The heart is the man; therefore you may deny the truth with your tongue, you may be present at false worship, (as at the mass,) you need not suffer to avoid the speaking of a word, or subscribing to an untruth or error, or doing some little thing; but as long as you keep your hearts to God, and mean well, or have an honest mental reservation, and are forced to it by others, rather than suffer, you may say, or subscribe, or swear anything which you can yourselves put a lawful sense upon in your own minds, or comply with any outward actions or customs to avoid offense and save yourselves."

The antinomians tell you that "The moral law is abrogated, and that the gospel is no law; (and if there be no law, there is no governor nor government, no duty, no sin, no judgment, no punishment, no reward); that the elect are justified before they are born, or repent, or believe; that their sin is pardoned before it is committed; that God took them as suffering and fulfilling all the law in Christ, as if it had been they who did it in him: that we are justified by faith only in our consciences: that justifying faith is but the believing that we are justified: that every man must believe that he is pardoned, that he may be pardoned in his conscience; and this he is to do by a divine faith, and that this is the sense of the article, 'I believe the forgiveness of sins,' that is, that my sins are forgiven; and that all are forgiven who believe it: that it is legal and sinful to work or do anything for salvation: that sin once pardoned need not be confessed and lamented, or at least, we need not ask pardon of sin daily, or of one sin often: that chastisements are no punishments; and yet no other punishment is threatened to believers for their sins; and consequently that Christ has not obtained for them a pardon of any sin after believing, but forestalled all necessity of pardon; and therefore they must not ask pardon of them, nor do anything to obtain it: that fear of hell must have no hand in our obedience, or restraint from sin. And some add, that he who cannot repent or believe must comfort himself that Christ repented and believed for him (a contradiction)." [74] Many such doctrines of licentiousness the abusers of grace have brought forth.

And the sect which imitates the father of pride in striving to be free from the government of God, and to be the lawgivers and rulers of themselves and all others, (which I therefore call the autonomians,) are licentious and much more. They equally contend against Christ's government, and for their own: they fill the world with wars and bloodshed, oppression and cruelty, and the ears of God with the cries of the martyrs and oppressed ones; and all that the spiritual and holy discipline of Christ may be suppressed, and seriousness in religion made odious, or banished from the earth, and that themselves may be taken for the center, and pillars, and lawgivers of the church, and the consciences of all men may be taught to cast off all scruples or fears of offending God, in comparison of offending them; and may absolutely submit to them; and never hesitate at any feared disobedience to Christ: they are the scorners and persecutors of strict obedience to the laws of God, and take those who fear his judgments to be men frightened out of their wits; and that to obey him exactly (which, alas! who can do, when he has done his best) is but to be hypocritical or too precise: but to question their domination, or break their laws, (imposed on the world, even on kings and states, without any authority,) this must be taken for heresy, schism, or a rebellion, like that of Korah and his company. This Luciferian spirit of the proud autonomians has filled the Christian world with bloodshed, and been the greatest means of the miseries of the earth, and especially of hindering and persecuting the gospel, and setting up a Pharisaical religion in the world: it has fought against the gospel, and filled with blood the countries of France, Savoy, Rhaetia, Bohemia, Belgia, Helvetia, Polonia, Hungary, Germany, and many more; that it may appear how much of the Satanic nature they have, and how exactly they fulfill his will.

And natural corruption contains in it the seeds of all these damnable heresies: nothing more natural to fallen man than to shake off the government of God, and to become a lawgiver to himself, and as many others as he can; and to turn the grace of God into wantonness. Therefore the profane, who never heard it from any heretics but themselves, make themselves such a creed as this, that "God is merciful, and therefore we need not fear his threatenings, for he will be better than his word: it belongs to him to save us, and not to us, and therefore we may cast our souls upon his care, though we care not for them ourselves. If he has predestined us to salvation, we shall be saved; and if he has not, we shall not; whatever we do, or how well soever we live. Christ died for sinners, and therefore though we are sinners, he will save us. God is stronger than the devil, and therefore the devil shall not have the most: That which pleases the flesh, and does God no harm, can never be so great a matter, or so much offend him, as to procure our damnation. What need of so much fuss to be saved, or so much haste to turn to God, when anyone who at last does but repent, and cry for God's mercy, and believe that Christ died for him, shall be saved? Christ is the Savior of the world, and his grace is very great and free, and therefore God forbid that none should be saved but those few who are of strict and holy lives, and make so much fuss for heaven. No man can know who shall be saved, and who shall not; and therefore it is the wisest way to do nobody any harm, and to live merrily, and trust God with our souls, and put our salvation to the gamble: nobody is saved for his own works or deserving; and therefore our lives may do just as well as if they were more strict and holy." This is the creed of the ungodly; by which you may see how natural it is to them to abuse the gospel, and plead God's grace to quiet and strengthen them in their sin, and to embolden themselves on Christ to disobey him.

But this is but to set Christ against himself; even his merits and mercy against his government and Spirit; and to set his death against the ends of his death; and to set our Savior against our salvation; and to run from God and rebel against him, because Christ died to recover us to God, and to give us repentance unto life; and to sin, because he died to save his people from their sins, and "to purify for himself a people who are truly his, who are eager to do good" (Matthew 1:21; Titus 2:14). "The one who practices sin is of the devil because the devil has been sinning from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was revealed: to destroy the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8; John 8:44).

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows the Latin in parentheses; the translation was prepared for this project and is not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check it before relying on fine points.

[73] Siquis est hoc robore animi, atque hac indole virtutis ac continentiæ, ut respuat omnes voluptates, omnemque vitæ suæ cursum labore corporis, atque in animi contentione conficiat, quem non quies, non remissio, non æqualium studia, non ludi, non convivia delectant; nihil in vita expetendum putet nisi quod est cum laude et honore conjunctum; hunc mea sententia divinis quibusdam bonis instructum atque ornatum puto. Cic. pro Cæl. (English: If anyone has such strength of mind and such a disposition of virtue and self-restraint that he rejects all pleasures, and spends the whole course of his life in bodily labor and mental exertion; one whom neither rest, nor relaxation, nor the pursuits of his equals, nor games, nor banquets delight; who thinks nothing in life worth seeking except what is joined with praise and honor: this man, in my opinion, I judge to be equipped and adorned with certain divine gifts. Cicero, *Pro Caelio*.)

[74] For sound principles in these points, read Mr. Gibbon's Sermon of Justification, in the Morning Exercises at St. Giles'; and Mr. Truman's two books before named, and Le Blank's Theses in Latin, with the Thes. Salmuriens. etc.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **1 Timothy 4:1, 3:** NET wording, fetched, for the quoted clause. Baxter's quotation begins "as forbidding to marry, and abstinence from meats which God hath created..." (King James). Because the NET reads "They will prohibit marriage and require abstinence from foods...," we kept his "as" outside the quotation and wrote "as those who 'prohibit marriage...'" so the sentence still reads; this adds "those who" to carry the grammar. The sense he relies on (the devil's strictness, in forbidding marriage and foods) is unchanged. His unquoted "seducing spirits and doctrines of devils" echoes 1 Timothy 4:1; we wrote "deceiving spirits and teachings of devils," following the NET's "deceiving spirits and demonic teachings" in part. The NET's "later times" and "desert the faith" are not quoted by Baxter.
- **Matthew 1:21; Titus 2:14:** Baxter's unquoted "to save his people from their sins" is Matthew 1:21 (the NET has the same sense). His quoted words are Titus 2:14 in the King James ("to purify a peculiar people to himself zealous of good works"). NET: "to purify for himself a people who are truly his, who are eager to do good." We moved his "and" outside the quotation marks. The NET gives up "peculiar" and "zealous," but his point (Christ died to make a people for himself who are eager for good works) is kept. The NET's "eager" is somewhat milder than the King James "zealous."
- **1 John 3:8; John 8:44:** NET wording. Baxter's "committeth sin" is the NET's "practices sin," and "sinneth from the beginning" is "has been sinning from the beginning"; both suggest ongoing practice, which fits his use. His reference to John 8:44 is printed after the quotation; in the NET, John 8:44 says the devil was "a murderer from the beginning" and a liar, and does not use the word "sin" there. We kept Baxter's reference as printed and flag that it supports his point about the devil's origin more loosely than 1 John 3:8 does.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "turn the grace of God into wantonness" (probably Jude 4; no reference in Baxter's text); "building the tombs of the prophets, and garnishing the sepulchres of the righteous" (apparently Matthew 23:29); "worship God in spirit and in truth" (John 4:24); "a rebellion, like that of Korah and his company" (Numbers 16); "I believe the forgiveness of sins" (the Apostles' Creed); "repentance unto life" (apparently Acts 11:18; not referenced by Baxter); "the father of pride" (Satan).
- **libertinism; libertines:** Baxter's word for the teaching, and those who hold it, that outward conduct or compliance does not matter if the heart is right, or that grace removes the need to obey. Kept.
- **antinomians:** those who teach that believers are not under the moral law. Kept. **autonomians:** Baxter's own coinage ("self-law-makers," from his explanation in the text), for those who claim the right to rule themselves and others without God's authority. Kept.
- **security** (in the heading): careless confidence, as in earlier sections. **carnal; sloth; wantonness:** kept. **claim free grace as cover for:** Baxter's "pretend free grace for." **think of:** his "conceive of." **seducing** (spirits): our "deceiving," following the NET; **doctrines of devils:** our "teachings of devils." **be careful:** his "take heed."
- **men loose:** "loose" here means morally lax. Kept. **suffer to avoid** (libertine's speech): "suffer" here means to endure suffering or penalty, not "allow"; kept. **subscribing:** signing one's name to a statement. Kept. **mental reservation:** the practice of silently adding a qualification so that a spoken statement is not a lie in one's own mind. Kept as Baxter's term.
- **abrogated; justified; legal:** kept. In the antinomian list, "legal" means "of the law" in the sense of working under the law for salvation. **divine faith:** kept; Baxter reports the antinomian claim that justifying faith is a supernatural faith in one's own pardon.
- **chastisements:** Baxter's "castigations." **forestalled:** his "prevented" (in the older sense of "gone before and made unneeded"). **often:** his "oft."
- **affecting** (now "striving"): Baxter's "affecting" means aiming at or seeking after. **precise:** a term of abuse for strict religion in his day; kept. **Luciferian:** after Lucifer, a name for Satan; kept. **Pharisaical:** kept, with capital.
- **Place names in the list** are Baxter's: "Rhaetia" (Rhætia), "Belgia," "Helvetia," and "Polonia" are Latin names (roughly the Alpine region, the Low Countries, Switzerland, and Poland). We have kept them as printed. The charge that the "autonomians" caused these wars is Baxter's polemic, kept as he wrote it.
- **natural corruption; fallen man:** Baxter's "lapsed man." **damnable:** kept. **profane:** kept; as Baxter uses it, those who show no regard for holy things. **cry for God's mercy:** his "cry God mercy." **put our salvation to the gamble:** his "put our salvation upon the venture." **fuss:** his "ado." **do just as well:** his "serve the turn." **deserving:** his "deservings." **predestined:** his "predestinated." **embolden themselves on Christ:** left as written (to take courage from Christ's death to disobey).
- **attribute them to Christ:** Baxter's "father them upon Christ."
- **Cicero, *Pro Caelio*** (note 73): Baxter's abbreviation "Cic. pro Cæl." probably means Cicero's speech *Pro Caelio* (we have not confirmed the passage). The passage describes a person of exceptional self-denial; the footnote's marker comes after Baxter's remark about the devil's splendid claims, and we have not confirmed how he means it to bear on that point. "Siquis" is printed as one word. **æqualium:** "of equals," that is, of one's peers.
- **Note 74:** the works named are Baxter's references, not checked. "Mr. Gibbon's Sermon of Justification, in the Morning Exercises at St. Giles'" probably refers to a sermon in a published collection of "Morning Exercises" (we have not confirmed which); "Mr. Truman's two books before named" refers to books named earlier in the Directory (not located here). "Thes. Salmuriens." is printed as an abbreviation, probably for *Theses Salmurienses* (theses from the Academy of Saumur); we have not confirmed it. Left as printed.
