# Chapter 3: Grand Direction VII (part 2 of 2)

*This section begins in the middle of Direction 5 (Fix your eye upon Christ himself as your pattern), at its numbered point 16, and the numbering continues from the previous file. It then runs through Directions 6 to 12 and ends with a margin note that introduces Grand Direction 8.*

16. Remember also that Christ was against the Pharisees' external, hypocritical, ceremonial worship, consisting in lip-labor, artificial repetitions, and much babbling; their "Do not handle! Do not taste! Do not touch!" and worshiping God in vain, according to their traditions, "teaching as doctrines the commandments of men." He taught us a serious, spiritual worship: not "to draw near to God with our mouth, and honor him with our lips, while our hearts are far from him;" but to "worship God who is a Spirit, in spirit and truth" (Matthew 15:6–9; John 4:23–24; Matthew 23).

17. Christ was a sharp reprover of hypocritical, blind, ceremonial, malicious Pharisees; and warns his disciples to beware of their leaven. When they are offended with him, he says, "Every plant that my heavenly Father did not plant will be uprooted. Leave them! They are blind guides," and so on (Matthew 15:12–14). To teach us to beware of autonomous, haughty, domineering, formal hypocrites, and false teachers, and to distinguish between the shepherds and the wolves.

18. Though Christ seems cautiously to avoid acknowledging the Romans' usurpation over the Jews, yet rather than offend them he pays tribute himself (Matthew 17:25–27), and tells them to "give to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's" (Matthew 22:21). The Pharisees bring their controversy to him hypocritically, "Is it right to pay taxes to Caesar or not?" (For that Caesar was a usurper over them, they took to be past controversy.) And Christ would give them no answer that should ensnare himself, or encourage usurpation, or lend support to their sedition: teaching us much more to pay tribute cheerfully to our lawful governors, and to avoid all sedition and offense.

19. Yet he is accused, condemned, and executed among criminals, as aspiring to be "King of the Jews," and the judge was called "no friend of Caesar" if he let him go: teaching us to expect that the most innocent Christians should be accused, as enemies to the rulers of the world, and mistaken governors be provoked and set against them, by the malicious slanders of their adversaries; and that we should, in this unrighteous world, be condemned of those crimes of which we are most innocent, and which we most abhor, and have borne the fullest testimonies against.

20. The furious mob of the enraged people mocks him by their words and deeds, with a purple robe, a scepter of reed, a crown of thorns, and the scornful name of "King of the Jews;" they spit in his face, and strike him, and then break jests upon him; and in all this "when he was maligned, he did not answer back; ... but committed himself to God who judges justly" (1 Peter 2:21–23). Teaching us to expect the rage of the ignorant rabble, as well as of deluded governors; and to be made the scorn of the worst of men; and all this without impatience, reviling, or threatening words; but quieting ourselves in the sure expectation of the righteous judgment, which we and they must shortly find.

21. When Christ is urged at Pilate's bar to speak for himself, he keeps silent: teaching us to expect to be questioned at the judgment-seat of man; and not to be overly anxious about the vindicating of our names from their most odious slanders, because the judgment that will fully justify us is sure and near.

22. When Christ is in his agony, his disciples fail him; when he is judged and crucified, they "left him and fled" (Matthew 26:56): to teach us not to be too confident in the best of men, nor to expect much from them in a time of trial, but to take our comfort in God alone, when all our nearest friends shall fail us.

23. Upon the cross he suffers the torments and disgrace of death for us, praying for his murderers: "leaving an example for you to follow in his steps" (1 Peter 2:21); and that we do not think life itself too dear to part with, in obedience to God, and for the love of Christ and one another (1 John 3:16); and that we forgive and pray for them that persecute us.

24. In all this suffering from men, he feels also so much of the fruit of our sin upon his soul, that he cries out, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" to teach us, if we fall into such calamity of soul, as to think that God himself forsakes us, to remember for our support, that the Son of God himself before us, cried out, "My God, why have you forsaken me?" and that in this also we may expect a trial, to seem to ourselves forsaken by God, when our Savior underwent the like before us.

I will cite no more of his example, because I do not want to be tedious. Hither now let believers cast their eyes: if you love your Lord, you should love to imitate him, and be glad to find yourselves in the way that he has gone before you. If he lived a worldly or a pleasure-loving life, do the same: if he was an enemy to preaching, and praying, and holy living, be so yourselves: but if he lived in the greatest contempt of all the wealth, and honors, and pleasures of the world, in a life of holy obedience to his Father, wholly preferring the kingdom of heaven, and seeking the salvation of the souls of others, and patiently bearing persecution, derision, slanders, and death, then take up your cross, and follow him in joyfully to the expected crown.

**Direction 6. If you will learn from Christ, you must learn from his ministers, whom he has appointed under him to be the teachers of his church.**

He purposely enables them, inclines them, and sends them to instruct you: not to have dominion over your faith, but to be your spiritual fathers, and "the ministers by whom you believe, as God shall give" (ability and success) "to every one" as he pleases; "to plant and water," while "God gives the increase; to open men's eyes, and turn them from darkness to light;" and to be "laborers together with God, whose husbandry and building you are;" and to be "workers with you for your joy." See 2 Corinthians 1:24; Acts 26:17–18; 1 Corinthians 3:5–9; 4:15. Seeing therefore Christ has appointed them, under him, to be the ordinary teachers of his church, he that "hears them," (speaking his message,) "hears him," and he "that despises them, despises him" (Luke 10:16). And he that says, I will hear Christ, but not you, does say in effect to Christ himself, I will not hear you, nor learn from you, unless you will dismiss your ushers, and teach me directly yourself.

**Direction 7. Listen also to the secret teachings of his Spirit, and your consciences, not as making you any new law or duty, or being to you instead of Scriptures or ministers; but as bringing that truth into your hearts and practices, which Scriptures and ministers have first brought to your eyes and ears.**

If you do not understand this, how the office of Scripture and ministers differs from the office of the Spirit and your consciences, you will be confused, as the sectarians of these times have been, that separate what God has joined together, and plead against Scripture or ministers under claim of extolling the Spirit, or the light within them. As your food must be taken into the stomach, and pass the first digestion, before the second can be performed, and chylification must be before sanguification; so the Scripture and ministers must bring truth to your eyes and ears, before the Spirit or conscience bring them to your hearts and practice. But they lie dead and ineffective in your brain or imagination, if you do not listen to the secret teachings of the Spirit and conscience, which would bring them further. As Christ is the principal Teacher outside us, and ministers are but under him; so the Spirit is the principal teacher within us, and conscience is but under the Spirit, being stirred up and informed by it. Those that learn only from Scriptures and ministers, (by hearing or reading,) may become men of learning and great ability, though they do not listen to the sanctifying teachings of the Spirit, or to their consciences. But it is only those that listen first to the Scriptures and ministers, and next to the Spirit of God, and to their consciences, that have an inward, sanctifying, saving knowledge, and are they that are said to be taught by God. Therefore, listen first with your ears, to what Christ has to say to you from outside; and then listen daily and diligently with your hearts, to what the Spirit and conscience say within. For it is their office to preach over all that again to your hearts, which you have received.

**Direction 8. It being the office of the present ordinary ministry, only to expound and apply the doctrine of Christ already recorded in the Scriptures, believe not any man that contradicts this recorded doctrine, whatever reason, authority, or revelation he may claim.**

Isaiah 8:20, "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to these, it is because there is no light in them." No reason can be reason indeed that is claimed against the reason of the Creator and God of reason. Authority claimed against the highest authority of God, is no authority: God never gave authority to any against himself; nor to deceive men's souls; nor to dispense with the law of Christ; nor to warrant men to sin against him; nor to make any supplements to his law or doctrine. The apostles had their authority "for building up, not for tearing down" (2 Corinthians 10:8; 13:10). There is no revelation from God that is contrary to his own revelation, already delivered as his perfect law and rule to the church; and therefore none supplemental to it. If an "apostle or an angel from heaven (*per possibile vel impossibile*; English: supposing it possible, or even impossible) shall evangelize to us besides what is evangelized," and we "have received," he must be held "accursed" (Galatians 1:6–8).

**Direction 9. Come not to learn from Christ with self-conceitedness, pride, or confidence in your prejudice and errors; but as little children, with humble, teachable, tractable minds.**

Christ is no teacher for those that in their own eyes are wise enough already: unless it is first to teach them to "become foolish" (in their own esteem, because they are so indeed) "so that [they] can become wise" (1 Corinthians 3:18). They that are already filled with false opinions, and resolve that they will never be persuaded of the contrary, are unfit to be scholars in the school of Christ. "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (1 Peter 5:5). Men that have a high notion of their own understandings, and think they can easily know truth from falsehood, as soon as they hear it, and come not to learn, but to censure what they hear or read, as being able at once to judge of all, these are fitter for the school of the prince of pride, and father of lies and error, than for the school of Christ. Except conversion makes men as little children, that come not to carp and cavil, but to learn, they are not "fit for the kingdom of Christ" (Matthew 18:3; John 3:3, 5). Know how blind and ignorant you are, and how dull of learning, and humbly beg of the heavenly Teacher, that he will accept you, and illuminate you: and give up your understandings completely to be informed by him, and your hearts to be the tablets on which his Spirit shall write his law; believing his doctrine on the sole ground of his infallible truthfulness, and resolving to obey it; and this is to be the disciples of Christ indeed, and such as shall be taught by God.

**Direction 10. Come to the school of Christ with honest, willing hearts, that love the truth, and would gladly know it, that they may obey it; and not with false and biased hearts, which secretly hinder the understanding from receiving the truth, because they do not love it, as being contrary to their carnal inclinations and interest.**

The word that was received into honest hearts was that which was like the seed that brought forth plentifully (Matthew 13:23). When the heart says sincerely, "Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening; teach me to know and do your will;" God will not leave such a learner in the dark. Most of the damnable ignorance and error of the world, is from a wicked heart, that perceives that the truth of God is against their fleshly interest and lusts, and therefore is unwilling to obey it, and unwilling to believe it, lest it torment them, because they disobey it. A will that is secretly poisoned with the love of the world, or of any sinful lusts and pleasures, is the most powerful hindrance to the believing of the truth.

**Direction 11. Learn with quietness and peace in the school of Christ, and make not divisions, and meddle not with others' lessons and matters, but with your own.**

Silence, and quietness, and minding your own business, is the way to profit. The turbulent wranglers that are quarreling with others, and are religious contentiously, in envy and strife, are more likely to be corrected or ejected, than to be edified. Read James 3.

**Direction 12. Remember that the school of Christ has a rod; and therefore learn with fear and reverence (Hebrews 12:28–29; Philippians 2:12).**

Christ will sharply rebuke his own, if they grow negligent and offend: and if he should cast you out and forsake you, you are undone forever. "Take care," therefore, "not to refuse the one who is speaking! For if they did not escape when they refused the one who warned them on earth, how much less shall we, if we reject the one who warns from heaven?" (Hebrews 12:25). For "how will we escape if we neglect such a great salvation? It was first communicated through the Lord and was confirmed to us by those who heard him, while God confirmed their witness with signs and wonders and various miracles and gifts of the Holy Spirit distributed according to his will?" (Hebrews 2:3–4). "Serve the Lord in fear. Repent in terror. Give sincere homage. Otherwise he will be angry, and you will die because of your behavior, when his anger quickly ignites" (Psalm 2:11–12).

*In the margin: To obey Christ as Physician in his healing work, and his spirit in its cleansing, mortifying work.*

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Latin:** the only Latin is *per possibile vel impossibile* (Direction 8). It is printed as Baxter has it. The English is a draft prepared for this project, rendered loosely (the sense: "supposing the possible, or even the impossible"), and needs a Latinist's check.
- **Colossians 2:21 (point 16, unmarked):** Baxter's "Touch not, taste not, handle not" is the King James order. The NET reads "Do not handle! Do not taste! Do not touch!" in the reverse order. We quote the NET. The reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **Matthew 15:9 (point 16):** NET "teaching as doctrines the commandments of men" (King James "for doctrines"). Same sense.
- **Matthew 15:8 and John 4:24 (point 16):** Baxter's "to draw nigh to God with our mouth, and honour him with our lips, while our hearts are far from him" is his own blend and paraphrase of Matthew 15:8 (itself from Isaiah), changed to "our." His "worship God who is a Spirit, in spirit and truth" is a paraphrase of John 4:24. We kept his wording, modernized. NET Matthew 15:8: "This people honors me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me." NET John 4:24: "God is spirit, and the people who worship him must worship in spirit and truth." (The NET has "God is spirit," without "a." Baxter's argument does not depend on the article.)
- **Matthew 15:13–14 (point 17):** NET wording. Baxter has "they be blind leaders of the blind." The NET has "They are blind guides" in the sentence we quote (the NET goes on, "If someone who is blind leads another who is blind, both will fall into a pit"); the "leaders of the blind" wording is not in the quoted part. Baxter's "&c." is rendered "and so on." **beware of their leaven** is an unmarked allusion (compare Matthew 16:6; the NET has "yeast"). **autonomous:** kept as printed; it seems to mean self-governing or answerable to no one, and we have not confirmed his precise sense. **distinguish:** Baxter's "difference" (as a verb). **haughty:** his "supercilious." **cautiously:** his "cautelously." **lend support to:** his "countenance."
- **Matthew 22:21 (point 18):** NET has "give to Caesar" where the King James has "render." Baxter's "render" is in his quotation; the sense is the same. **Matthew 22:17 (unmarked):** Baxter's "Whether it be lawful to give tribute to Cæsar, or not?" is rendered with the NET, "Is it right to pay taxes to Caesar or not?" The NET has "taxes" for "tribute"; Baxter's own prose uses "tribute," which we kept. The reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **Unmarked allusions in points 19–24** (kept in Baxter's wording): "King of the Jews" (the NET at Matthew 27:11 has "the king of the Jews"); the judge told "none of Cæsar's friend" if he released Jesus (John 19:12; the NET has "you are no friend of Caesar"; we use the NET phrase and Baxter's "was called," which supplies the verb); "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" (Matthew 27:46; NET "why have you forsaken me?"). The purple robe, reed, and crown of thorns are the Passion accounts.
- **1 Peter 2:21–23 (point 20):** NET "committed himself to God who judges justly." Baxter has "committed all to him that judgeth righteously." His "all" is not in the NET (and not in the King James either, which has "committed himself"). The "..." skips the NET's "when he suffered, he threatened no retaliation."
- **Matthew 26:56 (point 22):** NET "left him and fled" for Baxter's "forsook him and fled." The sense is close, but "forsook" carries a note of abandonment that "left" lacks. Flagged, not important for his argument, which is that the disciples failed.
- **1 Peter 2:21 (point 23):** NET "leaving an example for you to follow in his steps." The King James, and Baxter, have "us." The NET's "you" differs from his pronoun, and we have not checked why. Baxter's "that we should follow" turns on "us"; the NET addresses its readers as "you." Flagged. **1 John 3:16:** cited only; it is the verse on laying down our lives for one another.
- **to seem to ourselves forsaken by God (point 24):** Baxter's "to seem of ourselves forsaken of God." We read "of ourselves" as "to ourselves"; the grammar is old and we have not confirmed it. **follow him in joyfully:** kept as Baxter has it.
- **Direction 5 numbering:** points 16-24 continue Baxter's numbered list under Direction 5, which began in the previous file. The paragraph beginning "I will cite no more" closes Direction 5.
- **Direction 6, 1 Corinthians 3:5–9 and Acts 26:18:** Baxter's phrases here are King James wording adapted to his sentence: "the ministers by whom you believe, as God shall give … to every one," "to plant and water," "God gives the increase," and "laborers together with God, whose husbandry and building you are." We kept his words (modernized) and quote them as his, because the NET does not fit his syntax. NET 1 Corinthians 3:5: "Servants through whom you came to believe, and each of us in the ministry the Lord gave us." NET 3:6: "I planted, Apollos watered, but God caused it to grow." NET 3:9: "We are coworkers belonging to God. You are God's field, God's building." **Flagged:** Baxter leans on "laborers together with God" (King James spelling "labourers") (fellow workers with God). The NET has "coworkers belonging to God," which can be read as "workers who belong to God," not "working together with God." His "husbandry" is "field" (farmland). We quote the NET of Acts 26:18 only in part: Baxter's "to open men's eyes, and turn them from darkness to light" is kept as his; the NET has "to open their eyes so that they turn from darkness to light."
- **2 Corinthians 1:24 (Direction 6):** Baxter prints "2 Cor. ii. 4" but the phrase "helpers of your joy" is not in that verse (2:4 is about Paul's tears). The phrase matches 2 Corinthians 1:24, so we corrected the reference and say so here. The NET reads "workers with you for your joy," which we quote in place of "helpers of your joy." The NET context also says "we do not mean that we rule over your faith," which matches Baxter's "not to have dominion over your faith."
- **Luke 10:16 (Direction 6):** Baxter changes the persons ("hears them … hears him … despises them … despises him") to fit his sentence, and glosses "(speaking his message)." We kept his wording, modernized. NET: "The one who listens to you listens to me, and the one who rejects you rejects me." The NET has "rejects" where Baxter has "despises." His "despises" is also in the next sentence in his argument, so the shift matters slightly. Flagged.
- **sectarians (Direction 7):** Baxter's "sectaries," the separatist groups of his day. **chylification … sanguification:** old medical terms. Chylification is the making of chyle, the milky fluid in digestion, and sanguification the making of blood from it. He uses them as an image for stages in taking in truth. Kept as printed, with "concoction" modernized to "digestion." **taught by God:** Baxter's "taught of God," an unmarked allusion to John 6:45; the NET has "taught by God."
- **Isaiah 8:20 (Direction 8; important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to these, it is because there is no light in them." The NET reads quite differently: "Then you must recall the Lord's instructions and the prophetic testimony of what would happen. Certainly they say such things because their minds are spiritually darkened." The NET has no "if they speak not according to this word," and the NET reads "recall," not "to the law and to the testimony." Baxter's use of the text as a test for what teachers say rests on the King James wording. We kept his wording, modernized, and flagged it as not matching the NET.
- **2 Corinthians 10:8; 13:10 (Direction 8):** Baxter prints "1 Cor. x. 8; 2 Cor. xiii. 10." 1 Corinthians 10:8 is about immorality and has nothing about authority, so we read it as a misprint for 2 Corinthians 10:8, and corrected the reference. The NET reads "building you up and not for tearing you down" (10:8) and "for building up, not for tearing down" (13:10). We quote the latter. Baxter's "power" is the NET's "authority," and "edification … destruction" are "building up … tearing down." Same sense.
- **Galatians 1:6–8 (Direction 8; important):** Baxter's quotation is his paraphrase of the King James: "apostle or an angel from heaven … shall evangelize to us besides what is evangelized," "have received" (a phrase from verse 9), and "accursed." We kept his wording and put the NET in this note. NET 1:8: "But even if we (or an angel from heaven) should preach a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be condemned to hell!" NET 1:9: "if any one is preaching to you a gospel contrary to what you received, let him be condemned to hell!" **Flagged:** Baxter's argument is that nothing may be added to ("besides") the recorded doctrine, so he leans on "besides." The NET says "contrary to," which forbids a contradicting gospel and does not by itself forbid a supplementary one. The NET also has "condemned to hell" where the King James has "accursed." **evangelize:** Baxter uses it as a verb for preaching the gospel.
- **1 Corinthians 3:18 (Direction 9):** NET "let him become foolish so that he can become wise." Baxter has "become fools … that they may be wise." We quote the NET, changing "he" to "[they]" in brackets so that it fits his plural. The NET also begins "Guard against self-deception" and reads "wise in this age." Baxter's "in their own esteem" fits that sense.
- **1 Peter 5:5 (Direction 9):** NET "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble." Baxter has "more grace," which is not in the NET at 1 Peter 5:5; we have not confirmed where he took "more," and it may be from James 4:6. We kept to the NET quotation.
- **Matthew 18:3; John 3:3, 5 (Direction 9; flagged):** Baxter's "be converted" (King James) is the NET's "turn around"; he builds on conversion making men like children (see his sentence "Except conversion makes men as little children"), and the NET's "turn around" is a close but plainer rendering of the same Greek idea (we have not checked the Greek). Baxter's words "meet for the kingdom of Christ" are in quotation marks, but they are not a verbatim quotation of these verses. The NET at Matthew 18:3: "unless you turn around and become like little children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven"; at John 3:3, 5, "he cannot see (or enter) the kingdom of God." We kept Baxter's words, with "meet" (fit) modernized to "fit." **unmeet:** "unfit." **prepossessed:** "already filled." **tablets:** Baxter's "tables," as in the tables of the law. **carp and cavil:** left as written.
- **Matthew 13:23 (Direction 10):** cited by Baxter, who writes "honest hearts." The NET has "good soil … hears the word and understands." The phrase "honest" matches Luke 8:15 (NET: "an honest and good heart"), which Baxter does not cite. We give the NET only here. **fain:** "gladly." **unfeignedly:** "sincerely."
- **1 Samuel 3:9–10 (Direction 10, unmarked):** NET "Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening." Baxter has "thy servant heareth." The words after the semicolon, "teach me to know and do your will," are Baxter's own, inside his quotation marks. The reference is not in his text.
- **Direction 11:** "James 3" is cited only. The NET for James 3:13–18 sets "jealousy and selfishness" against "wisdom from above," which fits what Baxter says here (we did not need to quote it).
- **Hebrews 12:25 (Direction 12):** NET wording, with the verse's own question mark. Baxter has "them who refused him that spake on earth" and "much more shall not we." The NET has "warned" for "spake," and "how much less shall we" as a question. The point (they did not escape, much less shall we) is unchanged. The NET's "warned" for the King James "spake" is a small shift, and the question form is the NET's; noted, not important to his argument.
- **Hebrews 2:3–4 (Direction 12):** NET wording. The NET opens with "how will we escape if we neglect such a great salvation?" Baxter's "For" is in his quotation marks but not in the NET, so we put it outside ours. Baxter's "Holy Ghost" is the NET's "Holy Spirit" and his "divers miracles" is "various miracles." The NET has "distributed according to his will" where he has "according to his own will"; his question mark is kept.
- **Psalm 2:11–12 (Direction 12; important):** Baxter quotes "Serve the Lord therefore with fear, and rejoice with trembling: kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and you perish in the kindling of his wrath." The NET reads "Serve the Lord in fear. Repent in terror. Give sincere homage. Otherwise he will be angry, and you will die because of your behavior, when his anger quickly ignites." **Flagged:** the NET has no "Son" ("kiss the Son," the King James reading Baxter relies on, is not in the NET), and "Repent in terror" for "rejoice with trembling." We quote the NET, so the "therefore" that opens Baxter's sentence is dropped. The NET's quotation runs across several sentences; we kept its punctuation.
- **Hebrews 12:28–29; Philippians 2:12 (Direction 12):** cited only. **the rod:** the rod of correction in a school; an image, left as written. **ushers (Direction 6):** assistants to a teacher.
- **Word swaps:** hither now let believers cast their eyes (kept); instance (verb): cite; concoction: digestion; confounded: confused; meat: food; excited: stirred up; immediately: directly; engaged (against them): set; over-careful: overly anxious; hearken: listen; heed/take heed: beware; calumnies: slanders; malefactors: criminals; rout: mob; ceremonious: ceremonial; affected (repetitions): artificial; outside (worship): external; sensual: pleasure-loving; pretend: claim; **lip-labor, tractable, damnable ignorance:** left as written.
- **Margin note (last line):** this note begins Grand Direction 8 in the source (the heading "Remember that you are related to Christ as the Physician of your souls" is on the next line, outside our range). We have kept it here because it is inside the range.
