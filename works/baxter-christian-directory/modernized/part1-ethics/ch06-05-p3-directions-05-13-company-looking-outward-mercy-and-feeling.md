# Chapter 6: Title 5, Directions 5-13 to the melancholy

*This file continues Title 5 (directions to the melancholy) from the previous file; it begins at a new Direction.*

**Direction 5. Where you find yourselves unable for a secret duty, do not struggle too hard with yourselves, but go that pace that you are able to go quietly.**

For as every striving does not enable you, but vexes you, and makes duty wearisome to you, and disables you more, by increasing your disease: like an ox that draws unquietly, and a horse that chafes himself, that quickly tires. Preserve your willingness to duty, and avoid that which makes it burdensome to you. As to a sick stomach, it is not eating much, but digesting well, that tends to health; and little must be eaten when much cannot be digested; so it is here in the case of your meditations and secret prayers.

**Direction 6. Be most in those duties which you are best able to bear; which, with most, is prayer, with others hearing, and good conversation.**

As a sick man whose stomach is against other foods must eat of that which he can eat of. And God has provided variety of means, that one may do the work, when the others are lacking. Do not misunderstand me; in cases of absolute necessity, I say again, you must strive to do it whatever comes of it. If you are reluctant to believe, to repent, to love God and your neighbor, to live soberly, righteously, and godly, to pray at all; here you must strive, and not excuse it by any reluctance; for it is that which must be done, or you are lost. But a man that cannot read may be saved without his reading; and a man in prison or sickness may be saved without hearing the word, and without the church communion of saints: and so a man disabled by melancholy may be saved by shorter thoughts and brief prayers, without set and long meditations and secret prayers; and other duties which he is able for will supply the lack of these. Even as nature has provided two eyes, and two ears, and two nostrils, and two kidneys, and lungs, that when one is stopped or faulty, the other may supply its needs for a time; so it is here.

**Direction 7. Avoid all unnecessary solitariness, and be as much as possible in honest, cheerful company.**

You have need of others, and are not sufficient for yourselves; and God will use and honor others, as his hands, to deliver us his blessings. Solitariness is to those that are fit for it an excellent season for meditation and conversation with God and with our hearts; but to you, it is the season of temptation and danger. If Satan tempted Christ himself, when he had him fasting and solitary in a wilderness; much more will he take this as his opportunity against you. Solitude is the season of musings and thoughtfulness, which are the things which you must flee from, if you would not be deprived of all.

**Direction 8. When blasphemous or disturbing thoughts look in, or fruitless musings, meet them at once, and use that authority of reason which is left you, to cast them and command them out.**

If you have not lost it, reason and the will have a command over the thoughts as well as over the tongue, or hands, or feet. And as you would be ashamed to run up and down, or fight with your hands, and say, I cannot help it; or to let your tongue run all day, and say, I cannot stop it; so should you be ashamed to let your thoughts run at random, or on hurtful things, and say, I cannot help it. Do you do the best you can to help it? Can you not bid them be gone? Can you not turn your thoughts to something else? Or can you not rouse yourself up, and shake them off? Some by casting a little cold water in their own faces, or bidding another do it, can rouse themselves from melancholy musings as from sleep. Or can you not get out of the room, and set yourself about some business which will divert you? You might do more than you do, if you were only willing, and knew how much it is your duty.

**Direction 9. When you do think of any holy things, let it be of the best things; of God, and grace, and Christ, and heaven; or of your fellow believers, or the church: and carry all your meditations outward; but be sure you do not pore on yourselves, and do not spend your thoughts upon your thoughts.**

As we need to call the thoughts of careless sinners inwards, and turn them from the creature and sin, upon themselves; so we need to call the thoughts of self-perplexing, melancholy persons outwards; for it is their disease to be continually grinding upon themselves. Remember that it is a far higher, nobler, and sweeter work to think of God, and Christ, and heaven, than of such worms as we ourselves are. When we go up to God, we go to love, and light, and liberty; but when we look down into ourselves, we look into a dungeon, a prison, a wilderness, a place of darkness, horror, filthiness, misery, and confusion. Therefore, though such thoughts are needful, so far as without them our repentance and due watchfulness cannot be maintained, yet they are painful, ignoble, yes, and barren, in comparison with our thoughts of God. When you are poring on your hearts, to search whether the love of God is there or not, it would be wiser to be thinking of the infinite lovableness of God; and that will cause it, whether it was there before or not. So instead of poring on your hearts, to know whether they are set on heaven, lift up your thoughts to heaven, and think of its glory, and that will raise them there, and give you and show you that which you were searching for. Spend that time in planting holy desires in the garden of your hearts, which you spend in rooting about and puzzling yourselves in searching whether it is there already. We are such dark, confused things, that the sight of ourselves is enough to raise a loathing and a horror in our minds, and make them melancholy; but in God and glory there is nothing to discourage our thoughts, but all to delight them, if Satan does not misrepresent him to us.

**Direction 10. Do not overlook the miracle of love which God has shown us in the wonderful incarnation, office, life, death, resurrection, ascension, and reign of our Redeemer; but steep your thoughts most in these wonders of mercy, proposed by God to be the chief matter of your thoughts.**

You should in reason lay out many thoughts of Christ and grace, for one that you lay out on your sin and misery. God requires you to see your sin and misery, only so much as tends to magnify the remedy, and cause you to accept it. Never think of sin and hell alone; but as the way to the thoughts of Christ and grace. This is the duty even of the worst. Are your sins ever before you? Why is pardoning grace in Christ not before you? Is hell open before you? Why is the Redeemer not also before you? Do you say, Because sin and hell are yours, but Christ, and holiness, and heaven, are none of yours? I answer you, It is then because you will have it so: if you would not have it so, it is not so. God has set life first before you, and not only death. He has put Christ, and holiness, and heaven in his end of the balance; and the devil puts the pleasure of sin for a season in the other end. That which you choose sincerely is yours; for God has given you your choice. Nothing is truer than that God has so far made over Christ and life to all that hear the gospel, that nothing but their final obstinate refusal can condemn them: [315] Christ and life are brought to the will and choice of all, though all do not have wills to accept and choose him. And if you would not have Christ, and life, and holiness, what would you rather have? and why do you complain?

**Direction 11. Think and speak as much of the mercy which you have received as of the sin you have committed; and of the mercy which is offered you, as of what you lack.**

You dare not say that the mercy you have received is no more worthy to be remembered and mentioned than all your sins. Shall God do so much for you, and shall it be overlooked, belittled, and made nothing of? as if his mercies had been a bare bone, or barren wilderness, which would yield no sustenance to your thoughts. Do not be guilty of so great unthankfulness. Thoughts of love and mercy would breed love and sweetness in the soul; while thoughts of sin and wrath only breed aversion, terror, bitterness, perplexity, and drive away the heart from God.

**Direction 12. Tie yourselves daily to spend as great a part of your time in your prayers in the confessing of mercy received as in confessing sin committed; and in the praises of God as in the lamenting of your own miseries.**

You dare not deny that this is your duty, if you understand your duty; thanksgiving and praise are greater duties than confessing sin and misery. Resolve then that they shall have the largest share of time. If you will only do this much (which you can do if you will), it will in time take off the bitterness of your spirits; and the very frequent mention of sweeter things will sweeten your minds, and change their temperament and constitution, as change of diet changes the temperament of the body. I beg you, resolve, and try this course. If you cannot mention mercy as thankfully as you would, nor mention God's excellencies as holily and praisefully as you would, yet do what you can, and mention them as you are able. You may command your time (what shall have the greatest share in prayer), though not your affections; you will find the benefit very great, if you will only do this.

**Direction 13. Do not overvalue the emotional part of duty, but know that judgment, will, and practice, a high esteem of God and holiness, a resolved choice, and a sincere effort, are the life of grace and duty, whereas feeling passions are only lower, uncertain things.**

You do not know what you do when you lay so much on the emotional part; nor when you strive so much for deep and transporting perceptions; these are not the great things, nor essentials of holiness. Too much of this feeling may unhinge you. God knows how much you are able to bear. Emotional feelings depend much upon nature. Some persons are more sensitive than others; a little thing goes deep with some: the wisest and weightiest persons are usually least emotional; and the weakest hardly moderate their passions. God is not an object of sense, and therefore more fit for the understanding and will, than the passions, to work upon. That is the holiest soul which is most inclined to God, and resolved for him, and conformed to his will; and not that which is affected with the deepest griefs, and fears, and joys, and other such transporting passions; though it would be best, if even holy passions could be raised at the will's command, in that measure which fits us best for duty. But I have known many complain for lack of deeper feeling, who if their feeling (as they called their passion) had been more, it might have unhinged them. I would rather be that Christian that loathes himself for sin, resolves against it, and forsakes it, though he cannot weep for it; than one of those that can weep today, and sin again tomorrow, and whose sinful passions are quickly stirred, as well as their better passions.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). No Scripture is quoted in quotation marks in this range; the only references are in note 315, converted to modern form.
- **Title:** "Title" is Baxter's word for a heading or division of the chapter ("_Tit._" in the source). This file sits within Title 5, addressed to "the melancholy," and runs from Direction 5 to Direction 13. The range ends with the end of Direction 13; the next file continues Title 5 with Direction 14.
- **melancholy:** kept (see GLOSSARY.md): a lasting low, fearful, or brooding state, understood in Baxter's day as partly bodily. It is not a modern clinical diagnosis, and we have not substituted one.
- **Bold headings:** as in the neighboring files, the first sentence of each Direction is set in bold as its heading.
- **"For as every striving does not enable you ..."** (Direction 5): the sentence has no main clause after "For as"; Baxter's "as" clause is followed by a simile and then a new sentence. Kept as he wrote it. "Every striving does not enable you" means "not every striving enables you."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "to live soberly, righteously, and godly" (Direction 6) is the King James of Titus 2:12; the NET reads "to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age." Satan tempting Christ fasting in the wilderness (Direction 7; Matthew 4:1-11). "Are your sins ever before you?" (Direction 10) echoes Psalm 51:3 in the King James ("my sin is ever before me"); the NET reads "I am forever conscious of my sin." "God has set life first before you, and not only death" (Direction 10) recalls Deuteronomy 30:15, 19. "The pleasure of sin for a season" (Direction 10) is the King James of Hebrews 11:25; the NET reads "sin's fleeting pleasure." "Such worms as we ourselves are" (Direction 9) recalls Job 25:6 and Psalm 22:6. None of these references is in Baxter's text.
- **Word swaps:** *grievous* to "burdensome" (Direction 5) and to "painful" (Direction 9), by context; *meats* to "foods"; *wanting* to "lacking"; *want* to "lack" (several times); *backward, backwardness* to "reluctant, reluctance"; *must needs be done* to "must be done"; *reins* to "kidneys"; *converse* (noun) to "conversation"; *fly from* to "flee from"; *presently* to "at once"; *still* (continually) to "continually"; *amiableness* to "lovableness"; *thither* to "there"; *bestow* to "spend"; *unfeignedly* to "sincerely"; *extenuated* to "belittled"; *averseness* to "aversion"; *I beseech you* to "I beg you"; *had rather* to "would rather"; *sensible* to "sensitive"; *endeavour* to "effort"; *but* (only) to "only" several times.
- **ejaculations** (Direction 6): rendered "brief prayers." In Baxter's usage an ejaculation is a short prayer darted up to God, not a long set prayer.
- **solitariness; thoughtfulness** (Direction 7): "solitariness" kept. "Thoughtfulness" kept, but in this context it means anxious brooding, not considerateness.
- **if you would not be deprived of all** (Direction 7): Baxter's "if you will not be deprived," meaning "if you do not want to be."
- **and knew how much it is your duty** (Direction 8): the source has "and know"; we read it as following "if you were but willing" and give "knew." Possibly a misprint in the source.
- **brethren** (Direction 9): rendered "fellow believers."
- **the creature** (Direction 9): kept; Baxter's term for created things, as opposed to God.
- **rooting about** (Direction 9): Baxter's "routing," which we take as "rooting," digging about (as an animal roots in the ground), fitting his garden image. We have not confirmed this reading.
- **made over** (Direction 10): kept; a legal phrase meaning to convey or transfer by deed. Baxter's point is that Christ and life are offered by a kind of deed of gift to all who hear the gospel.
- **confessing of mercy** (Direction 12): kept. "Confess" here means to acknowledge with thanks, in parallel with confessing sin.
- **temperament and constitution** (Direction 12): Baxter's "temperature and habit." In the medicine of his day "temperature" meant the balance of a body's humors (its temperament), and "habit" its settled condition (see GLOSSARY.md, "habit (of body)"). Both are applied here to the mind.
- **passionate, passions** (Direction 13): "passionate" rendered "emotional" (see GLOSSARY.md); "passions" kept as Baxter's term for the strong emotions (grief, fear, joy, and the like), as distinct from understanding and will. **Emotional feelings:** his "passionate feelings."
- **whereas** (Direction 13 heading): Baxter's "when," used in the sense "whereas."
- **transporting perceptions** (Direction 13): Baxter's "transporting apprehensions"; "apprehension" here means perception or sense of a thing, not anxiety. "Transporting" (carrying one away, enrapturing) kept.
- **unhinge** (Direction 13, twice): Baxter's "distract." In his day "distracted" commonly meant mentally deranged or out of one's mind, not merely inattentive; his warning is that too much passionate feeling may overthrow a weak mind.
- **nature** (Direction 13): kept; a person's natural temperament or constitution. **weightiest:** kept; most serious or substantial.
