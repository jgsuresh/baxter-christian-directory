# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 4

**Direction 4. That your understanding may be enlightened, and your heart renewed, be diligent and serious in reading the word of God, and those books that are fitted to men in an unconverted state, and especially in hearing the plain and searching preaching of the word.**

There is a heavenly light, and power, and majesty in the word of God, which in the serious reading or hearing of it may pierce the heart, and prick it, and open it, so that corruption may go out, and grace come in. "The law of the Lord is perfect and preserves one's life. The rules set down by the Lord are reliable and impart wisdom to the inexperienced. The Lord's precepts are fair and make one joyful" (Psalm 19:7–8). Moreover, "your servant finds moral guidance there; those who obey them receive a rich reward" (verse 11). The eunuch was reading the Scripture when Philip was sent to explain it to him for his conversion (Acts 8). The preaching of Peter pierced many thousands to the heart, to their conversion (Acts 2:37). The heart of Lydia was opened to pay attention to the preaching of Paul (Acts 16:14). "For the word of God is living and active and sharper than any double-edged sword, piercing even to the point of dividing soul from spirit" (Hebrews 4:12). These weapons "are made powerful by God for tearing down strongholds. We tear down arguments and every arrogant obstacle that is raised up against the knowledge of God, and we take every thought captive to make it obey Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:4–5). Have you often read and heard already, and yet find no change upon your heart? Yet read and hear again and again: ministers must not give up preaching when they have labored without success; why then should you give up hearing or reading? As the farmer labors, and looks to God for rain, and for the blessing, so must we, and so must you. Look up to God: remember it is his word, in which he calls you to repentance, and offers you mercy, and deals with you concerning your everlasting happiness: lament your past neglect and disobedience, and beg his blessing on his word, and you shall find it will not be in vain.

And the serious reading of books which explain and apply the Scriptures, suitable to your case, may, by the blessing of God, be effective for your conversion. I have written so many for this use myself that I shall be the briefer on this subject now, and desire you to read them, or some of them, if you do not have more suitable ones at hand; namely, *A Call to the Unconverted*; *A Treatise of Conversion*; *Now or Never*; *Directions for a Sound Conversion*; *A Saint or a Brute*; *A Treatise of Judgment*; *A Sermon against Making Light of Christ*; *A Sermon of Christ's Dominion*; *Another of His Sovereignty*, etc.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Psalm 19:7–8 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James: "converting the soul," "making wise the simple," "rejoicing the heart." The NET renders the same Hebrew as "preserves one's life," "impart wisdom to the inexperienced," and "make one joyful." Baxter is arguing from the word "converting" (he is using this verse to show that Scripture changes the soul), and the NET's "preserves one's life" loses that. This passage is a good candidate for a different translation if you want the point to carry; the ESV reads "reviving the soul." Left as NET for consistency, and flagged here.
- **Hebrews 4:12:** the 1846 edition prints "Heb. iv. 11." The words quoted are verse 12, so we corrected the reference.
- **2 Corinthians 10:4–5:** the 1846 edition prints "2 Cor. iv. 5." The words quoted are from chapter 10, verses 4–5 (Baxter himself cites "2 Cor. x. 4" correctly in his note [25]), so we corrected the reference.
- **Acts 8; Acts 2:37; Acts 16:14:** Baxter paraphrases these. We kept his paraphrases, modernized, and cited the passages. "Prick … to the heart" (his wording from Acts 2:37) is rendered "pierced … to the heart." The NET reads "acutely distressed."
- **diligent** (heading): Baxter's "be much … in reading," meaning "give much time to."
- **farmer:** Baxter's "husbandman." **give up:** Baxter's "give over." **deals with:** Baxter's "treateth with."
- **Baxter's books** (paragraph 2): the titles are as Baxter gives them. *Now or Never* and *Directions for a Sound Conversion* are among his shorter works on conversion.
