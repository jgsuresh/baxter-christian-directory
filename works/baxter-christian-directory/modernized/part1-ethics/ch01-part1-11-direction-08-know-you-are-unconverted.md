# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 8

**Direction 8. If you are willing indeed to be converted, do your best to discover that you are still unconverted, and in a lost and miserable state.**

Who will endeavor to cure a disease which he thinks he does not have? or to vomit up the poison which he thinks he never took, or takes to be no poison? or to come out of the ditch, who thinks he is not in it? or who will turn back again, who will not believe anything but that he is in the right way? Who will labor to be converted, who thinks he is converted already? Or who will come to Christ as the physician of his soul, who thinks he is not sick, or is cured already? The common cause that men live and die without the grace of repentance, sanctification, and justification, which should save them, is that they will not believe but that they have it, when they have it not; and that they are penitent, and justified, and sanctified already. It is not my desire to make any of you think worse of your condition than it is; but if you will not know what it is, you will not be fit for restoring grace, nor use the means for your own recovery: you think it is so grim a conclusion to find yourselves in a state of condemnation that you are exceedingly unwilling to know it or confess it.

But I beg you to consider these two things: first, either it is true that you are in so miserable a state, or it is not true: if it is not true, the closest examination will but comfort you, by discovering that you are sanctified already; but if it is true, then do you think it will save you to be ignorant of your danger? Will it cure your disease to believe that you do not have it? Will thinking well of yourselves falsely prove that you are well indeed? [27] Is it the way to grace to think you have it when you do not have it? Will it bring you to heaven to think that you are going there when you are on the way to hell? Nay, do you not know that it is the principal temptation of the devil, to keep men from a state of repentance and salvation, to deceive them thus, and persuade them that they are in such a state already? Judge the case soberly. Do you think, if all the impenitent, unconverted sinners in the world were certain that they are indeed in a graceless state, in which if they died they were past all hope, that they would not quickly look about them, and better understand the offers of a Savior, and live in continual concern and fear until they found themselves in a safer state? If you were sure yourselves that you must yet be made new creatures, or be damned, would it not set you to work to seek grace more diligently than ever you have done? The devil knows this well enough; that he could scarcely keep you quiet this night in his snares, but you would be ready to repent and beg for mercy, and resolve on a new life before tomorrow, if you were but sure that you are still in a state of condemnation. And therefore he does all he can to hide your sin and danger from your eyes, and to quiet you with the notion that though you are sinners, yet you are penitent, pardoned, and safe.

Well, sirs, there can be no harm in knowing the truth. And therefore will you but try yourselves, whether you are unsanctified or not? You were baptized into the name of the Holy Spirit as your Sanctifier; and if now you neglect or mock sanctification, what do you do but deride your baptism, or neglect that which is its meaning and end? It does not concern you so much to know that you live the life of nature as to know whether sanctification has made you spiritually alive to God.

And let me tell you this for your encouragement, that we do not call you to know that you are unconverted, and unpardoned, and miserable, as men that have no remedy, but must sit down in despair, and be tormented with the foreknowledge of your endless pains before the time. No; it is but that you may speedily and thankfully accept Christ, the full remedy, and turn to God, and quickly get out of your sin and terror, and enter into a life of safety and of peace. We do not desire your continuance in that life which tends to despair and horror: we would have you out of it, if it were in our power, before tomorrow; and therefore it is that we would have you understand what danger you are in, so that you may go no further, but speedily turn back, and seek for help. And I hope there is no harm, though there is some present trouble, in such a discovery of your danger as this is.

Well, if you are but willing to know, I shall help you a little to know what you are.

*In the margin: Marks of an unconverted state.*

1. If you are persecutors, or haters, deriders of men for being serious and diligent in the service of God, and fearful of sinning, and because they do not go with the multitude to do evil, it is a certain sign that you are in a state of death: indeed, if you do not love such men, and do not desire rather to be such yourselves than to be the greatest of the ungodly. See Galatians 4:29; Acts 26:11; 1 Timothy 1:13; 1 Peter 4:2–5; Psalm 15:4; 1 John 3:8–15; John 13:35; Psalm 84:10.

2. If you love the world best, and set your affections most on things below, and mind most earthly things; nay, if you do not seek first God's kingdom, and its righteousness; and if your hearts are not in heaven, and your affections set on the things that are above; and you do not prefer your hopes of eternal life to all the pleasures and prosperity of this world, it is a certain sign that you are but worldly and ungodly men. See this in Matthew 6:19–21, 33; Philippians 3:18–20; Colossians 3:1–4; Psalm 73:25; 1 John 2:15–17; James 1:27; Luke 12:20–21; 16:25.

3. If your estimation, belief, and hopes of everlasting life through Christ are not such as will prevail with you to deny yourselves, and forsake father, and mother, and the nearest friends; and house, and land, and life, and all that you have, for Christ, and for these hopes of a happiness hereafter, you are no true Christians, nor in a state of saving grace. See Luke 14:26, 33; Matthew 10:37–39; 13:21–22.

4. If you have not been converted, regenerated, and sanctified by the Spirit of Jesus Christ, making you spiritual, and causing you to mind the things of the Spirit above the things of the flesh. If this Spirit is not in you, and you do not walk after it, but after the flesh; making provision for the flesh, to satisfy its desires, and preferring the pleasing of the flesh to the pleasing of God, it is certain that you are in a state of death. See Matthew 18:3; John 3:3, 5, 6; Hebrews 12:14; Romans 8:1, 5–13; 13:13–14; Luke 16:19, 25; 12:20–21; Hebrews 11:25–26; 2 Corinthians 4:16–18; 5:7; Romans 8:17–18.

5. If you have any known sin which you do not hate, and would not rather leave it than keep it, and do not pray, and strive, and watch against it as far as you know and observe it; but rather excuse it, plead for it, desire it, and are loath to part with it, so that your will is habitually more for it than against it, it is a sign of an impenitent, unrenewed heart. 1 John 3:3–10, 24; Galatians 5:16, 19–25; Romans 7:22, 24; 8:13; Luke 13:3, 5; Matthew 5:19–20; 2 Timothy 2:19; Psalm 5:5; Luke 13:27.

6. If you do not love the word as it is a light revealing your sin and duty, but only as it is a general truth, or as it reproves others: if you do not love the most searching preaching, and would not know how bad you are, and do not come to the light so that your deeds may be made manifest, it is a sign that you are not children of the light, but of the darkness (John 3:19–21).

7. If the laws of your Creator and Redeemer are not of greatest power and authority with you, and the will and word of God cannot do more with you than the word or will of any man; and the threats and promises of God are not more powerful with you than the threats or promises of any men, it is a sign that you do not take God for your God, but in heart are atheists and ungodly men. Luke 19:27; Matthew 7:21–23, 26; Daniel 3:16–18; 6:5, 10; Jeremiah 17:5–6; Luke 12:4; Acts 5:29; Psalm 14:1, etc.

8. If you have not, in a deliberate covenant or resolution, devoted and given up yourselves to God as your Father and happiness, to Jesus Christ as your only Savior, and your Lord and King, and to the Holy Spirit as your Sanctifier, to be made holy by him, desiring that your heart and life should be perfectly conformed to the will of God, and that you might know him, and love him, and enjoy him more; you are void of godliness and true Christianity; for this is the very covenant which you make in baptism, which you call your christening. Matthew 28:19–20; 2 Corinthians 8:5; 1 Corinthians 6:17; John 1:10–12; Galatians 4:6; Romans 8:14–15.

I have now plainly shown you, and fully proved, from the word of God, by what infallible signs an ungodly man may know that he is ungodly, if he will. Can you not know whether it is thus with you, if you are willing to know? Can you not know, if you will, whether your desire and aim in life is more for this world or that to come? and whether heaven or earth is preferred and sought first? and whether your fleshly prosperity and pleasure, or your souls, are principally cared for and regarded? Can you not know, if you will, whether you love or loathe the serious worshippers of God? [28] and whether you would rather be delivered from your sins or keep them? and whether your wills are more against them, or for them? and whether you love a holy life or not? and whether you would rather be perfect in holiness and obedience to God, or be excused from it, and please the flesh? and whether you would rather be such a one as Paul, or as Caesar? a persecuted saint in poverty and contempt, or a persecuting conqueror or king? Can you not know, if you will, whether you love a searching ministry that tells you of the worst and would not deceive you? Can you not know whether you are resolutely devoted and given up to God, the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, as your Father and happiness, your Savior and your Sanctifier; and whether the aim, purpose, and business of your lives is more for God, or for the flesh, for heaven, or earth; and which it is that bears the sway, and which it is that comes behind, and has but the leavings of the other, or only so much as it can spare? Certainly these are things so near you, and so remarkable in your hearts, that you may come to the knowledge of them if you will. But if you will not, who can help it?

What a foolish quibble it is then of those ignorant men, who ask us, when we tell them of these things, Whether ever we were in heaven? or ever saw the book of life? and how we can tell who shall be saved, and who shall be damned? If it were about a May-game, this jesting would be more seasonable; but to talk thus senselessly about the matters of salvation and damnation, and to make such a jest of the damning of souls, is a kind of foolery that has no excuse. What though we never were in heaven? and never saw the book of life? Do you think I never saw the Scriptures? Why, wretched sinner, do you not know that Christ came down from heaven to tell us who they are that shall come there, and who they are that shall be shut out? And did he not know what he said? Is God the Governor of the world, and does he not have a law by which he governs them? and can I not tell by the law who they are that the Judge will condemn or save? What else is the law made for but to be the rule of life, and the rule of judgment? Read Psalm 1 and 15; Matthew 5, 7, and 25, and all the texts which I just now cited, and see in them whether God has not told you who they are that shall be saved, and who they are that shall be condemned? nay, see whether this is not the very business of the word of God? And do you think that he has written in vain? But some men have loved ignorance and ungodliness so long until the Spirit of grace has cast them off, and left them to the foolishness of their carnal minds, so that "they have eyes and do not see, and ears and do not hear, and hearts and do not understand." But those that are willing and diligent to know their sin and duty in order to their recovery, God will not let them search in vain, nor hide the remedy from their eyes.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced exactly as in the source, except that Baxter's English is modernized. A draft English translation follows the Latin passage in parentheses; it was prepared for this project and is not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check it before relying on fine points.

[27] Bernard, de Grad. Humil. grad. 8. (*On the Steps of Humility*, eighth step) describes men's excusing their sins thus: "If possible, they will say, I did not do it; or else, It was no sin, but lawful; or else, I did not do it often or much; or else, I meant no harm; or else, I was persuaded by another, and drawn to it by temptation."

[28] Atque haud scio an pietate adversus Deos sublatâ, fides etiam, et societas humani generis, et una excellentissima virtus, justitia, tollatur. Cicero de Nat. Deor. p. 4. (And I do not know whether, if piety toward the gods is removed, faith also, and the fellowship of the human race, and the one most excellent virtue, justice, would not be removed. Cicero, *On the Nature of the Gods*.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture:** this section quotes Scripture only in one allusion ("they have eyes and do not see…"), and otherwise gives reference lists, which we converted to modern form. We did not check the individual lists against the passages. Scripture quotations elsewhere are from the NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **"eyes and do not see, and ears and do not hear, and hearts and do not understand"** (last paragraph): Baxter quotes this without a reference. It is a loose combination of Old Testament passages (compare Isaiah 6:9–10; Jeremiah 5:21; Ezekiel 12:2). We kept his wording, modernized. We have not matched it to a single NET verse.
- **still** (heading): Baxter's "yet," in the sense "still." **restoring grace** (paragraph 1): his "recovering grace," meaning grace that restores.
- **grim:** Baxter's "sad." **notion:** his "conceit." **concern:** his "solicitude." **closest examination:** his "closest trial." **set you to work:** his "set you on work."
- **happiness** (items 8 and the closing paragraph): Baxter's "felicity," meaning supreme happiness or blessedness. "Father and felicity" means God as the one who gives and is our highest happiness.
- **foolish quibble** (last paragraph): Baxter's "sottish cavil," meaning a foolish, petty objection. **foolishness:** his "sottishness." **senselessly:** his "distractedly."
- **May-game:** a May Day sport or festival, meaning an occasion for lighthearted fun. Left as Baxter's word.
- **Caesar** (closing paragraph): Baxter sets Paul, the persecuted saint, against Caesar, the persecuting conqueror, as two ways to want to be.
- **Bernard** (note 27): Bernard of Clairvaux. The note lists the excuses Bernard says people make for their sins, in order.
- **"you" and "thou"** (last paragraph): Baxter slips into "dost thou think I never saw the Scriptures?" when he imagines one scoffer answering him. We rendered it as "you."
