# Chapter 7: Title 8, against sinful fear (Directions 9-22, and a closing word on the fear of death)

**Direction 9. Remember how small and short the suffering will be, and how great and long the glorious reward.**

It is but a little while, and the pain and shame will all be past; but the glory will be never past: how much the worse now is Stephen for his stones, or John the Baptist for being beheaded, or Paul for his bonds and afflictions, which everywhere awaited him, or any holy martyr for the torment and death which they underwent? Oh how the case is altered with them, now God has wiped away all tears from their eyes! Are we so tender that we cannot endure the grief that is but for a night, when we know that joy will come in the morning? Psalm 30:5. "Therefore we do not despair, but even if our physical body is wearing away, our inner person is being renewed day by day. [352] For our momentary, light suffering is producing for us an eternal weight of glory far beyond all comparison because we are not looking at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen. For what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal," 2 Corinthians 4:16–18. "So do not throw away your confidence, because it has great reward. For you need endurance in order to do God's will and so receive what is promised. For just a little longer and he who is coming will arrive and not delay. But my righteous one will live by faith, and if he shrinks back, I take no pleasure in him," Hebrews 10:35–38.

**Direction 10. When you are delivered from the power of the devil himself, what cause have you to fear his instruments?**

Can they do more than he? If Goliath the champion and the general is overcome, the common soldiers are not likely to overcome us.

**Direction 11. Are you better than your Lord? look to him, and be confirmed.**

"A disciple is not greater than his teacher, nor a slave greater than his master. It is enough for the disciple to become like his teacher, and the slave like his master. If they have called the head of the house 'Beelzebul,' how much worse will they call the members of his household!" Matthew 10:24–25. "We must ... run with endurance the race set out for us, keeping our eyes fixed on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of our faith. For the joy set out for him he endured the cross, disregarding its shame, and has taken his seat at the right hand of the throne of God. Think of him who endured such opposition against himself by sinners, so that you may not grow weary in your souls and give up," Hebrews 12:1–3.

**Direction 12. Be of good cheer: our Lord has overcome the world, John 16:33.**

And shall we fear inordinately a conquered world? Indeed, he overcame it by suffering, to show us that by suffering we shall overcome it. He triumphed over principalities and powers (greater than mortals) "by the cross," Colossians 2:15. And therefore "all authority in heaven and on earth has been given" to him, Matthew 28:18, and he is "the Lord of both the dead and the living," Romans 14:9, and is made "head over all things" to the church, Ephesians 1:21, 22. And so, though "for his sake we are killed all the day long, and counted as sheep for the slaughter, yet in all these things we are more than conquerors, through him that loved us;" that is, we have a nobler victory than if we conquered them by the sword.

**Direction 13. Think how little your suffering is in comparison with what your sin deserved, and your Lord has freely saved you from.**

Should a man complain at the opening of a vein for his health, who deserved to have lost his life? Can you remember hell which was your due, and yet make a great matter of anything that man can do against you?

**Direction 14. Remember that to sin through fear of suffering is to leap into hell to escape a little pain on earth.**

Are you afraid of man? Be more afraid of God. Is not God more terrible? "It is a fearful thing to fall into his hands: for he is a consuming fire." [353] O hear your Lord. "I tell you, my friends, do not be afraid of those who kill the body, and after that have nothing more they can do. But I will warn you whom you should fear: Fear the one who, after the killing, has authority to throw you into hell. Yes, I tell you, fear him!" Luke 12:4–5. If their fire is hot, remember that hell is hotter: and that God is the best friend, and the most dreadful enemy.

**Direction 15. Remember that you shall suffer (and it is likely as much) even here from God, if you escape by sin your suffering from men.**

If you sin to escape death, you shall die when you have done; and oh! how quickly! And how much more joyful it is to die in Christ, than a little after unwillingly to part with that life, which you refused to part with for your Lord! and what sores will you feel in your guilty conscience both in life and at your death! So that even in this life, your fear would drive you into greater misery.

**Direction 16. Think of the dangerous effects of your immoderate fear.**

It is the way with Peter to deny your Lord: indeed, the way to apostasy, or any wickedness which men shall drive you to by terrors. If you were where the Turk is now tyrannizing among Christians, if you do not overcome your fear, he might overcome your fidelity, [354] and make you turn from Jesus Christ: and that is the sin which the apostle so dreadfully describes, Hebrews 10:26, 27, 29, "if we deliberately keep on sinning (that is, willfully renounce our Lord) after receiving the knowledge of the truth, no further sacrifice for sins is left for us, but only a certain fearful expectation of judgment and a fury of fire that will consume God's enemies." Oh how many have been drawn by the fear of men to wound their consciences, neglect their duties, comply with sin, forsake the truth, dishonor God, and undo their souls. And often in this life they do as poor Spira did, who, by sinning through the fear of man, cast himself into melancholy and self-murdering despair. Your fear is a more dangerous enemy to you than those that you fear are. "The fear of people becomes a snare, but whoever trusts in the Lord will be set on high. Many people seek the face of a ruler, but it is from the Lord that one receives justice," Proverbs 29:25–26. Fear is given to preserve you: do not use it to destroy you.

**Direction 17. Believe and remember God's special providence, extending to every hair of your head, and also the guard of angels which he has set over you.**

"Aren't two sparrows sold for a penny? Yet not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father's will. Even all the hairs on your head are numbered. So do not be afraid; you are more valuable than many sparrows," Matthew 10:29–31. Oh that this were well believed and considered! Psalm 34:7, "The angel of the Lord camps around the Lord's loyal followers and delivers them."

**Direction 18. Think what a vile dishonor it is to God to have his creature, even breathing dirt, to be feared more than him!**

As if he were less powerful to do good or hurt to you than man, and were not able or willing to secure you, so far as to see that no man shall ever be a loser by him, or anything which he suffers for his cause! [355] Isaiah 57:11, "Whom are you worried about? Whom do you fear, that you would act so deceitfully and not remember me or think about me? Because I have been silent for so long, you are not afraid of me." How did Daniel and the three confessors honor God, but by fearing him more than the king and the flaming furnace: saying, "We do not need to give you a reply concerning this. If our God whom we are serving exists, he is able to rescue us from the furnace of blazing fire, and he will rescue us, O king, from your power as well. But if he does not, let it be known to you, O king, that we don't serve your gods," etc. Daniel 3:16–18. Daniel would not cease praying three times a day openly in his house, for fear of the king, or of the lions. "[Moses] left Egypt without fearing the king's anger, for he persevered as though he could see the one who is invisible," Hebrews 11:27. "So we can say with confidence, 'The Lord is my helper, and I will not be afraid. What can people do to me?'" Hebrews 13:6. [356]

**Direction 19. Remember the dangers which you have been saved from already; especially from sin and hell.**

And is an uncircumcised Philistine more invincible than the lion and the bear?

**Direction 20. Remember the great approaching day of judgment, where great and small will be equal before God; and where God will right all that were wronged by men, and be the full and final avenger of his children!**

He has promised, though "he bears long, to avenge them speedily," Luke 18:7, 8. Can you believe that day, and yet not think that it is soon enough to justify you fully and finally, and to make you reparation for all your wrongs? Can you not wait till Christ comes to judge the living and the dead? You will then be unwilling to be found with those that, as Saul, made haste to sacrifice, because he could not wait till Samuel came; whose souls "drew back, because they could not live by faith." Matthew 10:26, "Do not be afraid of them, for nothing is hidden that will not be revealed, and nothing is secret that will not be made known." 2 Thessalonians 1:6–10, "For it is right for God to repay with affliction those who afflict you, and to you who are being afflicted to give rest together with us when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels. With flaming fire he will mete out punishment, etc. When he comes to be glorified among his saints and admired ... among all who have believed."

**Direction 21. Remember that the fearful and unbelieving shall be shut out of heaven, Revelation 21:8; that is, those that fear men more than God, and cannot trust him with their lives and all, but will rather risk his wrath by sin, than the wrath of man.**

**Direction 22. Turn your fear of the instruments of the devil into pity and compassion to men in such lamentable misery; and pray for them as Christ and Stephen did: foresee now the misery that is near them.**

When you begin to be afraid of them, suppose that just now it were the day of judgment, and you saw how they will then tremble at the bar of God (as conscience sometimes makes some of them do, at the hearing or remembering of it; as Felix before Paul): see them as ready to be sentenced to the fire prepared for the devil and his angels, as in Matthew 25. Can you fear him that is near such endless misery, whom you should grieve over and pity (as the ancient martyrs used to do)? 1 Peter 4:17, "What shall the end" of the persecutors "be, and where shall the ungodly sinners appear, if judgment begins at the house of God, and the righteous are saved with so much difficulty?"

About the fear of death, I have written at length already in my *Treatise of Self-denial*, and in the *Saints' Rest*, and in *The Last Enemy, Death*, etc. and in *The Believer's Last Work*. Therefore, I shall here pass it by. [357]

[354] Qui propter timorem reticet veritatem, veritatis proditor est. Hincmar. Rhemens. Dialog. de Statu. Eccl. (Whoever keeps silent about the truth out of fear is a betrayer of the truth. Hincmar of Reims, *Dialogue on the State of the Church*.)

Plutarch. dial. 66. li. 2. (I will say more: it is as much better to be justly condemned than to be unjustly acquitted, as an unpunished crime is worse than a punished one: for in the one, justice is joined to the crime, a huge good to a great evil; but in the other there is crime and impunity, which I do not know whether it is not worse than the crime itself. Plutarch, dialogue 66, book 2. Rendered loosely, reading the misprints as noted below.)

[357] See below, Part 3, Chapter 29, Title 3, and Chapter 30.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All quoted NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** "Title" (Baxter's "Tit.") is his word for each of the ten headed divisions of Chapter 7; this file finishes Title 8, against sinful fear. It begins at the start of Direction 9, which follows Direction 8 in the previous file.
- **Direction headings:** as in the other files, the first sentence of each Direction is set as its bold heading and the rest follows as the body. Direction 21 is a single sentence, so it stands as the heading alone.
- **Direction 9, "how much the worse":** Baxter's "what the worse"; "which everywhere awaited him" is his "which did every where abide him" (echoing Acts 20:23). **John the Baptist:** his "John Baptist." "God has wiped away all tears from their eyes" is an unmarked allusion (Revelation 7:17; 21:4).
- **Psalm 30:5 (not quoted):** Baxter's "the grief that is but for a night ... joy will come in the morning" is in his own words, not in quotation marks, so we kept it. NET: "One may experience sorrow during the night, but joy arrives in the morning."
- **2 Corinthians 4:16–18 (flag):** NET wording. The King James "For this cause we faint not" is NET "Therefore we do not despair"; "our light affliction, which is but for a moment" is "our momentary, light suffering," which keeps Baxter's point of shortness and lightness. The King James "while we look not at the things which are seen" makes the looking a condition of the glory; the NET's "because we are not looking" makes it a reason. Baxter's footnote 352 falls after "day by day," and we kept it there.
- **Hebrews 10:35–38 (flag):** NET wording. "Patience" is NET "endurance"; "For yet a little while" is "For just a little longer," which keeps Baxter's "a little while"; "the just shall live by faith; but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him" is "my righteous one will live by faith, and if he shrinks back, I take no pleasure in him."
- **Matthew 10:24–25 (reference):** Baxter cites "Matt. x. 24," but his quotation runs through verse 25. We cite 10:24–25. The King James "servant ... lord" is NET "slave ... master," so the echo of Baxter's own question, "Are you better than your Lord?", is weaker in the NET. "Beelzebub" is NET "Beelzebul."
- **Hebrews 12:1–3 (flag):** NET wording. The King James "Let us run" is in the NET "we must ... run," so we quoted with "...". "Patience" is "endurance"; "the author and finisher of our faith" is "the pioneer and perfecter of our faith"; "despising the shame" is "disregarding its shame"; "contradiction of sinners" is "opposition ... by sinners"; "lest ye be wearied, and faint in your minds" is "so that you may not grow weary in your souls and give up."
- **John 16:33 (Direction 12):** Baxter's "Be of good cheer: our Lord has overcome the world" is his own sentence with a reference, not a quotation; kept. NET: "take courage—I have conquered the world."
- **Colossians 2:15:** Baxter quotes only "on the cross"; NET "by the cross." His unquoted "principalities and powers" (kept) are NET "the rulers and authorities." **(greater than mortals)** is Baxter's own parenthesis.
- **Matthew 28:18 (corrected reference; flag):** Baxter prints "Matt. xxviii. 19," but the words are in verse 18; corrected. We quoted the NET phrase and kept Baxter's "to him" outside the quotation, since the verse is in Christ's first person ("to me"). The King James "all power" is NET "all authority."
- **Romans 14:9:** NET wording, "the Lord of both the dead and the living."
- **Ephesians 1:21, 22 (flag):** Baxter quotes "is made Head over all things to the church." The NET reads "God ... gave him to the church as head over all things," so we quoted only "head over all things" and kept Baxter's "is made ... to the church" outside the quotation marks.
- **Romans 8:36–37 (no reference in Baxter; adapted; important flag):** Baxter gives no reference and adapts the verse ("for his sake" where the verse has "for thy sake"), and his gloss, "we have a nobler victory than if we conquered them by the sword," explains the King James "more than conquerors." Because of both the adaptation and the gloss, we kept his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET reads: "For your sake we encounter death all day long; we were considered as sheep to be slaughtered. No, in all these things we have complete victory through him who loved us!" The NET has no "more than," so Baxter's gloss would not fit it. The reference is supplied here only; it is not in Baxter's text.
- **Direction 13:** "complain at" is Baxter's "grudge at." "The opening of a vein" refers to bloodletting, then a common medical treatment; this is Baxter's illustration, kept.
- **"It is a fearful thing to fall into his hands: for he is a consuming fire" (Direction 14; blend):** Baxter joins Hebrews 10:31 and 12:29 (his footnote 353 gives the references) and changes "the hands of the living God" to "his hands." We kept his blended wording. NET: "It is a terrifying thing to fall into the hands of the living God" (10:31); "For our God is indeed a devouring fire" (12:29).
- **Luke 12:4–5 (corrected reference):** Baxter prints "Luke xiii. 4, 5"; the words are Luke 12:4–5. Corrected. NET wording. **the most dreadful enemy:** his "the dreadfullest enemy."
- **Direction 15:** "(and it is likely as much)" is his "(and it is like as much)." **refused to part with:** his "denied to part with." **sores:** his "galls," meaning sores rubbed raw, a picture of the pain of a guilty conscience.
- **Hebrews 10:26, 27, 29 (flag):** Baxter cites verse 29 but quotes only verses 26–27; we kept his citation. NET wording, with Baxter's own parenthesis "(that is, willfully renounce our Lord)" kept where he put it. Baxter's gloss explains the King James "if we sin wilfully"; the NET reads "if we deliberately keep on sinning," which stresses a continuing practice. His gloss still fits, but the word "willfully" it explains is now only in his parenthesis. The King James "fire which shall devour the adversaries" is NET "a fury of fire that will consume God's enemies."
- **"the Turk ... tyrannizing among Christians":** Baxter's phrase for the Ottoman Empire's rule over Christian peoples; kept as his.
- **Spira:** probably Francesco Spiera (or Spira), the Italian lawyer whose despair after recanting his Protestant faith was, as far as we know, widely retold in Protestant writing (we have not confirmed the details); Baxter uses him as a warning. We have kept his account and add nothing to it. **self-murdering despair:** kept; Baxter's phrase for despair that drives a person toward suicide. **melancholy:** kept (see GLOSSARY.md).
- **Proverbs 29:25–26 (flag):** NET wording (a missing space in the fetched text has been added). The King James "shall be safe" is NET "will be set on high"; "every man's judgment cometh of the Lord" is "it is from the Lord that one receives justice." "The fear of man" is "The fear of people."
- **Matthew 10:29–31 (reference):** Baxter cites "Matt. x. 29, 30," but his quotation runs through verse 31 ("fear ye not therefore ..."). We cite 10:29–31. NET wording. The NET adds "will" ("apart from your Father's will"), which is not in the Greek or the King James; it fits Baxter's point about special providence. "Farthing" is NET "penny."
- **Psalm 34:7:** NET wording (a missing space in the fetched text has been added). "Them that fear him" is NET "the Lord's loyal followers," so the word "fear," which suits Baxter's subject, is lost.
- **Direction 18:** "breathing dirt" is Baxter's phrase for a human being (compare Genesis 2:7); kept. "the three confessors": Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego; "confessor" in Baxter's sense is one who confesses the faith under persecution. "Daniel would not cease praying three times a day" alludes to Daniel 6:10.
- **Isaiah 57:11 (flag):** NET wording (missing spaces in the fetched text have been added). The King James "that thou hast lied" is NET "that you would act so deceitfully"; "Have I not held my peace even of old, and thou fearest me not?" is a statement in the NET: "Because I have been silent for so long, you are not afraid of me."
- **Daniel 3:16–18 (important flag):** NET wording. Baxter quotes the King James "if it be so, the God whom we serve is able to deliver us," which he uses as a confession of confidence in God's power. The NET reads "If our God whom we are serving exists, he is able to rescue us," which turns the condition onto God's existence. This changes the sense of the words Baxter holds up as honoring God. The King James "We are not careful to answer thee" is NET "We do not need to give you a reply." Baxter's "&c." is rendered "etc."
- **Hebrews 11:27:** Baxter supplies "Moses" for the verse's "he" and omits "By faith." We kept his name in brackets and quoted the NET from "left Egypt." "He endured as seeing him that is invisible" is "he persevered as though he could see the one who is invisible."
- **Hebrews 13:6:** NET wording. "I will not fear what man shall do unto me" is NET "I will not be afraid. What can people do to me?"
- **Direction 19:** an unmarked allusion to David and Goliath (1 Samuel 17:34–37); as in Direction 10.
- **Luke 18:7, 8 (flag):** Baxter's "he bear long, to avenge them speedily" uses the King James "though he bear long with them" (be patient with them, take his time) and "he will avenge them speedily." The NET has no "bear long": "Will he delay long to help them? I tell you, he will give them justice speedily." Since the NET reads the first clause as a question expecting "no," his "though he bears long" cannot be matched to it, and we kept his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks.
- **Direction 20 word swaps:** "the living and the dead" is his "the quick and the dead"; "unwilling" is his "loth"; "wait" is his "stay" (twice); "make you reparation for" is his "make you reparations of." The Saul story is 1 Samuel 13:8–14 (unmarked allusion).
- **"drew back, because they could not live by faith" (paraphrase, no reference in Baxter):** Baxter's own wording, drawing on Hebrews 10:38–39; kept. NET: "if he shrinks back, I take no pleasure in him. But we are not among those who shrink back and thus perish, but are among those who have faith and preserve their souls." The reference is supplied here only.
- **Matthew 10:26:** NET wording. The King James "therefore" is not in the NET.
- **2 Thessalonians 1:6–10 (corrected reference):** Baxter prints "2 Thess. ii. 6–10"; the words are chapter 1. Corrected. NET wording; Baxter's "&c." is rendered "etc." inside his single quotation (as in the source), and we mark his skip in verse 10 with "...". The NET verse division differs slightly: "with flaming fire" begins a new sentence in verse 8, where the King James joins it to verse 7. "Taking vengeance" is NET "mete out punishment"; "admired in all them that believe" is "admired on that day among all who have believed."
- **Revelation 21:8 (flag):** Baxter's "the fearful and unbelieving" is the King James wording, not in quotation marks, so we kept it. His argument rests on "the fearful." The NET reads "the cowards, unbelievers," which keeps the sense but not the word "fearful." **risk his wrath:** his "venture upon his wrath."
- **Direction 22:** "grieve over" is Baxter's "condole" (used as a transitive verb, to lament for). "Felix before Paul" alludes to Acts 24:25; "the fire prepared for the devil and his angels" to Matthew 25:41. "As in Matthew 25" is his "as Matt. xxv."; we added "in" for sense.
- **1 Peter 4:17 (paraphrase):** Baxter cites verse 17 but rearranges verses 17–18 and inserts his own "of the persecutors," with "if judgment begin at the house of God" moved to the end; we kept his wording, modernized ("with so much difficulty" is his "with so much ado"). NET: "For it is time for judgment to begin, starting with the house of God. And if it starts with us, what will be the fate of those who are disobedient to the gospel of God? And if the righteous are barely saved, what will become of the ungodly and sinners?"
- **Baxter's books:** he names his own *Treatise of Self-denial*, *The Saints' Everlasting Rest* (his "Saints' Rest"), *The Last Enemy, Death*, and *The Believer's Last Work*, printed in quotation marks in the source; we put them in italics. "At length" is his "largely."
- **Note 352:** "amabiter" is apparently a misprint for "amabitur." The line closely matches a phrase in Horace (*Epistles* 2.1.14), but Baxter gives no source and we have not confirmed it.
- **Note 354:** attribution as Baxter prints it ("Hincmar. Rhemens.", Hincmar of Reims); we have not checked the quotation against the source.
- **Note 355:** references converted as printed. "Isaiah 42:2, 8" does not obviously concern fear; Baxter may have meant chapter 43 (43:1–2 and 43:5 say "fear not"), but we are not sure and have kept his references.
- **Note 356 (misprints):** "in juste" is read as "injuste" (unjustly), "celeri" as "sceleri" (to the crime), "el" as "et" (and), and "fit" as "sit." The source "Plutarch. dial. 66. li. 2." is as Baxter prints it; we have not traced the passage, and the attribution is his.
- **Note 357:** "See after" is rendered "See below," meaning later in the *Christian Directory*. We have not checked the cross-reference.
