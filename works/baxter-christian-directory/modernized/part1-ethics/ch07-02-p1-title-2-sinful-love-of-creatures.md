# Chapter 7: Title 2, Against Sinful Love of Creatures (Helps for Discovering and Mortifying It)

**Title 2. Directions against sinful love of creatures.**

Love is the master passion of the soul, because it has the chief object, even goodness, which is the object of the will; and simple love is nothing but complacency, which is nothing but the simple volition of good; and it is a passionate volition or complacency which we call the passion of love. [325] When this is good and when it is sinful I showed before; but yet because the one half of the cure here lies in the conviction, and it is so hard a thing to make any lover perceive a sinfulness in his love, I shall first help you in the trial of your love, to show the sinfulness of it; when I have first named the objects of it.

Any creature which seems good to us may possibly be the object of sinful love; as honor, greatness, authority, praises, money, houses, lands, cattle, food, drink, sleep, clothing, amusements, friends, relations, and life itself. As for lustful love, I shall speak of it later.

**Helps for discovering sinful love.**

**Direction 1. Make God's interest and his word the standard to judge all affections by.**

That which is against the love of God, and would lessen or hinder it, indeed, which does not directly or indirectly tend to further it, is certainly a sinful love; and so is all that is against his word. For the love of God is our final act upon our ultimate end, and therefore all that does not tend to it is a sin against our very end, and so against our nature and the use of our faculties.

**Direction 2. Therefore whatever creature is loved ultimately for itself, and not for a higher end, even for God, his service, his honor, his relation to it, or his excellence appearing in it, is sinfully loved.**

For it is made our god when it is loved ultimately for itself.

**Direction 3. Suspect all love to creatures which is very strong and violent, and easily kindled, and with difficulty moderated or quieted.**

Though you might think it is for some spiritual end or excellence that you love any person or anything, yet suspect it if it is so easy and strong; because that which is truly and purely spiritual is against corrupted nature, and comes from grace, which is but weak: we find no such easiness to love God, and Scripture, and prayer, and holiness; nor are our affections so violent toward these. It is well if all the fuel and blowing we can use will keep them alive. It is two to one that the flesh and the devil have put in some of their fuel or gunpowder, if it is fierce.

**Direction 4. Suspect all that love which selfishness and fleshly interest have a hand in.**

Is it some bodily pleasure and delight that you love so much? Or is it a good book or other help for your soul? We are so much more apt to exceed and sin in carnal, fleshly-mindedness than in loving what is good for our souls, that there we should be much more suspicious. If it is violent and for the body, it is ten to one there is sin in it.

**Direction 5. Suspect all that love to creatures which your reason can give no good account of, nor show you a justifiable cause.**

If you love one place or person much more than others, and do not know why, but love them because you cannot choose, this is much to be suspected: though God may sometimes kindle a secret love between friends, from an inexpressible unity or likeness of minds, beyond what reason will undertake to justify, yet this is rare, and commonly fancy, or folly, or carnality is the cause: however, it is more to be suspected and tested than rational love.

**Direction 6. Suspect all that fervent love to any creature which is hasty before sufficient trial; for commonly both persons and things have the best side outward, and seem better at the first appearance than they prove.**

Not but that a moderate love may be taken up upon the first appearance of any excellence, especially spiritual; but so as to allow for a possibility of being deceived, and finding more faultiness upon a fuller trial than we at first perceive. Have you lived in the house with the persons whom you so much admire? and have you tested them in their conduct? and seen them tested by hardships, losses, injuries, adversity, prosperity, or the offers of promotion or plenty in the world? you would little think what lurks undiscovered in the hearts of many that have excellent gifts, until trial reveals it!

**Direction 7. Test your affections in prayer before God, whether they are such as you dare boldly pray God either to increase or continue and bless; and whether they are such as conscience has no quarrel against.**

If they do not endure this trial, be the more suspicious, and search more narrowly: the name and presence of God in prayer does much to dispel the frauds of carnal reasonings. Yet persons who by melancholy are cast into diseased fears and scrupulosities are incapable of this way of trial.

**Direction 8. Consult with wise, impartial persons; and open your case to them without deceit, before affections have gone so far as to blind you, or leave you incapable of help.**

In this case, if in any case, the judgment of a bystander that is faithful and impartial is usually to be preferred before your own. For we are too near ourselves; and judgment will be bribed and biased even in the best and wisest persons.

**Direction 9. Yet do not cast away all because you discover much excess or carnality in your affections; for frequently there is a mixture both in the cause of love, and in the love itself, of good and evil.**

And when you have but taken out all that was selfish, and carnal, and erroneous in the cause, the carnal, violent love will cease; but not all love: for still there will and must remain the moderate, rational, and holy love, which is proportioned to the creature's worth and merit, and is terminated ultimately on God: the separation being made, this part must be preserved.

**Direction 10. Mere natural appetite in itself is neither morally good nor evil; but as it is well placed and ordered it is good, and as unruled or ill-ruled it is evil.**

**Helps to mortify sinful love.**

**Direction 1. The greatest of all means to cast out all sinful love is to keep the soul in the love of God (Jude 21), wholly taken up in admiring him, serving him, praising him, and rejoicing in him: of which see Chapter 3, Direction 11.**

We see that those who are taken up in the love and service of one person are not apt to be taken much with any other. [326] But it is not only by diversion, nor only by preoccupying and employing all our love, that the love of God cures sinful love; but besides these there is also a majesty in his objective presence which awes the soul, and commands all things else to keep their distance; and there is an unspeakable splendor and excellence in him, which obscures and annihilates all things else (though they are nearer, and clearly seen and known). And there is a heavenly kind of sweetness in his love, which puts the soul that has tasted it out of relish with transitory, inferior good. As he that has kept company with wise and learned men will no more admire the wit of fools. And as he that has been employed in the government of a kingdom or the loftiest studies will be no more in love with children's games, and paddling in the dirt.

**Direction 2. The next help is to see that the creature does not deceive you; and therefore that you are not rash and hasty; but wait until you come nearer to it, and see it unclothed of borrowed or put-on ornaments: and see it not only in the dress in which it appears in public, which often covers great deformities, but in its plain everyday clothes and nightclothes.**

Bring it to the light; and, if possible, also see it when it has endured the fire, which has taken off the paint and removed the dress. [327] Most of your inordinate love to creatures is by mistake and rashness. The devil dresses them up and paints them, that you may fall in love with them; or else he shows you only the outside of some common good, and hides the emptiness or rottenness within. Come nearer therefore, and stay longer, and prevent your shame and disappointments. Is it not a shame to see you dote on that place, or office, or thing this year, which you are weary of before the next? Or to see two persons impatiently fond of each other until they are married, and then to live in strife as weary of each other? How few persons or things have been too violently loved that were but sufficiently tested first!

**Direction 3. The next great help is to destroy self-love (as carnal and inordinate); for this is the parent, life, and root of all other sinful love whatever.**

Why does the worldly man over-love his wealth, and the proud man his greatness and reputation, and the pleasure-seeker his pleasures, but because they first over-love that flesh and self which all these are but the provision for? Why does a divisive sectarian overvalue and over-love all the party or sect that are of his own opinion, but because he first overvalues and over-loves himself? [328] Why do you love those above their worth who think highly of you, and are on your side, and are accustomed to praise you behind your back, or that do you a good turn, but because you first over-love yourselves? Why does lustful love inflame you, or the love of food, and drink, and amusement, and finery, carry you into such a gulf of sin, but that first you over-love your fleshly pleasure? What ensnares you in fondness to any person, but that you think they love you, or are suitable to your carnal end? See therefore that you mortify the flesh.

**Direction 4. Always remember how jealous God is of your love, and how much he is wronged when any creature encroaches upon his right.**

1. You are his own by creation; and did he give you love to lay out on others, and deny it to himself? 2. He daily and hourly maintains you; he gives you every breath, and bite, and mercy that you live upon; and will you love the creature with his part of your love? 3. How dearly he has bought your love in your redemption! 4. He has adopted you, and brought you into the nearest relation to him, that you may love him. 5. He has pardoned all your sins, and saved you from hell (if you are his own), that you may love him. 6. He has promised you eternal glory with himself that you may love him. 7. His excellence best deserves your love. 8. His creatures have nothing but from him, and were purposely sent to solicit your love for him rather than for themselves. And yet after all this shall they encroach upon his part? If you say it is not God's part that you give them, but their own; I tell you, all that love which you give the creature above its due, you take from God. But if it is such a love to the creature as does not exceed its worth, and is intended ultimately for God, and makes you not love him the less but the more, it is not that which I am speaking against, or persuading you to mortify.

**Direction 5. Look on the worst of the creature along with the best, and foresee what it will be when it withers, and what it will appear to you at the last.**

I have applied this against worldliness before, Chapter 4, Part 6, and I shall afterwards apply it to lustful love. Bring your beloved creature to the grave, and see it as it will appear at last, and much of the folly of your love will vanish.

**Direction 6. Understand well the most that it will do for you, and how short a time you must enjoy it, and do not flatter yourselves with the hopes of a longer possession than you have reason to expect.**

If men considered for how short a time they must possess what they dote upon, it would somewhat cool their fond affections.

**Direction 7. Remember that too much love has the present trouble of too much care, and the future trouble of too much grief, when you come to part with what you love.**

Nothing creates more care and grief to us than inordinate love. You know beforehand that you must part with it; and will you now be so glued to it that then it may tear your flesh and heart? Remember you caused all that yourselves.

**Direction 8. Remember that you provoke God to deprive you of what you over-love, or to allow it to grow unlovely to you.**

Many a man's horse that he over-loved has broken his neck: and many a man's child that he over-loved has died quickly, or lived to be his scourge and sorrow: and many a husband or wife that was over-loved has been quickly snatched away, or proved a thorn, or a continual grief and misery.

**Direction 9. If there are no other means left, prudently and moderately embitter to yourself the creature which you are fond of: which may be done many ways, according to the nature of it.**

By the less frequent or more abstemious use of it: or by using it more for benefit than delight; or by mixing in some mortifying, humbling exercises; or mixing in some self-denying acts, and minding more the good of others, etc.

**Direction 10. In the practice of all directions of this nature, there must be abundant difference made between a carnal, pleasure-loving heart, that is with difficulty drawn off from physical love, and a mortified, melancholy, or over-scrupulous person, who is running into the contrary extreme, and is afraid of every bite they eat, or of all they possess, or wear, or use, and sometimes of their very children and relations, and ready to run away from their mercies, or neglect their duties, suspecting that all is too much loved.**

And it is a very hard thing for us so to write or preach to one party but the other will misapply it to themselves, and make bad use of it. All that we can write or say is too little to mortify the fleshly man's affections: and yet, speak as cautiously as we can, the troubled soul will turn it into gall, to the increase of his trouble: and what we speak to his peace and settlement, though it proves too little and ineffective, yet will be effective to harden the misapplying pleasure-seeker in the sinful affections and liberty which he practices. Therefore it is best in such cases to have always a wise, experienced, faithful guide, to help you in the application in cases of difficulty and weight.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[325] Solus Amor facit hominem bonum vel malum. Paul Scaliger. Thes. p. 721. (Love alone makes a man good or evil. Paul Scaliger, *Thesaurus*, p. 721.)

[326] Nuptial love makes mankind; friendly love perfects it (much more divine love); but wanton love corrupts and debases it. Lord Bacon, Essay 10.

[327] Bias, in Laertio: Ita amandum quasi odio simus habituri: plurimos enim esse malos. Quam tamen sententiam Cicero in Lælio sapiente dicit plane indignum. Amicos sequere quos non pudeat elegisse. Idem ibid. (Bias, in Laertius: We should love as though we were later going to hate; for most people are bad. Yet Cicero, in the *Laelius*, says this saying is plainly unworthy of a wise man. Seek friends whom you would not be ashamed to have chosen. The same, same place. Rendered loosely.)

[328] See before, Chapter 4, Part 7.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). No verse is quoted in this section, so no NET text appears.
- **Title:** "Tit." in Baxter is his abbreviation for "Title," that is, a heading or division. Chapter 7 is divided into ten Titles, each on one passion; this file covers the opening of Title 2 (on sinful love of creatures), with its two lists of ten Directions. Numbering of the Directions restarts with the second list, as in Baxter.
- **Range:** the file begins at the heading of Title 2 and ends with the last Direction of "Helps to mortify sinful love." Nothing in the range was omitted.
- **Jude 21** (Helps to mortify, Direction 1): cited only; "keep the soul in the love of God" is Baxter's own phrase built on the verse, not a quotation, so it is left in his wording.
- **creature:** in Baxter, any created thing (a person, possession, place, or pleasure) as distinct from God. Kept.
- **passion; affections; melancholy; carnal; mortify:** Baxter's terms, kept (see GLOSSARY.md). "Passion" is his word for the emotions and affections.
- **complacency; volition** (opening paragraph): technical terms of his psychology, kept. "Complacency" is not self-satisfaction but delight or pleasure in a thing as good; "simple volition of good" is the will's plain willing of what is good. **passionate volition:** kept "passionate" here (rather than the glossary's "emotional") because Baxter means a volition that has become a passion, as the following words "the passion of love" show.
- **conviction; trial:** kept. "Conviction" is being convinced of the fault; "trial" is testing. Where "try/tried" means test, we use "test/tested."
- **food:** Baxter's "meat" (food generally). **clothing:** his "apparel." **amusements, amusement:** his "sports," "sport." **later:** his "anon." **lessen:** his "abate." **indeed:** his "yea." **with difficulty:** his "hardly" (Help 3; Mortify 10). **more apt:** his "apter."
- **inexpressible; likeness:** his "unexpressible; similitude." **fancy** (Help 5): imagination, whim; kept.
- **lived in the house:** his "dwelt in the house." **conduct:** his "conversations." **hardships:** his "crosses." **promotion:** his "preferment." **gifts:** his "parts" (abilities). **reveals:** his "manifest."
- **scrupulosities** (Help 7): kept; scruples of conscience, especially anxious ones. **incapable:** his "uncapable." **bystander:** his "stander-by."
- **is terminated ultimately on God** (Help 9): kept; Baxter's scholastic way of saying the love has God as its final object and end.
- **preoccupying** (Mortify 1): his "prepossessing," taking possession of the heart beforehand. **objective presence:** kept; God's presence as the object set before the soul. **heavenly:** his "celestial." **kept company with:** his "conversed with." **loftiest:** his "sublimest." **out of relish with:** kept; the soul loses its taste for lesser goods.
- **wait until:** his "stay while" ("while" meaning "until"). **put-on:** his "affected." **appears in public:** his "appeareth abroad." **plain everyday clothes and nightclothes:** his "homely habit and night attire." **dresses them up:** his "tricks them up." **prevent** (Mortify 2): kept in the modern sense of forestall.
- **worldly man:** his "worldling." **pleasure-seeker:** his "sensualist" (Mortify 3 and 10). **divisive sectarian:** his "dividing sectary." **are accustomed to:** his "use to." **finery:** his "bravery" (showy dress). **fondness / fond** (Mortify 3, 6): in Baxter, often foolish or doting love; kept. Baxter's questions "Why doth the worldling ... provision for." and "What insnareth you ... carnal end." end with a period in the source; we use a question mark. Likewise in Mortify 7, "and will you now be so glued to it ... heart." ends with a period in the source; we use a question mark.
- **Always remember** (Mortify 4): his "Still remember" ("still" meaning always); likewise "always a wise ... guide" (Mortify 10). **bite** (Mortify 4 and 10): his "bit" (of food). **solicit:** his "bespeak."
- **along with the best** (Mortify 5): his "with the best"; "along" added for clarity.
- **know beforehand:** his "foreknow." **allow:** his "suffer."
- **less frequent:** his "seldomer." **mixing in:** "in" added for modern idiom. **etc.:** his "&c."
- **yourself / you are** (Mortify 9): his "thyself / thou art"; Baxter shifts here to the singular "thou."
- **drawn off from** (Mortify 10): his "taken off from" (weaned from).
- **pleasure-loving:** his "voluptuous." **physical love:** his "sensual love," love of physical pleasures generally, not only sexual. **there must be abundant difference made:** his "there must abundance of difference be made," word order adjusted for modern grammar. **run away from their mercies:** his "overrun their mercies"; we take it to mean they refuse or flee from the good gifts God gives them, but the sense is not certain. **cautiously:** his "cautelously." **ineffective / effective:** his "uneffectual / effectual." **practices:** his "useth." **bad use:** his "ill use."
- **Cross-references not checked:** "I showed before" (when love is good and when sinful); Chapter 3, Direction 11 (on the love of God); Chapter 4, Part 6 (against worldliness); Chapter 4, Part 7 (note 328, probably on pride or self-love; we have not confirmed); the promised later treatment of lustful love.
- **Paul Scaliger** (note 325): probably Paul Skalich (Paulus Scaliger), a sixteenth-century writer; "Thes." is likely his *Thesaurus* or similar work. We have not confirmed the title.
- **Lord Bacon, Essay 10** (note 326): Francis Bacon's essay "Of Love." The parenthesis "(much more divine love)" appears to be Baxter's own addition to Bacon's sentence; we have not confirmed this against Bacon's text.
