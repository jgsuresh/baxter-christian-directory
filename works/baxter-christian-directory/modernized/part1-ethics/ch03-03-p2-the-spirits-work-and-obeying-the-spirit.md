# Chapter 3: Grand Direction III (part 2 of 2)

*This section begins in the middle of Grand Direction III ("Understand well what it is to believe in the Holy Spirit"): it picks up at the numbered points 2-5 that follow a longer list (the "ten things" mentioned in the first sentence). No new Grand Direction heading is supplied here.*

2. You may here discern also how the Spirit assures and comforts believers, and how palpably they err who think the Spirit comforts or assures us of our salvation without the use of its evidencing grace. The ten things mentioned above, is all that the Spirit does herein. But to expect his comforts without any measure of discerning his graces, which can only rationally prove our right to the blessings of the promise, this is to expect that he should comfort a rational creature not as rational, but darkly cause him to rejoice he knows not why: and that he should make no use of faith to our comfort: for faith rests understandingly upon the promise, and expects the performance of it to those that it is made to, and not to others. Indeed there is a common encouragement and comfort, which all men, even the worst, may take from the universal, conditional promise: and there is much easing of our fears and troubles that may be drawn from probabilities and uncertain hopes of our own sincerity and interest in the promise. But to expect any other assurance or comfort from the Spirit, without evidence, is but to expect direct revelations or inspirations to do the work, which the word of promise and faith should do. The soul's consent to the covenant of grace, and trusting acceptance of an offered Christ, is justifying, saving faith: every man has an object in the promise and offer of the gospel for this act, and therefore may rationally perform it. (Though all have not hearts to do it.) This may well be called faith of adherence; and is itself our evidence, from which we must conclude that we are true believers: the discerning of this evidence, called by some the reflex act of faith, is no act of faith at all, it being no believing of another, but the act of conscience, knowing what is in ourselves. The discerning and concluding that we are the children of God shares in faith and conscientious knowledge, which gave us the premises of such a conclusion.

3. You may hence perceive also how we are said to be "sealed" by the Spirit (Ephesians 1:13; Romans 8:9; Ephesians 4:30): even as a man's seal signifies the thing sealed to be his own; so the "Spirit of holiness in us" is God's seal upon us, signifying that we are his (2 Timothy 2:19). Every one that "has the Spirit" is sealed by having it: and that is his evidence, which, if he discerns it, he may know that he is thus sealed.

4. Hereby also you may see what the "earnest and first-fruits of the Spirit" is (2 Corinthians 1:22): the Spirit is given to us by God as the earnest of the glory which he will give us. To whoever he gives the spirit of faith, and love, and holiness, he gives the seed of life eternal, and an inclination thereto, which is his earnest of it.

5. Hereby also you may see how the Spirit witnesses that we are the children of God. The word "witness" is put here principally for evidence: if anyone questions our adoption, the witness or evidence which we must produce to prove it is the "Spirit of Jesus sanctifying us," and dwelling in us: this is the chief part (at least) of the sense of the text (Romans 8:16). Though it is true that the same Spirit witnesses by (1.) Showing us the grace which he has given us; (2.) And by showing us the truth of the promise made to all believers; (3.) And by helping us from those promises to conclude with boldness that we are the children of God; (4.) And by helping us to rejoice in it.

II. I have been the longer (though too short) in acquainting you with the office of the Holy Spirit, (supposing your belief that he is the third person in the Trinity,) because it is an article of great importance, neglected by many that profess it, and because there are so many and dangerous errors in the world about it. Your great care now must be, 1. To find this Spirit in you, as the principle of your operations: and, 2. To obey it, and follow its motions, as it leads you up to communion with God. Of the first I have spoken in the first chapter. For the second, observe these few directions.

**Direction 1. Be sure you do not mistake the Spirit of God and its motions, nor receive, instead of them, the motions of Satan, or of your passions, pride, or fleshly wisdom.**

It is easy to think you are obeying the Spirit when you are obeying Satan and your own corruptions against the Spirit. By these fruits the Spirit of God is known. 1. The Spirit of God is for heavenly wisdom, and neither for foolishness nor treacherous craftiness (Psalm 19:7; 94:8; Jeremiah 4:22; 1 Corinthians 2:4–7). 2. The Spirit of God is a Spirit of love, delighting to do good; its doctrine and motions are for love, and tend to good; abhorring both selfishness and harm to others (Galatians 5:21, 22). 3. He is a Spirit of concord, and is ever for the unity of all believers; abhorring both divisions among the saints, and carnal compromises and alliances with the wicked (1 Corinthians 12; Ephesians 4:3–6, 13; 1 Corinthians 1:10; 3:3; Romans 16:17, 18). 4. He is a Spirit of humility and self-denial, making us, and our knowledge, and gifts, and worth, to be very little in our own eyes; [91] abhorring pride, ambition, self-exalting, boasting, as also the actual debasing of ourselves by earthliness or other sin (Matthew 18:3; Ephesians 4:2). 5. He is a Spirit of meekness, and patience, and forbearance; abhorring dull insensibility, and excessive passion, boisterousness, tumult, envy, contention, reviling, and revenge (Matthew 11:28, 29; Ephesians 4:2; James 3; 1 Peter 2:20–23; Galatians 5:20; Romans 12:18–20; Ephesians 4:31; Colossians 3:8). 6. He is a Spirit of zeal for God, resolving men against known sin, and for known truth and duty; abhorring a furious, destroying zeal, and also an indifference in the cause of God; and a yielding compliance with that which is against it (Galatians 4:18; Numbers 25:11, 13; Titus 2:14; James 3:15, 17; Luke 9:55; Revelation 3:16). 7. He is a Spirit of mortification, crucifying the flesh, and still contending against it, and causing men to live above all the glory, and riches, and pleasures of the world: abhorring both carnal licentiousness and indulgence in physical pleasure, and also the destroying and disabling of the body, under pretense of true mortification (Romans 8:1, 13; Galatians 5:17; Romans 13:13, 14; 1 Corinthians 9:27; 2 Peter 2:19; Colossians 2:18, 21, 23). 8. The Spirit of Christ does not contradict the doctrine of Christ in the holy Scripture, but moves us to an exact conformity to it (Isaiah 8:20). This is the sure rule to try the claims and motions of every spirit by: for we are sure that the Spirit of Christ is the author of that word; and we are sure he is not contrary to himself. 9. The motions of the Spirit all tend to our good, and are neither frivolous, irrelevant, or hurtful in the end: they are all for the perfecting of sanctification, obedience, and for our salvation. Therefore worthless trifles, or despair, and harmful distractions and disturbances of mind, which drive from God, unfit for duty, and hinder salvation, are not the motions of the Spirit of God (2 Timothy 1:7; Romans 8:15; Isaiah 11:2; Galatians 5:22; Zechariah 12:10; 1 Peter 4:14; 2 Corinthians 3:6). 10. Lastly, The Spirit of God subjects all to God, and raises the heart to him, and makes us spiritual and divine, and is ever for God's glory (1 John 4:5–6; 1 Corinthians 6:11, 17, 20; Ephesians 2:18, 22; Philippians 3:3, 19, 20; 1 Peter 1:2; 4:6). Examine the texts here cited, and you will find that by all these fruits the Spirit of God is known from all seducing spirits, and from the fancies or passions of self-important men.

**Direction 2. Do not quench the Spirit, either by willful sin or by your neglecting of its offered help.**

It is as the spring to all your spiritual motions; as the wind to your sails: you can do nothing without it. Therefore reverence and regard its help, and pray for it, and obey it, and do not neglect it. When you are sure it is the Spirit of God indeed that is knocking at the door, do not behave yourselves as if you did not hear. 1. Obey him quickly: delay is an immediate, ungrateful refusal, and a kind of a denial. 2. Obey him thoroughly: a half-obedience is disobedience. Do not put him off with Ananias and Sapphira's gift; the half of that which he requires of you. 3. Obey him constantly: not sometimes listening to him, and more often neglecting him; but waiting on him in a learning, obedient course of life.

**Direction 3. Do not neglect those means which the Spirit has appointed you to use, for the receiving of its help, and which he uses in his holy operations.**

If you will meet with him, wait on him in his own way, and do not expect him in by-ways where he does not usually go. Pray, and meditate, and hear, and read, and do your best, and expect his blessing. Though your plowing and sowing will not give you a plentiful harvest without the sun, and rain, and the blessing of God, yet these will not do it either, unless you plow and sow. God has not appointed a course of means in nature or morality in vain, nor will he usually meet you in any other way.

**Direction 4. Do most when the Spirit helps you most.**

Do not neglect the extraordinary measures of his assistance: if he extraordinarily helps you in prayer, or meditation, make good use of that help, and do not break off so soon as at other times (without necessity): not that you should omit duty till you feel his help; for he usually comes in with help in the performance, and not in the neglect of duty: but do not tire yourself out with forced length, when you lack the life.

**Direction 5. Do not be ungrateful for the assistance he has given you.**

Do not deny his grace: do not ascribe it to nature: remember it to encourage your future expectations: ungratefulness and neglect are the way to be denied further help.

*Question.* But how shall I know whether good effects are from the means, or from my reason and effort, and when from the Spirit of God?

*Answer.* It is as if you should ask, How shall I know whether my harvest is from the earth, or sun, or rain, or God, or from my labor? I will tell you how. They are all joint causes: if the effect is there, they all concur; if the effect is wanting, some of them were wanting. It is foolish to ask which is the cause, when the effect is not produced except by the concurrence of them all. If you had asked, which cause did fail, when the effect fails? there would be reason in that question; but there is none in this. The more to blame those foolish atheists, that think God or the Spirit is not the cause, if they can but find that reason and means are in the effect. Your reason, and conscience, and means would fall short of the effect, if the Spirit did not put life into all.

*Objection.* But I am exceedingly troubled and confounded with continual doubts about every motion that is in my mind, whether it is from the Spirit of God, or not.

*Answer.* The more is your ignorance, or the malice of Satan causing your disquiet. In one word, you have sufficient direction to resolve those doubts, and end those troubles. Is it good, or evil, or indifferent, that you are moved to? This question must be resolved from the word of God, which is the rule of duty. If it is good, in matter, and manner, and circumstances, it is from the Spirit of God (either its common or special operation): if it is evil or indifferent, you cannot ascribe it to the Spirit. Remember that the Spirit does not come to you to make a new duty for you which the Scripture never made your duty, and so to bring an additional law; but to move and help you in that which was your duty before. (Only it may give the matter, while Scripture gives the obligation by its general command.) If you do not know what is your duty, and what is not, it is your ignorance of Scripture that must be cured: interpret Scripture well, and you may interpret the Spirit's motions easily. If any new duty is motioned to you, which Scripture does not command, take such motions as not from God (unless it were by extraordinary, confirmed revelation).

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible. Fetched from labs.bible.org for the verses below; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Baxter's long runs of references (Directions 1's ten marks) are supporting citations rather than quotations. They are converted to modern form but their contents were not checked against the NET here.
- **No verse is quoted at length in this section.** Baxter quotes only short phrases, which are his paraphrases or blends, so his wording is kept (modernized) and the NET is given here.
- **"sealed"** (point 3; Ephesians 1:13; Romans 8:9; Ephesians 4:30): NET Ephesians 1:13: "you were marked with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit"; Ephesians 4:30: "by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption." Romans 8:9 does not use the word "seal": the NET reads "if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, this person does not belong to him." Baxter uses it for his phrase "has the Spirit."
- **"Spirit of holiness in us"** (point 3; cited to 2 Timothy 2:19): this is Baxter's phrase, and it does not appear in the NET at 2 Timothy 2:19. The NET reads "God's solid foundation remains standing, bearing this seal: 'The Lord knows those who are his,' and 'Everyone who confesses the name of the Lord must turn away from evil.'" Baxter's point, that the seal shows "we are his," rests on "those who are his." We have not checked where else he may have drawn "Spirit of holiness."
- **"earnest and first-fruits of the Spirit"** (point 4; cited to 2 Corinthians 1:22): a blend of two verses. NET 2 Corinthians 1:22: "who also sealed us and gave us the Spirit in our hearts as a down payment." "First-fruits" is from Romans 8:23 (NET: "who have the firstfruits of the Spirit"), which Baxter does not cite here. **Flag:** the NET has "down payment" where Baxter's "earnest" (the traditional English word) stands. Baxter's argument (the Spirit is a pledge or first installment of glory) works with either word, and we keep his "earnest" and explain it as a first payment guaranteeing the rest.
- **"witness"** (point 5; Romans 8:16): NET: "The Spirit himself bears witness to our spirit that we are God's children." The NET says the Spirit bears witness "to our spirit," where the King James has "with our spirit." Baxter reads "witness" mainly as "evidence" and the Spirit's own sanctifying work in us as that evidence, and the NET's wording does not undercut that reading, though it puts more weight on the Spirit's direct testimony. "Spirit of Jesus sanctifying us" is Baxter's own phrase, not a quoted verse.
- **Unmarked allusions** (Baxter's wording kept): "Quench not the Spirit" (1 Thessalonians 5:19); the Spirit "knocking at the door" (compare Revelation 3:20); Ananias and Sapphira's gift (Acts 5:1–11); "the wind to your sails" (a figure of speech, not a quotation).
- **Further NET flags** (Baxter cites these in the ten marks of the Spirit; not quoted by him, but the NET changes the word he leans on). **Zeal** (mark 6; Galatians 4:18; Numbers 25:11; Titus 2:14): Baxter calls the Spirit "a Spirit of zeal for God." The NET of Galatians 4:18 reads "it is good to be sought eagerly for a good purpose at all times," and of Titus 2:14 "a people who are truly his, who are eager to do good"; only Numbers 25:11 keeps the word ("such zeal for my sake"). We have not checked the King James wording here, but "zeal" in Baxter's argument is carried in the NET by "eagerly" and "eager." **Romans 8:1** (mark 7): the NET reads only "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus," without the further clause about walking after the Spirit that appears in the King James text; Baxter cites the verse for mortification, which rests more on verse 13. **Isaiah 8:20** (mark 8): Baxter cites it for testing every spirit by Scripture; the NET reads "Then you must recall the Lord's instructions and the prophetic testimony of what would happen. Certainly they say such things because their minds are spiritually darkened," which does not state a test of spirits by Scripture as plainly as the wording Baxter evidently has in mind. **2 Timothy 1:7** (mark 9): NET "God did not give us a Spirit of fear but of power and love and self-control."
- **Reference punctuation:** where Baxter lists consecutive verses with a comma (for example "Gal. v. 21, 22") the comma is kept; ranges he prints with a hyphen use an en dash.
- **Heading structure:** the range begins at points "2." to "5." of a longer list begun above it, and then Baxter's "II." (the second main part of this Grand Direction). The "Direct." items I-V are Baxter's directions under "II." and are numbered 1-5 here, as he has them.
- **Question and Answer labels:** Baxter's "Quest.", "Answ.", and "Obj." appear as Question, Answer, and Objection.
- **reference to the "first chapter"** (paragraph II): Baxter points back to Chapter 1 of the Directory; not checked here.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): Holy Ghost, Holy Spirit; fiducial, trusting; fetched, drawn; abatement, easing; immediate revelations, direct revelations; participateth of, shares in; grand, great; pretences (in mark 8), claims; hurtfulness, harm to others; stupidity, dull insensibility; inordinate, excessive; indifferency, indifference; compliances and confederacies, compromises and alliances; sensuality, indulgence in physical pleasure; pretence, pretense; ludicrous, frivolous; impertinent, irrelevant; finally (in "hurtful finally"), in the end; unprofitable trifles, worthless trifles; self-conceited, self-important; wilful, willful; speedily, quickly; present (in "a present, unthankful refusal"), immediate; unthankful, ungrateful; obediential, obedient; hearkening, listening; attend him, wait on him; affected length, forced length (Baxter means length put on for show); want the life, lack the life; improve that help, make good use of that help; con-causes, joint causes; endeavour, effort; whomsoever, whoever; "make you new duty", "make a new duty for you."
- **Kept as Baxter's terms:** faith of adherence; the reflex act of faith (the believer's own awareness that he believes, which Baxter says is the act of conscience and not of faith); evidencing grace; premises; interest in the promise (a share in it, a claim to it); mortification; carnal; the "common or special operation" of the Spirit (the Spirit's ordinary workings on all people, as against his saving work in believers); atheists.
- **"conscientious knowledge"** (point 2): knowledge by conscience, that is, self-knowledge.
- **"Motion(s) of the Spirit"** means inward movements or promptings, which Baxter distinguishes from outward revelation.
- **Cicero** (note 91): Baxter's quotation, attributed to Cicero's work on the nature of the gods. We have not confirmed the passage.
