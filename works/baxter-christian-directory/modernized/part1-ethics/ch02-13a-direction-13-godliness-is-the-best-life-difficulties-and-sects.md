# Chapter 2: Direction 13 (part 1 of 2)

**Direction 13. Know that true godliness is the best life upon earth, and the only way to perfect happiness. Always regard it, therefore, and use it as the best; and with great diligence resist those temptations which would make it seem to you a bewildering, grievous, or unpleasant thing.**

*In the margin: What godliness is.*

There are all things coming together in a holy life to make it the most delightful life on earth, to a rational, purified mind that is not held captive to the flesh, and does not live on air or dung. The object of it is the eternal God himself, the infallible truth, the only satisfying good; and all these graciously stooping down and appearing to us in the mysterious but suitable mirror of a Mediator; redeeming, reconciling, teaching, governing, sanctifying, justifying, and glorifying all that are his own. The end of it is the pleasing and glorifying of our Maker, Redeemer, and Sanctifier; and the everlasting happiness of ourselves and others. The rule of it is the infallible revelation of God, delivered to the church by his prophets, and his Son, and his apostles, and contained in the Holy Scriptures, and sealed by the miracles and works of the Holy Spirit who dictated them. The work of godliness is a living to God, and preparing for everlasting life, by foreseeing, foretasting, seeking, and rejoicing in that endless happiness which we shall have with God; and by walking according to the Spirit, and avoiding the filthiness, delusions, and troubles of the world and the flesh. The nature of man is not capable of a more noble, profitable, and delightful life than this which God has called us to by his Son. And if we did but rightly know it, we should follow it with continual eagerness and delight. Be sure, therefore, to think of godliness as it is, and not as it is misrepresented by the devil and the ungodly. Read what I have written of this in my *A Saint or a Brute*.

As long as a man thinks of religion as it is, even the sweetest and most delightful life, so long he will follow it willingly and with his heart, and despise the temptations and distractions of fleshly gain and pleasure. He will be sincere, as not being only drawn by other men, or outward advantages, nor frightened into it by an emotion or fearfulness, but loving religion for itself, and for its excellent ends: and then he will be cheerful in all the duties, and under all the sufferings and difficulties of it; and he will be most likely to persevere to the end. We cannot expect that the heart or will should be any more for God and godliness than the understanding practically grasps them as good. Nay, we must always perceive in them a transcendent goodness, above all that is to be found in a worldly life; or else the apparent goodness of the creature will divert us, and carry away our minds. We may see in the very animals what a power perception has over their actions. If your horse is but going to his home or pasture, how freely will he go through thick and thin! but if he goes unwillingly, his travel is troublesome and slow, and you have much trouble to get him on. It will be so with you in your way to heaven.

It is therefore the principal design of the devil to hide the goodness and pleasantness of religion from you; and to make it appear to you as a terrible or tedious life. By this means it is that he keeps men from it; and by this means he is still trying to draw you back again, and frustrate your good beginnings and your hopes. If he can thus misrepresent religion to your understandings, he will quickly alienate your wills, and corrupt your lives, and make you turn to the world again, and seek for pleasure somewhere else, and only settle for some heartless lip-service, to keep up some deceitful hope of being saved. And the means which Satan uses to these ends are such as these:

*In the margin: How Satan would make religion seem to be a bewildering, unpleasant thing: by difficulties.*

1. He will do his work to overwhelm you with apparent doubts and difficulties, and leave you at a loss, and make religion seem to you a bewildering and not a satisfying thing. This is one of his most dangerous assaults upon the weak and young beginners. Difficulties and emotions are the things which he makes use of to bewilder you, and put you out of an orderly, cheerful seeking of salvation. When you read the Scriptures, he will remind you of an abundance of difficulties in all you read or hear. He will show you seeming contradictions; and tell you that you will never be able to understand these things. He will cast in thoughts of unbelief and blasphemy, and cause you, if he can, to roll them in your mind: if you do not cast them out with abhorrence, but argue with the devil, he hopes to prove too hard at least for such children and unequipped soldiers as you: and if you do reject them, and refuse to argue it with him, he will sometimes tell you that your cause is worthless, or else you need not be afraid to think of all that can be said against it; and this way he gets an advantage over you to draw you to unbelief: and if you escape better than so, at least he will harass and terrify you with the hideousness of his temptations; and make you think you are forsaken by God, because such blasphemous thoughts have been so often in your minds: and thus he will one while tempt you to blasphemy, and another while frighten and torment you with the thoughts of such temptations.

So also in the study of other good books, he will tempt you to fix upon all that seems difficult to you, and there to bewilder and perplex yourselves: and in your meditations, he will seek to make all tend but to bewilder and overwhelm you; keeping always either hard or fearful things before your eyes; or breaking and scattering your thoughts in pieces, so that you cannot bring them into any order, nor set them together, nor make anything of them, nor drive them to any desirable end. So in your prayers he would gladly bewilder you, either with fear, or with doubtful and distracting thoughts about God, or your sins, or the matter or manner of your duty, or questioning whether your prayers will be heard. And so in your self-examination, he will always seek to puzzle you, and leave you more in darkness than you began, and make you afraid of looking homeward, or keeping company with yourselves; like a man that is afraid to lie in his own house when he thinks it haunted with some apparitions. And thus the devil would make all your religion to be but like the unwinding of a ball of yarn, or a skein of silk that is tangled; so that you may cast it away in weariness or despair.

Your remedy against this dangerous temptation is to remember that you are yet young in knowledge, and that ignorance is like darkness, which will cause doubts, and difficulties, and fears; and that all these will vanish as your light increases: and therefore you must wait in patience, until your riper knowledge fits you for satisfaction. And in the meantime, be sure that you occupy your hearts most with the great, fundamental, necessary, plain, and certain points, which your salvation is laid on, and which are more suited to your state and strength. If you will be gnawing bones, when you should be sucking milk, and have not patience to wait until you are past your childhood, no wonder if you find them hard, and if they stick in your throats, or break your teeth. See that you live upon God in Christ, and love and practice what you know, and think of the excellency of so much as is already revealed to you. You know already what is the end that you must seek, and where your happiness consists; and what Christ has done to prepare it for you, and how you must be justified, and sanctified, and walk with God. Do you have God, and Christ, and heaven to think on, and all the mercies of the gospel to delight in, and will you set these aside as ordinary matters, or overlook them, and perplex yourselves about every difficulty in your way? Make clean work before you as you go, and live in the joyful acknowledgment of the mercies which you have received, and in the practice of the things you know, and then your difficulties will vanish as you go on.

*In the margin: By various sects.*

2. Another of Satan's tricks is to bewilder you with the noise of sectarians, and various opinions in religion: while the popish sect tell you that if you will be saved, you must be of their church; and others say you must be of theirs: and when you find that the sects are many, and their reasonings such as you cannot answer, you will be in danger either of taking up some of their deceits, or of being bewildered among them all, not knowing which church and religion to choose. [68]

But here consider that there is but one universal church of Christians in the world, of which Christ is the only King and Head, and every Christian is a member. You were sacramentally admitted into this catholic church by baptism, and spiritually by your being "born of the Spirit." You have all the promises of the gospel, that if you believe in Christ you shall be saved; and that all the living members of this church are loved by Christ as members of his body, and shall be presented unspotted to the Father, by him who is the Savior of his body (Ephesians 5:23-27, 29); "and that by one Spirit we are all baptized or entered into this one body" (1 Corinthians 12:12-13). If then you have faith, and love, and the Spirit, you are certainly a Christian, and a member of Christ, and of this universal church of Christians. And if there were any other church but what are the parts of this one, then this would not be universal, and Christ must have two bodies. You are not saved for being a member of the church of Rome, or Corinth, or Ephesus, or Philippi, or Thessalonica, or of any other such; but for being a member of the universal church or body of Christ, that is, a Christian. And as you are a subject of the King, and a member of this kingdom, whatever corporation you are a member of, (perhaps at one time of one, and at another time of another,) so you are a subject of Christ, whatever particular church you are of; for it is no church if they are not Christians, or subjects of Christ. For one sect then to say, Ours is the true church, and another to say, Nay, but ours is the true church, is as mad as to dispute whether your hall, or kitchen, or parlor, or coal shed is your house; and for one to say, This is the house, and another, Nay, but that, when a child can tell them that the best is but a part, and the house contains them all: and for the papists, who claim to be the whole, and deny all others to be Christians and saved, except the subjects of the pope of Rome, it is so irrational, antichristian a fiction and usurpation, and odious, cruel, and groundless a damnation of the far greatest part of the body of Christ, that it is fitter for detestation than dispute. And if such a boast would frighten the world out of their wits, no doubt other bishops also would make use of it, and say, All are damned that will not be subject to us. But if you would see the folly and mischief of popery, both in this and other points, I refer you to my treatise of the *Catholic Church*, and my *Key for Catholics*, and my *Safe Religion*, and my *Disputations against Johnson*, and my *Winding-sheet for Popery*.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). No verse is quoted here in NET wording; see the next two items.
- **Ephesians 5:23-27, 29:** Baxter printed "Eph. 23-27, 29" with no chapter number. The passage is Ephesians 5 (verse 23 calls Christ "the savior of the body"; verse 27 speaks of presenting the church "holy and blameless"; verse 29 "feeds it and takes care of it"). We supplied the chapter. It is a reference for his sentence, not a quotation.
- **1 Corinthians 12:12-13:** Baxter's quoted words ("by one Spirit we are all baptized or entered into this one body") are his paraphrase with a gloss ("or entered into"), so we kept his wording. The NET reads: "in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body." Baxter's "by one Spirit" reads the Spirit as the agent of baptism; the NET's "in one Spirit" may be closer to a sense of sphere or element (we have not checked the Greek) and can be read otherwise. His argument (membership in the one body is by the Spirit, not by a particular church) still stands on the NET wording, but the phrase he leans on is not exactly the NET's.
- **"born of the Spirit"** (paragraph on the universal church): Baxter gives no reference; the words match John 3:8 in the NET ("everyone who is born of the Spirit"), and the thought is that of John 3:5-8 (a reference supplied by us, not in Baxter's text).
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "walking after the Spirit" (modernized to "according to"; compare Romans 8:4, our suggestion); "one universal church ... Christ the only King and Head" (compare Ephesians 5:23; Colossians 1:18).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): concurrent, coming together; delectable, delightful; condescending, graciously stooping down; glass, mirror (as in "now we see through a glass"); comprised, contained; indite, dictate; operations, works; vexations, troubles; alacrity, eagerness; avocations, distractions; passion, emotion (a sudden strong feeling); appearing (doubts, goodness), apparent; brutes, animals; apprehension, perception; ado, trouble; suddenly, quickly; minds you of, reminds you of; confound, bewilder; regular, orderly; naught, worthless; dispute (with the devil), argue; unprovided, unequipped; scape, escape; molest, harass; fain, gladly; reduce (to order), bring; ravelled, tangled; bottom of yarn, ball of yarn; conversing with yourselves, keeping company with yourselves; employ, occupy; take on them, claim; crack, boast; sectaries, sectarians; divers, various; lay by, set aside; common (matters), ordinary; no marvel, no wonder.
- **"Still apprehend it" (Direction heading):** "still" here means "always," and "apprehend" means "take it to be," so "Always regard it ... as the best." (In the text we kept Baxter's "use it as the best.")
- **A living unto God; the creature:** "creature" means a created thing or created good, as opposed to the Creator.
- **dung:** kept as Baxter's blunt word for what the fleshly mind lives on.
- **convert/religion/godliness:** left as Baxter wrote.
- **Titles:** *A Saint or a Brute*, *Catholic Church*, *Key for Catholics*, *Safe Religion*, *Disputations against Johnson*, and *Winding-sheet for Popery* are Baxter's own books, as he names them; we have not checked the exact titles against any edition.
- **popish; papists; popery; "antichristian"; "odious, cruel, and groundless a damnation":** Baxter's polemic against the Roman Church, kept exactly as he wrote it. "Papist" is his word for a Roman Catholic.
- **corporation** (paragraph on the universal church): in Baxter's day a chartered body such as a town or borough; the point is that a person can belong to a town government and still be the king's subject.
- **sacramentally ... spiritually:** Baxter's contrast between outward admission by baptism and inward new birth.
- **catholic:** here means "universal," not Roman Catholic.
- **Source oddities:** "hall, or kitchen, or parlour, or coal-house" is a list of rooms and outbuildings of a house. The pointing "(perhaps sometime of one, and sometime of another,)" was kept as a parenthesis.
- **Cicero** (note 68): quoted by Baxter in support of the point that disagreement among people unsettles their confidence about what is true. We have not confirmed the passage in Cicero.
- **Cross-reference:** the Direction continues in the next file, starting at the margin note "By scrupulosity."
