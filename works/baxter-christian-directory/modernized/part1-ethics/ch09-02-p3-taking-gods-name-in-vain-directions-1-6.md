# Chapter 9: Against profane swearing (Title 2), taking God's name in vain; Directions 1–6

*This file continues Title 2 of Chapter 9 partway through; it begins at Baxter's numbered point III, after his answer to Question 1 on giving oaths.*

*In the margin: How God's name is taken in vain.*

III. As to the case of taking God's name in vain, which for brevity I join with swearing, it is done, 1. Either in the grossest and most heinous sort; 2. Or in a lower sort. 1. The grossest sort of taking God's name in vain is by perjury; or calling him in for witness to a lie. For among the Jews, vanity and a lie were words frequently taken in the same meaning. 2. But the lower sort of taking God's name in vain is when it is used lightly, irreverently, contemptuously, jestingly, or without just cause; and in these also there is profaneness and a very great sin, which is aggravated according to the degree of the contempt or profanation. [493] It is a great sin irreverently in common talk to make a byword of saying, O Lord, or O God, or O Jesus, or God help us, or Lord have mercy on us, or God send this or that, or any way to take God's name in vain; but to use it in jeers and scorns at religion, or make play-books or stage-plays with such profane contemptuous jeers, is one of the greatest villainies that man's tongue can be guilty of against his Maker. (Of which later.)

**IV. Direction 1. For the avoiding of all this profaneness in swearing and taking the name of God in vain, the first direction must be this general one, to use all the directions given in Chapter 1 for a wicked man's attaining true conversion; and also to observe how great an evidence this sin is of a graceless, ungodly, miserable soul.**

For it is supposed to be an ordinary or frequent sin, and therefore to have no effective principle in the heart which is against it; and therefore to have the principal room in the will; and therefore to be unrepented of (as to any saving, renewing repentance): if you had any true grace, it would teach you to fear and honor God more: to make light of God is inconsistent with godliness, if it is in a predominant degree; for they are directly contrary.

**Direction 2. Get your heart aware of the intrinsic evil of your sin.**

It would never be so easily and familiarly committed by you if you did not think it small. That you may know it, consider these following aggravations. [494]

1. Consider who that God is whom you abuse. [495] Is he not the great and terrible Majesty, that made the world, and upholds it, and orders it by his will? the Governor and Judge of all the earth, infinitely excelling the sun in glory? a God most holy, and in holiness to be mentioned? And will you make a byword of his fearsome name? Will you profanely swear by this holy name? and use the name of your God as you would scarcely use the name of your father or your king? Will you irreverently and contemptuously toss it like a football? Do you know no more difference between God and man? Know God, and you will sooner tremble at his name than thus irreverently abuse it.

2. Consider who you are that thus venture to profane the holy name of God. Are you not his creature and his subject, bound to honor him? Are you not a worm, unable to resist him? Can he not tread you into hell, or ruin you, and be avenged on you with a word or less? He needs to say no more but, Thus I will have it, to execute his vengeance on the greatest of his enemies: if he wills it, it will be done. And are you then a person fit to despise this God, and abuse his name? Is it not a wonder of condescension in him that he will give leave to such worms as we to pray to him, and to praise and worship him, and that he will accept it at our hands? and yet can you venture thus to slight him and despise him? I have often heard the same impious tongue reproach the prayers of the godly, as if they were too bold and familiar with God, and pleading against long or often praying, because man must not be so bold with God, and persuading others that God does not accept it, which yet itself was bold familiarly to swear by his name, and use it lightly and in common talk. And indeed God's servants must take heed of rude and irreverent boldness even in prayer. How much more then is the boldness of your profaning God's holy name to be condemned? Must they take heed how they use it in prayer and praise, and dare you abuse it by oaths, and curses, and empty speech?

3. Do you not sometimes pray by that name which you profanely swear by? If not, you seem utterly to renounce God, and are a miserable wretch indeed; but if you do, what a hypocrite do you show yourself to be in all your prayers, who take it upon yourself to reverence that name of God which you can toss irreverently, and swear and curse by when you are off your knees. It is part of Bishop Hall's character of the hypocrite that he bows to the name of Jesus, and swears by the name of God, and prays to God at church, whom he forgets or swears by the rest of the week. Does not your conscience pinch you for this hypocrisy, when in your prayers you think of this abuse of God?

4. Think, man, what use you will have for that holy name in your distress which you now abuse. When sickness and death come, then you will cry, Lord, Lord! then the name of God will be called on more reverently. And dare you now make a football of it? Do you not fear lest it should be then your terror, to remember on your deathbed, when you are calling upon God, Oh this is the name that I used to swear by, or to take in vain?

5. Remember that millions of glorious angels are magnifying that great and holy name which you are profaning and taking in vain. And do you not wonder that they do not, some of them, become the executioners of the vengeance of God against you? and that the earth does not open and swallow you up? Shall a worm on earth be tossing that holy name, or swearing by it profanely, which a world of glorious angels are magnifying?

6. Consider that you are more impious than they that profane things hallowed and consecrated to God. Was Belshazzar punished with the loss of kingdom and life for carousing in the vessels of the sanctuary? Would you think him to be profane that should make a stable of the church, and should feed his swine with the communion cup? And do you not know that the name of God himself has a higher degree of holiness than any place or utensils of his worship have? and therefore that it is a greater profaneness to abuse his name than to abuse any of these? Does not your tongue then convict you of hypocrisy, when you would exclaim against any that should thus profane the church, or font, or communion cup, or table, and yet you yourself ordinarily profane the very holy name of God, and use it as a common name?

7. Consider how unworthily you repay God for giving you your tongue and speech. He gave you this noble faculty to honor him by; and is this your thanks, to use it to dishonor him, by swearing and taking his name in vain?

8. Your infectious breath corrupts others. It tends to bring God into common contempt among his own creatures, when they hear his name contemptuously spoken of.

9. You forget how tender and jealous God has shown himself to be of the honor of his holy name; and what terrible threatenings he has pronounced against the profaners of it, and what judgments he has executed on them. [496] Leviticus 19:12, "You must not swear falsely in my name, so that you do not profane the name of your God. I am the Lord." So Leviticus 18:21. And of the priests it is said, Leviticus 21:6, "They must be holy to their God, and they must not profane the name of their God." So Leviticus 22:2, 31, 32, "You must be sure to do my commandments. I am the Lord. You must not profane my holy name, and I will be sanctified in the midst of the Israelites. I am the Lord who sanctifies you." Deuteronomy 28:58, 59, "If you refuse to obey all the words of this law, the things written in this scroll, and refuse to fear this glorious and awesome name, THE LORD YOUR GOD, then the Lord will increase your punishments and those of your descendants—great and long-lasting afflictions and severe, enduring illnesses." Worshiping God and trusting in him is called a "walking in his name," and "calling upon his name." See Micah 4:5; Psalm 99:6. The place of his public worship is called "The place where he puts or records his name," Exodus 20:24; Deuteronomy 12:5, 11, 21. Isaiah 29:23, "they will honor my name. They will honor the Holy One of Jacob; they will respect the God of Israel." Isaiah 48:11, "for how can I allow my name to be defiled? I will not share my glory with anyone else!" God tells Moses, and Moses tells Aaron when his sons were slain, "Among the ones close to me I will show myself holy, and in the presence of all the people I will be honored," Leviticus 10:3. So Leviticus 24:10, 14, a man that in striving with another blasphemed and cursed was stoned to death. And in the third commandment, it is terrible enough that God says, "the Lord will not hold guiltless anyone who takes his name in vain."

10. Do you not usually say the Lord's prayer, and in it, "may your name be honored," Matthew 6:9; Luke 11:2: and will you profane that name which you pray may be hallowed? Is it hallowing it, to swear by it, and use it irreverently and vainly in your common talk? Or will God endure such hypocrisy as this, or regard such hypocritical prayers?

11. Your customary swearing is an uncharitable accusation of the hearers, as if they were so incredulous that they would not believe a man without an oath, and so profane that they delight in the profanation of the name of God; which is the grief of every honest hearer.

12. You accuse yourself as a person suspected of lying, and not to be believed; for among honest men a word is credible without an oath. Therefore if you were only taken for an honest man, your bare word would be believed. And by swearing, you tell all that hear you that you suppose yourself to be taken for a person whose word is not to be believed. And what need have you to tell this so openly to others if it is so?

13. And by swearing you declare the suspicion to be true, and that indeed you are not to be believed: so far are you from making your sayings more credible by it. For he that has so little conscience and fear of God as to swear profanely can hardly be thought a person that makes any conscience of a lie. For it is the same God that is offended by the one as by the other. A swearer warrants you to suspect him for a liar.

14. Both swearing and taking God's name in vain are the greater sins because you have no stronger a temptation to them. Commonly they bring no honor, but shame: they bring no physical pleasure to the senses, as gluttony, and drunkenness, and uncleanness do; and usually they are committed without any profit to entice men to them. You do not get the worth of a penny by your sin; so that it is hard to find what draws you to it, or why you do it, unless it is to show God that you do not fear him, and unless you intend to bid defiance to him, and do that which you think will offend him, in mere spite. So that one would think a very little grace might serve to cure such a fruitless sin: and therefore it is a sign of gracelessness.

15. How terribly do you draw God's vengeance upon yourself! Cursing yourself is a begging for vengeance: profane swearing is a profane, contemptuous appeal to the judgment of God. And dare you, even in your sins, appeal to the judgment of God? Do you fear it no more? To this judgment then you shall go! But you will quickly have enough of it, and find what it was for stubble to appeal to the "devouring fire," Hebrews 12:29.

**Direction 3. Remember God's presence, and keep his fear upon your heart, and remember his judgment to which you are hastening, and keep a tender conscience, and a watch upon your tongue, and then you will easily escape such a sin as this.**

Dare you abuse God's name before his face?

**Direction 4. Write over your doors or bed, where you may often read it, the third commandment, or some of those terrible passages of holy Scripture:**

Matthew 5:34–37, "I say to you, do not take oaths at all—not by heaven ... not by earth ... Do not take an oath by your head ... Let your word be 'Yes, yes' or 'No, no.' More than this is from the evil one." James 5:12, "And above all, my brothers and sisters, do not swear, either by heaven or by earth or by any other oath. But let your 'Yes' be yes and your 'No' be no, so that you may not fall into judgment" (or hypocrisy, as Dr. Hammond thinks it should be read). Zechariah 5:3, "Everyone that swears shall be cut off." Jeremiah 23:10, "Because of swearing the land mourns." Hosea 4:2. Think well on such texts as these.

**Direction 5. Love God, and honor him as God, and you cannot thus despise and abuse his name.**

You will reverence and honor the name of that person that you love, and reverence, and honor. It is atheism and lack of love to God that makes you so profane his name.

**Direction 6. Punish yourself after every such crime with such a voluntary fine or penalty as may help to quicken your observation and remembrance.**

If none execute the law upon you (which is twelve pence an oath), lay more on yourself, and give it to the poor. Though you are not bound to do justice on yourselves, you may medicinally help to cure yourselves by that which has a rational aptitude for it.

## Baxter's notes

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Scripture references are left where Baxter puts them (usually before the quotation), converted to modern form.
- **Title:** "Tit." in Baxter's Chapter 9 is his abbreviation for "Title," that is, a heading or division of the chapter. This file belongs to Title 2 (against profane swearing); no Title heading falls in this range.
- **Where the range begins:** it opens at Baxter's numbered point "III." of Title 2's opening matter. The next paragraph is numbered "IV." and also headed "Direct. I." in the source; we keep both numbers ("IV. Direction 1").
- **vanity** (point III): kept, since Baxter's point is that "vanity" (emptiness, falsehood) and "a lie" were used in the same sense; this is what links "in vain" with perjury.
- **byword:** a set phrase used habitually. **anon:** rendered "later" (Baxter promises to return to profane jeering at religion in plays). **signification:** rendered "meaning."
- **Direction 2, "Get your heart aware of":** Baxter's "Get thy heart sensible of." **aggravations:** kept in Baxter's sense, circumstances that make a sin worse.
- **terrible Majesty; terrible threatenings; terrible passages:** "terrible" is kept in its older sense of "inspiring terror or awe." **fearsome name:** Baxter's "dreadful name" (awe-inspiring, not "very bad").
- **"a God most holy, and in holiness to be mentioned"**: kept as written; it may echo Psalm 111:9, but Baxter gives no reference and we have not pressed it.
- **give leave:** permission. **pinch you:** Baxter's "gripe thee" (afflict, distress). **convict you of hypocrisy:** Baxter's "condemn thee of hypocrisy." **repay:** Baxter's "requitest." **pronounced:** Baxter's "denounced" (in its older sense, proclaimed as a threat). **used to swear by:** Baxter's "was wont to swear by." **Do you not usually say:** Baxter's "Dost thou not use to say." **spite:** Baxter's "despite." **lack of love:** Baxter's "want of love." **a rational aptitude for it:** Baxter's "a rational aptitude thereto" (a reasonable fitness for that end).
- **makes any conscience of a lie** (point 13): an older idiom meaning "has any scruple about lying." **warrants you to suspect him for a liar:** kept; he gives you grounds to suspect him of lying.
- **uncleanness** (point 14): Baxter's usual word for sexual impurity. **physical pleasure:** Baxter's "sensual pleasure" (see GLOSSARY).
- **Bishop Hall's character of the hypocrite** (point 3): probably Joseph Hall's *Characters of Virtues and Vices* (1608), which includes a character of the hypocrite; we have not checked the wording Baxter summarizes.
- **Belshazzar** (point 6): Daniel 5 (not referenced by Baxter).
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording, references not in his text): "Lord, Lord!" (compare Matthew 7:21–22); the earth opening to swallow the offender (compare Numbers 16:32); stubble before fire (compare Isaiah 47:14; Malachi 4:1); "a worm" (compare Psalm 22:6; Job 25:6).
- **"Chapter 1"** (Direction 1): Baxter's "chap. i.", the directions for conversion at the start of Part 1 of the *Directory*.
- **Leviticus 19:12:** NET wording. The King James has "neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God" as a second command; the NET makes profaning God's name the result of false swearing ("so that you do not profane"). Baxter's argument is not affected.
- **Leviticus 18:21:** cited only. In context it concerns giving children to Molech; Baxter uses only its phrase about profaning God's name.
- **Leviticus 22:2, 31, 32:** Baxter cites three verses but quotes only 31–32 (NET wording). Verse 2 concerns the priests handling holy offerings "so that they do not profane my holy name." Baxter's opening "Therefore" is not in the NET. "Hallowed"/"hallow" in the King James is "sanctified"/"sanctifies" in the NET.
- **Deuteronomy 28:58–59:** NET wording. Baxter prints "THE LORD THY GOD" in capitals for emphasis; we keep the capitals on the NET's "the Lord your God." The King James "fearful name" and "make thy plagues wonderful" are "awesome name" and "increase your punishments" in the NET.
- **"walking in his name," "calling upon his name"** (Micah 4:5; Psalm 99:6): Baxter's phrases from the King James, kept because the NET drops the word "name" that his point rests on: the NET has "we will follow the Lord our God" (Micah 4:5) and "Samuel was one of those who prayed to him" (Psalm 99:6).
- **"The place where he puts or records his name"** (Exodus 20:24; Deuteronomy 12:5, 11, 21): Baxter's own blend of the King James phrases ("record my name," Exodus 20:24; "put his name," Deuteronomy 12), kept in his wording. The NET reads "every place where I cause my name to be honored" (Exodus 20:24) and "the place he chooses ... to establish his name" / "for his name to reside" / "to locate his name" (Deuteronomy 12:5, 11, 21).
- **Isaiah 29:23:** NET wording. Where the King James (and Baxter) has "sanctify my name ... sanctify the Holy One of Jacob ... fear the God of Israel," the NET has "honor ... honor ... respect." Since Baxter's theme is the hallowing of God's name, the King James "sanctify" bears more directly on his point.
- **Isaiah 48:11:** NET wording ("defiled" for the King James "polluted"; "I will not share my glory with anyone else" for "I will not give my glory unto another").
- **Leviticus 10:3:** NET wording. The King James "I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me" is "Among the ones close to me I will show myself holy" in the NET.
- **Leviticus 24:10, 14:** Baxter's paraphrase of the story; the NET says the man "misused the Name and cursed."
- **Exodus 20:7** (the third commandment): NET wording of the second half of the verse; the reference is not in Baxter's text.
- **Matthew 6:9; Luke 11:2:** the NET renders "Hallowed be thy name" as "may your name be honored." Baxter's following words "hallowed" and "hallowing" are kept in his own prose, since his argument turns on that word.
- **Hebrews 12:29:** NET "devouring fire" for the King James "consuming fire"; the sense is the same.
- **Matthew 5:34–37:** NET wording, keeping the extent Baxter quotes (his dashes mark skipped words, shown here as "..."). The NET opens "But I say to you"; Baxter's quotation begins at "I say." Where the King James has "cometh of evil," the NET has "is from the evil one."
- **James 5:12:** NET wording. The King James "lest ye fall into condemnation" is "so that you may not fall into judgment" in the NET. Baxter's parenthesis refers to a variant reading: some Greek manuscripts are usually said to read "into hypocrisy" here, which Henry Hammond preferred, according to Baxter; we have not checked Hammond's text.
- **Zechariah 5:3:** Baxter's wording kept (King James: "every one that sweareth shall be cut off"). The NET reads a different point: "whoever swears falsely will suffer the same fate." Baxter's use of the verse against all swearing depends on the King James wording.
- **Jeremiah 23:10:** Baxter's wording kept (King James: "because of swearing the land mourneth"). The NET reads "the land is dried up because it is under his curse," so the NET does not support Baxter's point about swearing.
- **Hosea 4:2:** cited only; the NET has "There is only cursing, lying, murder, stealing, and adultery."
- **fine** (Direction 6): Baxter's "mulct." **twelve pence an oath:** the penalty for profane swearing under English law in Baxter's day; we believe this was set by an act of 1624 under James I, but have not confirmed it.
- **Dr. Hammond's *Practical Catechism*** (note 493): Henry Hammond's catechism, which Baxter cites often.
- **Fitzherbert** (note 494): probably the Catholic writer Thomas Fitzherbert; we have not identified the work. The source prints "1. 1." where "l. 1." (book 1) is probably meant.
