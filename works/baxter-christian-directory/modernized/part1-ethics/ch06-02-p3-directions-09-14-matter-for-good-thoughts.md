# Chapter 6: Title 2, Directions 9–14 (temptations, the world, providence, grace, the Spirit, and duty as matter for thought)

*In the margin: Satan's temptations.*

**Direction 9. Do not be a stranger to the methods, and subtleties, and diligence of Satan, in his temptations to undo your soul, and you will find matter enough to keep your thoughts from idleness.**

He is thinking how to deceive you and destroy you; and does it not concern you to think how to defeat him and escape and save yourself? If the hare does not run as fast as the dog, he is likely to die for it. Oh that your eyes were but opened to see the snares that are laid for you in your nature, in your temperament and passions, in your interests, your relationships, your friends and acquaintances, and ordinary company; in your businesses, and possessions, your house, and goods, and lands, and cattle, and tenants, and servants, and all that you trade with, or have to do with; in your clothing and recreations; in your food and drink, and sleep, and ease, in prosperity and adversity; in men's good thoughts, or bad thoughts of you; in their praise and blame; in their benefits and their wrongs; their favor and their falling out; in their pleasing or displeasing you; in your thinking and in your speaking, and in everything that you have to do with! Did you but see all these temptations, and also see to what they tend, and where they would bring you, you would find matter to cure the idleness or irrelevances of your thoughts.

*In the margin: The whole world.*

**Direction 10. The world and every creature in it, which you daily see, and which reveals to you the great Creator, might be enough to keep your thoughts from idleness.**

If sun, and moon, and stars; if heaven and earth, and all therein, are not enough to employ your thoughts, let your idleness have some excuse. I know you will say that it is upon some of these things that you do employ them: yes, but do you not first destroy, and mortify, and make nonsense of that on which you meditate? Do you not first separate it from God, who is the life, and glory, and end, and meaning of every creature? You kill it, and turn out the soul, and think only on the corpse; or on the creature made another thing as food for your physical desires! As the kite thinks on the birds and chickens, to devour them to satisfy her greedy appetite; thus you can think of all God's works, so far as they serve your flesh. But the world is God's book, which he set man at first to read; and every creature is a letter, or syllable, or word, or sentence, more or less, declaring the name and will of God. There you may behold his wonderful almightiness, his unsearchable wisdom, his unmeasurable goodness, mercy, and compassions; and his singular regard for the sons of men! Though the ungodly, proud, and carnal wits do but play with, and study the shape, and beauty, and order of the letters, syllables, and words, without understanding the sense and end; yet those that with holy and enlightened minds come there to behold the footsteps of the great, and wise, and bountiful Creator may find not only matter to employ, but to profit and delight their thoughts; they may be carried up by the things that are seen into the sacred wonder, reverence, love, and praise of the glorious Maker of all, who is unseen: and thus to the sanctified all things will be sanctified; and the study of common things will be to them divine and holy.

*In the margin: Providence about the world.*

**Direction 11. Do not be a stranger to, or a neglectful disregarder of, the wonders of providence in God's administrations in the world, and you will find plenty of matter for your thoughts.**

The dreadfulness of judgments, the delightfulness of mercies, the mysteriousness of all, will be matter of daily search and wonder to you. Think of the strange preservations of the church; of a people hated by all the world! how such a flock of lambs is kept in safety among so many ravenous wolves. Think of God's sharp afflictions of his offending people; of his severe consuming judgments exercised sometimes upon the wicked, when he means to set up here and there a monument of his justice, for the warning of presumptuous sinners. Go see how the wicked are deceived by deluding pleasures, and how the prosperity of fools destroys them (Proverbs 1:32); how they flourish today as a green bay tree (Psalm 37:35), or as the flower of the field; and then go into the sanctuary and see their end, how tomorrow they are cut down and withered, and the place of their abode knows them no more. Go see how God delights to abase the proud, and to "scatter those whose pride wells up from the sheer arrogance of their hearts; to bring down the mighty from their thrones, and to lift up those of lowly position; to fill the hungry with good things, and to send the rich away empty" (Luke 1:51–53). "How great are his signs! How mighty are his wonders! His kingdom will last forever" (Daniel 4:3). "He is ruler over human kingdoms and gives them to whomever he wishes" (verses 25, 32). "For wisdom and power belong to him. He changes times and seasons, deposing some kings and establishing others. He gives wisdom to the wise; he imparts knowledge to those with understanding; he reveals deep and hidden things. He knows what is in the darkness, and light resides with him" (Daniel 2:20–22). "The Lord revealed himself; he accomplished justice. The wicked were ensnared by their own actions" (Psalm 9:16). Notice how the upright are afflicted daily, and how the feet of violence trample on them; and yet how they rejoice, and hold fast to that God who afflicts them, and pity and pray for their miserable persecutors and oppressors; and how "all things work together for their good" (Romans 8:28). "Wonderful are all the works of God, sought out by those that have pleasure in them" (Psalm 111:2). The histories of former ages, and the observation of the present, may show you a world of matter for your thought. [306]

*In the margin: God's image.*

**Direction 12. Understand all the features and beauty of God's image upon a holy soul, the excellence and use of every grace, and the harmony of all; and you will have plenty of profitable matter for your thoughts.**

Know the nature of every grace, and the place and order of it, and the office, use, and exercise of it; and the means and motives, the opposites, dangers, and preservatives of it: know it as God's image, and see and love your Maker, and Redeemer, and Regenerator in it: know how God loves it, and how useful it is to our serving and honoring him in the world; and how deformed and vile a thing the soul is that is without it: know well what faith is; what wisdom and prudence are; what repentance, and humility, and mortification are; what hope, and fear, and desire, and obedience, and meekness, and temperance, and sobriety, and chastity, and contentment, and justice, and self-denial are; especially know the nature and force of love to God, and to his servants, and to neighbors, and to enemies: know what a holy resignation and devotedness to God are; and what are watchfulness, diligence, zeal, fortitude, and perseverance, patience, submission, and peace: know what the worth, and use, the helps, and hindrances of all these are, and then your thoughts will not be idle.

*In the margin: The daily motions of the Spirit.*

**Direction 13. If you are not a stranger to the Spirit of grace, or a neglecter of his daily promptings, and persuasions, and operations on your heart, the heeding and right use of them will keep your thoughts from rusty idleness and a wandering course.**

It is not a small matter to be daily entertaining so noble a guest, and daily observing the offers and promptings of so great a Benefactor; and daily receiving the gifts of so bountiful a Lord, and daily accepting his necessary helps; and daily obeying the saving precepts of so great and beneficent a God. If you know how insufficient you are without him, to will or to do, to perform, or to think, or purpose any good, and that all your sufficiency is of him. [307] If you knew that it is the great skill and diligence required in all that will sail successfully to the desired land of rest, to know the winds of the Spirit's helps, and to set all your sails to the right use of them, and to stir yourselves while such breezes continue, you would find greater work than wandering for your thoughts.

*In the margin: All our duty to God and man.*

**Direction 14. Do not be ignorant or neglectful of that frame and course of holy duty to God and man in which all your lives should be employed; and you cannot lack matter to employ your thoughts upon.**

Your pulse, and breath, and natural motions will hold on whether you think of them or not; but so will not moral, holy motion, for that must be rational and voluntary. You have all the powers of soul and body to exercise either upon God or for God. You must know him, fear him, love him, obey him, trust him, worship him, pray to him, praise him, give thanks to him, bewail your sins, and hear his word, and reverently use his name and day. And is not the understanding and learning how to do all this, and the timely, serious practice of it all, sufficient to keep the thoughts from idleness? Oh what a great deal of work does a serious Christian find for his thoughts about some one of these! about praying aright, or hearing, or receiving the sacrament of Christ's body and blood aright! But besides all these, what a great deal of duty have you to perform, to magistrates, pastors, parents, masters, and other superiors; to subjects, people, children, servants, and other inferiors; to every neighbor, for his soul, his body, his estate, and name; and to do to all as you would be done by. And besides all this, how much have you to do directly for yourselves; for your souls, and bodies, and families, and estates! against your ignorance, unbelief, pride, selfishness, indulgence in physical pleasure, worldliness, passion, sloth, intemperance, cowardice, lust, uncharitableness, etc. Is not here matter for your thoughts?

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Every quoted verse was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Where this file falls:** this range begins at a new Direction (Direction 9 of Title 2), so no continuation line is needed. "Title" is Baxter's word ("Tit.") for one of the six divisions of Chapter 6; Title 2 gives directions for furnishing the mind with good thoughts. In the source the opening sentence of each Direction runs on into its paragraph; we set that sentence in bold as the Direction heading, as the finished files do, without changing any sentence.
- **Proverbs 1:32** (Direction 11): not in quotation marks in Baxter, so his wording is kept ("the prosperity of fools destroys them," the King James phrase). The NET reads "the careless ease of fools will destroy them." Baxter's point, that good fortune itself ruins the wicked, rests on the King James word "prosperity"; the NET's "careless ease" makes the cause complacency rather than success, though the two are close.
- **Psalm 37:35** (Direction 11): not quoted; Baxter's "green bay tree" (King James) is kept. The NET has "like a green tree grows in its native soil," with no bay tree.
- **Luke 1:51–53:** NET wording, with the NET's verbs ("has scattered," "has brought down," "has lifted up," "has filled," "has sent") put in the infinitive ("scatter," "to bring down," etc.; Baxter's quotation marks open at "scatter") to fit Baxter's sentence, as Baxter himself did with the King James. The NET's "those whose pride wells up from the sheer arrogance of their hearts" replaces the King James "the proud in the imagination of their hearts"; the sense Baxter uses (God abasing the proud) is unchanged.
- **Daniel 4:3:** NET wording. Baxter's "His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom" becomes the NET's "His kingdom will last forever."
- **Daniel 4, "ver. 26, 32" in the source:** the quoted words ("He rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomever he will") are not in 4:26 in either the King James or the NET; they are in 4:25 and 4:32 (and 4:17). We have corrected "26" to "25." The NET's wording ("the Most High is ruler over human kingdoms and gives them to whomever he wishes") is used, with Baxter's "He" kept in place of "the Most High," since Baxter quotes the clause with "He."
- **Daniel 2:20–22:** NET wording from "for wisdom and power belong to him." The NET's "deposing some kings and establishing others" replaces the King James "he removeth kings, and setteth up kings"; same sense.
- **Psalm 9:16:** NET wording. The King James (which Baxter quotes) reads "The LORD is known by the judgment which he executeth: the wicked is snared in the work of his own hands." The NET's "The Lord revealed himself; he accomplished justice" keeps the idea that God is made known through his judgments, which is Baxter's point; "the work of his own hands" becomes "their own actions."
- **Romans 8:28:** NET wording, except that Baxter's "their" (referring to the upright, and his own adaptation) is kept; the verse itself reads "all things work together for good for those who love God."
- **Psalm 111:2:** treated as Baxter's paraphrase and kept in his wording, modernized. The King James reads "The works of the LORD are great, sought out of all them that have pleasure therein"; Baxter has "Wonderful are all the works of God." **NET flag:** the NET reads "The Lord's deeds are great, eagerly awaited by all who desire them." Baxter leans on the King James "sought out," meaning studied and searched into, which fits his whole argument that God's works are matter for thought. The NET's "eagerly awaited" loses that sense of study.
- **Note 306:** Baxter's list of psalms is converted to modern form. Psalm 105:22 in the NET concerns Joseph ("giving him authority to imprison his officials and to teach his advisers"); the King James has "to teach his senators wisdom." We have not tried to settle why Baxter cites that verse in particular.
- **Note 307:** Philippians 2:13 ("to will and to do" in the King James; the NET has "both the desire and the effort"), 2 Corinthians 3:5 ("our sufficiency is of God" in the King James; the NET has "our adequacy is from God"), 2 Corinthians 12:9. These lie behind Baxter's sentence "to will or to do... all your sufficiency is of him."
- **Unmarked allusions** (Baxter's wording kept; references are ours, not in his text): "the flower of the field" (Psalm 103:15; Isaiah 40:6); "go into the sanctuary and see their end" (Psalm 73:17); "cut down and withered" (Psalm 37:2); "the place of their abode knows them no more" (Psalm 103:16; Job 7:10); "the things that are seen... who is unseen" (2 Corinthians 4:18; Romans 1:20); "to the sanctified all things will be sanctified" (compare Titus 1:15; 1 Timothy 4:5); "a flock of lambs... among so many ravenous wolves" (compare Luke 10:3); "do to all as you would be done by" (Matthew 7:12).
- **Grammar kept as printed:** in Direction 13, "If you know how insufficient you are... is of him." stands as a sentence with no main clause in the source; we keep it so.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): temperature (in "temperature and passions") to temperament; relations to relationships; apparel to clothing; meat to food; dispraise to blame; whither to where; impertinences to irrelevances; sensual to physical; accommodate (your flesh) to serve; comeliness to beauty; illuminated to enlightened; thither to there; rapt up to carried up; admirations / admiration to wonder; store of to plenty of; befooling to deluding; Mark to Notice; adhere to to hold fast to; lineaments to features; contentation to contentment; motions (of the Spirit) to promptings; attendance and improvement to heeding and right use; vagrant to wandering; requisite to required; successively to successfully (Baxter's "successively" here almost certainly means "with success," an older sense or a misprint); improvement to use; bestir you to stir yourselves; gales (gentle winds) to breezes; neglective to neglectful; want (lack) to lack; seasonable to timely; a deal of to a great deal of; infidelity (unbelief) to unbelief; sensuality to indulgence in physical pleasure; &c. to etc.
- **Kept:** "mortify" (here, to kill or deaden); "carnal" (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh); "motions" and "motion" in Direction 14 (movements of body and of will), where the physical sense still reads plainly; "kite" (a bird of prey); "wits" (clever people); "estate" (property, worldly circumstances) and "name" (reputation); "passion" (in his day, any strong disturbing emotion, often anger).
- **"the sons of men"** (Direction 10): kept; Baxter means humankind.
