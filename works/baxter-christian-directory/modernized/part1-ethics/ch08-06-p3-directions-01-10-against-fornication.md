# Chapter 8: Part 5, Title 1: the directions against fornication (Directions 1–10)

**Direction 1. If you would avoid uncleanness, avoid the things that dispose you to it; as gluttony, or fullness of diet, and pampering the flesh, idleness, and other things mentioned under the next title, of subduing lust.**

The abating of the filthy desires is the surest way to prevent the filthy act; which may be done if you are but willing.

**Direction 2. Avoid the present temptations.**

Do not go where the snare lies without necessity. Abhor the devil's bellows that blow up the fire of lust; such as enticing clothing, filthy talk and sights, of which more also under the next title.

**Direction 3. Carefully avoid all opportunity of sinning.**

"Do not go near the door of her house," says Solomon (Proverbs 5:8). Avoid the company of the person you are in danger of. Do not come where she is; this you can do if you are willing; none will force you. If you will go seek for a thief, no wonder if you are robbed. If you will go seek fire to put in the thatch, no wonder if your house is burnt. The devil will sufficiently play the tempter; you need not help him; that is his part, leave it to himself; it is your part to watch against him; and he will find you work; if you watch as closely and constantly as you can, it is well if you escape. As you love your soul, avoid all opportunities of sinning; make it impossible to yourself: much of your safety lies in this point. Never be in secret company with her you are in danger of; but either not at all, or only in the sight of others: especially do not contrive such opportunities as to be together in the night, in the dark, or on the Lord's day when others are at church (one of the devil's seasons for such works), or any such opportunity, leisure, and secrecy; for opportunity itself is a strong temptation. As it is the way to make a thief, to set money in his way, or so to trust him as that he can easily deceive or rob you and never be discovered; so it is the way to make yourself unclean, to get such an opportunity of sinning that you may easily do it, without any probability of impediment or discovery from men. The chief point in all the art or watch is to keep far enough off. If you touch the pitch you will be defiled. "No one who touches her will escape punishment" (Proverbs 6:29). "Can a man hold fire against his chest without burning his clothes? Can a man walk on hot coals without scorching his feet? So it is with the one who sleeps with his neighbor's wife" (verses 27–28). Do not bring the fire and the gunpowder too near. If you cannot keep at a distance, nor keep from the presence of the bait, you are not likely to keep from the sin.

**Direction 4. Reverence your own conscience.**

Note what it says now, for it will shortly say it in a more terrible manner: hear it voluntarily; for it is terrible to hear it when you cannot resist: come to terms with conscience in the way while it is reconcilable; for you do not know how terrible a tormentor it is. I do not doubt that it has given you some grips for your very lust, before it ever came to practice; but the sorest of its grips now are but like the playing of the cat with the mouse, before the killing grip is given. Does no man see you? Conscience sees you; and you are a wretch indeed if you do not reverence conscience more than man: as Chrysostom says, Suppose no man knows the crime but himself and the woman with whom he committed it! How will he bear the rebukes of conscience, when he carries about with him so sharp and bitter an accuser? For no man can outrun himself; and no man can avoid the sentence of this court within him: it is a tribunal not to be corrupted with money, nor perverted by flattery; for it is divine, being placed in the soul by God himself: the less the adulterer now feels it, the more he hastens to the perdition of his soul. Do you not feel a sentence passed within you? a terrible sentence, telling you of the wrath of a revenging God? Bless God that it is not yet an irreversible sentence; but sue out your pardon quickly lest it come to that. Do you not feel that you are afraid and ashamed to pray or to address yourself to God? much more afraid to think of dying, and appearing before him? If your sin makes you ready to flee from him now, if you knew how, can you look him in the face at last; or can you hope to stand with comfort at his bar? Are you fit to live in heaven with him, you that make yourself unfit to pray to him? Even lawful procreation (as I said before) blushes to come too near to holy exercises: [456] as Chrysostom says, *Die quo liberis operam dedisti legitime, quamvis crimen illud non sit, orare tamen non audes--Quod si ab incontaminato lecto resurgens times ad orandum accedere; quum in diaboli lecto sis, cur horribile Dei nomen audes invocare?* Conscience is a better friend to you than you imagine when it would reclaim you from your sin; and will be a sharper enemy than you can now imagine, if you do not obey it.

**Direction 5. Suppose you saw written upon the door of the house or room where you enter to sin, "God will judge sexually immoral people and adulterers" (Hebrews 13:4).**

And write that, or such sentences, upon your room door, or at least upon your heart. Keep your eye upon the terrible threats of the dreadful God. Do you dare sin, when vengeance is at your back? Will not the thought of hell-fire quench the fire of lust, or restrain you from your presumptuous sin? Do you not say with Joseph, "How could I do such a great evil and sin against God?" (Genesis 39:9). As it is written of a chaste woman, that being tempted by a fornicator, wished him first at her request to hold his finger in the fire: and when he refused, answered him, Why then should I burn in hell to satisfy you? So ask yourself, Can I more easily overcome the flames of hell than the flames of lust?

**Direction 6. Remember, man, that God stands by.**

If he were not there, you could not be there; for in him you live, and move, and are. He that made the eye must see, and he that made the light and darkness sees as well in the dark as in the light; if you imagine that he is absent or ignorant, you do not believe that he is God; for an absent and ignorant God is no God. And do you dare, I say do you dare, commit such a villainy and God behold you? What! that which you would be ashamed a child should see! which you would not do if a mortal man stood by! Do you think that your locks, or secrecy, or darkness have darkened or shut out God? Do you not know that he sees not only within your curtains, but within your heart? Oh what a hardened heart you have, that in the sight of God, your Maker and your Judge, dare do such wickedness! Ask your conscience, man, Would I do this if I were to die tomorrow, and go to God? would I do this if I saw God, indeed, or even an angel, in the room? If not, should you do it, when God is as surely there as if you saw him? O remember, man, that he is a holy God, and hates uncleanness, and that he is a "devouring fire" (Hebrews 12:29).

**Direction 7. Suppose all the while that you saw the devil opening the door for you, and bringing you your mate, and driving on the match, and persuading you to the sin.**

What if he appeared to you openly to play his part, as surely as he now plays it unseen; would not your lust be cooled? and would not the devil cure the disease which he has excited in you? Why then do you obey him now, when he is as certainly the instigator as if you saw him? Why, man, have you so little reason that seeing and not seeing will make so great a difference with you? What if you were blind, would you play the fornicator before all the company, because you do not see them, when you know they are there? If you know anything, you know God is there; and you may feel by the temptation that Satan is in it. Will you not be ruled by the laws, unless you see the king? Will you not fear the infection of the plague unless you see it? Use your reason for your soul as well as for your body, and do in the case as you would do if you saw the devil tempting you, and Christ forbidding you.

**Direction 8. If you are unmarried, marry, if easier remedies will not serve.**

"It is better to marry than to burn with sexual desire" (1 Corinthians 7:9). It is God's ordinance partly for this end. "Marriage must be honored among all and the marriage bed kept undefiled" (Hebrews 13:4). It is a resemblance of Christ's union with his church, and is sanctified to believers (Ephesians 5; 1 Corinthians 7). Perhaps it may cast you upon great troubles in the world, if you are unready for that state (as it is with apprentices). Give up your sin then at easier rates, or else the lawful means must be used though it undoes you. It is better your body be undone than your soul, if you must have it be one of them. But if you are married already you are a monster, and not a man, if the remedy does not prevail with you: but yet the other directions may also be useful to you.

**Direction 9. If lesser means do not prevail, open your case to some able, faithful friend, and engage them to watch over you; and tell them when you are most endangered by the temptation.**

This will shame you from the sin, and lay more obligations on you to refrain from it. If you tell your friend, Now I am tempted to the sin, and now I am going to it; he will quickly stop you: break your secrecy and you lose your opportunity. You can do this if you are willing; if ever your conscience prevails so far with you as to resolve against your sin, or to be willing to escape, then take the time while conscience is awake, and go tell your friend: and tell him who it is that is your wicked companion, and let him know all your haunts, that he may know the better how to help you. Do you say that this will shame you? It will do so to him that it is known to: but that is the benefit of it, and that is the reason I advise you to it, that shame may help to save your soul. If you go on, the sin will both shame and damn you: and a greater shame than this is a gentle remedy in so foul and dangerous a disease.

**Direction 10. Therefore, if still all this will not serve the purpose, tell it to many, indeed, rather tell it to all the town than not be cured: and then the public shame will do much more.**

Confess it to your pastor, and ask him openly to beg the prayers of the congregation for your pardon and recovery. Begin thus to crave the fruit of church discipline yourself; so far should you be from fleeing from it, and kicking against it as the desperate, hardened sinners do. If you say this is a hard lesson, remember that the suffering of hell is harder. Do not say that I wrong you by putting you upon scandal and open shame: it is you that put yourself upon it, by making it necessary, and refusing all easier remedies. I put you on it, but on supposition that you will not be more easily cured: almost as Christ puts you upon "cutting off a right hand," or "plucking out a right eye, lest all the body be cast into hell." This is not the way that he commands you first to take: he would have you avoid the need of it: but he tells you that it is better to do so than worse; and that this is an easy suffering in comparison with hell. And so I advise you, if you love your reputation, give up your sin in a cheaper way; but if you will not do so, take this way rather than damn your soul. If the shame of all the town is upon you, and the boys should hoot after you in the streets, if it would drive you from your sin, how easy would your suffering be in comparison with what it is likely to be! Concealment is Satan's great advantage. It would be hard for you to sin thus if it were only disclosed.

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized).

This file begins at Direction 1 of the section Baxter heads "II. The Directions against Fornication" (source line 41421, just before this range, so the heading itself is not repeated here). The range ends with Direction 10; the next line in the source begins Title 2, "Directions against inward, filthy Lusts" (the "next title" Baxter refers to in Directions 1 and 2).

We have not traced the passage in Chrysostom. The English sentences before it ("Suppose no man knows the crime ...") are Baxter's own rendering of Chrysostom; they are kept as he wrote them, without quotation marks, as in the source.
- **"(as I said before)"** (Direction 4): Baxter's cross-reference to an earlier passage on the same point; we have not checked which.
- **Note 456:** "Plutarch's Roman. Quest. 65" is rendered as *Roman Questions* 65; we have not checked the numbering against a modern edition. "Happily" in the source is the old "haply," meaning "perhaps"; changed to "Perhaps." "Company of man and woman" means sexual relations.
- **Proverbs 5:8:** NET wording, the second half of the verse only (NET: "Keep yourself far from her, and do not go near the door of her house"). Baxter's "Come not near the door" is the King James.
- **Proverbs 6:29 (flag):** the King James "Whosoever toucheth her shall not be innocent" becomes the NET "no one who touches her will escape punishment." The King James speaks of guilt; the NET speaks of punishment. Baxter's point (contact itself is dangerous) still stands, but "not innocent" was a stronger fit for his "you will be defiled."
- **Proverbs 6:27–28:** NET wording. Baxter cites verses 27–28, but his last clause, "So he that goeth in to his neighbour's wife," is from verse 29; the NET for that clause is "So it is with the one who sleeps with his neighbor's wife." The King James "take fire in his bosom" is "hold fire against his chest" in the NET. Baxter's citation "ver. 27, 28" is kept.
- **Hebrews 13:4 (Direction 5, flag):** the King James "Whoremongers and adulterers God will judge" becomes the NET "God will judge sexually immoral people and adulterers." The NET's "sexually immoral people" is broader than the King James "whoremongers"; Baxter's use (a sentence to write over the door of the place of sin) is unaffected. The NET also puts God first, where the King James put the offenders first.
- **Hebrews 13:4 (Direction 8, flag):** the King James "Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled" is a statement; the NET "Marriage must be honored among all and the marriage bed kept undefiled" is a command. Baxter uses the verse to show that marriage is honorable and lawful as a remedy; the NET reading still supports that, but as a duty rather than a description.
- **Genesis 39:9:** NET wording ("So how could I do such a great evil and sin against God?"; we dropped the opening "So" to keep Baxter's extent). The source cites "Gen. xxxix. 7," but the words are in verse 9 (verse 7 is the master's wife's invitation); corrected to 39:9.
- **Hebrews 12:29 (flag):** the King James "consuming fire" is "devouring fire" in the NET. The sense is the same; Baxter quotes only the phrase.
- **1 Corinthians 7:9 (flag):** the King James "It is better to marry than to burn" leaves "burn" unexplained; the NET reads "to burn with sexual desire." That is the sense Baxter takes, so the NET makes his reading explicit rather than changing it.
- **Matthew 5:29–30 (Direction 10):** Baxter puts "cutting off a right hand" and "plucking out a right eye, lest all the body be cast into hell" in quotation marks but gives no reference; this is his paraphrase of the King James, so his wording is kept (modernized). The reference is added here only, not in Baxter's text; compare also Matthew 18:8–9. NET (5:29–30): "If your right eye causes you to sin, tear it out and throw it away! It is better to lose one of your members than to have your whole body thrown into hell. If your right hand causes you to sin, cut it off and throw it away! It is better to lose one of your members than to have your whole body go into hell."
- **Ephesians 5; 1 Corinthians 7** (Direction 8): cited only.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "in him you live, and move, and are" (Acts 17:28; NET "in him we live and move about and exist"); "He that made the eye must see" (Psalm 94:9; NET "Does the one who forms the human eye not see?"); God seeing alike in dark and light (compare Psalm 139:11–12); "If you touch the pitch you will be defiled" (probably Ecclesiasticus/Sirach 13:1, in the Apocrypha); "come to terms with conscience in the way" (compare the King James of Matthew 5:25, "Agree with thine adversary quickly, whiles thou art in the way"); "kicking against it" (compare Acts 9:5 and 26:14 in the King James, "kick against the pricks"; we have not confirmed Baxter had this in mind).
- **The chaste woman** (Direction 5): the story is Baxter's; we have not traced its source.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): fulness → fullness; apparel → clothing; narrowly → closely; forbear the presence of the bait / forbear the sin → keep from the presence of the bait / keep from the sin; mark → note; treat with → come to terms with; gripes → grips (pinches or clutches, matching the cat-and-mouse image); overrun himself → outrun himself; chamber → room; threatenings → threats; easilier → more easily; villany → villainy; yea → indeed; but an angel → even an angel; forbear → give up / refrain from; if thou wilt needs have it → if you must have it; serviceable → useful; less means → lesser means; engagements → obligations; serve turn → serve the purpose; desire him → ask him; spurning against it → kicking against it; credit → reputation; in comparison of → in comparison with; were but opened → were only disclosed.
- **Words kept:** uncleanness (sexual sin); present temptations (temptations close at hand); the thatch (a thatched roof); the Lord's day (Sunday); reverence (as a verb: honor, stand in awe of); sue out your pardon (a legal phrase: apply for and obtain a pardon); his bar (the judge's bar, the place of judgment); holy exercises (acts of worship, such as prayer); driving on the match (pushing the pairing forward); at easier rates (at a cheaper price); apprentices (bound by indentures; Baxter's point is that marrying would put them in difficulty, probably because apprentices were not usually free to marry during their term, though we have not checked the terms of the period); church discipline (the church's formal correction of open sin); abating (lessening); if you are but willing (if you are only willing).
- **Plain words:** Baxter's direct language about fornication, adultery, and "filthy" desires is kept as he wrote it.
