# Chapter 4: Directions against Unbelief, Directions 11-30

*This file begins at Direction 11 of the section "Directions against Unbelief," which is continued from the file before it, and it ends at Direction 30; the section continues in the next file.*

**Direction 11.** Consider how impossible it is in itself that so many men should agree together to deceive the world, and that for nothing, and at the rate of their own undoing and death: and that they should all agree in the same narratives and doctrines so unanimously: and that none of these should ever confess the deceit, and disgrace the rest. All things well considered, this will appear not only a moral, but a natural impossibility; especially considering their quality and distance, there being thousands in several countries that never saw the faces of the rest, much less could enter a confederacy with them, to deceive the world.

**Direction 12.** Consider the certain way by which the doctrine and writings of the apostles, and other evangelical messengers, have been handed down to us, without any possibility of material alteration. Because the holy Scriptures were not left only to the care of private men, or of the Christians of one country, who might have agreed upon corruptions and alterations; but it was made the office of the ordinary ministers to read, and explain, and apply them. And every congregation had one or more of these ministers: and the people received the Scriptures as the law of God, and that by which they must live and be judged, and as their charter for heaven. So that it was not possible for one minister to corrupt the Scripture text, but the rest, with the people, would have quickly reproved him; nor for those of one kingdom to bring all other Christians to it throughout the world, without a great deal of consultation and opposition (if at all); which was never recorded to us.

**Direction 13.** Be acquainted as fully as you can with the history of the church, so that you may know how the gospel has been planted, and spread, and assaulted, and preserved until now: which will satisfy you much better than general, uncertain talk of others.

**Direction 14.** Judge whether God, being the wise and merciful Governor of the world, would allow the most honest and most obedient subjects that he has on earth, to be deceived in a matter of such importance, by the claim of doctrines and miracles proceeding from himself, and which none but himself (or God by his special grant) is able to do, without disowning them, or giving any sufficient means to the world to discover the deceit. [138] For certainly, he does not need deceit to govern us. If you say that he permits Mahometanism, I answer, 1. The main, positive doctrine of the Mahometans, for the worshipping of one only God, against idolatry, is true: and the side notions of their supposed prophet are not commended to the world on the claim of attesting miracles at all, but upon the affirmation of revelations, without any credible seal or divine attestation, and forced on the world by the power of the sword. 2. And God has given the world sufficient safeguards against them, in the nullity of the proof of them, and the evident foolishness of the writings and the things themselves. So that honesty and diligence will easily escape them.

**Direction 15.** Observe the supernatural effects of the gospel upon the souls of believers: how it plants on man the image of the holy God; powerfully subduing both the senses and the greatest interest of the flesh, to the will of God; and making men wise and good; and putting an admirable difference between them and all other men. And then judge whether it is not God's seal, having his image first upon itself, which he uses and honors to be the instrument of imprinting his image upon us. [139]

**Direction 16.** Mark well the certain emptiness of all other religions that are powerful on the earth. Idolatry and Mahometanism, which openly bear the mark of their own shame, have shared between them almost all the rest of the earth; for mere deism is scarcely anywhere in possession; and Judaism has no considerable inheritance; and both of them as perceptibly refuted by man's corruption, necessity, and desert.

**Direction 17.** Mark the great difference between the Christian part of the world, (those that receive Christianity seriously and in sincerity,) and all the rest. [140] Those that are furthest from Christianity, are furthest from piety, honesty, civility, or any praiseworthy qualities or conduct: most of them are beastly and ungodly; and the rest are only a little better: and ignorance and brutishness cannot be the perfection of a man. Nay, among professed Christians, the multitudes that have only the name, and hate the nature and practice of it, are like swine or wolves; and some of the worst, near kin to devils. Whereas all that receive Christianity practically into their hearts and lives, are heavenly and holy, and (in the same measure that they receive it) their sins are all mortified, and they are devoted to God, and possessed with justice, charity, and patience toward men, and are carried up above this world, and despise that which the rest make their happiness and delight. So that if that is good which does good, then the goodness of the Christian faith is apparent to all, that have any acquaintance, reason, and impartiality to judge.

**Direction 18.** Think about what you yourselves would have been, if you had not been Christians? Yes, what would yet be the result if you should fall from the Christian faith? Would you not look at the life to come as doubtful? and resolve to take your pleasure in the world, and to gratify the flesh, and to neglect your souls, and to venture upon almost any vice, that seems necessary to your carnal ends? Christianity has cleansed and sanctified you, if you are sanctified: and if (which God forbid!) you should forsake Christianity, it is most likely you would quickly show the difference, by your dirty, fleshly, worldly lives. [141]

**Direction 19.** When you see the evidence of divine revelation and authority, it is enough to silence your doubts and quibbles about particular words or circumstances. For you know that God is true and infallible; and you know that you are feeble, ignorant worms, that are utterly at a loss, when you have not one at hand to open every difficulty to you: and that all arts and sciences seem full of difficulties and contradictions to ignorant, inexperienced novices.

**Direction 20.** Allow all along in your learning, for the difficulties which must needs arise, from the translation, ambiguity of all human language, change and variety of words and customs, time, place, and other circumstances, and especially from your own unfamiliarity with all these: so that your own weaknesses, and ignorance, and mistakes in reasoning, may not be ascribed to the truth.

**Direction 21.** Understand the proper use of holy Scripture, and so how far it is divine; so that you are not tempted to unbelief, by expecting in it that which was never intended, and then finding your groundless expectations frustrated. It is not so divine as to the terms, and style, and order, and such modal and circumstantial matters, as if all the exactness might be expected in it, that God could put into a book: nor is it intended as a system of physics, or logic, or any subservient sciences or arts: but it is an infallible revelation of the will of God, for the government of the church, and the leading of men to life eternal: and it is ordered and worded so as to partake of such human weakness, as yet shall in no way impeach the truth or efficacy of it; but rather make it more suitable to the generality of men, whose weakness requires such a style and manner of handling. So that as a child of God has a body from parents, which yet is of God, but so of God, as to partake of the weaknesses of the parents; or rather, as Adam had a body from God, but yet from earth, and accordingly frail; but a soul more directly from God, which was more pure and divine: so Scripture has its style, and language, and method so from God, as to have nothing in it unsuitable to its ends; but not so from God, as if he himself had shown in it his own most perfect wisdom to the utmost, and as if there were nothing in it of human imperfection. But the truth and goodness which are the soul of Scripture, are more directly from God. The style and method of the writers may be various; but the same soul animates all the parts. It is no dishonor to the holy Scriptures, if Cicero is preferred for purity of style, and phrase, and oratory, as for other common uses; but certainly it is to be preferred as to its proper use: that being the best style for an act of parliament, which is next to the worst in an oration. The means are for the end.

**Direction 22.** Consider how great assistance apparitions, and witchcrafts, and other perceptible evidences of spirits conversing with mankind, give to faith. Of which I have written in the aforementioned treatises, and therefore now pass it over.

**Direction 23.** Consider what advantage faith may have, by observing the nature and tendency of the soul, and its hopes and fears of a life to come, together with the superior, glorious worlds, which certainly are possessed by nobler inhabitants. [142] He that sees every corner of the earth, and sea, and air inhabited, and thinks what earth is in comparison of all the great and glorious orbs above it, will hardly once dream that they are all void of inhabitants, or that there is not room enough for souls.

**Direction 24.** The ministry of angels, of which particular providences give us a great probability, gives some help to that doctrine which tells us, that we must live with angels, and that we shall ascend to more familiarity with them, who condescend to so great service now for us.

**Direction 25.** The universal, wonderful, implacable enmity of corrupted man to the holy doctrine, and ways, and servants of Christ, and the open war which in every kingdom, and the secret war which in every heart, is kept up between Christ and Satan through the world; with the tendency of every temptation, their violence, constancy, in all ages, to all persons, all making against Christ, and heaven, and holiness, notoriously declare that the Christian doctrine and life do tend to our salvation; which the devil so maliciously and incessantly opposes: and thus his temptations give great advantage to the tempted soul against the tempter. For it is not for nothing that the enemy of our souls makes so much opposition. And that there is such a devil, that thus opposes Christ and tempts us, not only perceptible apparitions and witchcrafts prove, but the too perceptible temptations, which, by their matter and manner, plainly tell us where they come from. Especially when all the world is formed as into two hostile armies, the one fighting under Christ, and the other under the devil; and so have continued since Cain and Abel to this day.

**Direction 26.** The prophecies of Christ himself of the destruction of Jerusalem, and the gathering of his church, and the cruel treatment of it through the world, give great assistance to our faith, when we see them all so exactly fulfilled.

**Direction 27.** Mark whether it is not a respect to things temporal that assaults your belief; and do not come with a biased, pleasure-loving mind to search into so great a mystery. Worldliness, and pride, and indulgence in physical pleasure are deadly enemies to faith; and where they are powerful they will show their enmity, and blind the mind: if the soul is sunk into mud and filth, it cannot see the things of God.

**Direction 28.** Come with humility and a sense of your ignorance, and not with arrogance and self-conceit; as if all must needs be wrong that your empty, foolish minds cannot at once perceive to be right. The most famous apostates that I ever knew, were all men of notorious pride and self-conceitedness.

**Direction 29.** Do not provoke God by willful sinning against the light, which you have already received, to forsake you, and give you over to unbelief. 2 Thessalonians 2:10-12, "because they found no place in their hearts for the truth so as to be saved. Consequently God sends on them a deluding influence so that they will believe what is false. And so all of them who have not believed the truth but have delighted in evil will be condemned." Obey Christ's doctrine so far as you know it, and you shall know more fully that it is of God, John 7:17; 10:4.

**Direction 30.** Do not tempt yourselves to unbelief, by supposed humility in abasing your natural faculties, when you should be humbled for your moral depravity. Vilifying the soul, and its reason, and natural free will, tends to unbelief, by making us think that we are only like other inferior animals, incapable of a life above with God: whereas self-abasing, because of the corruption of reason and free will, tends to show us the need of a physician, and so assists our faith in Christ.

## Translator's notes

- **Range and labels:** the file begins at the start of Direction 11 and ends at the end of Direction 30; no Direction is split. Baxter's "_Direct._ XI." and so on are rendered "Direction 11." and so on, with the label in bold and his text following in the same paragraph, since he gives no separate heading line.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:10-12 (important):** Baxter's quotation is close to, but not exactly, the King James wording (his "men receive not" where the King James has "they received not"), "Because men receive not the love of the truth, that they might be saved; for this cause God sends them strong delusions to believe a lie; that they all might be damned who believed not the truth, but had pleasure in unrighteousness." The NET differs in ways that touch his point: it has "found no place in their hearts for the truth" where the King James has "receive not the love of the truth"; "a deluding influence" for "strong delusions"; "believe what is false" for "believe a lie"; "will be condemned" for "might be damned"; and "delighted in evil" for "had pleasure in unrighteousness." The NET also makes the passage begin partway through verse 10 (it begins "and with every kind of evil deception"), and we start at "because." The King James sets it as a purpose ("that they might be saved"); the NET's "so as to be saved" reads as result. His argument, that God gives over those who sin against the light, stands in both, but "the love of the truth" is lost. Flagged.
- **John 7:17; 10:4:** Baxter gives these references without quoting them; the words "you shall know more fully that it is of God" are his own. John 7:17 in the NET speaks of the one who wants to do God's will knowing whether the teaching is from God, which fits. John 10:4 speaks of the sheep recognizing the shepherd's voice; we have kept Baxter's reference as printed and have not confirmed whether he meant a different verse (for instance 10:27).
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** vanity (of religions): emptiness; causeless: groundless; thy/thou: your/you (Directions 27, 29); do/doth give and similar verb forms: plain verbs; presently: at once; delivered down: handed down; expound: explain; propagated: spread; suffer: allow; pretence (of doctrines): claim; honestest and obedientest: most honest and most obedient; by-fancies: side notions; pretended (prophet): supposed; obtruded: forced; preservatives: safeguards; foppery: foolishness; prevail (of religions): are powerful; sensibly confuted: perceptibly refuted; sensible (evidences, temptations): perceptible; sense (Direction 15, "subduing both sense and ... the flesh"): the senses; parts or conversations: praiseworthy qualities or conduct; contemn: despise; When all that receive: Whereas all that receive; felicity: happiness; Bethink you: Think about; Yea: Yes; silly (worms): feeble; cavils: quibbles; unacquaintedness: unfamiliarity; infirmities: weaknesses; conducting: leading; immediately (from God): directly; penmen: writers; "apostates" is kept; frustrate: frustrated; forementioned: aforementioned; sensual: pleasure-loving; sensuality: indulgence in physical pleasure; biassed: biased; famousest: most famous; wilful: willful; fullier: more fully; infidelity: unbelief; pravity: depravity; free-will: free will; whenas: whereas; "whence" (where they come from): where.
- **Kept as Baxter's terms:** "Mahometanism" and "Mahometans" are his words for Islam and Muslims; he writes as a seventeenth-century Protestant polemicist, and we leave his words and his claims as his own. "Deism" in his sense is belief in one God without revelation. "Judaism" and the sentence about it are his. His "carnal" is kept. "Modal and circumstantial matters" means matters of manner and incidental detail. "Subservient sciences" means subordinate studies. "Orbs" means the heavenly bodies. "Apparitions and witchcrafts" are his evidence for spirits and are kept as his claims; the "forementioned treatises" are his earlier books, which we have not identified.
- **Capitalization and numerals:** Baxter's lowercase "christians," "christianity," and "christian" are capitalized; his "Direct. XI." numerals are written as Arabic numbers; footnote 140's "Lib. viii." is rendered "Book 8."
- **Direction 14:** the claims about Islam (that its main doctrine of one God is true, that its prophet's claims have no miracles, and that it was spread by the sword) are Baxter's, not our assertions. The phrase "or God by his special grant" is his parenthesis and is kept as printed.
- **Direction 21:** "the same soul animates all the parts" is Baxter's figure of the body and soul applied to Scripture. His remark about Cicero and "an act of parliament" is kept in his words.
- **Direction 25:** the long subject ("The universal, wonderful, implacable enmity ... do notoriously declare") and its plural verb are Baxter's grammar and are left as written.
- **Direction 29:** the verb "forsake you, and give you over" depends on "Do not provoke God"; it is left as Baxter wrote it.
- **Footnote 138:** Baxter prints "Marsil. Ficin. de. Rel. Chris." The work title in our draft translation is a guess from the abbreviation.
- **Footnote 139:** "Cic. pro Plan." is read as Cicero's speech for Plancius; this is our reading of the abbreviation.
- **Footnote 140:** "Zenophon" is Baxter's printed spelling (Xenophon). The passage is Baxter's English report, not a quotation in Greek, so it is modernized: "endued" becomes "endowed," "reporteth" becomes "reports." "Familiars" is kept (close associates).
- **Footnote 141:** the printed "de Nat. Deo. 4" is kept; we have not checked the book number in Cicero.
- **Footnote 142:** *A Saint or a Brute* is a book of Baxter's.
