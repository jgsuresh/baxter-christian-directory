# Chapter 4: False appearances of pleasure-loving, and seven directions against flesh-pleasing

*This range begins partway through the discussion of the master sin, sensuality (the material before it is in the earlier parts of this section): it opens with Baxter's numbered point "VII." and then runs on into his numbered Directions. No new Direction heading is invented for the opening paragraph. The seven Directions below are Baxter's own numbered list; the Direction numbers are Baxter's.*

*In the margin: False appearances of pleasure-loving.*

VII. Yet many are wrongfully taken by others (if not by themselves) to be pleasure-loving, by such mistakes as these: 1. Because they do not live as meanly and scantily as the poor, who lack things necessary or helpful to their duty. But by that rule I must not be well, because other men are sick; or I must not go swiftly, because the lame can go but slowly! If poor men have bad horses, I may ride on the best I can get, to dispatch my business, and redeem my time, provided I do not prefer costly, useless ostentation before true usefulness. 2. Others are accused as pleasure-loving, because the weakness of their bodies requires a more tender treatment, and diet, than healthy men's: some bodies are unfitter for duty if they fast; and some are useless through sickness and infirmities, if they are not treated with very great care. And it is as truly a duty to cherish a weak body to enable it for God's service, as to tame an unruly, lustful body, and keep it from offending him. 3. Some melancholy, conscientious persons are constantly accusing themselves, through mere scrupulosity; questioning almost all they eat, or drink, or wear, or do, whether it is not too much or too pleasing. But it is a cheerful sobriety that God requires, which neither pampers the body, nor yet disables or hinders it from its duty; and not an unprofitable, wrangling scrupulosity.

**Direction 1.** The first and grand direction against flesh-pleasing is, that you be sure, by a serious, living faith, to see the better things with God, and to be heartily taken up in minding, loving, seeking, and securing them. All the other directions are but subservient to this. For certainly man's soul will not be idle, being a living, active principle: and it is as certain, that it will not act but upon some end, or for some end. And there are no other ends to take us up, but either the things temporal or eternal. And therefore there is no true cure for a physical love of temporal things, but to turn the heart to things eternal. Believingly think first of the certainty, greatness, and eternity of the joys above; and then think that these may more certainly be yours, than any worldly riches or delights, if you do not contemptuously reject them. And then think that this is the time in which you must make sure of them, and win them, if ever you will possess them; and that you are sent into the world on purpose on this business. And then think with yourselves, how fleshly pleasures are the only competitors with the everlasting pleasures; and that, if ever you lose them, it will be by over-loving these transitory things; and that one half of your work for your salvation lies in killing your affections to all below, that they may be alive to God alone. And lastly, think how much higher and sweeter pleasures, even in this life, the godly enjoy than you; and you are losing them while you prefer these sordid pleasures. Do you think that a true believer has not a more excellent delight in his forethoughts of his immortal blessedness with Christ, and in the assurance of the love of God, and communion with him in his holy service, than you, or any pleasure-seeker, has in fleshly pleasures? Sober and serious meditation on these things will turn the mind to the true delights.

**Direction 2.** Be acquainted with the range of physical desires, and follow them, and watch them in all their extravagances. Otherwise, while you are stopping one gap, they will be running out at many more. I have given you many instances in my *Treatise of Self-denial*. I will here briefly set some before your eyes.

1. Watch your appetites as to food and drink, both quantity and quality. Gluttony is a common, unnoticed sin: the flesh in no way enslaves men more than by the appetite; as we see in drunkards and gluttons, who can no more refrain than one who thirsts in a burning fever.

2. Be careful of the lust of uncleanness, and all degrees of it, and approaches to it; especially immodest embraces and behavior.

3. Be careful of ribald, filthy talk, and love songs, and of such inflaming snares.

4. Be careful of too much sleep and idleness.

5. Be careful of taking too much delight in your riches, and lands, your buildings, and delightful conveniences.

6. Be careful lest honors, or worldly greatness, or men's applause, become your too great pleasure.

7. And lest you grow to make it your delight, to think on such things when you are alone, or talk idly of them in company with others.

8. And be careful lest the success and prosperity of your affairs please you too much, as him, Luke 12:20.

9. Do not take up any excessive pleasure in your children, relations, or nearest friends.

10. Be careful of a delight in worthless, unprofitable, sinful company.

11. Or in fineness of clothing, to show you off to the eyes of others.

12. Be careful of a delight in romances, plays, made-up stories, useless news, which corrupt the mind, and waste your time.

13. Be careful of a delight in any recreations which are excessive, needless, devouring time, unsettling the mind, enticing to further sin, hindering any duty, especially our delight in God. They are miserable souls that can delight themselves in no more safe or profitable things, than cards, and dice, and stage-plays, and immodest dancings.

**Direction 3.** Next to the universal remedy mentioned in the first direction, see that you have the particular remedies still at hand, which your own particular way of flesh-pleasing most requires. And do not let the love of your emptiness prejudice you against fair information, but impartially consider the disease and the remedy. Of the particulars soon.

**Direction 4.** Always remember that God would give you more pleasure, and not less; and that he will give you as much of the delights of sense as is truly good for you, provided you will take them in their place, in subordination to your heavenly delights. And is not this to increase and multiply your pleasure? Are not health, and friends, and food, and convenient housing, much sweeter as the fruit of the love of God, and the foretastes of everlasting mercies, and as our helps to heaven, and as the means to spiritual comfort, than of themselves alone? All your mercies are from God: he would take none from you, but sanctify them, and give you more.

**Direction 5.** See that reason keeps up its authority, as the governor of sense and appetite. And so take an account, whatever the appetite would have, of the ends and reasons of the thing, and to what it leads. Take nothing and do nothing merely because the sense or appetite would have it; but because you have reason so to do, and to gratify the appetite. Else you will act like brutes, if reason is laid aside (in human acts).

**Direction 6.** Go to the grave, and see there the end of fleshly pleasure, and what is all that it will do for you at the last. One would think it should cure the mad desire of plenty and pleasure, to see where all our wealth, and mirth, and amusement, and pleasure must be buried at last.

**Direction 7.** Lastly, always be aware that flesh is the grand enemy of your souls, and flesh-pleasing the greatest hindrance of your salvation. The devil's enmity and the world's are both but subordinate to this of the flesh: for its pleasure is the end, and the world's and Satan's temptations are both but the means to attain it. Besides the malice explained before, consider,

*In the margin: The enmity of the flesh.*

1. How contrary a voluptuous life is to the blessed example of our Lord, and of his servant Paul, and all the apostles! Paul tamed his body and brought it into subjection, lest, having preached to others, he himself should be a castaway (1 Corinthians 9:27). And all who are Christ's have crucified the flesh, with its affections and lusts (Galatians 5:24). This was signified in the ancient manner of baptizing, (and so is still by baptism itself,) when they went over their heads in the water and then rose out of it, to signify that they were dead and buried with Christ (Romans 6:3-4), and rose with him to newness of life. This is called our being "baptized into his death"; and seems the plain sense of 1 Corinthians 15:29, of being "baptized for the dead"; that is, "for dead" to show that we are dead to the world, and must die in the world, but shall rise again to the kingdom of Christ, both of grace and glory.

2. Physical indulgence shows that there is no true belief of the life to come, and proves, so far as it is powerful, the absence of all grace.

3. It is a home-bred, continual traitor to the soul; a continual tempter, and nurse of all sin; the great withdrawer of the heart from God; and the common cause of apostasy itself: it always fights against the Spirit (Galatians 5:17); and is seeking advantage from all our liberties (Galatians 5:13; 2 Peter 2:10).

4. It turns all our outward mercies into sin, and strengthens itself against God by his own benefits.

5. It is the great cause of our afflictions; for God will not spare that idol which is set up against him: flesh rebels, and flesh shall suffer.

6. And when it has brought affliction, it is most impatient under it, and makes it seem intolerable. A flesh-pleaser thinks he is undone, when affliction deprives him of his pleasure.

7. Lastly, it exceedingly unfits men for death; for then flesh must be cast into the dust, and all its pleasure be at an end. Oh sorrowful day to those who had their good things here, and their portion in this life! when all is gone that ever they valued and sought; and all the true happiness lost, which they brutishly despised! If you would joyfully then bear the dissolution and ruin of your flesh, oh master it, and mortify it now. Seek not the ease and pleasure of a little walking, breathing clay, when you should be seeking and foretasting the everlasting pleasure. Here lies your danger and your work. Strive more against your own flesh, than against all your enemies in earth and hell: if you are saved from this, you are saved from them all. Christ suffered in the flesh, to tell you that it is not pampering, but suffering, that your flesh must expect, if you will reign with him.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Only two phrases are in Baxter's quotation marks ("baptized into his death"; "baptized for the dead"); the others are his unquoted wording, kept as his and compared with the NET below.
- **Where this range begins:** the source opens with Baxter's numbered point "VII.", with its three sub-points (1., 2., 3.), which belongs to an earlier discussion; no heading is supplied for it here. The earlier items are in the previous parts. The seven numbered "Direct." items that follow are the Directions Baxter gives against flesh-pleasing (the first and grand one is Direction 1).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): sensual to pleasure-loving (VII, "taken to be pleasure-loving"; "accused as pleasure-loving"); sensual love to physical love; sensual desires to physical desires; sensualist to pleasure-seeker; sensuality to physical indulgence (Direction 7, point 2); want to lack; apace to swiftly; despatch to dispatch; serviceableness to usefulness; healthful to healthy; used (treated) to treated; still (constantly) to constantly/always; meat to food; unobserved to unnoticed; incensing to inflaming; delectable to delightful; inordinate to excessive; vain (empty) to worthless; sports to amusements (Direction 6; "recreations" in Direction 2, point 13, is kept as modern English); set you out to show you off; discomposing to unsettling; anon to soon; habitation to housing; conduce to lead; malignity to malice and opened (explained) to explained (Direction 7); just (information) to fair; vanity (empty) to emptiness; deal (as brutes) to act; laid by to laid aside; all that are to all who are; contemned to despised; felicity to happiness; hinderance to hindrance; doleful to sorrowful; sensible (aware) to aware; "go over head" to "went over their heads" (immersed). Where Baxter wrote "so" meaning "provided that," it is "provided."
- **"Take heed of"** is rendered "Be careful of" or "Be careful lest," as the style guide allows.
- **"Play-books"** is rendered "plays" (printed play texts); **"romances"** is left as Baxter's word for long prose tales of adventure and love; **"stage-plays"** is left.
- **"Uncleanness"** (Direction 2, point 2) is Baxter's word for sexual impurity. **"Ribald"** means coarse and vulgar. **"Voluptuous"** means given to pleasure. **"Competitors"** means rivals. **"Sordid"** means base. **"Scrupulosity"** is left as Baxter wrote it; it means excessive anxiety over small moral points. **"Melancholy"** is used in his sense of a long-lasting low or fearful mood, tied in his day to bodily "humors". **"Flesh-pleasing"** is Baxter's term for living to gratify the body's desires. **"Mortify"** is a technical term kept (to put to death).
- **"Treatise of Self-denial"** (Direction 2): Baxter's own book, titled in italics. We have not checked the cross-reference. **"In human acts"** (Direction 5) is Baxter's parenthesis, probably a scholastic phrase for matters of free and rational choice (we have not confirmed this).
- **"Of the particulars anon"** (Direction 3): a promise of later directions; we have not checked where Baxter fulfills it.
- **"Direct." numbering:** the source prints "Direct. I." to "Direct. VII."; rendered as Directions 1 to 7. Direction 2 carries its own numbered list of thirteen watch-points, kept as numbered.
- **Luke 12:20** (Direction 2, point 8): Baxter's "as him, Luke xii. 20" refers to the rich man in the parable whom God calls a fool. It is an allusion, not a quotation. For reference, the NET reads: "But God said to him, 'You fool! This very night your life will be demanded back from you, but who will get what you have prepared for yourself?'"
- **1 Corinthians 9:27** (Direction 7, point 1): Baxter's wording is his own, in the form of the King James Version ("keep under my body, and bring it into subjection...castaway"), unquoted. NET: "Instead I subdue my body and make it my slave, so that after preaching to others I myself will not be disqualified." Note that the NET has "disqualified," where the King James and Baxter have "castaway" (rejected); Baxter's "cast-away" (his spelling) is kept as "castaway." **Whether this is the loss of salvation or of a ministry's approval** is a matter of interpretation that the NET word leaves more open than Baxter's does; we do not settle it.
- **Galatians 5:24:** Baxter's "crucified the flesh, with the affections and lusts thereof" is his paraphrase of the King James, unquoted. NET: "Now those who belong to Christ have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires." The NET's "passions and desires" is the same pair as Baxter's "affections and lusts"; we keep "affections" as his word (his "affections" are emotions and inclinations, as elsewhere in this project).
- **Romans 6:3-4:** Baxter's "baptized into his death" matches the NET phrase exactly. NET verse 4 also reads "buried with him through baptism into death," which supports his point about immersion and burial. The ancient practice of immersion that Baxter describes is his claim; we have not checked it.
- **1 Corinthians 15:29:** Baxter's quoted "baptized for the dead" matches the NET phrase; his explanation, "for dead," is his own reading (baptized as those who are dead to the world). **This is where the NET changes the sense Baxter leans on:** the NET verse goes on, "If the dead are not raised at all, then why are they baptized for them?" which most naturally reads "for the dead" as on behalf of other people who have died, a different reading from his. We keep Baxter's gloss and quotation marks as he wrote them, and flag that his is a minority interpretation of a hard verse. (We state this cautiously; the passage is much debated.)
- **Galatians 5:17:** Baxter cites it without quoting: "it always fights against the Spirit." NET: "For the flesh has desires that are opposed to the Spirit, and the Spirit has desires that are opposed to the flesh..." Baxter's "flesh" is his word for the pleasure-seeking appetites; Paul's "flesh" in the NET carries a wider sense of human nature in opposition to God, which Baxter narrows in his own argument.
- **Galatians 5:13; 2 Peter 2:10** (Direction 7, point 3): cited for "seeking advantage from all our liberties." NET Galatians 5:13: "For you were called to freedom, brothers and sisters; only do not use your freedom as an opportunity to indulge your flesh, but through love serve one another." NET 2 Peter 2:10: "especially those who indulge their fleshly desires and who despise authority." The NET's "indulge" in both verses is narrower than Baxter's "seeking advantage from all our liberties," which stretches the verses.
- **"Home-bred"** (Direction 7, point 3): Baxter's word for an enemy within, born in us (kept with a hyphen). **"Dissolution"** (point 7) means the breaking up of the body at death.
- **Source oddities:** none noted. There are no footnote markers in lines 28272-28461. The two margin notes ("False appearances of sensuality" and "The enmity of the flesh") are given as "In the margin" lines; the first is rendered with "pleasure-loving" in place of "sensuality" to match the swaps above (Baxter's printed word is "sensuality").
