# Chapter 3: Grand Direction XIII (part 1 of 3)

*This file covers the opening of Grand Direction XIII: Baxter's Directions 1 to 4 on delighting in God. It ends with a margin note ("Reasons for delight in God") that belongs to the paragraph at the start of the next file.*

**Grand Direction 13. Diligently labor that God and holiness may be your chief delight: and this holy delight may be the ordinary temperament of your religion.**

*Directions for Delighting in God.*

**Direction 1. Rightly understand what delight in God it is that you must seek and exercise.**

It is not a mere sensory delight, which is exercised about the objects of the senses or the imagination, and is common to beasts with men: nor is it the delights of direct sight of God, such as the blessed have in heaven: nor is it an enthusiastic delight, consisting in irrational raptures and joys, of which we can give no account of the reason. [121] Nor is it a delight inconsistent with sorrow and fear, when they are duties; but it is the solid, rational complacency of the soul in God and holiness, arising from the perceptions of that in him which is justly delightful to us. And it is such, as, in its valuing of its object, and inward complacency and gladness, though not in passionate joy or mirth, must excel our delight in temporal pleasure; and must be the end of all our humblings, and other lesser duties.

**Direction 2. Understand how much of this holy delight may be hoped for on earth.**

[122] Though too many Christians feel much more fear and sorrow in their religion than delight, yet every true Christian does esteem God more delightful, or fit and worthy of his delights, if he could enjoy him; whereas to the carnal, fleshly things do seem more fit to be their delights. And though most Christians do not reach very high in their delights in God, yet God has prescribed us such means, in which, if we faithfully used them, we might reach much higher. And this much we might well expect: 1. So much as might make our lives incomparably more quiet, contented, and pleasant to us, than are the lives of the greatest or happiest worldly people. 2. So much as might make our thoughts of God and the life to come, to be ready, welcome, pleasant thoughts to us. 3. So much as might greatly prevail against our excessive griefs and fears, and our reluctance toward duties, and weariness in them, and might make religion an ordinary pleasure. 4. So much as might take off our hankering desire after unnecessary recreations and unlawful pleasures of the flesh. 5. So much as might sweeten all our mercies to us, with a spiritual perfume or relish. 6. So much as might make some sufferings joyful, and the rest easier to us. 7. And so much as might make the thoughts of death less terrible to us, and make us desire to be with Christ.

*In the margin: Psalm 68:3–5; 69:30, etc.*

**Direction 3. Understand what there is in God and holiness, which is fit to be the soul's delight.**

As, 1. Behold him in the infinite perfections of his being; his omnipotence, omniscience, and his goodness; his holiness, eternity, immutability, etc. And as your eye delights in an excellent picture, or a comely building, or fields, or gardens, not because they are yours, but because they are a delightful object to the eye; so let your minds delight themselves in God, considered in himself, as the only object of highest delight. 2. Delight yourselves also in his relative attributes, in which his goodness to his creatures is expressed: such as his all-sufficiency, and faithfulness or truth, his kindness, his mercy, and compassion, and patience toward sinners, and his justice to all. 3. Delight yourselves in him as his glory appears in his wondrous works, of creation and daily providence. 4. Delight yourselves in him as he is related to you, as your God and Father, and as all your interest, hope, and happiness are in him alone. 5. Delight yourselves in him as his excellencies shine forth in his blessed Son. 6. And as they appear in the wisdom and goodness of his word, in all the precepts and promises of the gospel, Psalm 119:162; Jeremiah 15:16. 7. Delight yourself in his image, though only imperfectly printed on your soul; and also on his holy servants, Galatians 2:20; 1 Corinthians 15:10; 2 Corinthians 7:18. 8. Delight yourselves in the consideration of the glory which he has from all his creatures, and the universal fulfilling of his will: as the prosperity and happiness of your friend delights you, and the success of any excellent enterprises, and the praise of excellent things and persons, and as you have a special delight in the success of truth, and the flourishing order, and unity, and peace, and prosperity of kingdoms, especially of the church, much more than in your personal prosperity (unless you have selfish, private, base, unmanly dispositions); so much more should you delight in the glory and happiness of God. 9. Delight yourselves in the safety which you have in his favor and defense: and the treasury which you have in his all-sufficiency and love, for your continual supplies in every want, and deliverance in every danger; and the ground of quiet contentedness and confidence which is offered to fearful souls in him. 10. Delight yourselves in the particular discoveries of his common mercies to the world, and his special mercies to his saints; and his personal mercies to yourselves, from your birth to this moment; both upon your souls, and bodies, and friends, and reputation, and property, and affairs in all relations. 11. Delight yourselves in the privilege you enjoy of speaking to him, and of him, and hearing from him, and adoring and worshiping him, and singing and publishing his praise, and in the communion which your souls may have with him through Christ, on his days, and at all times, in his sacraments, and in all your lives. And say as Solomon, 1 Kings 8:27, "And will God indeed dwell on earth? Will he dwell and walk with sinful men? When the heaven of heavens cannot contain him." Psalm 40:16, "May all those who seek you be happy and rejoice in you;" and 122:1, let us be glad to go to the house of the Lord, and join with his holy assemblies in his worship. Psalm 46:4–5, "The river's channels" of his grace "bring joy to the city of God, the special, holy dwelling place of the Most High: God lives within it, it cannot be moved." 12. Delight yourselves above all in the anticipations and hope of the glory which you shall see and enjoy forever. I only name all these to remind you, because they are spoken of before in the directions for love.

*In the margin: How much God is for his servants' delights.*

**Direction 4. Understand how much these holy delights are pleasing to God, and how much he is for his people's pleasure.**

For it much hinders the joy of many Christians, that they think it is against the will of God, that such as they should so much rejoice; or at least that they do not understand how much he has commanded it, and how great a duty it is, and how much pleasing to their God. Consider, 1. It is not for nothing that the nature of man is made capable of higher and larger delights, than the brutish, pleasure-loving nature is: [123] and that in this we are made little lower than angels. 2. Nor is it for nothing that God has made delight and complacency, the most powerful, commanding affection, and the end of all the other passions, which they professedly serve and seek; and the most natural, inseparable affection of the soul, there being none that does not desire delight. 3. Nor is it in vain that God has provided and offered such plenty of most excellent objects for our delight, especially himself, in his attributes, love, mercy, Son, Spirit, and kingdom: which brutes were not made to know or to enjoy. 4. Nor has he given us in vain, such excellent, convenient, and various helps, and lesser preparations which tend to our delight; even for body and mind, to further our delight in God. 5. Nor is it in vain that he makes us even more nearly capable by his Spirit; even by stirring humblings, and mortifying, cleansing, illuminating, and quickening works: and that the kingdom of heaven consists in righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit: and that the Spirit has undertaken to be the comforter of believers, who is sent on no low or needless work. 6. Nor did Christ purchase his people's joys in vain, by the price of his grievous sufferings and sorrows. Having borne our griefs, and being made a man of sorrows, that we who do not see him might rejoice in believing, with joy unspeakable and full of glory. 7. Nor is it in vain that he has filled his word with such matter of delight and comfort, in the gladdest tidings that could come to man, and in such free, and full, and faithful promises. 8. Nor has he multiplied his commands for his rejoicing and delight, in vain; again and again commanding us to rejoice, and always to rejoice. 9. Nor is it insignificant that he has forbidden those worldly cares, and fears, and griefs which would devour their joys: nor that he has so clearly shown them the way to joy, and blames them if they do not walk in it. 10. He fills up their lives with mercies, and matter of delight, by his direction, support, provisions, and arrangements: and all this in their way of trial, and in the valley of tears. 11. How tender he is of their sufferings and sorrows; not afflicting willingly, nor delighting to grieve the sons of men. 12. He does not take away their delight and comfort, until they cast it away themselves, by sinning, or self-afflicting, or neglecting his proposed pleasures. 13. He never fails to meet them with his delights, while they walk in the way prescribed to that end: unless when it tends to their greater pleasure, to have some temporary interruption of the pleasure. [124] 14. In their greatest needs, when they themselves and other helps must fail, he often gives them the greatest joys. 15. And he takes their delights and sorrows as if they were his own. In all their afflictions he is afflicted, and he delights in their welfare, and rejoices over them to do them good. Cannot you see the will of your Father in all this? 16. If you cannot, yet lift up your heads, and foresee the eternal delights which he has prepared for you, when you shall enter into your Master's joy: and then judge whether God is for your delight?

*In the margin: Reasons for delight in God.*

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). The NET text was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Heading:** the source has "Diligently labour that God and holiness may be thy chief delight: and this holy delight may be the ordinary temperament of thy religion." We keep "ordinary" and "temperament" (his word for the settled disposition of one's religion). The source runs each Direction's heading into its body with "--"; we set the heading in bold. The line "Directions for Delighting ourselves in God" is a sub-title in the source.
- **1 Kings 8:27 (Solomon):** Baxter's quotation is a paraphrase or blend: "Will he dwell and walk with sinful men?" is not in the verse. We keep his wording, modernized, and cite the verse as he does. The NET reads: "God does not really live on the earth! Look, if the sky and the highest heaven cannot contain you, how much less this temple I have built!" The NET states what Baxter's wording puts as a question, and speaks to God ("you") where Baxter speaks of him ("him"). His extra clause about sinful men is his own application.
- **Psalm 40:16:** NET wording. Baxter's "Let those that seek him rejoice and be glad in him" is in the third person; the NET addresses God directly ("May all those who seek you be happy and rejoice in you"). The sense he leans on is unchanged.
- **Psalm 122:1:** Baxter paraphrases ("let us be glad to go to the house of the Lord") and does not quote. The NET reads: "I was glad because they said to me, 'We will go to the Lord's temple.'" We give no quotation marks in the text.
- **Psalm 46:4–5:** NET wording. Baxter's own words "of his grace" stand outside his quotation marks: they are his application of "the river's channels" (King James: "streams"). The NET has no plural "tabernacles"; it has the singular "dwelling place," and "God lives within it" begins a new verse (verse 5). We keep Baxter's colon before it, and cite 46:4–5 because the quotation runs into verse 5 (Baxter's printed reference is "xlvi. 4").
- **2 Corinthians 7:18 (item 7):** Baxter's printed reference. Second Corinthians 7 has only 16 verses, so this reference appears to be a misprint, but we have not determined the intended verse. We keep it as printed. **Galatians 2:20; 1 Corinthians 15:10** (same item): kept as printed; these concern the Spirit's work and grace in Paul rather than saying the words "his image" in so many words, so they are probably cited for the idea that Christ's grace appears in his servants. This is our reading only.
- **Other references to check:** Isaiah 58:19 in note [123] does not appear to exist (Isaiah 58 has 14 verses); we keep it as printed and have not found the intended verse. Baxter's inline references in item 6 (Psalm 119:162; Jeremiah 15:16) and in the margin note before Direction 3 (Psalm 68:3–5; 69:30) are converted but not quoted by him, so we give no quotations.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "little lower than angels" (probably Psalm 8:5); "righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit" (probably Romans 14:17); "a man of sorrows" and "borne our griefs" (probably Isaiah 53:3–4, which also appears in note [123]); "joy unspeakable and full of glory" (probably 1 Peter 1:8, also in note [123]); "rejoice, and always rejoice" (probably Philippians 4:4; 1 Thessalonians 5:16); "not afflicting willingly" (probably Lamentations 3:33); "In all their afflictions he is afflicted" (probably Isaiah 63:9, cited in note [124]); "enter into your Master's joy" (probably Matthew 25:21). These identifications are ours and are not in Baxter's text.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): sensitive, sensory; fantasy, imagination; immediate intuition, direct sight; delectable, delightful; worldlings, worldly people; inordinate, excessive; backwardness, reluctance; benignity, kindness; subserve, serve (the sense is "serve as means to"); brutish, sensual nature, brutish, pleasure-loving nature; disposals, arrangements; present interruption, temporary interruption; forethoughts, anticipations; name (as in a good name), reputation; estates, property; humiliations, humblings; "lesser duties" for "inferior duties"; "for your memory," to remind you.
- **Direction 4, item 8:** "his rejoicing and delight" is Baxter's wording; "his" means each believer's (his people's).
- **Technical terms kept:** *complacency* (his word for settled satisfaction in an object; not smugness); *enthusiastic* (claiming direct inspiration, apart from reason and Scripture; see his note [121]); *raptures*; *affection* and *passions* (emotions and desires); *carnal* (ruled by the flesh); *mortifying* (putting to death sinful desires); *religion* (worship and devotion); *relative attributes* (those that describe God in relation to creatures, as opposed to his being in himself). *Stirring humblings* (his "affecting humiliations") means the heart-moving acts of self-humbling before God, such as repentance.
- **"Man"** in "the nature of man" and "tidings that could come to man" is kept as Baxter's general term for humanity.
- **Item 10 in Direction 3:** the "discoveries" of mercies means the disclosures or revealings of God's mercies to the person.
- **Sentence structure:** in Direction 3, item 11, the source has Scripture references opening each quotation ("1 Kings viii. 27, ..."). We keep that order, but convert the references to modern form.
- **Cross-reference:** "the directions for love" (end of Direction 3) refers to Baxter's earlier Grand Direction on the love of God (Chapter 3, Grand Direction XI); we have not checked the exact passage.
- **Note [121]:** the books named (*Directions for the Cure of Church Divisions* and Baxter's defense of it) are as Baxter titles them; we have not checked details of them.
- **Margin notes:** the first margin note, "Psalm 68:3–5; 69:30, etc.," is only a list of references, and in the source it sits before Direction 3. The last margin note ("Reasons for delight in God") sits at the end of this range, just before the next paragraph.
