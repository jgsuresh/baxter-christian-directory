# Chapter 9: Title 6, Direction 1 continued: what the scorner derides (points 9-20), and the scorner as God's open enemy

*This section begins partway through Direction 1, inside its first point ("Consider what it is that you deride"), at sub-point 9.*

9. You oppose and scorn men for loving themselves; yes, for loving their soul, and taking care of its health and welfare. For how can a man truly love himself, and not love his soul which is himself? And how can a man love his soul, and not prefer it before the low concerns of his flesh? and not take the greatest care of its greatest everlasting happiness? Can a man truly love himself, and yet damn himself, or lose the little time in which he must, if ever, work out his salvation? You will not scorn him that is careful of your children, or your very cattle? You love them, and therefore are careful of them yourselves. And shall not he that loves his soul be careful of it? To love ourselves is natural to us as men: and how shall he love his neighbor that does not love himself?

10. You scorn men because they love heaven above earth, and because they are desirous to live forever with God and all the holy hosts of heaven. For what is it that these men do so diligently, but seek to be saved? What do they but "above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness"? "and work ... for the food" that does not perish (John 6:27); and lay up their "treasures in heaven" (Matthew 6:20); and set their "heart" there (verse 21); "and keep seeking the things above," and have their "citizenship ... in heaven" (Colossians 3:1–3; Philippians 3:19–20). And if it is so scornful a matter to seek for heaven, surely you never think of coming to heaven yourself; unless you think to come there by scorning at the seekers of it.

11. You deride men because they are unwilling to be damned, and unwilling to do that which they know would damn them; or to neglect that without which there is no hope of escaping hell. They believe the threatenings of God, and therefore they think no pains too great to escape his wrath. They think a holy life is both a necessary and an easy way to prevent everlasting torment: but if you think otherwise, keep your opinion till grace or hell shall make you wiser; and do not mock at a man that will not play with his own damnation, and leap into hell as desperately as yourself.

12. You deride men because they will not be the voluntary destroyers of themselves. Would it not be enough for you to betray them to others? or to murder any of your neighbors yourself? but you must wish them to do it with their own hands, and deride them if they will not? O cruel monster! that would wish a man to lie in the fire of hell forevermore! and to go there willfully of his own accord! which is ten thousand times worse than to wish him to cut his own throat. Do you say, God forbid! I desire no such thing. Why, man, are you doing you know not what? Does not he tempt a man to be hanged, that tempts him to kill and steal? When the righteous God has unchangeably determined in his law, that "without holiness none shall see God, and that Christ shall come in flaming fire to render vengeance to those that do not obey his gospel, and that all those shall be damned that do not obey the truth, but have pleasure in unrighteousness;" [553] when God has resolved that hell shall be the wages of ungodliness, do you not desire them to damn themselves, when you desire them to be ungodly? If you believe that there is any hell at all, then tell me what it is possible for any man to do, to murder his soul and damn himself, but only to be ungodly? If this way does not do it, there is no danger of any other. Tell me, do you think the devil deserves to be called a murderer of souls? If not, it seems you will openly take the devil's part; but if he does deserve it, then let the reason of all the world be judge, whether that man does not deserve it much more, that will do much more against himself than the devil ever did or can do? The devil can only tempt, but you would have men do the thing that he tempts them to, and actually to sin, and neglect a holy life. And which is the worse, he that does the evil, or he that only persuades them to it? If the devil is called, "Our adversary, that like a roaring lion goes about night and day seeking whom he may devour" (1 Peter 5:8), what should that man be called that does far more against himself than all the devils in hell do against him? Surely he is a devourer or destroyer of himself. Tell me, you deranged scorner! is the devil's work, do you think, good or bad? If it is good, take your part of it, and boast of it when you see the end. If it is bad (to deceive souls and entice them to sin and hell), why would you have men do worse by themselves? He that sins does worse than he that tempts. Tell me, what way does the devil take to do men harm, and damn their souls, but only by drawing them to sin? He has no other way in the world to ruin any man, but by tempting him to that which you tempt men to; even to sin against God and to neglect a holy life. So that it is plain that you scorn and oppose men because they will not be worse than devils to themselves.

13. Moreover you oppose men for not forsaking God! What is it to forsake God, but to refuse to love, and honor, and obey him, as God? He has told us himself that "the one who approaches God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him" (Hebrews 11:6). And is it not this diligent seeking him that you deride? It is plain then that you would scorn men away from God, and have them forsake him as you have done.

14. You scorn men for not being hypocrites; because they will be that in good earnest which you hypocritically call yourself, and would be thought. You call yourself a Christian; and what is it but for being serious Christians that you deride them? You claim to believe in God; and what is it but for obeying and serving God that you deride them? You claim to believe the Scripture to be the word of God; and what is it but for following the holy Scriptures that you deride them? You say you believe the communion of saints; and deride those that hold the communion of saints in practice. You say you believe that Christ shall judge the world; and yet scorn those that are serious in preparing for his judgment. You pray that God's name may be hallowed, and his kingdom come, and his will be done on earth as it is in heaven; and yet you deride those that hallow his name, and are subjects of his kingdom, and try to do his will. O wretched hypocrite! And yet that tongue of yours claims that it is their hypocrisy for which you hate and deride them, when you do it because they are not such blind and senseless hypocrites as yourself! Can there be grosser hypocrisy in the world than to hate and scorn the serious practice of your own profession? and the diligent living according to that which your own tongue professes to believe? If you say that it is for doing too much, and being too strict, I answer you, if it is not the will of God that they do, though I would not deride them, I would seek to change them as well as you! But if it is the will of God, then tell me, do you think they do more than those that are in heaven do? or do they live more strictly than those in heaven? If they do, then oppose them and do not spare them. If not, why do you pray that God's will may be done on earth as it is in heaven?

15. You deride men for doing that which they were made for, and that which they have their reason, and will, and all their faculties for; take them off this, and they are good for nothing: a beast is good to serve man, and the plants to feed him; but what is man good for, or what was he made for, but to serve his Maker? And do you scorn him for that which he came into the world for? You may as well hate a knife because it can cut, or a scythe for mowing, or a clock for telling the hour of the day, when it was made for nothing else.

16. You deride men for being saved by Christ, and for imitating his example. What did Christ come into the world for but "to destroy the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8); and to "save his people from their sins" (Matthew 1:21); and "to set us free from every kind of lawlessness and to purify for himself a people who are truly his, who are eager to do good" (Titus 2:14)? And has Christ, to the astonishment of men and angels, come down into flesh, and lived among men, and given them his holy doctrine and example, and suffered death for them, and all this only to bring men to zealous purity, and do you dare make a scorn of it after this? What is this but to scorn your Savior, and scorn all the work of redemption, and tread underfoot the Son of God, and despise his blood, his life, and precepts?

17. You scorn men for being renewed and sanctified by the Holy Spirit. What is the work of the Holy Spirit on us, but to sanctify us? and what is it to sanctify us, but to cleanse us from sin, and cause us entirely to devote our souls and lives to God? Do you believe in the Holy Spirit, or not? If you do, what is that but to believe in him as the sanctifier of God's elect? And what did you take sanctification to be, but this purity and holiness of heart and life? and yet do you dare deride it?

18. You deride men for imitating those ancient saints whose names you seem yourself to honor, and in honor of whom you keep holidays. You claim to honor the names of Peter, and Paul, and Stephen, and John; of Augustine, Jerome, Chrysostom, and other such saints of God; and yet will you make a scorn of those that strive to imitate them? Search and see; if any of these men did, after their conversion, live in luxury, card-playing, dice-playing, profaneness, and if any of them were against a holy life, against much praying, hearing, reading the Scriptures, meditating, exact obedience to God; then let the shame not be yours, but mine. He that is most unlike them, let him have the scorn.

19. You deride men for repenting of their former sin, and for accepting that mercy which Christ has purchased, and God has offered them, and sent his messengers to entreat them to accept. Can they repent of their former ungodliness, and not turn from it and amend? If you knew what they know, you would repent yourself, and not deride men for repenting: if you knew the gift of God, you would beg it, and gladly accept it yourself, and not deride those that accept it.

20. You scorn men for keeping that covenant which you also made with God in your baptism yourself. At the same time you speak against the anabaptists, that will not have their children baptized, and deride those that keep their covenant which in baptism they made. What a monster of contradictions is an ungodly hypocrite! Did you not in baptism renounce the flesh, the world, and the devil, and give yourself up in covenant to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit? And do you not yet know what you did? but scorn those that perform it? What is it to be given up to God in baptism, but to take him for your God, your Savior, and Sanctifier, whom you must love, and seek, and obey in holiness, with all your heart, and soul, and might? He is a covenant-breaker indeed, that hates the keeping of it.

I have so far been showing you what it is that you oppose and deride: I shall now tell you further what you do, in showing you the aggravations of the sin, and its importance.

2. Consider in all this what an open enemy you are to God, and an open soldier for the devil: what can you do more against God, and do your worst, than make a scorn of all his work and servants? He does not fear your power or rage; you cannot hurt him. How many millions of such worms as you can he tread to hell, or destroy in a moment! It is in his servants and service that he is honored or opposed here, and that mortals show their love or hatred to him. And how can you devise, if you would do your worst, to serve the devil more notoriously than by opposing and deriding the service of God? If such are not Satan's servants, he has none.

## Baxter's notes

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** Chapter 9 ("Directions for the Government of the Tongue") is divided into six "Titles" (Baxter's *Tit.*), that is, headed divisions. This file belongs to Title 6, against profane deriding and scorning. It continues Direction 1 ("For the cure of those that are already infected with so heinous a sin, the chief direction is, to understand the greatness of it, and the miserable consequents"). That Direction's first point, "Consider what it is that you deride," has numbered sub-points 1–20; this file runs from sub-point 9 to the end of that list, then gives the linking sentence and the Direction's second point ("2. Consider in all this..."), which the next file continues. The numbering is Baxter's.
- **you:** throughout, Baxter addresses the scorner in the singular "thou"; rendered "you."
- **Matthew 6:33** (point 10; Baxter gives no reference): KJV "seek first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness"; NET "above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness." The sense is unchanged.
- **John 6:27:** Baxter quotes "labour for the meat" and adds "that perisheth not" outside his quotation marks. NET reads "Do not work for the food that disappears, but for the food that remains to eternal life"; we quote "work ... for the food" and keep his unquoted words, modernized ("that does not perish").
- **Matthew 6:20–21:** NET "treasures in heaven" (KJV "treasure"); Baxter's "hearts there" follows KJV "there will your heart be also"; NET "there your heart will be also." We quote only "heart."
- **Colossians 3:1–3; Philippians 3:19–20:** Baxter runs these together in one quotation: "and seek the things that are above, and have their conversation in heaven." We give the NET phrases separately: "keep seeking the things above" (Colossians 3:1) and "citizenship ... in heaven" (Philippians 3:20). **NET flag:** the KJV "conversation" in Philippians 3:20 (which in 1611 meant way of life or conduct, and translates a Greek word about belonging to a commonwealth) becomes "citizenship" in the NET. Baxter's point is that the godly live for and toward heaven; "citizenship" stresses belonging rather than conduct, though the argument still stands.
- **Footnote 553 quotation** (point 12): "without holiness none shall see God ... have pleasure in unrighteousness" is Baxter's blend of Hebrews 12:14, 2 Thessalonians 1:8, and 2 Thessalonians 2:12, so his wording is kept, modernized. NET: "Pursue peace with everyone, and holiness, for without it no one will see the Lord" (Hebrews 12:14); "With flaming fire he will mete out punishment on those who do not know God and do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus" (2 Thessalonians 1:8); "And so all of them who have not believed the truth but have delighted in evil will be condemned" (2 Thessalonians 2:12). Note that the NET of Hebrews 12:14 has "the Lord," not "God," and 2 Thessalonians 2:12 has "have not believed the truth" where Baxter has "obey not the truth."
- **1 Peter 5:8:** Baxter's quotation adds "night and day," which is in neither the KJV nor the Greek, so it is his loose quotation and is kept, modernized. NET: "Your enemy the devil, like a roaring lion, is on the prowl looking for someone to devour."
- **Hebrews 11:6 — NET flag:** KJV "he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him"; NET "he rewards those who seek him." Baxter's next sentence leans on the KJV word: "is it not this *diligent* seeking him that you deride?" The NET has no "diligently," so his follow-up rests on the King James wording.
- **1 John 3:8; Matthew 1:21:** NET wording matches Baxter's quoted phrases.
- **Titus 2:14 — corrected reference and NET flag:** the source prints "Tit. ii. 42"; Titus 2 has 15 verses and the quoted words are in 2:14, so we give Titus 2:14. KJV "redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works"; NET "to set us free from every kind of lawlessness and to purify for himself a people who are truly his, who are eager to do good." Baxter's next sentence ("to bring men to zealous purity") picks up the KJV "zealous"; the NET's "eager" carries the same idea more mildly.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording, references not in his text): "work out his salvation" (Philippians 2:12); love of neighbor as of oneself (Leviticus 19:18; Matthew 22:39); the Lord's Prayer, "hallowed ... kingdom come ... will be done on earth as it is in heaven" (Matthew 6:9–10); "tread underfoot the Son of God" (Hebrews 10:29); "if you knew the gift of God" (John 4:10); "the wages of ungodliness" (compare Romans 6:23); "the communion of saints" is a phrase of the Apostles' Creed.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): concernments, concerns; thither, there; wilfully, willfully; take on thee (to believe), claim (to believe); pretendeth, claims; endeavour, try; spare not, do not spare them; distracted (out of one's mind), deranged; undo, ruin; do men hurt, do men harm; carding, dicing, card-playing, dice-playing; Hierom, Jerome; Holy Ghost, Holy Spirit; hitherto, so far; were it not enough, would it not be enough; "then the reason of all the world be judge," "then let the reason of all the world be judge" (one word added for modern grammar).
- **aggravations:** kept; Baxter's term for the circumstances that make a sin heavier or worse.
- **holidays** (point 18): holy days, here the saints' days kept in their honor.
- **anabaptists** (point 20): Baxter's term for those who rejected infant baptism. His remark is aimed at the scorner's inconsistency, not at the anabaptists' case.
- **Augustine, Jerome, Chrysostom** (point 18): early church fathers whom the scorner nominally honors.
- **"Why, man, are you doing you know not what?"** (point 12): Baxter's "dost thou do thou knowest not what?"; word order kept as closely as modern English allows.
