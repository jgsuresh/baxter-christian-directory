# Chapter 7: Title 8, Directions against sinful fear (fear of God, Directions 1-12; fear of the devil, Directions 1-7)

**Title 8. Directions against sinful fear.**

The chief of my advice concerning this sin, I have given you before, chapter 3, direction 12. Yet something I shall here add. Fear is a necessary passion in man, which is planted in nature for the restraining of us from sin, and driving us on to duty, and preventing misery. It is either God, or devils, or men, or inferior creatures, or ourselves, that we fear. God must be feared as he is God; as he is great, and holy, and just, and true; as our Lord, and King, and Judge, and Father; and the fear of him is the beginning of wisdom. Devils must be feared only as subordinate to God, as the executioners of his wrath; and so must men, and beasts, and fire, and water, and other creatures be feared, and no otherwise. We must so discern and fear a danger as to avoid it. Ourselves we are less apt to fear, because we know that we love ourselves. But there is no creature that we have so much cause to fear, as our folly, weakness, and willfulness in sin.

Fear is sinful, 1. When it proceeds from unbelief, or a distrust of God. 2. When it ascribes more to the creature than is its due: as when we fear devils or men, as great, or bad, or as our enemies, without due respect to their dependence upon the will of God: when we fear a chained creature, as if he were unchained. 3. When we fear God upon mistake or error, or fear that in him which is not in him, or is not to be feared. As when we fear that he will break his promise; that he will condemn the keepers of his covenant; that he will not forgive the penitent that hate their sin; that he will despise the contrite; that he will not hear the prayers of the humble, faithful soul; that he will fail them, and forsake them; that he will not cause all things to work together for their good; that he will forsake his church; that Christ will not come again; that our bodies shall not be raised; that there is no life of glory for the just, or no immortality of souls: all such fears as these are sinful. 4. When our fear is so immoderate in degree, as to distract us, or hinder us from faith and prayer, and make us melancholy: or when it hinders love, and praise, and thanks, and necessary joy; and tends not to drive us to God, and to the use of means to avoid the danger, but to drive us from God, and kill our hope, and make us sit down in despair.

**Directions against sinful fear of God.**

**Direction 1. Know God in his goodness, mercifulness, and truth, and it will banish sinful fears of him: for they proceed from the ignorance or unbelief of some of these; or not considering and applying them to the cause that is before you.**

"Those who know your name will put their trust in you" (Psalm 9:10).

**Direction 2. Know God in Jesus Christ the Mediator, and come to him by him.**

And then you may have "boldness and confident access" (Ephesians 3:12). We have "confidence to enter the sanctuary by the blood of Jesus, by the fresh and living way that he inaugurated for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh, and since we have a great priest over the house of God, let us draw near with a sincere heart in the assurance that faith brings" (Hebrews 10:19-22). The sight of Christ by faith should banish immoderate fear. "Have courage! It is I. Do not be afraid" (Matthew 14:27).

**Direction 3. Understand the tenor of the gospel, and the freeness of the covenant of grace, and then you will there find abundant encouragement against the matter of inordinate fears.**

**Direction 4. Employ yourselves as much as possible in love and praise: for love expels tormenting fear; there is no fear in love (1 John 4:18).**

**Direction 5. Remember God's particular mercies to yourselves: for those will persuade you that he will treat you kindly, when you find that he has done so already.**

As when Manoah said, "We will certainly die because we have seen a supernatural being"; his wife answered, "If the Lord wanted to kill us, he would not have accepted the burnt offering and the grain offering from us. He would not have shown us all these things" (Judges 13:22-23).

**Direction 6. Labor to clear up your title to the promises and special interest in Christ.**

Otherwise the doubts of that will be always feeding and justifying your fears.

**Direction 7. Consider what a horrible injury it is to God, to think of him as you do of the devil, as an enemy to humble, willing souls, and a destroyer of them, and an adversary to them that diligently seek him; of whom he is a lover and rewarder (Hebrews 11:6).**

And so to think of God as evil, and fear him upon such misapprehensions.

**Direction 8. Observe the sinfulness of your fear in the effects; how it drives you from God, and hinders faith, and love, and thankfulness, and discourages you from prayer, and sacraments, and all duty.**

And therefore it must be pleasing to the devil, and displeasing to God, and no way to be pleaded for or justified.

**Direction 9. Mark how you contradict the efforts of God, in his word, and by his ministers.**

Do you find God driving any from him, and frightening away souls that would gladly be his? Or does he not prepare the way himself, and reconcile the world to himself in Christ, and then send his ambassadors in his name and stead to beseech them to be reconciled to God, and to tell them that all things are ready, and compel them to come in. [343]

**Direction 10. Consider how you wrong others, and keep them from coming home to God.**

When they see you terrified in a way of piety, they will flee from it as if some enemies or robbers were in the way. If you tread fearfully, others will fear there is some quicksand. If you tremble when you enter the ship with Christ, others will think he is an unfaithful pilot, or that it is a leaking vessel. Your fear discourages them.

**Direction 11. Remember how remediless, as to comfort, you leave yourselves, while you inordinately fear him, who alone must comfort you against all your other fears.**

If you fear your remedy, what shall cure the fear of your disease? If you fear your food, what shall cure your fear of hunger? If you fear him that is most good and faithful, and the friend of every upright soul, what shall ease you of your fear of the wicked and the enemies of holy souls? If you fear your Father, who shall comfort you against your foes? You cast away all peace, when you make God your terror.

**Direction 12. Yet be careful that under this pretext you do not cast away the necessary fear of God; even such as belongs to men in your condition, to drive them out of their sin and security to Christ, and such as the truth of his threatenings requires.**

For a senseless presumption and contempt of God are a sin of a far greater danger.

**Directions against sinful fear of the devil.**

**Direction 1. Remember that the devil is chained up, and wholly at the will and command of God.**

He could not touch Job, nor an ox, nor a donkey of his, till he had permission from God (Job 1). He cannot appear to you nor hurt you unless God gives him leave.

**Direction 2. Labor therefore to make sure of the love of God, and then you are safe; then you have God, his love and promise, always to set against the devil.**

**Direction 3. Remember that Christ has conquered the devil in his temptations, on the cross, by his resurrection and ascension.**

He "destroyed through death the one who holds the power of death (that is, the devil), and set free those who were held in slavery all their lives by their fear of death" (Hebrews 2:14-15). The prince of this world is conquered and cast out by him, and will you fear a conquered foe?

**Direction 4. Remember that you are already delivered from his power and dominion, if you are renewed by the Spirit of God.**

And therefore let his own be afraid of him, that are under his power, and not the freemen and redeemed ones of Christ. God has delivered you in the day that he converted you, from a thousandfold greater calamity than the seeing of the devil would be; and having been saved from his greatest malice, you should not over-fear the less.

**Direction 5. Remember what an injury it is to God, and to Christ that conquered him, to fear the devil, while God is your protector (any otherwise than as the instrument of God's displeasure): it seems as much as to say, I fear that the devil is too hard for God; or that God cannot deliver me from him.**

**Direction 6. Remember how you honor the devil by fearing him, and please him by thus honoring him.**

And will you not abhor to honor and please such an enemy of God and you? This is what he would have; to be feared instead of God: he glories in it as part of his dominion: as tyrants rejoice to see men fear them, as those that can destroy them when they will, so the devil triumphs in your fears as his honor. When God rebukes the idolatry of the Israelites, it is as they feared their idols of wood and stone. To fear them showed that they took them for their gods (2 Kings 17:38-39; Daniel 6:26).

**Direction 7. Consider that it is a folly to be inordinately fearful of that which never did befall you, and never befalls one of many hundred thousand men: I mean any terrible appearance of the devil.**

You never saw him; nor hear credibly of any but very few in an age that see him (besides witches). This fear therefore is irrational, the danger being utterly improbable.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** "Tit." is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," his name for each of the ten main divisions of Chapter 7 ("Directions for the Government of the Passions"). This file begins Title 8, "against sinful fear"; the Title's heading line is the first line of this file's range (source line 35927).
- **Labels:** Baxter numbers the Directions with Roman numerals; we give them in Arabic numerals. The numbering restarts at 1 under each sub-heading ("Directions against sinful Fear of God," "Directions against sinful Fear of the Devil"), as in the source. Following the project's format, each Direction's first sentence is set as the bold line and the rest forms the body; Directions 3 and 4 (fear of God) and 2 and 5 (fear of the devil) are single sentences and have no separate body. This file ends with Direction 7 against fear of the devil; Direction 8 continues in the next file.
- **Cross-reference:** "chap. iii. direct. xii." is rendered "chapter 3, direction 12," Baxter's cross-reference to his earlier treatment of fear. We have not checked it against the modernized Chapter 3 files.
- **Passions, melancholy:** kept as Baxter's words (see GLOSSARY.md).
- **Security** (Direction 12, fear of God): kept as Baxter's technical term for a careless, false confidence about one's spiritual state.
- **Psalm 9:10 (NET flag):** Baxter quotes the King James "They that know thy name will put their trust in thee," and his Direction 1 rests on the word "know" ("Know God in his goodness..."). The NET reads "Your loyal followers trust in you, for you, Lord, do not abandon those who seek your help," with no mention of knowing God's name. Since the word he leans on is not in the NET, we keep Baxter's wording (modernized: thy and thee to your and you) and flag it here.
- **Ephesians 3:12:** Baxter's "access with boldness and confidence" (King James) becomes the NET "boldness and confident access." The sense he uses is kept.
- **Hebrews 10:19-22 (NET flag, minor):** Baxter quotes from "boldness to enter into the holiest" to "full assurance of faith." NET wording is used over the same extent, so his "We have" now leads into the NET "confidence to enter the sanctuary," and "And having an High Priest" becomes the NET "and since we have a great priest." The King James "boldness," which ties this verse to Baxter's point against fear and to Ephesians 3:12 just before it, is "confidence" in the NET; the sense is close. NET "the blood of Jesus" for King James "his blood"; "fresh and living way" for "new and living way"; "curtain" for "veil"; "in the assurance that faith brings" for "in full assurance of faith."
- **Matthew 14:27:** NET "Have courage! It is I. Do not be afraid" for King James "Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid." The NET's "Have courage" fits Baxter's point against fear as well as or better than the King James.
- **1 John 4:18 (NET flag):** not set in quotation marks by Baxter. "There is no fear in love" is also the NET wording. But Baxter's "love expels tormenting fear" leans on the King James "fear hath torment"; the NET has "fear has to do with punishment," and "perfect love drives out fear." Baxter's own wording is kept.
- **Judges 13:22-23 (NET flag):** the King James has Manoah say "we have seen God," which is why Baxter uses the story in a Direction against sinful fear of God. The NET reads "we have seen a supernatural being" (the Hebrew word can mean God or a divine being). The point of the story still stands, since the wife answers about "the Lord," but the direct statement that Manoah feared because he had seen God is softened. The NET has an exclamation mark after "being"; we drop it to keep Baxter's semicolon. The wife's words follow the NET over the extent Baxter quotes ("He would not have shown us all these things"); the NET goes on "or have spoken to us like this just now," which Baxter does not quote. NET "accepted the burnt offering and the grain offering" for King James "received a burnt offering and a meat offering"; Baxter had "an offering."
- **Hebrews 11:6:** cited, not quoted. "Lover and rewarder" is Baxter's phrase; the NET says God "rewards those who seek him."
- **Hebrews 2:14-15 (adapted quotation):** Baxter adapts the King James "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil; and deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage," changing "might destroy" to "destroyed" so that it states Christ's victory as done. We keep his verb "destroyed" and his word order ("destroyed through death") and use the NET wording for the rest ("the one who holds the power of death (that is, the devil), and set free those who were held in slavery all their lives by their fear of death"). The NET itself reads "so that through death he could destroy the one who holds the power of death."
- **2 Kings 17:38-39; Daniel 6:26 (NET flag):** cited, not quoted, for the point that Israel "feared" their idols. The King James has "neither shall ye fear other gods" (17:38) and "the Lord your God ye shall fear" (17:39); the NET has "you must not worship other gods" and "you must worship the Lord your God." So in the NET the 2 Kings passage no longer uses the word "fear" that Baxter's argument rests on. Daniel 6:26 keeps it in the NET ("people are to revere and fear the God of Daniel").
- **Footnote 343:** references converted to modern form. Direction 9 draws on these passages: God reconciling the world to himself and sending ambassadors (2 Corinthians 5:19-20), "all things are ready" (Luke 14:17; NET "everything is now ready"; compare Matthew 22:8, NET "The wedding is ready"). "Compel them to come in" is the King James of Luke 14:23 (NET "urge people to come in"; the NET's "urge" is milder than the King James "compel"), kept in Baxter's wording since he does not set it in quotation marks; that reference is not in Baxter's text. Baxter ends the question "Or does he not..." with a period; kept.
- **Unmarked allusions:** "the fear of him is the beginning of wisdom" (opening paragraph) echoes Proverbs 9:10 (NET "The beginning of wisdom is to fear the Lord") and Psalm 111:10 (NET "To obey the Lord is the fundamental principle for wise living"); references not in Baxter's text. "Cause all things to work together for their good" echoes Romans 8:28. "Prepare the way" (Direction 9) echoes Isaiah 40:3. "The ship with Christ" (Direction 10) alludes to the Gospel stories of Christ in the boat with his disciples. "The prince of this world is conquered and cast out" (Direction 3, fear of the devil) echoes John 12:31 (NET "now the ruler of this world will be driven out"). None of these references is in Baxter's text.
- **Job 1** (Direction 1, fear of the devil): Baxter's "Job i." is a chapter reference, rendered "(Job 1)." "An ass" becomes "a donkey" (the animal).
- **Witches** (Direction 7, fear of the devil): kept as Baxter wrote it; he assumes, as most of his contemporaries did, that witches see the devil.
- **lest** (opening, section 3; Direction 5 against fear of the devil; Direction 12 against fear of God): Baxter's "fear lest he will..." is rendered "fear that he will...," "lest there be" as "that there is," "lest the devil be too hard for God" as "that the devil is too hard for God," and "take heed lest under this pretence you cast away" as "be careful that under this pretext you do not cast away." "Too hard for" (too strong for) is kept.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): somewhat (something); proceedeth (proceeds); wilfulness (willfulness); ascribeth (ascribes); hindereth (hinders); tendeth (tends); expelleth (expels); use you kindly (treat you kindly); hath (has); labour (labor); still feeding (always feeding; "still" meant continually); must needs be (must be); endeavours (efforts); frighting (frightening); would fain be (would gladly be); doth he not (does he not); unto God (to God); thou wrongest, keepest (you wrong, keep); fly from it (flee from it); discourageth (discourages); meat (food; "meat" meant food in general); take heed (be careful); pretence (pretext); belongeth (belongs); require (requires, agreeing with "truth"); beck (command, as in "beck and call"); thou art, thou hast (you are, you have); wilt thou (will you); free-men (freemen); seemeth (seems); pleasure him (please him); this is it that (this is what); glorieth (glories); triumpheth (triumphs); honour (honor); reprehendeth (rebukes); befalleth (befalls); sawest, hearest (saw, hear); but very few (any but very few; "any" is added for modern sense, since Baxter's "but" means "only").
- **Kept words:** "the cause that is before you" (Direction 1: the matter or case in hand); "tenor" (Direction 3: the general sense or drift of the gospel); "freeness" (Direction 3: the free, unearned character of the covenant of grace); "inordinate" (out of due order or measure); "title to the promises" and "special interest in Christ" (Direction 6: a rightful claim to the promises and a personal share in Christ); "stead" (Direction 9: place, as in "in his name and stead"); "remediless" (Direction 11: without remedy); "over-fear" (Direction 4: fear too much); "abhor" (Direction 6).
- **Grammar:** Direction 7 (fear of God), "And so to think of God as evil, and fear him upon such misapprehensions," is a fragment continuing the Direction's first sentence; kept as Baxter wrote it. Likewise "As when Manoah said..." (Direction 5). In Direction 12, "a senseless presumption and contempt of God, are a sin" loses the comma between subject and verb.
