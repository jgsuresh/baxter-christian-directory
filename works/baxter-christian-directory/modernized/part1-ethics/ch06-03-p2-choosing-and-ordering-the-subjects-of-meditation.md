# Chapter 6: Title 3, Directions 12-14, and the first Directions about the work itself

**Direction 12. Lay yourselves under the urgency of necessity, and the power of those motives which should most effectually engage your thoughts.**

In the aforementioned instance, what is it that makes a wicked preacher that he can study divine things orderly from year to year, but that he is continually under the power of his carnal motives, profit and honor, and some delight? And if you will put yourselves habitually and at set times also under the sense and power of your far greater motives, as always perceiving how much it does concern you, for yourselves, and others, and the honor of God; this would be a constant weight and spring, which being duly wound up would keep the wheels in equal motion.

**Direction 13. Thus you must make the service of your Master, and the saving of yourselves and others, your business in the world, which you follow daily as your ordinary calling, and then it will carry on your thoughts.**

Whereas he that serves God only on the side, with some occasional service, will think of him or his work only on the side, with some occasional thoughts. A close and diligent course of holy living is the best help to keep a constant, profitable course of holy thinking.

**Direction 14. The chief point of skill and holy wisdom, for this and other religious duties, is to take that course which tends to make religion pleasant, and to draw your souls to delight in God, and to beware of that which would make all burdensome to you.**

It will be easy and sweet to think of that which you take pleasure in. But if Satan can make all irksome and unpleasant to you, your thoughts will avoid it as you do a carcass when you hold your nose and hurry away. The psalmist says, "My meditation of him shall be sweet; I will be glad in the Lord" (Psalm 104:34).

**Directions about the work itself.**

**Direction 1. As you must never be unprovided with holy store, so you must prudently make choice of your particular subject.**

As the choice of a fit text is half a good sermon; so the choice of the fittest matter for you is much of a good meditation. Which requires some good acquaintance both with the truth, and with yourselves.

*In the margin: The order of subjects to be meditated on, as to their excellence.*

**Direction 2. To this end you must know in their respective degrees what subjects are in themselves most excellent to be meditated on.**

As the first and highest is the most blessed God himself, and the glorious person of our Redeemer, and the New Jerusalem or heaven of glory, where he is revealed to his saints. And then, the blessed society which there enjoys him, and the holy vision, love, and joy, by which he is enjoyed. And next is the wonderful work of man's redemption, and the covenant of grace, and the sanctifying operations of the Holy Spirit, and all the graces that make up God's image on the soul. And then is the state and privileges of the church, which is the body of Christ, for whom all this is done and prepared. And next is the work of the gospel, by which this church is gathered, edified, and saved. And then, the matter of our own salvation, and our state of grace, and way to life. And then, the salvation of others. And then, the common, public good, in temporal respects. And then, our personal, bodily welfare. And next, the bodily welfare of our neighbors. And lastly, those things that only remotely tend to these. This is the order of desirableness and worth, which will tell you what should have precedence in esteem in your thoughts and prayers.

**Direction 3. You must also know what subject is then most timely for your thoughts, and refuse even an untimely good.**

For good may be used by untimeliness to do hurt. It may be thrust in by the tempter, on purpose to divert you from some greater good, or to mar some other duty in hand; so he will often put in some good meditation to turn you from a better, or in the midst of sermon or prayer: or if he sees you out of sorts to perform a duty of meditation, or that you have no leisure without neglecting your more proper work, he will then drive you on, that by the outcome he may discourage and hurt you, and make the duty unprofitable and burdensome to you, and make you more averse to it afterwards. Untimely duty may be no duty, but a sin, which is covered with the material good. As the Pharisees' Sabbath rest was, when mercy called them to violate it.

**Direction 4. Examine well, and decide on the end and use of your meditations, before you set about them, and then labor to fit them to that particular end.**

The end is first in the intention, and from the love of it the means are chosen and used. If it is knowledge that you are to increase, it is evidence of truth, with the matter to be known, in a convincing, scientific way, that you must meditate on. If it is divine belief that is to be increased or exercised, it is divine revelations, both matter, and evidence of credibility, which you have to meditate on. If you would stir up the fear of God, you have his greatness, and dreadfulness, his justice, and threats to meditate on. If you would stir up the love of God, you have his goodness, mercy, Christ, and promises to meditate on. If you would prepare for death and judgment, you have your hearts to test, your lives to repent of, your graces to discover, and revive, and exercise, and your soul's diseases to feel, and the remedies to apply: so whenever you mean to make anything of a set meditation, decide first on the end, and by it on the means.

**Direction 5. Clear up the truth of things to your minds as you can, before you take much pains to work them on your affections, lest you find afterward that you only misinformed yourselves, and spend all your labor in vain, to make deluding images on your minds, and bring your affections to bow before them.**

As many have done by embracing errors, who have laid out their zeal upon them many years on end, and made them the reason of hatred, and contention, and bitter condemnations of opposing brethren; and have made parties, and divisions, and disturbances in the church for them, and after so many years' zealous sinning, have found them to be only like Michal's image, a man of straw instead of David; and that they made all this filthy fuss only in a dream.

**Direction 6. Next labor to perceive the weight of everything you think about, be it good or evil: and to that end be sure that God and eternity are taken in, in every meditation, and all things judged as they stand related to God, and to your eternal state; which alone can give you the true estimate and sense of good and evil: there will always be the life, and soul, and power lacking in your most excellent meditations, except so far as God is in them, and they are divine.**

When you meditate on any Scripture truth, think of it as a beam from the Eternal Light, written by the Holy Spirit, to lead men by obedience to happiness. Look on it with reverence, as a letter or message sent from heaven, and as a thing of grand importance to your souls. When you meditate on any grace, think of it as a part of the image of God, implanted and activated by the Holy Spirit, to advance the soul into communion with God, and prepare it for him. When you meditate on any duty, remember who commands it, and whom you are chiefly to regard in your obedience; and what will be the end of obeying or disobeying. When you meditate on any sin, remember that it is the defacing or privation of God's image, and the rebel that rises up against him in all his attributes, to depose him from the government of the soul and of the world; and foresee the end to which it tends. Take in God, if you would feel life and power in all that you meditate on.

**Direction 7. Let your ordinary meditations be on the great and necessary things; and think less frequently about the less necessary matters.**

Meditation is only a means to a further end: it is to work some good upon the soul: use therefore those subjects which are most powerful and fit to work it. Great truths will do great works upon the heart. They are usually the surest and most beyond controversy and doubt. There is more weight, and substance, and power in one article of the creed, or one petition of the Lord's Prayer, or one commandment of the Decalogue, to benefit the soul, than in abundance of the disputed opinions which men have troubled themselves and others with in all ages; as one purse of gold will buy more than a great quantity of farthings. Meditating on great and weighty truths makes great and weighty Christians. And meditating inordinately on light and disputed opinions makes light, opinionated, contentious professing Christians. Little things may have their time and place, but it must be only a little time and the last place; except when God makes any little thing to be the matter of our lawful calling and employment (as all the common matters of the world are little); and then they may have a larger proportion of our time, though still they must have the lowest place in our estimation and in our hearts.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Psalm 104:34 (departure from the NET):** Baxter quotes the King James, "My meditation of him shall be sweet; I will be glad in the Lord," to close a Direction about making meditation pleasant: his point rests on "meditation" being "sweet" to the one who meditates. The NET reads "May my thoughts be pleasing to him. I will rejoice in the Lord," a wish that the psalmist's thoughts please God. The word "meditation" and the idea of its sweetness to the believer are both gone, and the direction of the pleasure is reversed, so the NET wording would not carry Baxter's point. As with Psalm 10:4 in `ch01-part2-12-...`, we kept Baxter's (King James) quotation in the text, which needs no modernizing, and flag it here. If you want the NET text used everywhere, say so. Baxter's reference stood before the quotation ("Psal. civ. 34, saith the psalmist"); we moved the reference into parentheses after it, per the project's convention, and put "The psalmist says" first.
- **Title:** "Title" is Baxter's word for a heading or division of the chapter ("_Tit._" in the source). This file sits within Title 3 and runs on into its second series of numbered Directions, which Baxter heads "Directions about the work itself" and numbers from 1 again. In the source the opening sentence of each Direction runs on into its paragraph; we set that sentence in bold as the Direction heading on its own line, as the finished files do, without changing any sentence.
- **the aforementioned instance** (Direction 12): Baxter's "aforesaid instance," referring back to the example of the preacher in the preceding Direction (in the previous file; not checked here).
- **continually:** Baxter's "still" (in its older sense, always). **at set times:** his "statedly." **weight:** his "poise," the weight that drives a clock, paired with the spring; not "composure."
- **on the side:** Baxter's "on the by" (incidentally, as a side matter). **beware of:** his "take heed of." **burdensome:** his "grievous" (Directions 14 and 3). **carcass ... hold your nose and hurry away:** his "carrion ... stop your nose and haste away."
- **unprovided with holy store** (Direction 1): his "unfurnished of holy store," that is, without a stock of holy matter to think on.
- **respective degrees** (Direction 2): his "several degrees." **precedence in esteem:** his "estimative precedency," that is, which should rank first in how highly you value it.
- **timely / untimely / untimeliness** (Direction 3): his "seasonable / unseasonable / unseasonableness." **out of sorts:** his "out of temper" (not in the right frame of mind), not "angry." **outcome:** his "issue." **material good:** kept; Baxter's scholastic term for the good in the outward matter of an act (here, the outward act of meditation) as distinct from whether the act is right in its circumstances. **the Pharisees' Sabbath rest ... when mercy called them to violate it:** an unmarked allusion to the Gospel Sabbath disputes (for example Matthew 12:1-14; Luke 13:10-17), not cited by Baxter.
- **decide on** (Direction 4): his "determine of." **set about:** his "set upon." **particular:** his "special." **scientific:** his "scientifical," meaning by rigorous, demonstrative reasoning, not the modern laboratory sense. **stir up:** his "excite." **dreadfulness:** his "terribleness." **threats:** his "threatenings" (God's threatened judgments). **test:** his "try."
- **affections** (Direction 5): kept; emotions and desires (see GLOSSARY.md). **embracing:** his "espousing." **many years on end:** his "many years together." **condemnations:** his "censurings." **brethren:** kept (fellow believers). **fuss:** his "pudder" (a variant of "pother," a commotion). **Michal's image, a man of straw instead of David:** an unmarked allusion to 1 Samuel 19:13-16, where Michal put an idol (King James "image") in David's bed to deceive Saul's messengers; "a man of straw" is Baxter's own phrase.
- **written** (Direction 6): his "indited" (composed, dictated). **happiness:** his "felicity" (per CONVENTIONS.md). **Look on:** his "Behold." **activated:** his "actuated" (made active). **regard:** his "respect" (have regard to). **privation:** kept; the technical term for the absence of a good that ought to be present. **meditate on:** his "meditate of." **except so far as God is in them:** his "further than God is in them"; we also put "there will always be ... lacking" for his inverted "there will still ... be wanting."
- **disputed:** his "controverted" (Direction 7). **beyond controversy:** his "past controversy." **farthings:** kept; the farthing was the smallest English coin of Baxter's day (a quarter of a penny). **opinionated:** his "opinionative." **professing Christians:** his "professors" (people who profess religion, not teachers); one word added for sense. **the Decalogue:** the Ten Commandments.
- **Holy Spirit:** Baxter's "Holy Ghost" throughout, per CONVENTIONS.md.
