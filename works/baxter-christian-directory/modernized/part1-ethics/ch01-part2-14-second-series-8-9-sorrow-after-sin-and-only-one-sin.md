# Chapter 1, Part 2: Second Series, Temptations 8–9

**Temptation 8 (second series).** But, says the tempter, it is not unpardoned sin, because you are sorry and repent of it when you have committed it; and all sin is pardoned that is repented of.

**Direction 8.** All the aforesaid causes which may make some resistance of sin in the ungodly may cause also some sorrow and repenting in them. There is repenting and sorrow for sin in hell. All men repent and are sorry at last; but few repent so as to be pardoned and saved. When a sinner has had all the sweetness out of sin that it can yield him, and sees that it is all gone, and the sting is left behind, it is no wonder if he repents. I think there is scarcely any drunkard, or whoremonger, or glutton (that is not a flat unbeliever) but he repents of the sin that is past, because he has had all out of it that it can yield him, and there is nothing left of it that is attractive: but yet he keeps on, which shows that his repentance was unsound. True repentance is a thorough change of the heart and life; a turning from sin to a holy life, and such a sorrow for what is past as would not let you do it if it were to be done again. If you truly repent, you would not do so again if you had all the same temptations.

**Temptation 9 (second series).** But, says the tempter, it is but one sin, and the rest of your life is good and blameless; and God judges by the greater part of your life, whether the evil or the good is greater.

**Direction 9.** If a man is a murderer, or a traitor, will you excuse him because the rest of his life is good, and it is but one sin that he is charged with? One sort of poison may kill a man; and one stab at the heart, though all his body else is whole: you may make yourself sick on one dish: one leak may sink a ship. "The one who obeys the whole law but fails in one point has become guilty of all of it" (James 2:10). See Ezekiel 18:10–11. Indeed God does judge by the bent of your heart, and the main drift and effort of your life. But can you say that the bent of your heart, and the main effort of your life, is for God, and heaven, and holiness? No: if it were, you would be regenerate; and this would not let you live in any one beloved, chosen, willful sin. The bent of a man's heart and life may be sinful, earthly, fleshly, though it runs but in the channel of one way of gross sinning: as a man may be greedy that has but one trade; and a whoremonger that has but one whore; and an idolater that has but one idol. If you loved God better, you would let go your sin; and if you love any one sin better than God, the whole bent of your heart and life is wicked: for it is not set upon God and heaven, and therefore is ungodly.

## Baxter's notes

None in these two pairs.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **James 2:10:** Baxter's "Whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, is guilty of all" is "the one who obeys the whole law but fails in one point has become guilty of all of it" in the NET.
- **Ezekiel 18:10–11** (Direction 9): cited only. In the NET, these verses begin the description of a violent son who does evil things; Baxter cites them for the principle that one kind of grave sin is enough to condemn.
- **aforesaid causes** (Direction 8): Baxter's "foresaid causes," the six causes of resistance to sin listed in the previous Direction (Direction 7 of the second series).
- **"There is repenting and sorrow for sin in hell"** (Direction 8): Baxter means that regret, even deep regret, is not the same as true repentance, since the lost feel it too.
- **no wonder:** Baxter's "no marvel." **unbeliever:** his "infidel." **attractive:** his "lovely." **keeps on:** his "goeth on still."
- **the sting is left behind** (Direction 8): the pleasure of sin is over, but its pain remains.
- **whoremonger:** kept as Baxter's word. **a flat unbeliever:** one who does not believe in God or judgment at all.
- **make yourself sick on one dish:** Baxter's "surfeit on one dish." **greedy:** his "covetous." **effort:** his "endeavour."
- **bent of your heart** (Direction 9): left as written, meaning the direction or main inclination of the heart. **drift:** the general direction.
- **a man may be greedy that has but one trade …** (Direction 9): Baxter's point is that a person can be wholly given over to a vice that shows up in only one form.
- **the rest of thy life is good and blameless** (Temptation 9): Baxter's tempter argues that God weighs a whole life, with a majority of good outweighing one bad. Baxter answers by looking at the direction of the heart instead of a count.
