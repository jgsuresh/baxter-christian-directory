# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 20 (the last)

**Direction 20. Lastly, be sure that you renounce all notion of self-sufficiency or merit in anything you do, and wholly rely on the Lord Jesus Christ, as your Head, and Life, and Savior, and Intercessor with the Father.**

Remember Christ's words, "apart from me you can accomplish nothing" (John 15:5). Nor can anything you do be acceptable to God any other way than in him, the beloved Son, in whom he is well pleased. As your persons would never have been accepted but in him, no more can any of your services. All your repentings, if you had wept out your eyes for sin, would not have satisfied the justice of God, nor procured you pardon and justification, without the satisfaction and merit of Christ. If he had not first taken away the sins of the world, and reconciled them so far to God as to procure and offer them the pardon and salvation contained in his covenant, there would have been no place for your repentance, nor faith, nor prayers, nor efforts, as to any hope of your salvation. Your believing would not have saved you, nor indeed had any justifying object, if he had not purchased for you the promise and gift of pardon and salvation to all believers.

*Objection.* But perhaps you will say that if we had loved God without a Savior, we would have been saved; for God cannot hate and damn those that love him. To which I answer: You could not have loved God as God without a Savior: to have loved him as the giver of your worldly prosperity, with a love subordinate to the love of sin and your carnal selves, and to love him as one that you imagine so unholy and unjust as to give you leave to sin against him, and prefer every empty thing to him, this is not to love God, but to love an image of your own fantasy; nor will it at all procure your salvation. But to love him as your God and happiness, with a superlative love, you could never have done without a Savior. For, 1. Objectively; God not being your reconciled father, but your enemy, engaged in justice to damn you forever, you could not love him as thus related to you, because he could not seem lovable to you; and therefore the damned hate him as their destroyer, as the thief or murderer hates the judge. 2. And as to the efficiency; your blinded minds and depraved wills could never have been restored so far to their uprightness as to have loved God as God, without the teaching of Christ, and the renewing, sanctifying work of his Spirit. And without a Savior, you could never have expected this gift of the Holy Spirit. So your supposition itself is groundless.

Indeed conversion is your implanting into Christ, and your uniting to him, and marriage with him, that he may be your life, and help, and hope. Christ says, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me" (John 14:6). "God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. The one who has the Son has this eternal life; the one who does not have the Son of God does not have this eternal life" (1 John 5:11–12). "He is the Vine, and we are the branches: as the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, so neither can we unless we abide in him: he that does not abide in Christ is cast forth as a branch, and withered, to be burned" (John 15:4–6). All your life and help is in him, and from him: without Christ, you cannot believe in the Father as in one that will show you any saving mercy, but only as the devils, who believe him just, and tremble at his justice. Without Christ, you cannot love God, nor have any vivid awareness of his love. Without Christ, you can have no hope of heaven, and therefore no efforts for it. Without him, you cannot come near to God in prayer, as having no confidence, because no admittance, acceptance, or hope. Without him, how terrible are the thoughts of death! which in him we may see as a conquered thing: and when we remember that he was dead, and is now alive, and the Lord of life, and has the keys of death and hell, with what boldness may we lay down this flesh, and suffer death to undress our souls! It is only in Christ that we can think with comfort of the world to come; when we remember that he must be our Judge, and that in our nature, glorified, he is now in the highest, Lord of all; and that he is "preparing a place for us, and will come again to take us to himself, that where he is, there we may be also" (John 14:3). Alas! without Christ, we do not know how to live an hour; nor can we have hope or peace in anything we have or do; nor look with comfort either upward or downward, to God or the creature; nor think without terror of our sins, of God, or of the life to come. Resolve, therefore, that as true converts you are wholly to live on Jesus Christ, and to do all that you do by his Spirit and strength; and to expect all your acceptance with God on his account. When other men are regarded as philosophers, or wise, for some unsatisfying knowledge of these transitory things, do you desire to know nothing but a crucified and glorified Christ: study him, and take him (objectively) for your wisdom. When other men have confidence in the flesh, and in their show of wisdom, in will-worship, and humility, after the commandments and doctrines of men (Colossians 2:20–23), and would establish their own righteousness, do you rejoice in Christ your righteousness; and set continually before your eyes his teaching and example as your rule: look always to Jesus, the "pioneer and perfecter" of your faith, who despised all the glory of the world, and trampled upon its emptiness, and subjected himself to a life of suffering, and made himself of no reputation, but "for the joy set out for him he endured the cross, disregarding its shame" (Hebrews 12:2), and bore the opposition of sinners against himself. Live so that you may truly say, as Paul, "I have been crucified with Christ, and it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me. So the life I now live in the body, I live because of the faithfulness of the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me" (Galatians 2:20).

Having given you these directions, I most earnestly beg you to study and practice them, that my labor may not rise up as a witness against you, which I intend for your conversion and salvation. Think on it, whether this is an unreasonable course, or an unpleasant life, or a thing unnecessary? and what is reasonable, necessary, and pleasant, if this is not?

And if you meet with any of those distracted sinners who would deride you away from Christ and your salvation, and say, this is the way to make men mad, [38] or, this is more ado than needs; I will not stand here to demonstrate their brutishness and wickedness, having done it at length already in my books called *A Saint or a Brute* and *Now or Never*, and in the third part of *The Saints' Rest*: but only I ask you, as a full safeguard against all the idle talk of the enemies of a holy, heavenly life, to take good notice of these three things.

1. Note well the language of the holy Scriptures, and see whether it does not speak contrary to these men; and consider whether God or they are wiser, and whether God or they must be your judge?

2. Note whether these men do not change their minds, [39] and turn their tongues when they come to die? Or think whether they will not change their minds when death has sent them into that world where there are none of these deceits? And think whether you should be moved by that man's words who will shortly change his mind himself, and wish he had never spoken such words?

3. Observe well whether their own profession does not condemn them; and whether the very thing that they hate the godly for is not that they are serious in practicing that which these hostile people themselves profess as their religion? And are they not then notorious hypocrites, [40] to profess to believe in God, and yet scorn those that "seek him" (Hebrews 11:6); to profess faith in Christ, and hate those that obey him? to profess to believe in the Holy Spirit as the sanctifier, and yet hate and scorn his sanctifying work? to profess to believe the day of judgment, and everlasting torment of the ungodly, and yet to deride those that endeavor to escape it? to profess to believe that heaven is prepared for the godly, and yet to scorn those that make it the chief business of their lives to attain it? to profess to take the holy Scripture for God's word and law, and yet to scorn those that obey it? to pray after each of the ten commandments, "Lord, have mercy upon us, and incline our hearts to keep this law," and yet to hate all those that desire and endeavor to keep them? What impudent hypocrisy is joined with this malignity! Note whether the greatest diligence of the most godly is not justified by the formal profession of those very men that hate and scorn them? The difference between them is that the godly profess Christianity in earnest, and when they say what they believe, they believe as they say; but the ungodly customarily, and for company, take on them to be Christians when they are not, and by their own mouths condemn themselves, and hate and oppose the serious practice of that which they say they themselves believe.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source, except that Baxter's English is modernized.

[39] As the Athenians, who condemned Socrates to death, and then lamented it, and erected a bronze statue for his memorial.

[40] Acosta says that he that will be a pastor to the Indians must not only resist the devil and the flesh, but must resist the custom of men which has grown powerful by time and multitude: and must oppose his breast to receive the darts of the envious and malevolent, who, if they see anything contrary to their profane fashion, cry out, A traitor! a hypocrite! an enemy! ## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **John 15:5:** Baxter's quotation, "without him ye can do nothing," is the King James with "him" for "me." The NET reads "apart from me you can accomplish nothing," so we present it as Christ's own words.
- **John 14:6:** NET wording, in Christ's first person ("I am the way…"). Baxter writes it in the third person ("He is the way…"), adapting the verse to his sentence.
- **John 15:4–6** and **John 14:3:** Baxter's quotations "He is the Vine, and we are the branches…" and "preparing a place for us, and will come again to take us to himself…" are his paraphrases of Christ's words, turned into the third person. We kept his wording, modernized. The NET reads "Just as the branch cannot bear fruit by itself, unless it remains in the vine, so neither can you unless you remain in me" (15:4) and "I will come again and take you to be with me, so that where I am you may be too" (14:3).
- **1 John 5:11–12:** NET wording, as in Direction 9.
- **Hebrews 12:2–3:** Baxter's "for the joy that was set before him, endured the cross, despising the shame" appears in the NET as "For the joy set out for him he endured the cross, disregarding its shame." The NET's "pioneer and perfecter of our faith" replaces the King James "author and finisher of our faith"; Baxter writes "author and finisher." The phrase "the contradiction of sinners against himself" (Hebrews 12:3) is rendered as "the opposition of sinners against himself" and is unquoted.
- **Galatians 2:20 (important):** the King James reads "the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God," which Baxter takes as a believer's faith in Christ. The NET reads "I live because of the faithfulness of the Son of God." This is a different (and debated) reading of the Greek. Baxter's emphasis here is on living by faith, so the NET changes the sense he is using. Flagged; this is a place where another translation may serve Baxter better.
- **Hebrews 11:6:** Baxter's "those that diligently seek him" is "those who seek him" in the NET, without "diligently." Baxter's argument is about the diligent being scorned, so this loses a word he leans on. Flagged.
- **Colossians 2:20–23** (cited only): the passage about human rules and "will-worship." **will-worship:** worship invented by human will rather than commanded by God. Kept as Baxter's word.
- **"keys of death and hell"** (paragraph 4): an unmarked allusion to Revelation 1:18, kept in Baxter's wording.
- **"Lord, have mercy upon us, and incline our hearts to keep this law"** (item 3): the response in the Church of England's *Book of Common Prayer* after each of the Ten Commandments. Baxter's point is that the people who say it hate those who try to keep the commandments.
- **notion:** Baxter's "conceit." **lovable:** his "amiable." **efforts:** his "endeavours." **vivid awareness:** his "lively apprehensions."
- **Objectively** and **efficiency** (the Objection paragraph): scholastic terms. "Objectively" means as an object of love or knowledge; "efficiency" means causal power or ability. Kept as Baxter wrote them.
- **will-worship; philosophers; the "Indians"; "papists and barbarians"** (note 40): all as Baxter wrote. His point in note 40 is that the good are mocked by the worldly everywhere, in "Roman" and "pagan" lands as in England; this is his language, kept as written.
- **hostile people** (item 3): Baxter's "malignants," a term often used in his day for those opposed to the godly. **idle talk:** his "pratings." **safeguard:** his "defensative."
- **ten commandments, third part of *The Saints' Rest*:** Baxter's own references. *A Saint or a Brute* and *Now or Never* are his books.
- **This is the last Direction of Chapter 1, Part 1.** Footnote [41] and the next section, Chapter 1, Part 2, follow.
