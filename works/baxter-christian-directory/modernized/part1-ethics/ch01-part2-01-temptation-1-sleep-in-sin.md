# Chapter 1, Part 2

**The Temptations by Which the Devil Hinders Men's Conversion; with the Proper Remedies Against Them**

The most holy and righteous Governor of the world has so restrained Satan and all our enemies, and so far given us free will, that no man can be forced to sin against his will; it is not sin if it is not (positively or privatively) voluntary. All our enemies in hell or on earth cannot make us miserable without ourselves; nor keep a sinner from true conversion and salvation if he does not do it himself; no, nor compel him to one sinful thought, or word, or deed, or omission, but by tempting and enticing him to be willing: all that are graceless are willfully graceless. None go to hell but those that choose the way to hell and would not be persuaded out of it: none miss heaven but those that thought so lightly of it as to prefer the world and sin to it, and refused the holy way that leads to it. And surely man, who naturally loves himself, would never take so mad a course if his reason were not laid asleep, and his understanding were not woefully deluded: and this is the business of the tempter, who does not drag men to sin by violence, but draws and entices them by temptations. I shall therefore take it for the next part of my work to lay open these temptations, and tell you the remedies.

**Temptation 1.** The first effort of the tempter is, in general, to keep the sinner asleep in sin: so that he shall be as a dead man who has no use of any of his faculties; who has eyes and does not see, and ears but does not hear, and a heart that does not understand, nor feel anything that concerns his peace. The light that shines upon a man asleep is of no use to him; his work lies undone; his friends, and wealth, and greatest concerns are all forgotten by him, as if there were no such things or persons in the world: you may say what you will against him, or do what you will against him, and he can do nothing in his own defense. This is the case that the devil most labors to keep the world in; even in so dead a sleep that their reason and their wills, their fear and hope, and all their powers shall be of no use to them: so that when they hear a preacher, or read the Scripture or good books, or see the holy examples of the godly, indeed, when they see the grave, and know where they must shortly lie, and know that their souls must stay here but a little while; yet they shall hear, and see, and know all this as men asleep, who do not mind it, as if it did not concern them at all; never once soberly considering and laying it to heart. [41]

**Direction 1. For the remedy against this deadly sin: 1. Beware of sleeping opinions, or doctrines and notions which tend to the lethargy of security. 2. Do not sit still, but be up and doing: stirring tends to shake off drowsiness. 3. Come into the light: live under an awakening minister and in waking company that will not sleep with you, nor easily let you sleep: agree with them to deal faithfully with you, and promise them to take it thankfully. 4. And meditate often on waking considerations.**

Think whether a sleepy soul suits one in your dangerous condition. Can you sleep with such a load of sin upon your soul? Can you sleep under the thundering threats of God, and the curse of his law; with so many wounds in your conscience, and ulcers in your soul? If your body were sick, or in the case of Job, indeed, if you had but an aching tooth, it would not let you sleep; and is not the guilt of sin a more grievous thing? If thorns, or toads and adders, were in your bed, they would keep you waking; and how much more odious and dangerous a thing is sin! If your body lacks but food, or drink, or covering, it will break your sleep; and is it nothing for your soul to be destitute of Christ and grace? A condemned man will be easily kept awake; and if you are unregenerate, you are already condemned (John 3:18, 3, 5). You sleep in irons, in the captivity of the devil, among the walking judgments of God, in a life that is still expecting an end, in a boat that is swiftly carried to eternity, just at the entrance of another world; and that world will be hell if grace does not awaken you: you are going to see the face of God, to see the world of angels or devils, and to be accompanied by one of them forever; and is this a place or case to sleep in? Is your bed so soft? your dwelling so safe? God stands over you, man, and do you sleep? Christ is coming, and death is coming, and judgment is coming, and do you sleep? Did you never read of the foolish virgins, who slept out their time, and knocked and cried in vain when it was too late (Matthew 25:5)? You might more wisely sleep on the pinnacle of a steeple in a storm than have a soul asleep in so dangerous a case as you are in. The devil is awake, and is rocking your cradle! How busy he is to keep off ministers, or conscience, or any that would awaken you! None of your enemies are asleep; and yet will you sleep in the thick of your foes? Is the battle a sleeping time, or your race a sleeping time, when heaven or hell must be the end? While he can keep you asleep, the devil can do almost what he wants with you. He knows that you have now no use of your eyes, or understanding, or power to resist him: the most learned doctor in his sleep is as unlearned in fact as an idiot, and will dispute no better than an unlearned man: this makes many learned men ungodly; they are asleep in sin. The devil could never have made such a drudge of you, to do his work against Christ and your soul, if you had been awake. You would never have followed his whistle to the alehouse, the playhouse, the gaming house, and to other sins, if you had been in your wits, and well awake. Read Proverbs 7:23–24. I cannot believe that you long to be damned, or so hate yourself as to have done as you have done, to have lived a godless, a graceless, a prayerless, and yet a merry, careless life, if your eyes had been opened, and you had known, and feelingly known, that this was the way to hell. Nature itself will hardly go to hell awake. But it is easy to abuse a man that is asleep. You have reason; but did you ever awaken it to one hour's serious consideration of your endless state and present case? Oh dreadful judgment, to be given over to the spirit of slumber (Romans 11:8)! Is it not high time now to awake out of sleep (Romans 13:11); when the light has risen and shines about you! when others that care for their souls are busy at work! when you have slept out so much precious time already! Many a mercy, and perhaps some ministers, have been as candles burnt out to light you while you have slept. How often have you been called already! "How long, you sluggard, will you lie there? When will you rise from your sleep?" (Proverbs 6:9–10). Yet you have thundering calls and alarms to awaken you. God calls, and ministers call; mercies call, and judgments call; and yet will you not awake? "The Lord's shout is powerful; the Lord's shout is majestic. The Lord's shout breaks the cedars ... The Lord's shout shakes the wilderness"; and yet can it not awaken you? You will not sleep about far smaller matters; over food or drink, or in common talk, or at market. But O! how much greater business you have to keep you awake! You have yet an unholy soul to be renewed; and an ungodly life to be reformed; an offended God to be reconciled to; and many thousand sins to be forgiven! You have death and judgment to prepare for; you have heaven to win, and hell to escape! You have many a needful truth to learn, and many a holy duty to perform; and yet do you think it time to sleep? Paul, who had less need than you, did watch, and pray, and labor, day and night (Acts 20:31; 1 Thessalonians 3:10). O that you knew how much better it is to be awake. While you sleep, you lose the benefit of the light, and all the mercies that attend you: the sun is but as a clod to a man asleep; the world is as no world to him; the beauty of heaven and earth is nothing to him; princes, friends, and all things are forgotten by him! So does your sleep in sin make nothing of health and patience, time and help, ministers, books, and daily warnings. O what a day you have for everlasting, if you had but a heart to use it! What a price you have in your hand! Do not sleep out your day, your harvest time, your tide time (Proverbs 10:5). "For those who sleep, sleep at night" (1 Thessalonians 5:7). "Awake, O sleeper! Rise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you!" (Ephesians 5:14; Romans 13:12). "Sober up as you should, and stop sinning!" (1 Corinthians 15:34). O when you see the light of Christ, what a wonder it will fill you with at the things which you now forget! What joy it will fill you with! and with what pity for the sleepy world! But if you will insist on sleeping on, let it be known to you, sinner, it shall not be long. If you will wake no sooner, death and vengeance will awaken you. You will wake when you see the other world, and see the things which you would not believe, and contest before your dreadful Judge! Your damnation does not slumber: as Scripture says of the ungodly, "their destruction is not asleep" (2 Peter 2:3). There are no sleepy souls in heaven or hell; all are awake there: and the day that has awakened so many shall waken you. Stay alert, then, if you love your soul, lest your Lord come suddenly and find you sleeping: "What I say to you I say to everyone: Stay alert!" (Mark 13:34–37).

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **1 Corinthians 15:34 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "Awake to righteousness, and sin not." The NET reads "Sober up as you should, and stop sinning!" Baxter's whole Direction rests on the picture of waking from sleep, and the NET loses it here (the Greek verb is about coming to one's senses, as from drunkenness or sleep). Flagged as another place where the translation choice matters. The same goes for **Ephesians 5:14**, which in the NET is introduced as a quotation: "For this reason it says: 'Awake, O sleeper!'"
- **Psalm 29:4–5, 8:** Baxter quotes this without a reference ("The voice of the Lord is powerful; full of majesty; breaketh the cedars; shaketh the wilderness"). The NET reads "The Lord's shout is powerful; the Lord's shout is majestic. The Lord's shout breaks the cedars … The Lord's shout shakes the wilderness." We quote the NET for the verses he is using, with "..." for what he omits. The reference was not in Baxter's text.
- **2 Peter 2:3:** Baxter's "Thy damnation slumbereth not" is his application to the reader of a verse that in the NET is about false teachers: "their destruction is not asleep." We say so in the sentence.
- **Mark 13:34–37:** Baxter quotes "suddenly and find thee sleeping. What I say to one, I say to all, Watch." The NET reads "or else he might find you asleep when he returns suddenly. What I say to you I say to everyone: Stay alert!" We quote the second sentence from the NET.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:7:** NET wording. **Proverbs 6:9–10:** NET wording; the NET's "you sluggard" keeps Baxter's word.
- **1 Corinthians 15:35** (note 41): the source prints "xv. 35"; the verse about waking up is 15:34, which Baxter cites in the text. We kept the printed reference in the note and flag it.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "hath eyes and seeth not, and ears but heareth not" (opening of Temptation 1; compare Psalm 115:5–6, Isaiah 6:9–10); "the case of Job" (Job's sufferings); "the foolish virgins" (Matthew 25:1–13); "the spirit of slumber" (Romans 11:8).
- **positively or privatively** (opening paragraph): scholastic terms. A thing is "positive" if it is an act, and "privative" if it is the lack of one. Baxter means a sin must be voluntary whether it is something done or something left undone. Kept.
- **sleeping opinions** (Direction 1): opinions that lull a person to sleep. **security:** in the older sense of careless confidence, as in "false security." **waking:** Baxter's "wakening."
- **walking judgments of God** (Direction 1): left as written; the phrase is obscure but seems to mean that God's judgments are moving about, near at hand.
- **idiot** (Direction 1): Baxter's word for an unlearned or untrained person, not a medical term. **abuse:** to mistreat or take advantage of; left as written.
- **will insist on sleeping on:** Baxter's "wilt needs sleep on." **thought so lightly of:** his "did set so light by." **did watch, and pray:** left as written.
- **foolish virgins; harvest time; tide time:** left as in Baxter. **tide time** means the time when the tide is in, the opportunity.
- **"the learnedest doctor"** (Direction 1): rendered "the most learned doctor" (doctor here means a scholar).
- **"thou" and "you"** (Direction 1): Baxter shifts between "you" and "thou" in this Direction; we use "you" throughout.
- **Structure of Part 2:** after this opening, Baxter lists thirty numbered "Temptations," each followed by a "Direction" that is the remedy, and then a second series of ten subordinate temptations. We number them as he does and bold each Temptation and Direction label.
