# Chapter 3: Grand Direction 4, the knowledge of God (opening, with the scheme of God's attributes and the twenty acts of piety)

**Grand Direction 4. Let it be your chief study to attain a true, orderly, and practical knowledge of God, in his various attributes and relations; and to find a due impression from each of them upon your hearts, and a distinct, effectual use of them in your lives.**

Because I have written of this point more fully in another treatise, *Of the Knowledge of God, and Converse with Him*, I shall only briefly touch on it here, as not willing to repeat what is delivered there: only let me briefly remind you of these few things: 1. That the true knowledge of God is the sum of godliness, and the end of all our other knowledge, and of all that we have or do as Christians. [92] As Christ is a teacher who came from God, so he came to call and lead us to God; or else he would not have come as a Savior. It is from God that we fell by sin, and to God that we must be restored by grace. To save us is to restore us to our perfection and our happiness; and that is to restore us to God.

2. That the true knowledge of God is powerful and effective upon the heart and life: and every attribute and relation of God is to be so known as to make its proper impression on us: and the measure of this saving knowledge is not to be judged by its extent, or the number of truths about God which we know, so much as by its clearness, and intensity, and the measure of its holy effects upon the heart.

3. This is what marks both ourselves and all our duties as holy: when God's image is thus imprinted on us; and we are like him by the new birth, as children are like their father; and by his knowledge both our hearts and lives are made divine; being disposed toward God, devoted to him, and employed for him; he being our life, and light, and love.

4. This is the sum of the covenant of God with man, "I will be your God, and you shall be my people." And the other parts of the covenant, "that Christ be our Savior, and the Holy Spirit our Sanctifier," are both subordinate to this; there being now no coming to God except as reconciled in Christ our Mediator, and by the teaching and drawing of the Holy Spirit. To be our God is to be to us an absolute Owner, a most righteous Governor, and a most bountiful Benefactor or Father; as having created us, redeemed and regenerated us; and this according to his most blessed nature, properties, and perfections.

5. It is not only a loose and inconstant effect of your particular thoughts of God that is the necessary impression of his attributes (such as to fear him when you remember his greatness and justice): but it must be a habit or holy nature in you, every attribute having made its fixed image upon you; and that habit or image being in you, a constant principle of holy, spiritual actions. A habit of reverence, belief, trust, love, etc., should be, as it were, your nature.

6. Not that the knowledge of God in his perfections should provoke us to desire his properties and perfections: for to have such an aspiring desire to be gods would be the greatest pride and wickedness. But only we must desire, (1.) To be as like God, in all his communicable excellencies, as is fitting for our created state and capacity. (2.) And to have as near and full communion with him as we can attain and enjoy.

7. The will of God, and his goodness and holiness, are set before us more directly as the rule of our conformity than his power and his knowledge. Therefore his law is most immediately the expression of his will; and our duty and goodness lie in our conformity to his law; being holy as he is holy.

Because I cannot dwell on the particulars, I shall give you a brief, imperfect scheme of that of God which you must thus know.

God is to be known by us:

**I. As in Himself.**

- **I. In his BEING** (*Quod sit.*)
  - 1. One; and indivisible: in Three Persons. [a]
    - [a] 1. The FATHER, 2. The SON, 3. The HOLY SPIRIT.
  - 2. Immeasurable and incomprehensible.
  - 3. Eternal. [b]
    - [b] 1. Necessary, 2. Independent, 3. Unchangeable.
- **II. In his NATURE** (*Quid sit.*)
  - A SPIRIT:
    - 1. Simple: uncompounded.
    - 2. Without passions, incorruptible, immortal.
    - 3. Invisible, untouchable, etc.
  - and LIFE itself:
    - 1. POWER, 2. UNDERSTANDING, 3. WILL.
- **III. In his PERFECTIONS** (*Qualis sit.*)
  - 1. OMNIPOTENT, 2. OMNISCIENT, 3. MOST GOOD.
    - 1. MOST GREAT, 2. MOST WISE, 3. MOST HOLY and HAPPY. [c]
      - [c] 1. BEING HIMSELF, 2. KNOWING HIMSELF, 3. LOVING and ENJOYING HIMSELF.

**II. As related to His Creatures.**

- **I. The EFFICIENT Cause of all things:** "FROM HIM" (Romans 11:36).
  - 1. CREATOR and Conserver.
- **II. The DIRIGENT Cause:** "THROUGH HIM."
  - 2. REDEEMER and Savior.
- **III. The FINAL Cause:** "TO HIM are all things. To him be glory forever! Amen."
  - 3. REGENERATOR and Sanctifier.

In the three columns beside these (the source prints them side by side; we give each in order):

- **God as related to us:**
  - 1. Our OWNER or LORD: most absolute, free, and irresistible.
  - 2. Our RULER or KING: 1. By Legislation; 2. Judgment; 3. Execution: absolute, perfect, true, holy, just, merciful, patient, fearsome.
  - 3. Our BENEFACTOR or FATHER: 1. Most loving; 2. Most bountiful; 3. Most lovable: (patient, merciful, constant.) Causally and Objectively. (*d*)
- **What he is to us** (*d*):
  - 1. Our *Life*, and Strength, and Safety.
  - 2. Our *Light*, and Wisdom.
  - 3. Our *Love* and *Joy*: and so our *End*, and Rest, and Happiness hereafter. (*e*)
- **What we shall have in him hereafter** (*e*):
  - 1. Perfecting our natures in heavenly life.
  - 2. Whom we shall behold in glorious light.
  - 3. Whom we shall please and love; and be pleased in him, and loved by him, rejoice in him, praise him, and so enjoy him, perfectly and perpetually.

See these practically opened up and put to use in the First Part of my *Divine Life*. The fuller explanation of the attributes, fit for the more capable readers, is reserved for another treatise.

For the right use of the knowledge of all these attributes of God, I must refer you to the treatise mentioned above. The acts which you are to exercise toward God are these: 1. The clearest knowledge you can attain. [93] 2. The firmest belief. 3. The highest esteem. 4. The greatest wonder. 5. The heartiest and sweetest delight or love. 6. The strongest desire. 7. A childlike awe, reverence, and fear. 8. The boldest, most quieting trust and confidence in him. 9. The most settled waiting, dependence, hope, and expectation. 10. The most absolute self-surrender to him. 11. The fullest and quietest submission to his dealings. 12. The humblest and most absolute subjection to his governing authority and will, and the exactest obedience to his laws. 13. The boldest courage and strength in his cause, and acknowledging him before the world in the greatest sufferings. 14. The greatest thankfulness for his mercies. 15. The most faithful use of his talents, and use of his means, and performance of our trust. 16. A reverent and holy use of his name and word: with a reverence for his secrets; refraining from intruding or meddling with them. 17. A wise and cautious observance of his providences, public and private; neither neglecting them nor misinterpreting them; neither running ahead of them nor struggling discontentedly against them. 18. A discerning, loving, and honoring of his image in his children, in spite of their weaknesses and faults; without any friendship toward their faults, or over-magnifying or imitating them in any evil. 19. A reverent, serious, spiritual adoration and worship of him, in public and private, with soul and body, in the use of all his holy ordinances; but especially in the joyful celebration of his praise, for all his perfections and his mercies. 20. The highest delight and fullest contentment and comfort in God that we can attain: especially a delight in knowing him, and obeying and pleasing him, worshiping and praising him; loving him, and being loved by him, through Jesus Christ; and in the hopes of the perfecting of all these in our everlasting enjoyment of him in heavenly glory.

All these are the acts of piety toward God; which I lay together for your easier observation and memory: but some of them must be more fully opened up and dwelt on.

*In the margin: Of self-resignation to God as our Owner.*

Cæterum esse opificem immensi hujus operis, sicat et patrem omnium.--Eumque multis appellari nominibus juxta proprietates suas.--Quosdam item esse dæmones dicunt quibus insit hominum miseratio, inspectores rerum humanarum; heroas quoque solutas corporibus, sapientum animas.--Bonos aiunt esse divinos, quod in seipsis quasi habeant Deum. (English: The Stoics say that God is an immortal living being, rational, perfect and blessed, farthest removed from all evil, governing by his providence the world and everything in the world; yet the features of a human form are not in him. But the bad person is impious and without God, which is understood in two ways, either that he is called contrary to God or that he spurns God; yet this does not apply to all bad people. The wise, however, are pious and religious, all of them expert in divine law. Piety is the knowledge of divine worship. They will also offer sacrifices to the gods and will be chaste. ## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Romans 11:36:** the source reference "Rom. x. 36" is a misprint (Romans 10 has no verse 36); the words match Romans 11:36, so we use that and cite it. The scheme prints the three phrases "OF HIM," "THROUGH HIM," and "TO HIM, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen." The NET reads "from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be glory forever! Amen." NET flag: the King James has "Of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever." Baxter's "OF HIM" becomes the NET's "from him," and his "to whom be glory" becomes "To him be glory." The sense he relies on (origin, direction, and end of all things) is unchanged, so his threefold scheme of efficient, dirigent, and final cause is not affected. We use the NET wording in the scheme.
- **Unmarked quotations and allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording, no reference in his text): "I will be thy God, and thou shalt be my people" (item 4) is a covenant formula found in several places; we have not identified which one he had in mind. The NET at Jeremiah 31:33 reads "I will be their God, and they will be my people," in the third person; that is given only as a comparison and is not asserted to be his source. "That Christ be our Savior, and the Holy Spirit our Sanctifier" is Baxter's own summary, not a quotation. "A teacher that came from God" probably alludes to John 3:2 (not confirmed). "Being holy as he is holy" probably alludes to Leviticus 11:44-45 or 1 Peter 1:16 (not confirmed).
- **Baxter's other writings:** *Of the Knowledge of God, and Converse with Him* and *Divine Life* are his own titles for treatises he refers the reader to; we have not checked the cross-references.
- **The scheme:** Baxter prints the scheme as a table of brackets and braces. We have rendered it as a nested outline, keeping every item and its order. In the second part he runs three columns side by side beside the three causes; we have separated them under "God as related to us," "What he is to us," and "What we shall have in him hereafter" (headings are ours, added for clarity). Where the column alignment is ambiguous the grouping above is our best reading. The marks [a], [b], [c] and (*d*), (*e*) in the scheme look like footnote letters, but no notes with those letters appear in the source, so we have kept the marks and supplied none. Each of [a], [b], [c], (*d*), and (*e*) is printed twice in the source scheme, and we have kept both occurrences.
- **Latin labels in the scheme:** *Quod sit* (that it is), *Quid sit* (what it is), *Qualis sit* (of what sort it is): the traditional three questions about a thing's existence, essence, and qualities. "Efficient," "dirigent," and "final" cause: the cause that produces, the cause that directs, and the cause that is the end or purpose. "Causally and Objectively": as the cause of our love and as its object (our reading).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): *chiefest* to chief; *several* (attributes) to various; *improvement* (of attributes and of talents) to use; *operations* (of holy actions) to actions; *agreeable to* to fitting for; *more nearly* to more directly; *most fixed* (waiting) to most settled; *filial* to childlike; *disposals* (God's ordering of events) to dealings; *fortitude* to strength; *forbearing* to refraining; *infirmities* to weaknesses; *content* (noun) to contentment; *insisted on* to dwelt on; *beloved of him* to loved by him; *mind you of* to remind you of; *intensiveness* to intensity; *denominateth* (makes called or named) to marks as; *subservient* to subordinate; *propounded* to set before; *stated* (image) to fixed; *Holy Ghost* to Holy Spirit; *Saviour* to Savior; *immense* to immeasurable; *impassionate* to without passions (the traditional term is "impassible," not subject to suffering or emotional change); *intactible* to untouchable; *irresistable* to irresistible; *terrible* to fearsome; *amiable* (here printed "Amiable") to lovable; *estimation* to esteem; *admiration* to wonder; *complacency* to delight (his word means taking pleasure in); *awfulness* to awe; *owning him* to acknowledging him; *cautelous* to cautious; *fruition* to enjoyment; *tractate* to treatise; *capacious* to capable.
- **Kept as Baxter's terms:** *Sanctifier*, *Mediator*, *regenerated*, *communicable excellencies* (those attributes of God that creatures can share in some measure, such as goodness and holiness, as against his power or eternity), *providences* (events as God's ordering), *talents*, *ordinances* (the church's appointed means of worship).
- **Note 93:** Baxter writes "convince atheism" (Bacon's sense: refute or overcome); we render it "refute." "Lord Bacon, Essay 16" probably refers to Francis Bacon's essay "Of Atheism" (we have not confirmed the numbering or page). The Bias citation and the Scaliger reference are given as printed. "Ep. Cath." is unclear to us.
- **Note 92 misprint:** the source prints "sicat" for *sicut* (as). We keep the Latin as printed.
- **Last line of the range:** the margin note "Of self-resignation to God as our Owner" stands at the end of the range and belongs to the paragraph that begins the next section. We have placed it as printed.
