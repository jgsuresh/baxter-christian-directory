# Chapter 7: Title 3, against sinful desires and discontent (Directions 1-8)

**Title 3. Directions against sinful desires and discontent.**

I shall say but little here of this subject, because I have already treated it so fully in my book of *Self-Denial*, and in that of *Crucifying the World*; and here before in chapter 4, parts 6 and 7, against worldliness and flesh-pleasing, and here against sinful love, which is the cause. [329]

How sinful desires may be known, you may gather from the signs of sinful love: as, 1. When you desire that which is forbidden you. 2. Or that which will do you no good, upon a mistaken notion that it is better or more needful than it is. 3. Or when you desire it too eagerly, and must have it, or else you will be impatient or discontented, and cannot quietly be ruled and disposed of by God, but are grumbling at his providence and your lot. 4. Or when you desire it too hastily, and cannot wait for God's time. 5. Or else too greedily as to the measure, not being content with God's allowance, but must have more than he thinks fit for you. 6. Or especially when your desires are perverse, preferring lesser things before greater; desiring bodily and transitory things more than the mercies for your souls which will be everlasting. 7. When you desire anything ultimately and merely for the flesh, without referring it to God, it is a sin. Even your daily bread, and all your comforts, must be desired only as fodder for your horse, that he may better go his journey, even as provision for your bodies, to fit them for the better and more cheerful service of your souls and God. 8. Much more when your desires are for wicked ends (as to serve your lust, or pride, or greed, or revenge), they are wicked desires. 9. And when they are harmful to others.

**Direction 1. Be well acquainted with your own condition, and consider what it is that you have most need of; and then you will find that you have so much grace and mercy to desire for your souls, without which you are lost forever, and that you have a Christ to desire, and an endless life with God to desire, that it will quench all your thirst after the things below. [330]**

This, if anything, will make you wiser, when you see you have greater things to mind. A man that is in present danger of his life will not be anxious about pins or trinkets: and the hopes of a lordship or a kingdom will cure the desire of little things: a man that needs a physician for the dropsy or consumption will scarcely long for children's balls or tops. And I think a man that is going to heaven or hell should have something greater than worldly things to long for. Oh what an empty and foolish thing is a carnal mind, that has pardon, and grace, and Christ, and heaven, and God to think of, and that with speed before it is too late; and can forget them all, or not regard them, and eagerly long for some little insignificant trifle; as if they said, I must taste such a dish before I die; I must have such a house, or a child, or friend, before I go to another world! O study what need your distressed soul has of a Christ, and of peace with God, and preparation for eternity, and what need your darkened mind has of more knowledge, and your dead and carnal heart of more life, and tenderness, and love to God, and communion with him; feel these as you have cause, and the eagerness of your carnal desires will be gone.

**Direction 2. Remember how much your carnal desires aggravate the weakness of your spiritual desires, and make the sin more odious and inexcusable.**

Are you so eager for a husband, a wife, a child, for wealth, for advancement, or such things, while you are so cold and indifferent in your desires after God, and grace, and glory? Your desires after these are not so earnest! They do not make you so insistent and restless; they do not take up your thoughts both day and night; they do not set you so much on schemes and efforts: you can live as quietly without more grace, or assurance of salvation, or communion with God, as if you were indifferent in the matter; but you must have that which you desire in the world, or there is no quiet with you. Do you consider what a horrible contempt of God, and grace, and heaven is shown by this? Either you are regenerate or unregenerate. If you are regenerate, all your instructions, and all your experience of the worth of spiritual things, and the emptiness of things temporal, make it a heinous sin in you to be now so eager for those things which you have so often called empty, while you are so cold toward God, whose goodness you have had so great experience of. Do you not yet know better the difference between the creature and the Creator? Do you not yet better understand your necessities and interest, and what it is that you live upon, and must trust to for your everlasting blessedness and contentment? If you are unregenerate (as all are that love anything better than God), what a madness it is for one that is condemned in law to endless torments, and shall quickly be there if he is not regenerate and justified by Christ, to be thirsting so eagerly for this or that thing, or person, upon earth, when he should at once stir himself with all his might to save his soul from endless misery! How incongruous are these desires to the good and bad!

**Direction 3. Let every sinful desire humble you, for the worldliness and fleshliness which it reveals to be yet unmortified in you; and turn your desires to the mortifying of that flesh and concupiscence which is the cause.**

If you did not yet love the world, and the things that are in the world, you would not be so eager for them. If you were not too carnal, and did not mind too much the things of the flesh, you would not be so earnest for them as you are. It should be a grievous thing to your hearts to consider what worldliness and fleshliness this shows to be yet there. That you should value the creature so much as to be unable to bear the lack of it; is this renouncing the world and flesh? The thing you need is not that which you much desire; but a better heart, to know the emptiness of the creature, to be dead to the world, and to be able to bear the lack or loss of anything in it; and a fuller mortification of the flesh: mortifying and not satisfying it is your work.

**Direction 4. Ask your hearts seriously whether God in Christ is enough for them, or not?**

If they say, no, they renounce him and all their hope of heaven; for no man takes God for his God that does not take him for his portion, and as enough for him: if they say, yes, then you have enough to stop the mouth of your fleshly desires, while your hearts confess that they have enough in God. Should that soul that has a child's interest in God, and an inheritance in eternal life, be eager for any conveniences and contentments to the flesh? If God is not enough for you, you will never have enough. Turn to him more, and know him better, if you would have a satisfied mind.

**Direction 5. Remember that every sinful desire is a rebelling of your wills against the will of God; and that it is his will that must govern and dispose of all, and your wills must be conformed to his; indeed, that you must take pleasure and rest in the will of God.**

Reason the case with your hearts, and say, Who is it that is the governor of the world? and who is to rule me and dispose of my affairs? Is it I or God? Whose will is it that must lead, and whose must follow? Whose will is better guided, God's or mine? Either it is his will that I shall have what I desire, or not; if it is, I need not be so eager, for I shall have it in his time and way; if it is not his will, is it fit for me to grumble and strive against him? Remember that your discontents and carnal desires are so many accusations brought in against God; as if you said, You have not dealt well or wisely, or mercifully with me; I must have it better: I will not abide by your will and government; I must have it as I will, and have the disposal of myself.

**Direction 6. Observe how your eager desires are condemned by yourselves in your daily prayers, or else they make your prayers themselves condemnable.**

If you pray that the will of God may be done, why do your wills rebel against it, and your desires contradict your prayers? And if you ask no more than your daily bread, why do you thirst after more? But if you pray as you desire, Lord, let my will be done, and my selfish, carnal desire be fulfilled, for I must have this or that; then what an abominable prayer this is! Desire as you must pray.

**Direction 7. Remember what covenant you have made with God; that you renounced the world and the flesh, and took him for your Lord, and King, and Father, and yielded up yourselves as his own, as his subject, and as his child, to be disposed of, ruled, and provided for by him; and this covenant is essential not only to your Christianity, but to your taking him for your God.**

And do you repent of it? or will you break it, and forfeit all the benefits of the covenant? If you insist on having the disposal of yourselves, you discharge God of his covenant and fatherly care for you; and then what will become of you, if he so forsakes you?

**Direction 8. Consider how unfit you are to be the choosers of your own condition.**

You do not foresee what that person, or thing, or place will prove to you, which you so eagerly desire: for all you know it may be your undoing, or the greatest misery that ever befell you. Many a one has cried with Rachel, "Give me children or I'll die!" (Genesis 30:1), that have died by the wickedness and unkindness of their children. Many a one has been violent in their desires of a husband or a wife, that afterward have broken their hearts, or proved a greater affliction to them than any enemy they had in the world. Many a one has been eager for riches, and prosperity, and advancement, that has been ensnared by them, to the damnation of his soul. Many a one has been earnest for some office, dignity, or place of trust, which has made it a great increaser of his sin and misery. And it is flesh and self that is the eager desirer of things that are against the will of God, and nothing is so blind and biased as self and flesh. You do not think your child a competent judge of what is best for him, and do not make his desires, but your own understanding, the guide and rule of your dealings with him, or arrangements for him. And are you fitter choosers for yourselves in comparison with God than your child is in comparison with you? Either you take God for your Father, or you do not. If you do not, do not call him Father, and do not hope for mercy and salvation from him: if you do, is he not wise and good enough to dispose of you, and to determine what is best for you, and to choose for you?

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced as in the source (English modernized). A draft English translation follows the Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[329] Read Mr. Burroughs's excellent treatise called *The Jewel of Contentment*; and that excellent tract of a heathen, Plutarch, *De Tranquillitate Animi*. (*On Tranquility of Mind*.)

[330] Mentem nullis imaginibus depictam habeat: nam si corde mundus et ab universis imaginibus liber esse cupit, nil penitus cum amore possidere, nulli homini per voluntarium affectum singulari familiaritate, nullius ipsi, adhærere debet. Omnis namque familiaritas aut conversatio pure propter Dei amorem non inita, variis imaginibus inficit et perturbat hominum mentes, cum non ex Deo, sed ex carne originem ducat. Quisquis in virum spiritualem et divinum proficere cupit, is, carnali vitâ penitus renunciata, Deo soli amore adhæreat eundemque interiori homine suo peculiariter possideat, quo habito mox omnis multiplicitas, omnes imagines, omnis inordinatus erga creaturas amor fortiter ab eo profligabuntur; Deo quippe per amorem intus possesso protinus ab universis homo imaginibus liberatur. Deus spiritus est, cujus imaginem nemo proprie exprimere aut effigiare potest. Thaulerus flor. p. 79, 80. (Let him have a mind painted with no images: for if he desires to be pure in heart and free from all images, he ought to possess nothing at all with love, and to cling to no person by willing affection in special familiarity, nor [let] anyone [cling] to him. For every familiarity or association not entered into purely for the love of God infects and disturbs people's minds with various images, since it draws its origin not from God but from the flesh. Whoever desires to advance into a spiritual and godly man, let him, having wholly renounced the carnal life, cling to God alone by love, and possess him in a special way in his inner man; and when he has him, soon all multiplicity, all images, all disordered love toward creatures will be strongly driven away from him; for when God is possessed inwardly by love, a person is at once freed from all images. God is a spirit, whose image no one can properly express or portray. Tauler, *Flores*, pp. 79, 80.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **Title:** Baxter divides Chapter 7 ("Directions for the Government of the Passions") into ten divisions headed "Tit. 1." to "Tit. 10."; "Tit." is his abbreviation for "Title," meaning a heading or division. This file begins Title 3. The Direction numbering restarts at 1 here, as in Baxter.
- **Direction headings:** for Directions 1-8 we set Baxter's opening sentence(s) as the bold heading and the rest as the body, following the finished files. In Direction 1 the heading runs through the first sentence, which ends with the footnote marker [330].
- **Genesis 30:1 (Direction 8):** NET wording, "Give me children or I'll die!" The King James has "Give me children, or else I die." No change in the sense Baxter uses.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording; references are ours, not Baxter's): "your daily bread" and "the will of God may be done" (the Lord's Prayer, Matthew 6:10-11); "love the world, and the things that are in the world" (1 John 2:15, which the NET renders "the world or the things in the world"); "mind ... the things of the flesh" (Romans 8:5, where the NET has "have their outlook shaped by the things of the flesh"); "God is a spirit" in note 330 (John 4:24).
- **Baxter's books:** *Self-Denial* and *Crucifying the World* are his own works, named as he names them ("my book of Self-denial," "that of Crucifying the World"). "Here before in chapter 4, parts 6 and 7" refers to earlier parts of this *Directory* (against worldliness and flesh-pleasing); we have not checked the cross-reference. "Here against sinful love" refers to the preceding Title 2 of this chapter.
- **Note 329:** "Mr. Burroughs's ... *The Jewel of Contentment*" is probably Jeremiah Burroughs's *The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment*; we have not confirmed which edition Baxter had in mind. Plutarch's *De Tranquillitate Animi* is his essay *On Tranquility of Mind*.
- **Note 330:** "Thaulerus flor." is probably Johannes Tauler, the fourteenth-century German preacher and mystic, cited from a collection of extracts (*Flores*); we have not identified the edition. The phrase "nullius ipsi" is compressed and has been rendered loosely ("nor [let] anyone [cling] to him").
- **Word swaps (Baxter's word, then ours):** discoveries → signs; misconceit → mistaken notion; must needs → must; murmuring → grumbling; stay God's time → wait for God's time; specially → especially; but as provender → only as fodder; covetousness → greed; injurious → harmful; solicitous for → anxious about; fool-gawds → trinkets; methinks → I think; somewhat → something; vain and doting → empty and foolish; inconsiderable → insignificant; unexcusable → inexcusable; preferment → advancement; importunate → insistent; contrivances and endeavours → schemes and efforts; business → matter; manifested → shown; vanity → emptiness / empty; content (noun) → contentment; presently bestir him → at once stir himself; discovereth → reveals; set so much by → value so much; want → lack; yea → yes / indeed; filial → child's; stand to → abide by; will needs have → insist on having; bethink you → consider; unmeet → unfit; for aught → for all; insnared → ensnared; partial → biased; disposals of him → arrangements for him.
- **Kept words:** *carnal* (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh; see GLOSSARY.md); *mortify, mortification, unmortified* (putting sin to death); *concupiscence* (sinful desire or lust, a theological term); *the creature* (created things, as opposed to the Creator); *portion* (one's allotted share or inheritance, here God as the believer's whole good); *interest* (a share or claim, as in "a child's interest in God," Baxter's "filial interest"); *regenerate / unregenerate*; *providence*; *dispose of* (arrange, settle the affairs of); *dropsy* (an older name for swelling from fluid retention) and *consumption* (a wasting disease, often tuberculosis); *aggravate* in its older sense of "make heavier or worse"; *incongruous* (unfitting).
- **Word order:** In Direction 8 Baxter's clause order is kept ("what that person, or thing, or place will prove to you, which you so eagerly desire"): the relative clause "which you so eagerly desire" refers back to "that person, or thing, or place." In the opening paragraph "treated so largely of it" became "treated it so fully."
- **"as provender for your horse"** (point 7): Baxter's image is that bodily comforts are to be wanted the way a rider wants feed for his horse, so that it can carry him on his journey. Kept as written.
