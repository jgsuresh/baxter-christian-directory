# Chapter 6: Title 2, Directions 4–8 (the love of God, Christ, Scripture, ourselves, and our sins as matter for thought)

*This file continues Title 2 (furnishing the mind with good thoughts), begun in the previous file; it starts at the beginning of Direction 4.*

**Direction 4. Only get the love of God well kindled in your heart, and it will find employment, even the most high and sweet employment, for your thoughts.**

You yourselves shall be the judges whether your love does not for the most part rule your thoughts, assigning them their work, and directing them when, and how long to think on it. Just see how a lustful lover is carried after a beloved, frail piece of flesh! Their thoughts will so easily and so constantly run after it, that they need no spur! Notice in what a stream it carries them! how it feeds and enlivens their inventiveness, and elevates an ordinary imagination into a poetic and passionate strain! What abundance of matter can a lover find, in the narrow compass of a dirty corpse, for his thoughts to work on night and day! And will not the love of God then much more fill and feast your thoughts? How easily can the love of money find matter for the thoughts of the worldling from one year to another? It is easy to think of anything which you love. Oh what a happy spring of meditation is a rooted, predominant love of God! Love him strongly, and you cannot forget him. You will then see him in everything that meets you; and hear him in everyone that speaks to you: if you miss him, or have offended him, you will think on him with grief; if you taste of his love, you will think of him with delight; if you have only hope, you will think of him with desire, and your minds will be taken up in seeking him, and in understanding and using the means by which you may come to enjoy him. Love is ingenious, and full, and lively, and active, and resolute; it is valiant, and patient, and exceedingly industrious, and delights to encounter difficulties, and to appear in labors, and to show itself in advantageous sufferings; and therefore it makes the mind in which it reigns exceedingly busy, and finds the thoughts a world of work. If God is not in all the thoughts of the ungodly (Psalm 10:4), it is because he is not in his heart. He may be "near their mouths," but he is "far from their hearts" (Jeremiah 12:2). Do those men believe themselves, or would they be believed by anyone that is wise, who say they love God above all, and yet neither think of him, nor love to think of him; but are unwearied in thinking of their wealth, and honors, and the pleasures of their flesh? "Carefully consider this, you who reject God. Otherwise I will rip you to shreds and no one will be able to rescue you" (Psalm 50:22).

*In the margin: Jesus Christ and all the work of redemption.*

**Direction 5. Soundly understand the wonderful mystery of man's redemption, and know Jesus Christ, and you need not lack employment for your thoughts.**

For in him "are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3). He is "the power of God and the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24). If the study of Aristotle, Plato, Plotinus, and their numerous followers and commentators can find work for the thoughts of men that would know the works of God, or would be considered good philosophers, even for many years together, or a great part of their lives, what work then may a Christian find for his thoughts in Jesus Christ, "who became for us wisdom from God, and righteousness and sanctification and redemption" (1 Corinthians 1:30). "For God was pleased to have all his fullness dwell in the Son" (Colossians 1:19). And therefore in him there is fullness of matter for our meditations. As Paul "determined to know nothing" (or make display of no other knowledge) "but Christ crucified" (1 Corinthians 2:2); so if your thoughts had nothing to work upon many years together but Christ crucified, they need not stand still a moment for lack of most suitable and delightful matter. The mystery of the incarnation alone may find you work to search and admire many ages! But if from there you proceed to that world of wonderful matter which you may find in his doctrine, miracles, example, sufferings, temptations, victories, resurrection, ascension; and in his kingly, prophetic, and priestly offices; and in all the benefits which he has purchased for his flock; oh, what full and pleasant work is here for the daily thoughts of a believer! The soul may dwell here with continual delight, till it says with Paul (Galatians 2:20), "I have been crucified with Christ, and it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me. So the life I now live in the body, I live because of the faithfulness of the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me." Therefore daily "kneel before the Father, ... from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named," that "according to the wealth of his glory he will grant you to be strengthened with power through his Spirit in the inner person, that Christ will dwell in your hearts through faith, so that, because you have been rooted and grounded in love, you will be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and thus to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, so that you will be filled up to all the fullness of God" (Ephesians 3:14–19).

*In the margin: The holy Scriptures.*

**Direction 6. Search the holy Scriptures, and acquaint yourselves well with the oracles of God, which are able to make you wise to salvation, and you will find abundant matter for your thoughts.**

If you cannot find work enough for your minds among all those heights and depths, those excellencies and difficulties, it is because you never understood them, or never set your hearts to search them. What mysterious doctrines, how sublime and heavenly, are there for you to meditate on as long as you live! What a perfect law, a system of precepts most spiritual and pure! What terrible threats against offenders are there to be matter of your meditations. What wonderful histories of love and mercy! What holy examples! What a treasury of precious promises, on which lies our hope of life eternal! What full and free expressions of grace! What a joyful act of pardon and amnesty to penitent, believing sinners! In a word, the character of our inheritance, and the law which we must be governed and judged by, are there before us for our daily meditation! David, that had much less of it than we, says, "O how I love your law! All day long I meditate on it" (Psalm 119:97). And God said to Joshua, "This law scroll must not leave your lips. You must memorize it day and night so you can carefully obey all that is written in it" (Joshua 1:8). And Moses commanded the Israelites that "these words should be in their hearts, and that they teach them diligently to their children, and talk of them when they sat in their houses, and when they walked by the way, and when they lay down, and when they rose up, and to write them on the posts of their houses, and on their gates," etc. that they might be sure to remember them (Deuteronomy 6:7).

*In the margin: Ourselves as we are God's work.*

**Direction 7. Know yourself well as you are the work of God, and in yourself you will find abundant matter for your meditations.**

There you have the natural image of God to meditate on and admire; even the noble faculties of your understanding and free will, and executive power. And you have his moral or spiritual image to meditate on, if you are not unregenerate: even your holy wisdom, will, and power, or your holy light, and love, and power with readiness for holy practice; and all in the unity of holy life. [304] And there you have his relative image to meditate on; even your being, 1. The lord or owner. 2. The ruler. 3. The benefactor to the inferior creatures, and their end. Oh the world of mysteries which you carry continually about you in that little room. What abundance of wonders are in your body, which is fearfully and wonderfully made! And the greater wonders in your soul. You are yourself the clearest mirror that God is to be seen in under heaven, as you are a man and a saint! And therefore the worthiest matter for your own meditations (except that holy word, which is your rule, and the holy church, which is but a coalition of many such). What a shame is it that almost all men live and die such strangers to themselves, as to be utterly unacquainted with the innumerable excellencies and mysteries which God has laid up in them; and yet to let their thoughts run out upon empty things and trifles, and complain of their barrenness, and lack of matter, to feed their better meditations.

*In the margin: Our sins and needs.*

**Direction 8. Do not be a stranger to the many sins, and needs, and weaknesses of your soul, and you never need be empty of matter for your meditations.**

And though these thoughts are not the sweetest, yet your own folly has made them necessary. If you are dangerously sick, or only painfully sore, you can scarcely forget it: if poverty afflicts you with pinching needs, your thoughts are taken up with cares and trouble day and night. If another wrongs you, you can easily think on it. And have you so often wronged your God and Savior, and so unkindly vilified his mercy, and so unthankfully made light of saving grace, and so presumptuously and securely ventured on his wrath, and yet do you find a scarcity of matter for your meditations? Have you all the sins of your youth and ignorance to think on, and all the sins of your rashness and indulgence in physical pleasure, and of your negligence and sloth, and of your worldliness and selfishness, ambition and pride, your passions and your omissions, and all your sinful thoughts and words, and yet are you short of matter for your thoughts? Do you carry about you such a body of death? so much selfishness, pride, worldliness, and carnality; so much ignorance, unbelief, aversion to God, and backwardness to all that is spiritual and holy; so much passion, and readiness to sin; and yet do you not find enough to think on? Look over the sins of all your life: see them in all their aggravations; as they have been committed against knowledge, or means and helps, against mercies and judgments, and your own vows or promises; in prosperity and under affliction itself; in secret and with others; in your general and particular calling, and in all your relations; in every place, and time, and condition that you have lived in; your sins against God directly, and your injuries or neglects of man: sins against holy duties, and sins in holy duties; in prayer, hearing, reading, sacraments, meditation, conference, reproofs, and receiving of reproofs from others: your negligent preparations for death and judgment; the strangeness of your soul to God and heaven. [305]—Is not here work enough for your meditations? certainly if you think so, it is because your heart never felt the bitterness of sin, nor was ever yet acquainted with true repentance; but the time is yet to come that light must show you what sin is, and what you are, and what you have done, and how full your heart is of the serpent's brood, and that your sin must find you out! Do you not know that your sins are as the sands of the shore, or as the hairs upon your head for number? and that every sin has deadly poison in it, and malignant enmity to God and holiness; and yet are they not enough to keep your thoughts from being idle? Judge by their language whether it is so with penitents: Psalm 51:2–3, "Wash away my wrongdoing. Cleanse me of my sin. For I am aware of my rebellious acts; I am forever conscious of my sin." Psalm 40:12, "For innumerable dangers surround me. My sins overtake me so I am unable to see; they outnumber the hairs of my head so my strength fails me." Psalm 119:59, "I consider my actions and follow your rules." True repentance is thus described: Ezekiel 36:31, "Then you will remember your evil behavior and your deeds that were not good; you will loathe yourselves on account of your sins and your abominable deeds." Yes, God's forgiving and forgetting your sins must not make you forget them. Ezekiel 16:60–63, "I will establish a lasting covenant with you. Then you will remember your conduct and be ashamed. ... I will establish my covenant with you ... Then you will remember, be ashamed, and remain silent because of your disgrace when I make atonement for all you have done, declares the Sovereign Lord."

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Every verse given in NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org for this file.
- **Title:** "Tit." in Baxter is his abbreviation for "Title," a heading or division of the chapter. This file belongs to Title 2 (furnishing the mind with good thoughts). The margin note for Direction 4 ("The work of love.") stands just before line 31581 in the source and so falls in the previous file.
- **Direction 8** ends where this file ends; the next file opens with Direction 9.
- **Psalm 10:4** (Direction 4): Baxter does not put quotation marks here; his words echo the King James "God is not in all his thoughts." The NET reads the verse differently: "The wicked man is so arrogant he always thinks, 'God won't hold me accountable; he doesn't care.'" The NET does not say that God is absent from the wicked man's thoughts, which is the point Baxter draws. We keep Baxter's wording.
- **Jeremiah 12:2** (Direction 4): Baxter's quoted words ("nigh their mouths," "far from their reins") follow the King James ("thou art near in their mouth, and far from their reins"). The NET has "They always talk about you, but they really care nothing about you," which does not contain the quoted words, so we keep Baxter's wording modernized. "Reins" (literally the kidneys, then the seat of the inward feelings) is rendered "hearts."
- **Psalm 50:22** (Direction 4): NET wording. **Flag:** the King James has "ye that forget God," and Baxter's whole Direction is about not forgetting God and not thinking of him; the NET has "you who reject God," so the verbal link to forgetting is lost. Baxter's reference prints "Psal. 1. 22" (a numeral 1 for the Roman numeral l, i.e. 50); corrected to Psalm 50:22.
- **Colossians 2:3; 1 Corinthians 1:24; 1 Corinthians 1:30** (Direction 5): NET wording. In 1:24 Baxter's quotation begins "He is"; we keep "He is" outside the quotation marks, since the NET's subject is "Christ."
- **Colossians 1:19** (Direction 5): NET wording. The King James has "it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell"; the NET has "God was pleased to have all his fullness dwell in the Son." Baxter's inference ("therefore in him there is fullness") is unaffected.
- **1 Corinthians 2:2** (Direction 5): Baxter's quotation is a compression of the King James ("I determined not to know any thing among you, save Jesus Christ, and him crucified"), and his parenthesis glosses the word "know." We keep his wording, modernized. **Flag:** the NET reads "I decided to be concerned about nothing among you except Jesus Christ, and him crucified," with no word "know," so Baxter's parenthetical gloss has nothing to attach to in the NET.
- **Galatians 2:20** (Direction 5): NET wording. **Flag:** the King James has "I live by the faith of the Son of God"; the NET has "because of the faithfulness of the Son of God." The NET also has "in the body" for "in the flesh," and "it is no longer I who live" for "nevertheless I live; yet not I." Baxter uses the verse as the believer's confession of union with Christ, which the NET keeps, but the "faith/faithfulness" change shifts the stress from the believer's faith to Christ's faithfulness.
- **Ephesians 3:14–19** (Direction 5): Baxter turns Paul's prayer into an instruction ("bow your knees ... that he would grant you"). We use the NET wording within his frame. The King James words "of our Lord Jesus Christ" after "the Father" are not in the NET text (a textual variant), so they are marked with "...". The NET's "I pray that" is left out because Baxter's own frame replaces it. "Bow your knees" becomes NET "kneel."
- **"able to make you wise to salvation"** (Direction 6): an unmarked allusion to 2 Timothy 3:15 (King James "able to make thee wise unto salvation"; NET "able to give you wisdom for salvation"); kept in Baxter's wording.
- **Psalm 119:97** (Direction 6): NET wording.
- **Joshua 1:8** (Direction 6): NET wording. **Flag:** the King James has "thou shalt meditate therein day and night," which is the reason Baxter quotes it in a chapter on meditation; the NET has "You must memorize it day and night." The NET also has "This law scroll must not leave your lips" for "shall not depart out of thy mouth." The NET drops the word "meditate" on which Baxter's use depends.
- **Deuteronomy 6:7** (Direction 6): Baxter's quotation is an indirect, third-person paraphrase blending Deuteronomy 6:6–9 (and perhaps 11:19–20). We keep his wording, modernized, in his quotation marks. The NET of 6:6–9 reads: "These words I am commanding you today must be kept in mind, and you must teach them to your children and speak of them as you sit in your house, as you walk along the road, as you lie down, and as you get up. You should tie them as a reminder on your forearm and fasten them as symbols on your forehead. Inscribe them on the doorframes of your houses and gates." Note that the NET has "kept in mind" for the King James "in thine heart," and drops "diligently."
- **"fearfully and wonderfully made"** (Direction 7): unmarked allusion to Psalm 139:14, kept in Baxter's (King James) wording. The NET reads the verse differently ("your deeds are awesome and amazing").
- **"body of death"** (Direction 8): unmarked allusion to Romans 7:24. **"your sin must find you out"**: unmarked allusion to Numbers 32:23 (NET "your sin will find you out"). **"as the sands of the shore ... as the hairs upon your head"**: echoes of common biblical images (compare Psalm 40:12, quoted just after); not referenced by Baxter.
- **Psalm 51:2–3** (Direction 8): NET wording. The King James "my sin is ever before me," on which Baxter leans (sin kept in the thoughts), becomes "I am forever conscious of my sin"; the sense he needs survives.
- **Psalm 40:12** (Direction 8): NET wording. The King James "innumerable evils have compassed me about" becomes "innumerable dangers surround me," and "I am not able to look up" becomes "I am unable to see." The NET keeps "My sins ... outnumber the hairs of my head," the point Baxter uses.
- **Psalm 119:59** (Direction 8): Baxter prints "Psal. cxix. 57," but the quoted words ("I thought on my ways, and turned my feet unto thy testimonies") are verse 59. Corrected. **Flag:** the NET reads "I consider my actions and follow your rules"; the thinking on one's ways is still there.
- **Ezekiel 36:31** (Direction 8): NET wording. The King James "loathe yourselves in your own sight" becomes "loathe yourselves"; the remembering on which Baxter builds is kept.
- **Ezekiel 16:60–63** (Direction 8): Baxter quotes selectively from these verses; we give the NET wording for the parts he quotes, with "..." where he skips. **Flags:** (1) Baxter's "that thou mayst remember, and be confounded" (purpose) is in the NET a plain future ("Then you will remember, be ashamed"). (2) The King James "when I am pacified toward thee for all that thou hast done" is in the NET "when I make atonement for all you have done"; both carry the forgiveness Baxter's point needs (remember sins even after God forgives). (3) Baxter ends "saith the Lord God of hosts"; the King James has only "saith the Lord GOD," so "of hosts" appears to be Baxter's (or his printer's) addition; the NET has "declares the Sovereign Lord."
- **Word swaps:** "Get but" to "Only get"; "See but" to "Just see"; "silly" (feeble, poor) to "frail"; "Mark" to "Notice"; "quickeneth their invention" to "enlivens their inventiveness"; "fancy" to "imagination"; "quick" (living, lively) to "lively"; "want" (lack) to "lack"; "accounted" to "considered"; "ostentation" to "display"; "thence" to "from there"; "threatenings" to "threats"; "act of pardon and oblivion" to "act of pardon and amnesty" (GLOSSARY: act of oblivion); "promptitude" to "readiness"; "glass" (mirror) to "mirror"; "vanities and toys" to "empty things and trifles"; "wants" (needs) to "needs" in Direction 8 and its margin note; "set light by" to "made light of"; "sensuality" to "indulgence in physical pleasure" (GLOSSARY); "scanted of" to "short of"; "averseness" to "aversion"; "Yea" to "Yes."
- **finds the thoughts a world of work** (Direction 4): "finds" here means "provides"; left as Baxter wrote it.
- **securely** (Direction 8): kept; in Baxter it means with careless, unworried confidence (his technical sense of "security"), not safely.
- **character of our inheritance** (Direction 6): "character" here means the written description or record of our inheritance; kept.
- **aggravations** (Direction 8): the circumstances that make a sin worse. **conference**: religious conversation. **general and particular calling**: the calling common to all Christians and one's particular occupation or station. **relations**: one's roles toward others (parent, servant, neighbor, etc.). All kept.
- **relative image** (Direction 7): Baxter's term for the image of God in man's relation to other creatures (as owner, ruler, and benefactor of them and their end); kept. "Executive power" means the power to carry out what the understanding and will decide.
- **"a lustful lover ... a dirty corpse"** (Direction 4): Baxter's blunt words for the human body loved lustfully; kept as written.
- **Aristotle, Plato, Plotinus** (Direction 5): named by Baxter as the philosophers whose study occupies scholars for years; no further claim is made here.
- **Note 304:** Baxter refers to his own book on self-ignorance; we give the title as he gives it and have not checked its exact published title.
- **Punctuation:** "[305]--Is not here" keeps Baxter's dash as an em dash; the lower-case "certainly" after the question mark is as in the source.
