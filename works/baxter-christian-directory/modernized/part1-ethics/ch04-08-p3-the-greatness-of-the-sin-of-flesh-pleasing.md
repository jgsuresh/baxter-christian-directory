# Chapter 4: The Greatness of the Sin of Flesh-Pleasing

*This section begins partway through Baxter's Direction "against the Master Sin; Sensuality, Flesh-pleasing," at its third point; it also ends before that Direction does, and the next file continues it.*

*In the margin: The greatness of the sin.*

III. I am next to show you the evil or malignity of predominant flesh-pleasing: for if the greatness of the sin were known, it would contribute much to the cure. And, 1. Understand that it is the sin of sins; the end of all sin, and therefore the very sum and life of all. All the evil wicked men commit is ultimately to please the flesh: the love of flesh-pleasing is the cause of all. Pride, and greed, and whoredom, and wantonness, and gluttony, and drunkenness, and all the rest, are but either the immediate works of physical indulgence and flesh-pleasing, or the distant service of it, by laying in provision for it. And all the malicious enmity and opposition to God and godliness is from hence, because they cross the interest and desires of the flesh: the final cause is it for which men invent and use all the means that tend to it. Therefore all other sin being nothing but the means for the pleasing of our fleshly appetites and fancies, it is evident that flesh-pleasing is the common cause of them all; and is to all other sin as the spring is to the watch, or the poise to the clock; the weight which gives them all their motion. Cure this sin and you have taken off the poise, and cured all the positive sins of the soul: though the privative sins would be still uncured, if there were no more done; because that which makes the clock stand still is not enough to make it go right. But, indeed, nothing but the love of pleasing God can truly cure the love of flesh-pleasing: and such a cure is the cure of every sin, both positive and privative, active and defective.

2. Flesh-pleasing is the grand idolatry of the world; and the flesh the greatest idol that ever was set up against God. Therefore Paul says of pleasure-loving worldlings, that "their god is the belly," and thence it is that they "think about earthly things," and "exult in their shame, and are enemies of the cross of Christ," that is, to sufferings for Christ, and the doctrine and duties which would cause their sufferings. That is a man's god which he takes for his chief good, and loves best, and trusts in most, and is most desirous to please: and this is the flesh to every pleasure-seeker. He is "loving pleasure rather than loving God" (2 Timothy 3:2, 4). He "savors" or "minds" the "things of the flesh," and "lives" to it, and "walks after it" (Romans 8:1, 5-8, 13). He "makes provision for it to satisfy its appetite or lusts" (Romans 13:14). He "sows to his own flesh" (Galatians 6:8); and fulfills his lust, when it "lusts against the Spirit" (Galatians 5:16, 17). And thus, while concupiscence or physical indulgence has dominion, sin is said to have dominion over them, and they are servants to it (Romans 6:14, 20). For "to whom men yield themselves servants to obey, his servants they are whom they serve or obey" (Romans 6:16). It is not bowing the knee and praying to another that is the chief idolatry. As loving, and pleasing, and obeying, and trusting, and seeking, and delighting in him are the chiefest parts of the service of God, which he prefers before a thousand sacrifices or compliments; so loving the flesh, and pleasing it, and obeying it, and trusting in it, and seeking and delighting in its pleasures, are the chief service of the flesh; and more than if you offered sacrifice to it, and therefore is the grand idolatry. And so the flesh is the chief enemy of God, which has the chiefest love and service which are due to him, and robs him of the hearts of all mankind that are carnal and unsanctified. All the Baals, and Jupiters, and Apollos, and other idols of the world set together, have not so much of the love and service due to God as the flesh alone has. If other things are idolized by the pleasure-seeker, it is but as they serve his flesh, and therefore they are made but inferior idols. He may idolize his wealth, and idolize men in power and worldly greatness; but it is but as they can help or hurt his flesh: this has his heart. By the interest of the flesh, he judges of his condition; by this he judges of his friends; by this he chooses his actions or refuses them; and by this he measures the words and actions of all others. He takes all for good which pleases his flesh, and all for bad that is against his pleasure.

3. The flesh is not only the common idol, but the most devouring idol in all the world. It has not, as subordinate, flattered idols have, only a knee and compliment, or now and then a sacrifice or ceremony, but it has the heart, the tongue, the body to serve it; the whole estate, the service of friends, the use of wit and utmost diligence; in a word, it has all. It is loved and served by the pleasure-seeker, as God should be loved and served by his own, even "with all the heart, and soul, and might:" they "honor it with their substance, and the firstfruits of their increase." It is as faithfully served as Christ requires to be of his disciples: men will part with father, and mother, and brother, and sister, and nearest friends, and all that is against it, for the pleasing of their flesh. Nay, Christ required men to part with no greater matter for him than transitory, earthly things, which they must shortly part with whether they will or no; but they do for the flesh ten thousand thousandfold more than ever they were required to do for Christ. They forsake God for it. They forsake Christ, and heaven, and their salvation for it. They forsake all the solid comforts of this life, and all the joys of the life to come for it. They sell all that they have, and lay down the price at its feet; yes, more than all they have, even all their hopes of what they might have to all eternity. They suffer a martyrdom in the flames of hell forever, for their flesh. All the pains they take is for it. All the wrong they do to others, and all the stirs and ruins they make in the world, is for it. And all the time they spend is for it: and had they a thousand years more to live, they would spend it accordingly. If anything seems excepted for God, it is but the bones, or crumbs, or leavings of the flesh; or rather, it is nothing: for God has not indeed the hours which he seems to have; he has but a few fair words and compliments, when the flesh has their hearts in the midst of their hypocritical worship, and on his holy day; and they serve him but as the Indians serve the devil, that he may serve their turns, and do them no hurt.

4. How base an idol is the flesh! If all the derision used by Elijah and the prophets against the heathenish idolatry is due, is not as much due against the idolatry of all the physical-pleasure-seekers? Is it so great a madness to serve an idol of silver, or gold, or stone, or wood? what better is it to serve an idol of flesh and blood; a paunch of guts; that is full of filth and excrements within, and the skin itself, the cleanest part, is ashamed to be uncovered? We may say to the carnal worldling, as Elijah to the Baalists, and more; "Call upon your god in the hour of your distress: cry aloud; perhaps he is asleep, or he is blowing his nose, or vomiting, or purging: certainly he will be shortly rotting in the grave, more loathsome than the dirt or dung upon the earth." And is this a god to sacrifice all that we can get to? and to give all our time, and care, and labor, and our souls and all to? O judge of this idolatry, as God will make you judge at last!

5. And here next consider how impious and horrid an abasement it is of the eternal God, to prefer so vile a thing before him! And whether every ungodly, physical-pleasure-loving man is not a constant, practical blasphemer? What do you but say continually by your practice, This dunghill, nasty flesh is to be preferred before God; to be more loved, and obeyed, and served? It deserves more of my time than he: it is more worthy of my delight and love. God will be judge, (and judge in righteousness before long,) whether this is not the daily language of your life, though your tongue is taught some better manners. And whether this is blasphemy, judge yourself. Whether you judge God or the flesh more worthy to be pleased, and which you think it better to please, ask your own heart, when cards, and dice, and eating, and drinking, and gallantry, and idleness, and greatness, and abundance, all seem so sweet to you, in comparison of your thoughts of God, and his holy word and service! and when morning and night, and whenever you are alone, those thoughts can run out with tirelessness or pleasure, upon these provisions for your flesh, which you can hardly force to look up to God, a quarter of an hour, though with unwillingness.

6. Think also what a contempt of heaven it is, to prefer the pleasing of the flesh before it. There are but two ends which all men aim at; the pleasing of the flesh on earth, or the enjoying of God in heaven (unless any are deluded to think that he shall have a physical life hereafter too, as well as here). And these two stand one against the other. And he that sets up one does renounce (or as good as renounce) the other. "The person who sows to his own flesh will reap corruption from the flesh, but the one who sows to the Spirit will reap eternal life from the Spirit" (Galatians 6:8). Your wealth, and honor, and amusements, and pleasures, and appetites are put in the scales against heaven, and all the joys and hopes hereafter; (to say you hope to have them both is the cheat of unbelief, that believes not God; and is not heaven most basely esteemed of by those that prefer so base a thing before it?

7. Remember that flesh-pleasing is a great contempt and treachery against the soul. It is a great contempt of an immortal soul, to prefer its corruptible flesh before it, and to make its servant become its master, and to ride on horseback, while it goes, as it were, on foot. Is the flesh worthy of so much time, and cost, and care, and so much ado as is made for it in the world, and is not a never-dying soul worth more? Nay, it is a betraying of the soul: you set up its enemy before it; and put its safety into an enemy's hands; and you cast away all its joys and hopes for the gratifying of the flesh. Might it not complain of your cruelty, and say, Must my endless happiness be sold to purchase so short a pleasure for your flesh? Must I be undone forever, and lie in hell, that it may be satisfied for a little time? But why talk I of the soul's complaint? Alas! it is itself that it must complain of! for it is its own doing! It has its choice: the flesh can but tempt it, and not constrain it: God has put the chief power and government into its hands; if it will sell its own eternal hopes, to pamper worm's meat, it must fare accordingly. You would not think very honorably of that man's wit or honesty, who would sell the patrimony of all his children, and all his friends that trusted him with it, and afterward sell their persons into slavery, and all this to purchase him a delicious feast, with amusements and gallantry for a day! And is he wiser or better that sells (in effect) the inheritance of his soul, and betrays it to hell and devils forever, and all this to purchase the fleshly pleasure of so short a life?

8. Remember what a beastly life it is to be a pleasure-seeker. It is an unmanning of yourselves. Physical pleasures are brutish pleasures; beasts have them as well as men. We have the higher faculty of reason, to subdue and rule the beastly part. And reason is the man; and has a higher kind of happiness to delight in. Do you think that man is made for no higher matters than a beast? and that you have not a more noble object for your delight than your swine or dog has, who have the pleasure of meat, and lust, and play, and ease, and fancy, as well as you? Certainly where physical pleasures are preferred before the higher pleasures of the soul, that man becomes a beast, or worse, subjecting his reason to his brutish part.

9. Think what an inconsiderable, pitiful happiness it is that fleshly persons choose; how small and short, as well as sordid. Oh how quickly will the game be ended! and the delights of boiling lust be gone! How quickly will the drink be past their throats, and their delicate dishes be turned into filth! How short is the amusement and laughter of the fool! And how quickly will that face be the index of a pained body, or a grieved, self-tormenting mind! It is but a few days till all their stately greatness will be leveled; and the most adorned, pampered flesh will have no more to show of all the pleasure which was so dearly bought, than a Lazarus, or the most mortified saint. A few days will turn their pleasure into anguish, and their jollity into groans, and their ostentation into lamentation, and all their glory into shame. As every moment puts an end to all the pleasures of their lives that are past, and they are now to them as if they had never been; so the last moment is at hand, which will end the little that remains. And then the sinner will with groans confess that he has made a miserable choice, and that he might have had a more durable pleasure if he had been wise. When the skull is cast up with the spade, to make room for a successor, you may see the hole where all the meat and drink went in, and the hideous seat of that face, which once was the disclosure of wantonness, pride, and scorn; but you will see no signs of mirth or pleasure.

10. Lastly, consider that there is scarcely a sin in the world more inexcusable than this. The flesh-pleaser sees the end of all his physical delights, in the faces of the sick, and in the corpses that are daily carried to the earth, and in the graves, and bones, and dust of those that once had as merry a life as he. His reason can say, All this is gone with them, and is as if it had never been; and so it will shortly be with me. He knows that all the pleasure of his life past is now of no value to himself. His warnings are constant, close, and sensible; and therefore he has the greater sin.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®, fetched from labs.bible.org; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). Baxter's quotations are mostly King James wording, so several are kept as his paraphrase (below).
- **Beginning and end:** the file opens at Baxter's third point ("III.") under the Direction against sensuality and flesh-pleasing; the numbered sub-points 1-10 are Baxter's own. The Direction continues in the next file.
- **Philippians 3:18-19** (paragraph 2, unmarked, no reference in Baxter): the quoted phrases are in NET wording: "their god is the belly" (Baxter: "their belly is their god"), "think about earthly things" (Baxter: "mind earthly things"), "exult in their shame" (Baxter: "glory in their shame"), "enemies of the cross of Christ" (NET: "the enemies of the cross of Christ"). Baxter's own gloss "that is, to sufferings for Christ ..." is his, not Paul's. We have not put a reference in the text; the passage appears to be Philippians 3:18-19.
- **2 Timothy 3:2, 4:** NET "loving pleasure rather than loving God." Baxter has "loveth pleasure more than God"; the NET phrase is a participle, so "He is" is placed before it and the sentence reads slightly differently from Baxter's. (NET at verse 2 has "lovers of themselves"; Baxter cites verse 2 with verse 4.)
- **Romans 8:1, 5-8, 13:** Baxter's "savoureth" or "mindeth" the "things of the flesh," "liveth" to it, and "walketh after it" are King James wording (Romans 8:5 KJV "mind the things of the flesh"; 8:1 and 8:4 "walk after the flesh"). The NET has "have their outlook shaped by the things of the flesh" (8:5) and "live according to the flesh" (8:13). The NET does not use "mind," "savor," or "walk after" in these verses, so we keep Baxter's words in his quotation marks as his paraphrase. Only "things of the flesh" is the same in the NET. His point, that the flesh is what the person lives for, is not lost.
- **Romans 13:14:** Baxter's "maketh provision for it to satisfy its appetite or lusts" is his paraphrase; the NET reads "make no provision for the flesh to arouse its desires." Baxter turns the prohibition into a description of the pleasure-seeker.
- **Galatians 6:8:** in paragraph 2 NET "sows to his own flesh." Baxter's "soweth to the flesh" is KJV. In paragraph 6 Baxter's quoted sentence (KJV: "If ye sow to the flesh, of the flesh ye shall reap corruption; but if ye sow to the Spirit, of the Spirit ye shall reap life everlasting") is given in full NET wording: "the person who sows to his own flesh will reap corruption from the flesh, but the one who sows to the Spirit will reap eternal life from the Spirit." The NET's sentence begins with a lowercase "because" clause, so we capitalize its first word after the quotation mark. Baxter's "everlasting life" and the NET's "eternal life" are the same.
- **Galatians 5:16, 17:** Baxter's "lusteth against the Spirit" is KJV. The NET has "the flesh has desires that are opposed to the Spirit" (5:17) and "carry out the desires of the flesh" (5:16). We keep his quotation as his paraphrase ("lusts against the Spirit").
- **Romans 6:14, 16, 20:** Baxter's "dominion" (6:14) and "servants" (6:16, 20) are KJV words; the NET has "mastery" and "slaves." The quotation "to whom men yield themselves servants to obey ..." is KJV-based; the NET reads "if you present yourselves as obedient slaves, you are slaves of the one you obey" (6:16). Baxter's quoted wording is kept as his paraphrase. "Servants" is Baxter's word; the NET's "slaves" is stronger and matches the point he draws.
- **Deuteronomy 6:5 and Proverbs 3:9** (paragraph 3, unmarked, no references in Baxter): "with all the heart, and soul, and might" and "honor it with their substance, and the firstfruits of their increase" are KJV-style wording. We have not confirmed the exact references; they appear to be Deuteronomy 6:5 and Proverbs 3:9. NET Deuteronomy 6:5: "You must love the Lord your God with your whole mind, your whole being, and all your strength." NET Proverbs 3:9: "Honor the Lord from your wealth and from the firstfruits of all your crops." Baxter's text is kept in his quotation marks as his own wording; "honour" is modernized to "honor."
- **Elijah** (paragraph 4, unmarked): Baxter's quotation, "Call upon your god in the hour of your distress ...," is his own expansion of 1 Kings 18:27 ("and more," as he says). The NET reads: "Yell louder! After all, he is a god; he may be deep in thought, or perhaps he stepped out for a moment or has taken a trip. Perhaps he is sleeping and needs to be awakened." Only "perhaps he is asleep" corresponds. The nose-blowing, vomiting, purging, and rotting are Baxter's additions. Baxter prints "your God" with a capital; we write "your god" because the object of the mockery is the idol.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): giving up father, mother, brother, sister (compare Matthew 10:37 and Luke 14:26, probably); selling all one has (compare Matthew 13:44-46, probably); a "Lazarus" (probably the beggar of Luke 16:19-31); "the bones, or crumbs, or leavings"; "a thousand sacrifices." These identifications are our guesses and not stated by Baxter.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): sensuality / sensual / sensualist(s) became physical indulgence, physical, and pleasure-seeker(s), by context ("sensual worldlings" became "pleasure-loving worldlings," "sensual man" became "physical-pleasure-loving man," "sensual life" became "physical life"); covetousness became greed; sports became amusements; felicity became happiness; infidelity became unbelief; scarce became scarcely; unexcusable became inexcusable; ere long became before long; honour became honor; subservient (idols) became subordinate; subserve became serve; speed accordingly became fare accordingly; unweariedness became tirelessness; discovery (of wantonness) became disclosure; sometime (formerly, twice in paragraphs 9 and 10) became once; yea became yes; for ever became forever; "thou/thee/thy" and -eth endings became modern forms; "therewith" became "with it"; "after" became "afterward".
- **Kept as Baxter's words:** *poise* (the weight that drives a clock); *positive and privative sins* (sins of doing wrong and sins of failing to do right); *concupiscence*; *whoredom*, *wantonness*, *gluttony* (his blunt words); *compliments* (outward courtesies, empty show); *wit* (intelligence); *stirs* (disturbances); *paunch of guts*; *dunghill, nasty flesh* ("nasty" here means filthy); *ado* (fuss); *gallantry* (fashionable pleasure-seeking); *index* (a pointer or sign); *worm's meat*; *patrimony* (inherited estate); *mortified* (one who has put sinful desires to death); *sensible* in the last paragraph (felt, perceived: "constant, close, and sensible" warnings); *carnal*.
- **"pleasure-loving worldlings"** and **"physical-pleasure-seekers"** (paragraph 4, Baxter's "sensual"): chosen for the context; "the idolatry of all the sensual" is rendered "the idolatry of all the physical-pleasure-seekers."
- **Baals, Jupiters, Apollos:** Baxter's plural forms for pagan gods. **the Baalists:** the prophets of Baal (1 Kings 18).
- **"the Indians serve the devil"** (paragraph 3): Baxter's claim, using his usual word for the native peoples of the Americas; kept as his. We have not checked the source of the comparison.
- **Punctuation oddities in the source:** the sentence in paragraph 6 beginning "(to say you hope to have them both ..." opens a parenthesis that is never closed, and "(and judge in righteousness ere long,)" in paragraph 5 has a comma inside the closing parenthesis. Both are kept as printed. In paragraph 6, the sentence "Your wealth, and honor ..." is Baxter's, and the NET quotation before it stands as a sentence of its own.
- **"his own"** (paragraph 3, "as God should be loved and served by his own"): God's own people.
