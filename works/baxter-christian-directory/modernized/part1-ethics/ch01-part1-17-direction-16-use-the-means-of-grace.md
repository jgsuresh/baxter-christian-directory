# Chapter 1, Part 1: Direction 16

**Direction 16. Engage yourself in the cheerful, constant use of the means and helps appointed by God, for your confirmation and salvation.**

He can never expect to attain the end who will not be persuaded to use the means. Of yourselves you can do nothing. God gives his help by the means which he has appointed and fitted to your help. Of the use of these I shall treat more fully afterward; I am now only to name them to you, that you may know what it is that you have to do. [34]

1. That you must hear or read the word of God, and other good books which explain it and apply it, I showed you before. The newborn Christian inclines to this, as the newborn child does to the breast: "So get rid of all evil and all deceit and hypocrisy and envy and all slander. And yearn like newborn infants for pure, spiritual milk, so that by it you may grow up to salvation" (1 Peter 2:1–2). The blessed man "finds pleasure in obeying the Lord's commands; he meditates on his commands day and night" (Psalm 1:2–3).

2. Another means is the public worship of God in communion with his church and people. Besides the benefit of the word there preached, the prayers of the church are effective for the members; and it raises the soul to holy joys to join with well-ordered assemblies of the saints in the praises of the Almighty. The assemblies of holy worshippers of God are the places of his delight, and must be the places of our delight. They are most like the celestial society that sound forth the praises of the glorious Jehovah with purest minds and cheerful voice. "Everyone in his temple says, 'Majestic!'" (Psalm 29:9). In such a choir, what soul will not be caught up with delight, and desire to join in the concert and harmony? In such a flame of united desires and praises, what soul is so cold and dull that it will not be inflamed, and with more than ordinary ease and eagerness fly up to God?

3. Another means is private prayer to God. When God would tell Ananias that Paul was converted, he says of him that "he is praying" (Acts 9:11). Prayer is the breath of the new creature. The spirit of adoption given to every child of God is a spirit of prayer, and teaches them to cry, "Abba, Father," and helps their infirmities; when they do not know what to pray for as they ought, and when words are lacking, it (as it were) intercedes for them with groans which they cannot express in words (Galatians 4:6; Romans 8:15, 26, 27). And God knows the meaning of the Spirit in those groans. The first workings of grace are in desires after grace, provoking the soul to fervent prayer, by which more grace is speedily obtained. "Ask," then, "and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened for you" (Luke 11:9).

4. Another means to be used is confession of sin; not only to God (for so every wicked man may do, because he knows that God is already acquainted with it all, and this is no addition to his shame: he so little regards the eye of God that he is more ashamed when it is known to men), but in three cases confession must be made also to man. First, in case you have wronged man, and are thus bound to make him amends: as if you have robbed him, defrauded him, slandered him, or borne false witness against him. Second, in case you are children or servants who are under the government of parents or masters, and are called by them to give an account of your actions: you are bound then to give a true account. Third, in case you have need of the counsel or prayers of others for the settling of your consciences in peace: in this case you must so far open your case to them as is necessary to their effective help for your recovery; for if they do not know the disease, they will be unfit to apply the remedy. In these cases it is true that "the one who covers his transgressions will not prosper, but whoever confesses them and forsakes them will find mercy" (Proverbs 28:13).

5. Another means to be used is the familiar company and holy conversation of humble, sincere, experienced Christians. The Spirit that is in them, and breathes and acts through them, will kindle the like holy flames in you. Away with the company of idle, chattering, pleasure-loving men that can talk of nothing but their worldly wealth, or business, or their reputations, or their appetites and lusts; associate yourselves with those that go the way to heaven, if you resolve to go in it. O what a difference you will find between these two sorts of companions! The one sort, if you have any thoughts of repentance, would stifle them, and laugh you out of the use of your reason into their own distracted mirth and foolishness: and if you have any serious thoughts of your salvation, or any inclinations to repent and be wise, they will do much to divert them, and hold you in the power and snares of Satan until it is too late: if you have any zeal, or heavenly-mindedness, they will do much to quench it, and bring your minds down to earth again. The other sort will speak of things of so great weight and importance, and that with seriousness and reverence, as will tend to raise and quicken your souls; and give you a taste of the heavenly things which they talk about; they will encourage you by their own experiences, and direct you by that truth which has directed them, and zealously share what they have received: they will pray for you, and teach you how to pray: they will give the example of holy, humble, obedient lives, and lovingly admonish you of your duties, and reprove your sins. In a word, as the carnal mind savors the things of the flesh, and is enmity against God, the company of such will be a powerful means to infect you with their plague, and make you such, if you had escaped from them; much more to keep you such, if you have not escaped: and as those that are spiritual mind the things of the Spirit, so their company tends to make you spiritually minded, as they are (Romans 8:7–8). Though there are some useful qualities and gifts in some that are ungodly, and some lamentable faults in many that are spiritual; yet experience will show you so great a difference between them in the main, in heart and life, as will make you more easily believe the difference that will be between them in the life to come.

6. Another means is serious meditation on the life to come, and the way to it; which, though all cannot manage so methodically as some, yet all should in some measure and season be acquainted with it.

7. The last means is to choose some prudent, faithful guide and counselor for your soul, [35] to open to him those cases which are not fit for all to know, and to resolve and advise you in cases that are too hard for you: not to lead you blindfold in the cause of any misled or ambitious men, nor to bind you to his peculiar notions, against the Scripture or the church of God; but to be to your soul as a physician to your body, or a lawyer to your property, to help you where they are wiser than you, and where you need their help.

Resolve now that instead of your idle company and pastime, your excessive cares and sinful pleasures, you will wait on God in the timely use of these his own appointed means; and you will find that he has not appointed them in vain, and that you shall not lose your labor.

## Baxter's notes

Reproduced exactly as in the source, except that Baxter's English is modernized. A draft English translation follows each Latin passage in parentheses; the translations were prepared for this project and are not from a published edition, so have a Latinist check them before relying on fine points.

[34] How penitents of old did rise even from a particular sin, judge by these words of Pacianus, Parænes. ad Pœnit. Bibl. Pat. To. 3. p. 74. (Pacian's *Exhortation to Penitents*, in the *Library of the Fathers*, vol. 3, p. 74): "You must not only do that which may be seen by the priest, and praised by the bishop—to weep before the church, to lament a lost or sinful life in a sordid garment, to fast, to pray, to roll on the earth; if anyone invites you to the bath (or such pleasures) to refuse to go: if anyone bids you to a feast, to say, These things are for the happy; I have sinned against God, and am in danger of perishing forever! What should I do at banquets, who have wronged the Lord? Besides these, you must take the poor by the hand, you must plead with the widow, lie at the feet of the presbyters, beg the church to forgive you and pray for you: you must try all means rather than perish."

[35] Of how great concernment faithful pastors are for the conversion of the ungodly, see a Jesuit, Acosta, lib. 4. c. l, 4. (book 4, chapters 1 and 4) Infinitum esset cætera persequi, quæ contra hos fatuos principes tanaos, contra pastores stultos, vel potius idola pastorum, contra seipsos potius pascentes, contra væsanos prophetas, contra sacerdotes contemptores, atque arrogantes, contra stercus solennitatum, contra popularis plausus captatores, contra inexplebiles pecuniæ gurgites, cæterasque pestes, propheticus sermo declamat. Vix alias sancti patres plenioribus velis feruntur in Pelagiis, quam cum de sacerdotali contumelia oratio est. Acosta, ib. p. 353. (It would be endless to pursue the rest of what the prophetic word thunders against these foolish princes of Tanis, against foolish shepherds, or rather idols of shepherds, against those who feed themselves rather than the flock, against raving prophets, against contemptuous and arrogant priests, against the dung of solemn feasts, against hunters of popular applause, against insatiable whirlpools of money, and the other plagues. The holy fathers are scarcely ever carried with fuller sails on the open sea than when the discourse is about the contempt of priests. Acosta, same place, p. 353.) Non est iste sacerdos, non est sed infestus, atrox, dolosus, illusor sui, et lupus in dominicum gregem ovina pella armatus. Ibid. (He is not a priest, he is not, but a hostile, savage, deceitful man, a deceiver of himself, and a wolf against the Lord's flock, armed with a sheepskin. Same place.)

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **1 Peter 2:1–2 (important):** Baxter quotes the King James, "desire the sincere milk of the word." The NET reads "pure, spiritual milk," without "of the word." Baxter is using the verse to make the point that new Christians should long for Scripture, and the NET's "spiritual milk" loses that. The NET also has "slander" for "evil speakings." Flagged; an alternative translation would carry it better.
- **Psalm 1:2–3:** Baxter's "the law of the Lord" is "the Lord's commands" in the NET. Baxter cites verses 2–3 but quotes only verse 2.
- **Psalm 29:9:** the King James has "In his temple doth every one speak of his glory." The NET reads "Everyone in his temple says, 'Majestic!'" The NET's exclamation is shorter and less like Baxter's "glory," but it is the same verse.
- **Acts 9:11:** Baxter's "Behold, he prayeth" is "For he is praying" in the NET.
- **Galatians 4:6; Romans 8:15, 26, 27:** the sentence about the Spirit helping our weakness and interceding with groans is Baxter's paraphrase of Romans 8:26–27, not a quotation. We kept it modernized. "Abba, Father" is as in the NET.
- **Luke 11:9; Proverbs 28:13:** NET wording. The NET uses "transgressions" where the King James has "sins."
- **"the carnal mind … is enmity against God"** (item 5): an unmarked allusion to Romans 8:7, which Baxter cites at the end of the sentence. We kept his wording; the NET's rendering of the verse is in the note to Direction 9.
- **garbled sentence** (item 7): the source reads "to open those cases to which are not fit for all to know," which looks like a missing word ("to whom"). We read it as "to open to him those cases which are not fit for all to know."
- **make him amends** (item 4): Baxter's "make him satisfaction," meaning to put right a wrong done. **pleasure-loving:** his "sensual." **chattering:** his "prating." **foolishness:** his "dotage."
- **the cause of … men** (item 7): Baxter's "after the interest of," meaning in the cause or faction of. **misled:** his "seduced." **peculiar notions:** his "singular conceits."
- **Jehovah** (item 2): kept as Baxter's name for God.
- **Pacianus** (note 34): Pacian of Barcelona (fourth century). Baxter quotes his advice to penitents in English. **Acosta** (note 35): see the notes to Direction 5. The Latin names Old Testament targets: "princes of Tanis" is Isaiah 19:11, the foolish counsellors of Pharaoh; "the dung of solemn feasts" echoes Malachi 2:3.
