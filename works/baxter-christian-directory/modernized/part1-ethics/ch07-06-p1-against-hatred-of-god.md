# Chapter 7: Title 6, against sinful hatred of God

**Title 6. Directions against sinful hatred, aversion, or backwardness towards God.** [336]

The hatred to God and backwardness to his service, which is the chief part of this sin, is to be cured according to the directions in the first chapter, as a state of wickedness is: and more I shall say later, about the worship of God; and chapter 3, direction 11 contains the cure also. Only here I shall add a few directions to a God-hating generation.

**Direction 1. The first thing you have to do is to discover this to be your sin.**

For you are confident that you love God above all, while you hate him above all, even above the devil. You will confess that this is horrid wickedness, where it is found, and well deserves damnation: be careful that your own confession does not judge you. Remember then that it is not the bare name that we now speak of: I know that God's name is most honored, and the devil's name is most hated. Nor is it everything in God that is hated: none hates his mercifulness and goodness as such. Nor is it everything in the devil that is loved: none loves his hatred to man, nor his cruelty in tormenting men. But the holiness of God, which is what man must receive the image of, and be conformed to, is hated by the unholy; and the devil's unholiness, and friendship to men's sin and indulgence in physical pleasure, is loved by the pleasure-loving and unholy. And this hatred of God (and love of the devil) one would think you might easily perceive:

1. In that you would rather God were not so just and holy; you would rather he had never commanded you to be holy, but left you to live as your flesh would have you: you would rather God were indifferent as to your sins, and would give you leave to follow your lusts. Such a God you would have: and a God that will damn you unless you are holy, and hate your sins and forsake them, you do not like, you cannot abide, but indeed do hate him.

2. Therefore you will not believe that God is such a holy, sin-hating God: because you would not have him so, you will not believe he is so; and so hate his nature, while you believe that you love him; and love but an idol of your unholy fantasies. [337] Psalm 50:21–22, "When you did these things, I was silent, so you thought I was exactly like you. But now I will condemn you and state my case against you. Carefully consider this, you who reject God. Otherwise I will rip you to shreds and no one will be able to rescue you."

3. You do not love the holiness of the word of God, which bears his image. You do not love these strict and holy passages in it, John 3:3, 5; Luke 14:26, 33; Matthew 18:3; Romans 8:13; Colossians 3:1–4; 2 Corinthians 5:17, with abundance more. You would rather have had a Scripture that would have left you your ambition, greed, lust, and appetite to their liberties; and that had said nothing for the absolute necessity of holiness, nor had condemned the ungodly.

4. You do not love the holiest ministers or servants of Christ, that most powerfully preach his holy word, or that most carefully, seriously, and zealously obey it; your hearts rise against them, when they bring in the light, which shows that your deeds and you are evil, John 3:19–20. They are an eyesore to you: your hearts do not rise so much against whoremongers, swearers, liars, drunkards, atheists, or unbelievers, as against them. What sort of persons on the face of the earth are so hated by the ungodly in all nations, and of all ranks, and treated by them so cruelly, and pursued by them so implacably, as the holiest servants of the Lord are?

5. You do not love to call upon God in serious, fervent, spiritual prayer, praises, and thanksgiving: you are quickly weary of it; you would rather be at a play, or gambling, or a feast: your hearts rise against holy worship as a tedious, irksome thing.

6. You do not love holy, edifying talk of God, and of heavenly things: your hearts rise against it, and you hate and scorn it, as if all serious talk of God were but hypocrisy, and God were to be banished out of our conversation.

7. You cannot abide the serious, frequent thoughts of God in secret; but would rather stuff your minds with thoughts of your horses, or hawks, or finery, or honor, or promotions, or amusements, or entertainments, or business and labors in the world; so that one hour of a thousand or ten thousand was never spent in serious, delightful thoughts of God, his holy truths, or works, or kingdom.

8. You do not love the blessed day of judgment, when Christ will come with his holy angels to judge the world, to justify his accused and abused servants, "to be glorified among his saints and admired ... among all who have believed," 2 Thessalonians 1:8–11. And can you be so blind after all this as not to see that you are haters of God?

**Direction 2. Know God better, and you cannot hate him; especially know the beauty and glorious excellency of that holiness and justice which you hate.**

Should the sun be darkened or disgraced, because sore eyes cannot endure its light? Must kings and judges be all corrupt, or change their laws, and turn all men loose to do what they please, because criminals and licentious men would have it so?

**Direction 3. Know God and holiness as they are to you yourself; and then you will know them not only to be best for you, as the sun is to the world, and as life and health are to your body, but to be your only good and happiness; and then you cannot help but love them.**

Your prejudice and false notions of God and holiness cause your hatred.

**Direction 4. Cast away your cursed unbelief.**

If you do not believe what the Scripture says of God and man, and of the soul's immortality, and the life to come, you will then hate all that is holy as a deceit, and needless troubler of the world. But if once you believe well the word of God, and the life everlasting, you will have another heart.

**Direction 5. Away with your beastly, blinding indulgence in physical pleasure.**

While you are a slave to your flesh, and lusts, and appetite, and its interest reigns in you, you cannot help but hate that holiness which is against it, and hate that God that forbids it, and tells you that he will judge you and damn you for it if you do not forsake it: this is the true cause of the hatred of God and godliness in the world. God's laws condemn the very life and pleasure of the fleshly man. Godliness is irreconcilable to concupiscence and the carnal interest. Lay aside your fleshly mind and interest, or, as sure as you are a man, you will be judged and damned as an enemy to God. Do you not feel that this is the cause of your enmity, that God puts you on unpleasing (holy) courses, and will not let you please your flesh, but frightens you with the threatenings of hell? [338] Romans 8:6–8, "For the outlook of the flesh is death, but the outlook of the Spirit is life and peace, because the outlook of the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to the law of God, nor is it able to do so. Those who are in the flesh cannot please God." Verse 13, "if you live according to the flesh, you will die." "It is hard for you to kick against the goads," Acts 9:5. "One who argues with his Creator is in grave danger," Isaiah 45:9. Read Luke 19:27.

**Direction 6. Draw near and accustom your soul to serious thoughts of God; for it is unfamiliarity that makes you the more averse to him.**

We have less pleasure in the company of strangers than of familiar acquaintance. Reconciliation must be made by coming nearer, and not by keeping at a distance still.

**Direction 7. Study well the wonderful love and mercy which he has manifested to your soul in the redemption wrought by Jesus Christ, in the covenant of grace, in all the patience he has exercised towards you, and all his offers of mercy and salvation, entreating you to turn and live.**

Can you remember what God has done for you all your life, and how patiently and mercifully he has dealt with you, and yet can you hate him, or your heart be against him?

**Direction 8. Do not judge God or holiness by the faults of any men that have seemed holy.**

No more than you will blame the sun, because thieves rob by the light of it; or because some men are half-blind. God hates sin in them and you, wherever he finds it. Judge God and holiness by his own nature and true effects, and by the holy Scripture, and not by the crimes of sinners which he condemns, who, if they had been more holy, would have offended less.

**Direction 9. Come among the godly, and try a holy life for a while, and do not judge it or them that use it by the reports of the devil and wicked men.**

Malice will speak ill of God himself, and of his holiest servants. Can worse be said than was said of Christ himself, and his apostles? The devil was not ashamed to slander Job to God's own face, and tell God that he was such a one as that a little trial to his flesh would turn him from his godliness. But those that come near and try the ways and servants of God do find that the devil slandered them.

**Direction 10. Remember your near approaching end, and how dreadful it will be to be found and judged among the malignant enemies of holiness.**

"And if the righteous are barely saved, what will become of the ungodly and sinners?" 1 Peter 4:18. Then what would you give to be one of those holy ones that now you hate? and to be judged as those that lived in that holiness which your malignant heart could not abide? Then you will wish that you had lived and died as the righteous, that your latter end might have been like his.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording here was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title:** "Tit." in the source is Baxter's abbreviation for "Title," his word for one of the ten divisions of Chapter 7 (Directions for the Government of the Passions). We keep "Title."
- **Heading:** "Aversation" becomes "aversion." "Backwardness towards God" (reluctance, unwillingness) is kept.
- **Direction headings:** Baxter runs each Direction as one paragraph beginning "_Direct._ I." Following the other Chapter 7 files, we set his opening sentence as the bold heading and the rest as the body; no words are added or dropped.
- **Cross-references:** "the first chapter" (on conversion) and "chapter 3, direction 11" are Baxter's; not checked against those files. "More I shall say later, about the worship of God" and note 336 ("Of hatred to men I shall speak later") point forward; not checked.
- **Psalm 50:21–22:** The source prints "Psal. 1. 21, 22," which is the Gutenberg text's rendering of "Psal. l." (Roman numeral 50); Psalm 1 has only six verses. We cite Psalm 50:21–22. NET wording. Where the King James has "ye that forget God," the NET has "you who reject God," and for "set them in order before thine eyes" it has "state my case against you." Baxter's point (people imagine God is like themselves) is carried by both; "forget" versus "reject" is a shift worth noticing. Flagged.
- **Romans 8:6–8 (important):** Baxter leans on the King James "carnally minded" and "the carnal mind is enmity against God," and his own sentence just before speaks of "your enmity." The NET reads "the outlook of the flesh is hostile to God." The sense (hostility to God) holds, but the word "enmity" that links Baxter's sentence to the verse is gone, and "carnal mind" becomes "outlook of the flesh." Flagged.
- **Romans 8:13:** Baxter quotes only the first clause ("If ye live after the flesh ye shall die"); we give the NET clause without its opening "for" and parenthesis. His "Ver. 13" is rendered "Verse 13."
- **Acts 9:5 (Baxter's wording kept):** "It is hard for thee to kick against the pricks" is in the King James at Acts 9:5, but the NET text of 9:5 does not contain it (it reads only "Who are you, Lord?" ... "I am Jesus whom you are persecuting!"), following manuscripts that lack the words there. So we keep Baxter's wording, modernized ("pricks" to "goads"), and keep his reference. The NET has the saying at Acts 26:14: "You are hurting yourself by kicking against the goads."
- **Isaiah 45:9:** The King James "Woe to him that striveth with his Maker" becomes the NET "One who argues with his Creator is in grave danger." The cry of "woe" is softened to a warning; the sense is close.
- **2 Thessalonians 1:8–11:** Baxter's reference kept; the quoted words are from verse 10. NET wording; "..." marks the NET's "on that day," which Baxter's King James phrase does not have. The King James "glorified in his saints" becomes "glorified among his saints."
- **1 Peter 4:18:** NET wording. The King James "where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear?" (an image of appearing at judgment, which fits Baxter's "found and judged") becomes "what will become of the ungodly and sinners?" Flagged lightly.
- **Cited only:** John 3:3, 5; Luke 14:26, 33; Matthew 18:3; Romans 8:13; Colossians 3:1–4; 2 Corinthians 5:17; John 3:19–20; Luke 19:27.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "bring in the light, which shows that your deeds and you are evil" (John 3:19–20, which he cites); "turn and live" (Ezekiel 18:32, 33:11); the devil slandering Job to God's face (Job 1:9–11, 2:4–5); "that your latter end might have been like his" (Numbers 23:10, Balaam's "let my last end be like his" in the King James). These references are ours, not in Baxter's text.
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): anon to later; containeth to contains; take heed lest ... judge thee to be careful that ... does not judge you; sensuality to indulgence in physical pleasure; sensual to pleasure-loving; had rather to would rather; covetousness to greed; infidels to unbelievers; degrees to ranks; used (treated) to treated; gaming to gambling; discourse to talk / conversation; bravery (fine clothes, show) to finery; preferments to promotions; sports to amusements; list (wish) to please; malefactors to criminals; conceits to notions; canst not choose but to cannot help but; unreconcilable to irreconcilable; lay by to lay aside; affrighteth to frightens; strangeness (being a stranger to him) to unfamiliarity; censure to blame; purblind to half-blind; proper (own) nature to own nature; had less offended to would have offended less; awhile to for a while; belie to slander.
- **Kept:** "cannot abide" (cannot bear); "give you leave"; "whoremongers" (Baxter's blunt word); "carnal" (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh); "concupiscence" (a theological term for sinful, disordered desire); "malignant" (in Baxter's day, of a will hostile to good, not a medical word); "the carnal interest" (the concerns of the flesh).
- **Note 338:** "Pene" is printed for classical "paene" (almost); kept as printed. "ad Eccles. Cath." is taken as Salvian's work addressed to the Catholic (universal) church, usually known as *Ad Ecclesiam*; we have not confirmed the edition Baxter used. "Catholic" here means the universal church. The Jerome sentence is attributed by Baxter to Jerome's commentary on Galatians 5; it may echo Galatians 4:16 (Paul asking whether he has become their enemy by telling them the truth). We have not checked the exact place in Jerome.
