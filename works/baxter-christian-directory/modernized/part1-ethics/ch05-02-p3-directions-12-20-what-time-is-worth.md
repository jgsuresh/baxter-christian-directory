# Chapter 5: Title 1, Directions 12-20 (what time is worth)

**Direction 12. Never forget what attendance you have while you are idling or sinning away your time; how the patience and mercy of God are waiting for you; and how sun and moon and all the creatures are all the while attending on you.**

And must God stand by, while you are yet a little longer abusing and offending him? Must God wait till your cards, and dice, and pride, and worldly, unnecessary cares will dismiss you, and spare you for his service? Must he wait on the devil, the world, and the flesh, to take their leavings, and wait till they have done with you? Can you marvel if he makes you pay for this? if he turns away, and leaves you to spend your time in as much emptiness and idleness as you desire? Must God and all his creatures wait on a careless sinner, while he is at his fleshly pleasures? Must life and time be continued to him, while he is doing nothing that is worthy of his life and time? "They were disobedient long ago when God patiently waited in the days of Noah" (1 Peter 3:20); but how dearly did they pay for the contempt of this forbearance!

**Direction 13. Consider soberly the ends for which your life and time are given you by God.**

God did not make such a creature as man for nothing; he never gave you an hour's time for nothing. The life and time of animals and plants are given them to be serviceable to you; but what is yours for? Do you think in your conscience that any of your time is given you in vain? When you are lazing, or idling, or playing it away, do you think in your conscience that you are wisely and honestly answering the ends of your creation, and redemption, and hourly preservation? Do you think that God is so unwise, or disregardful of your time and you, as to give you more than you have need of? You will blame your tailor if he cuts out more cloth than will make your garments fit for you, and suitable to your use: and you will blame your shoemaker, if he makes your shoes too big for you: and do you think that God is so lavish of time, or so unskillful in his works of providence, as to cut you out more time, than the work which he has cut you out requires? He that will call you to a reckoning for all, has certainly given you none in vain. If you can find an hour that you have nothing to do with, and must give no account for, let that be the hour of your pastime. But if you knew your need, your danger, your hopes, and your work, you would never dream of having time to spare. For my own part, I must tell you, if you have time to spare, your case is very much different from mine. It is the daily trouble and burden of my mind, to see how slowly my work goes on, and how hastily my time; and how much I am likely to leave undone which I would gladly dispatch! How great and important businesses are to be done, and how short that life is likely to be, in which they must be done, if ever! It seems to me if every day were as long as ten, it would not be too long for the work which is every day before me, though not incumbent on me as my present duty, (for God does not require impossibilities,) yet exceedingly desirable to be done. It is the work that makes the time a mercy; the time is for the work. If my work were done, which the good of the church and my soul requires, what cause would I have to be glad of the ending of my time, and to say with Simeon, "Now, ... Sovereign Lord, permit your servant to depart in peace." Remember then that God never gave you one minute to spend in vain; but your very ease, and rest, and recreations must be but such and so much as fit you for your work; and as help it on, and do not hinder it. He redeemed and preserves us, that we "may serve him ... in holiness and righteousness before him for as long as we live" (Luke 1:74, 75).

**Direction 14. Remember always that the time of this short, uncertain life is all that you will ever have, for your preparation for your endless life.**

When this is spent, whether well or ill, you will have no more. God will not try those with another life on earth, that have cast away and misspent this. [282] There is no returning here from the dead, to mend that which you did amiss here. What good you will do, must now be done; and what grace you would get, must now be gotten; and what preparation for eternity you will ever make, must now be made! "Look, now is the acceptable time; look, now is the day of salvation!" (2 Corinthians 6:2). "Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says, 'Oh, that today you would listen as he speaks! Do not harden your hearts ...' ... But exhort one another each day, as long as it is called 'Today,' that none of you may become hardened by sin's deception" (Hebrews 3:7, 13). Do you have but one life here to live, and will you lose that one, or any part of it? Your time is already measured out: the hourglass is turned upon you. "Then the angel ... raised his right hand to heaven and swore by the one who lives forever and ever, ... 'There will be no more delay!'" (Revelation 10:5, 6). Therefore "whatever you find to do with your hands, do it with all your might, because there is neither work nor planning nor knowledge nor wisdom in the grave, the place where you will eventually go" (Ecclesiastes 9:10). What then remains, but that "the time being short, and the form of these things passing away," you use the world as if you did not use it, and redeem this time for your eternal happiness (1 Corinthians 7:29).

**Direction 15. Remember always that sin and Satan will lose no time; and therefore it concerns you to lose none.**

"Your enemy the devil, like a roaring lion, is on the prowl looking for someone to devour" (1 Peter 5:8). "Be sober" therefore and "vigilant to resist him" (verses 8, 9). If he is busy, and you are idle, if he is at work in spreading his nets, and laying his snares for you, and you are at play and do not mind him, it is easy to foretell for you what will be the outcome. If your enemies are fighting, while you sit still or sleep, it is easy to predict who will have the victory. The weeds of corruption are continually growing; sin, like a constant spring, is always running; the world is always enticing; and the flesh is always inclining to its prohibited delights. None of these enemies will make a truce or a cease-fire with you, to sit as long as you sit still. So far are they from sparing you, while you are idle, or gratifying the flesh, that even this is the fruit and evidence of their industry and success. Lose no time then, and allow no interruptions of your work, till you can persuade your enemies to do the same.

**Direction 16. Consider what a senseless contradiction it is of you, to over-love your lives, and yet to cast away your time.**

What is your time but the duration of your lives? You are reluctant to die, and reluctant that your time should be at an end; and yet you can as wastefully cast it away, as if you were weary of it, or longed to be rid of it. Is it only the last hours that you are reluctant to lose? Are not the middle parts as precious, and to be spared and used well? Or is it only to have time, and not to use it, that you desire? No means are good for anything, but to further the attainment of the end: it is not good to you, if it does you no good. To have food or clothing without any use of them, is as bad as not to have them. If you saw a man tremble with fear lest his purse be taken from him, and yet take out his money himself, and cast it away, or give it all for a straw or feather, what would you think of that man's wits? And do you not do the like and worse, when you are afraid lest death should end your time, and yet you yourselves will idle it away, and play it away, and give it for a little worldly gain? But I know how it is with you: it is for the present pleasure of the flesh, and for the sweetness of life itself, that you value life, and are so reluctant to die, and not for any higher ends: but this is to be brutish, and to unman yourselves, and simply to cheapen your lives, while you idolize them. Such mad contradictions sin brings in. You make your life your ultimate end, and desire to live but for life itself, or the pleasures of life, and so you make it instead of God and heaven, which should be intended as your proper end: and yet while you do not refer it to these higher ends, and use it but for the present pleasure, you cheapen yourselves and it, as if man differed from a dog or other animal, but in some poor degree of present pleasure.

**Direction 17. Consider that in your loss of time, you lose all the mercies of that time.**

For time is pregnant with great, invaluable mercies. It is the case that contains the jewels. If you throw away the purse, you throw away the money that is in it. O what might you get in those precious hours which you cast away! How much better a treasure than money might you win! How much sweeter a pleasure than all your games and amusements might you enjoy! You might be petitioning God for life eternal. You might be using and increasing grace. You might be viewing by faith the blessed place and company in which you may abide forever. All this, and more, you are losing while you are losing time. You choose as a pleasure that heavy curse, "Your strength will be used up in vain" (Leviticus 26:20). Why do you not also take it for a pleasure, to cast away your gold or health? I tell you, a very little time is worth a great deal of gold and silver. You cast away a more precious commodity.

**Direction 18. Think seriously how Christ, and his apostles, and holiest servants in all ages spent their time.**

They spent it in praying, and preaching, and holy conversation, and in doing good, and in the works of their outward callings in subservience to these: but not in cards, or dice, or dancing, or stage-plays, or pampering the flesh, nor in the pursuit of the profits and honors of the world. I read where Christ "spent all night in prayer" (Luke 6:12), but not where he spent an hour in playing. I know you will say, that you do not expect to reach their degree of holiness. But let me remind you, that he is not sincere that does not desire to be perfect. And that he is graceless, who willfully keeps any beloved sin, which he would not rather be delivered from; and that willfully refuses any duty, and would not rather perform it as he ought. And that you are the more needy, though Christ, and his apostles, and servants, were the more holy. And that the poor have more need to beg, and work, and be sparing of what they have, than the rich. And therefore, if Christ and his holiest servants were sparing of their time, and spent it in works of holiness and obedience, have not you greater need to do so than they? Have not you more need to pray, and learn God's word, and prepare for death, than Christ and his apostles? Are you not more behind, as having lost much time? Let your needs instruct you.

**Direction 19. Do not forget that a spending time may come, when you will think all too little, that now you can provide, by the most diligent redeeming of your time.**

If a garrison expects a siege, so sharp and long as will use up their provisions, they will prepare accordingly, that they do not perish by famine. Temptations may be stronger, and then you will find that you should now have gathered strength to overcome them, and have stirred yourself in the getting day, that you might be able to stand in the evil day (Ephesians 6:13). It is those that now loiter and lose their time, and do not gather knowledge and strength of grace, who fall in trial: when sufferings for righteousness' sake will be as a siege to you, and when poverty, wrongs, provocations, sickness, and the face of death, will be as a siege to you, then you will find all your faith, and hope, and love, and comfort to be too little; and then you will wish that you had now stirred yourself, and laid in better provision, and "laid up a good foundation or treasure in store for the time to come" (1 Timothy 6:19).

**Direction 20. Lastly, do not forget how time is valued by the damned, whose time and hope are gone forever; and how you will value it yourself if you sin your soul into that woeful state.**

What do you think those miserable creatures would now give (if they had it) but for one day's time, upon those terms of mercy on which you now enjoy it? [283] Would they sleep it away, or be at their games and merriments, while God is offering them Christ and grace? Do you think they do not set a higher price on time and mercy, than sinners upon earth? Does it not tear their very hearts forever, to think how madly they consumed their lives, and wasted the only time that was given them to prepare for their salvation? Do those in hell now think them wise, that are idling or playing away their time on earth? Oh no! Their feeling and experience sufficiently refutes all that time-wasters now plead for their foolish wastefulness. I do not believe that you can at once believe the word of God, concerning the state of damned souls, and yet believe that your idle and empty spending of time would not vex your conscience, and make you even rage against yourself, if ever sin should bring you there! O then you would see, that you had greater matters to have spent your time in, and that it deserved a higher esteem and better use. O man! beg the Lord to prevent such a conviction, and give you a heart to prize your time before it is gone; and to know the worth of it, before you know the want of it.

## Translator's notes

- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md). All NET wording was fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Title; Directions 12-20:** "Title" is Baxter's word (abbreviated "Tit.") for a heading or division of Chapter 5. This file finishes Title 1, "The contemplative directions for redeeming time," with Directions 12 through 20; the range begins at the start of Direction 12.
- **1 Peter 3:20** (Direction 12): Baxter's King James-style wording was "The long-suffering of God did wait on the disobedient in the days of Noah." The NET puts the disobedience first ("They were disobedient long ago when God patiently waited ..."). The sense Baxter leans on (God's patient waiting on the disobedient) is the same.
- **Luke 2:29** (Direction 13, Simeon): no reference in Baxter's text. His King James wording "Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace" leaves out "according to thy word"; we mark that with "..." in the NET wording.
- **Luke 1:74-75** (Direction 13): Baxter leaves out "without fear" (which the King James has, as the NET does); marked with "...". NET "for as long as we live" stands for his "all the days of our lives."
- **2 Corinthians 6:2:** NET "acceptable time" for Baxter's "accepted time." Same point.
- **Hebrews 3:7, 13** (Direction 14): Baxter quotes 3:7-8a and 3:13 together; the gap is marked "... ...". **NET flag:** the King James "To-day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts" is a condition followed by a warning; the NET renders 3:7 as a wish ("Oh, that today you would listen as he speaks!"). Baxter's stress on "today" survives, and the NET keeps the warning in "Do not harden your hearts." In 3:13 the NET "sin's deception" stands for "the deceitfulness of sin."
- **Revelation 10:5-6** (Direction 14): **NET flag, and an important one.** Baxter's King James text ends "that time should be no longer," and he uses it to mean that the time allotted to you will run out. The NET reads "There will be no more delay!", which reads as a declaration that God's purpose will no longer be delayed, not as a statement about the end of time as such. The verse still bears on his warning, but the phrase "time should be no longer" that his point leans on is not in the NET. Baxter also leaves out "I saw standing on the sea and on the land" and "who created heaven ... and the sea and what is in it" (marked "..."); the NET has "his right hand" where Baxter has "his hand."
- **Ecclesiastes 9:10:** NET "planning" for King James "device," and "the place where you will eventually go" for "whither thou goest."
- **1 Corinthians 7:29** (end of Direction 14): "the time being short, and the form of these things passing away" is Baxter's paraphrase blending 7:29 and 7:31, kept in his quotation marks. NET: "The time is short" (7:29) and "For the present shape of this world is passing away" (7:31). "form" is our word for his "fashion" (in the old sense of shape or form). "use the world as if you did not use it" (not in his quotation marks) echoes 7:31, NET "those who use the world as though they were not using it to the full."
- **1 Peter 5:8-9** (Direction 15): the first quotation is the NET of 5:8. Baxter's "Be sober" matches the NET of 5:8; "vigilant to resist him" is his blend of 5:8 ("vigilant," NET "alert") and 5:9 ("Resist him"), kept in his wording. **Reference corrected:** Baxter printed "ver. 7, 9"; "Be sober" is in verse 8, not 7, so we give "verses 8, 9."
- **Leviticus 26:20:** NET "will be used up in vain" for "shall be spent in vain."
- **Luke 6:12:** Baxter's "all night in praying" is a loose form of the King James "continued all night in prayer to God"; we give the NET phrase "spent all night in prayer" (so his "was" before the quotation drops out of the sentence).
- **Ephesians 6:13** (Direction 19): not in quotation marks in Baxter; kept in his wording with his reference. NET: "so that you may be able to stand your ground on the evil day."
- **1 Timothy 6:19:** Baxter's quotation is his own blend of the King James "laying up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come," with "or treasure" added; kept in his wording. NET: "In this way they will save up a treasure for themselves as a firm foundation for the future."
- **attendance** (Direction 12): those waiting on you, as servants attend a master: God's patience and the creatures. **waiting:** Baxter's "staying"; "wait" also for his "stay till." **emptiness:** his "vanity."
- **animals:** his "brutes" (Direction 13) and "brute" (Direction 16). **brutish** is kept. **lazing:** his "slugging" (lying idle, like a sluggard). **fit; suitable:** his "meet; agreeable." **likely:** his "like." **gladly:** his "fain." **It seems to me:** his "Methinks." **incumbent** is kept.
- **Remember always:** his "Remember still" (Directions 14 and 15); "always" also for "still" in Direction 15's list. **hourglass:** his "glass."
- **foretell for you:** his "foretell you." **predict:** his "prognosticate." **cease-fire:** his "cessation" (of fighting). **sparing:** his "forbearing." **allow:** his "admit of."
- **reluctant:** his "loth." **wastefully:** his "prodigally." **used well:** his "improved" (to improve time is to make good use of it, the theme of the chapter). **clothing:** his "raiment." **worldly gain:** his "worldly pelf" (money, with a sense of contempt). **cheapen:** his "vilify" (to make vile or of little worth, not to slander). **brings in:** his "infers."
- **case:** his "cabinet" (a small case for valuables). **amusements:** his "sports." **petitioning:** his "soliciting."
- **conversation:** his "conference." **subservience:** his "subserviency." **remind:** his "remember" (used transitively). **behind:** his "behindhand." **needs:** his "wants" (Direction 18).
- **a spending time** (Direction 19): kept; a time when stores are used up, as in a siege, set against "the getting day" (a time for gathering stores), also kept. **stirred yourself:** his "bestirred you."
- **refutes:** his "confuteth." **foolish wastefulness:** his "sottish prodigality." **esteem and better use** (Direction 20): his "estimation and improvement"; "better" is added because "higher" does not read naturally with "use." **beg:** his "beseech." **prevent** here means forestall. **conviction:** being convicted, brought to see one's guilt. **want** (last sentence) is kept in the sense of lack, to keep his play on "worth" and "want."
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "redeem this time" (Ephesians 5:16; Colossians 4:5); the devil's nets and snares (2 Timothy 2:26); "sufferings for righteousness' sake" (Matthew 5:10; 1 Peter 3:14).
- **Note 282:** Baxter's *Now or Never* is his own book; the source lacks a final period after the title, added here. **Note 283:** Baxter cites "Cicero. Parad. 1.", presumably Cicero's *Paradoxa Stoicorum*; we have not checked the exact location of the sentence.
