# Chapter 1, Part 2 (continued): Temptations 10–12

**Temptation 10.** If this does not prevail, the devil will seek to carry the day by noise instead of reason; and will seek to keep you in jovial, merry, pleasure-loving company, who shall plead with pots of ale, and plays, and pleasures; and shall daily make a jest of godliness, and speak of the godly with scorn, as a company of fanatic hypocrites.

**Direction 10.** But consider that this is but the rage of fools, who speak of what they never understood. Did they ever try the way they speak against? Are they to be believed above God himself? Will they not eat their words at last themselves? Will their merry lives last always? Do they die as merrily as they live? and get themselves out of it as well as they promised to get you out of it? See Proverbs 13:20; 28:7; Ephesians 5:7, 11. He that will be cheated of his salvation, and forsake his God, for the ranting scorn of a distracted sinner, is worthy to be damned.

**Temptation 11.** Next he tells them that a godly life is so hard and tedious that if they should begin they would never endure to hold on, and therefore it is in vain to try it.

**Direction 11.** But this pretense is compounded of wickedness and madness. What but a wicked heart can make it so hard a thing to live in the love of God, and holiness, and in the hope and seeking of eternal life? Why should not this be a sweeter and pleasanter life than drinking, and roaring, and gambling, and fooling away time in vain; or than the enjoying of all the delights of the flesh? There is nothing but a sick, disordered heart against it, that is nauseated by that which in itself is most delightful. When grace has changed your hearts, it will be easy. Do you not see that others can hold on in it, and would not be as they were for all the world? And why may not you? God will help you: it is the office of Christ and the Spirit to help you: your encouragements are innumerable. The hardness is most at first; it is the longer, the easier. But what if it were hard? Is it not necessary? Is hell easier, and to be preferred to it? And will not heaven pay for all your cost and labor? Will you sit down in desperation, and resolve to let your salvation go, upon such foolish bugbear words as these?

**Temptation 12.** Next the devil's effort will be to find them so much employment with worldly cares, or hopes, or business that they shall find no leisure to be serious about the saving of their souls.

**Direction 12.** But this is a snare, though often successful, yet so irrational, and against so many warnings and witnesses, even of all men in the world, either first or last, at conversion or at death, that he who after all this will neglect his God and his salvation because he has worldly things to mind is worthy to be turned over to his choice, and to have no better help or portion in the hour of his need and distress. Of this sin I have spoken later, in chapter 4, part 6.

## Baxter's notes

None in these three pairs. (Footnote [43] and later belong to the sections that follow.)

## Translator's notes

- **No Scripture is quoted in quotation marks.** Baxter gives only references (Proverbs 13:20; 28:7; Ephesians 5:7, 11), which we converted to modern form. Scripture quotations elsewhere are from the NET Bible®; see [NOTICE.md](../../../../NOTICE.md).
- **pleasure-loving:** Baxter's "voluptuous," meaning given to pleasure and indulgence. **plead with pots of ale, and plays, and pleasures:** Baxter's "plead by pots, and plays, and pleasures," meaning they argue their case with drink and amusements rather than reasons. "Pots" are drinking vessels.
- **fanatic:** kept as Baxter's word. In the mouth of the scorners it is the label for serious Puritans.
- **carry the day:** Baxter's "carry it," meaning win the point.
- **get themselves out of it:** Baxter's "bring off themselves," meaning escape from danger. "Bring off" was a military phrase for rescue.
- **eat their words:** left as written; the idiom means retract what one said.
- **distracted:** left as in the earlier sections: deranged or out of one's mind.
- **pretense** (Direction 11): Baxter's "pretence," meaning an excuse or false reason, not "make-believe."
- **roaring:** left as written. In Baxter's day it meant riotous drinking and carousing.
- **disordered:** Baxter's "distempered," meaning out of order, unhealthy. **is nauseated by:** his "nauseateth."
- **bugbear:** an imaginary thing meant to frighten, here the claim that holiness is too hard. **foolish:** Baxter's "silly."
- **it is the longer, the easier** (Direction 11): the longer one continues in a godly life, the easier it becomes. Left as in Baxter's wording.
- **turned over to his choice** (Direction 12): left as written; it means given over to the consequences of his own choice. **often successful:** Baxter's "frequently prevalent." **need:** his "necessity."
- **"chap. iv. part 6"** (Direction 12): a later section on this same sin. The reference is Baxter's.
- **"either first or last, at conversion or at death"** (Direction 12): Baxter means that everyone who thinks about it comes to the same view, either when converted or when dying.
