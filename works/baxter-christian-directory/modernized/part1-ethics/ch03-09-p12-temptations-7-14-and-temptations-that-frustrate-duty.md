# Chapter 3: Grand Direction IX (part 12 of 13)

*This section begins partway through a Direction of Grand Direction 9, in the series of Temptations against holy duty (Temptation 7 and its Direction); it then moves to a new series, "Temptations to frustrate holy duties," and ends partway through it, at Temptation 3, whose Direction continues in the next file.*

**Temptation 7.** It will be so hard and long to learn, that you will never overcome it.

**Direction 7.** Willingness and diligence have the promise of God's help. Remember, it is a thing that must be done. When your own disuse and sin has made it hard, will you put God and your souls off with that as an excuse? If you had neglected to teach your child to speak or walk when it is young, should he therefore never learn? Will you despair, and let go of all your hope on this pretext? or will you hope to be saved without prayer and other holy duty? How foolish are both of these! Sick men must eat, though their stomachs are against it; they cannot live otherwise.

**Temptation 8.** But you find you are only the worse for duty, and never the better for it.

**Direction 8.** Satan will do what he can to make it go worse with you after than before. He will discourage you if he can, by hindering your success, so that he may make you think it is to no purpose: so, many preachers, because they have fished long and caught nothing, grow cold and heartless, and ready to sit down and say, "I will make no mention of his message. I will not speak as his messenger anymore" (Jeremiah 20:9). So in prayer, the sacrament, reproof, and so on, the devil makes great use of this: What good has it done you? But patience and perseverance win the crown. The beginning is seldom a time to perceive success: the carpenter is long at work before he raises a house; nature does not bring forth the plant or give birth the first day. Your lifetime is your working time. Do your part, and God will not fail in his part. It is his part to give success; and do you dare accuse him, or suspect him? There is more of the success of prayer to be believed than to be felt. If God has promised to hear, he does hear, and we must believe it whether we feel it or not. Prayers are often heard long before the thing is sent to us that we prayed for: we pray for heaven, but shall not be there till death. If Moses's message to Pharaoh ten times seemed lost, it was not lost for all that. What work would ever have been done, if on the first notion of unsuccessfulness it had been given up? Be glad that you have time to plow and sow, to do your part, and if God will give you fruit at last.

**Temptation 9.** But, says the tempter, it goes worse with you in the world, since you set yourself to read, and pray, and live obediently; you have been poorer, and sicker, and more despised since, than ever before: you are "a constant laughingstock. Everyone ridicules" you (Jeremiah 20:7). This is what you get by it.

**Direction 9.** He did not begin well that did not count that it might cost him more than this to be a holy Christian. If God in heaven is not enough to be your portion, never serve him, but find something better if you can. He that cannot lose the world cannot use it as he ought. If you would rather be at the devil's finding and treatment than at God's, you deserve to fare accordingly. Nay, if you think your soul itself worse, remember that we are not worst when we are troubled most: medicine makes a person sick when it works rightly.

**Temptation 10.** Satan fills many with an abundance of scruples about every duty, so that they come to it as sick persons to their food, with an irritable, quarreling disposition. This troubles them, and that troubles them; something is always amiss, so that they cannot get it down; this fault the minister has in praying or preaching; or some other circumstance is amiss, or some other fault is in the company that joins with them: and all is to turn them off from everything.

**Direction 10.** But do you mend the matter by casting off everything, or by running into greater inconveniences? Is not their imperfect prayer and communion better than your idle neglect of everything, or unwarranted division? It is a sign of an upright heart to be most about heart-observation, and quarrelsome with themselves; and the mark of hypocrites to be most quarrelsome against the manner of other men's performances, and to be easily driven by any pretenses from the worship of God and communion of saints.

**Temptation 11.** The devil will set one duty against another: reading against hearing; praying against preaching; private against public; outward and inward worship against each other; mercy and justice, piety and charity, against each other; and still labor to drive out the greater.

**Direction 11.** The work of God is a harmonious and well-composed frame: if you leave out a part you spoil the whole, and disadvantage yourselves in all the rest; place them rightly, and each part helps and does not hinder another; plead one for another, but cast by none.

**Temptation 12.** The commonest and sorest temptation is by taking away our appetite for holy duties, by reducing our feeling of our own need: when the soul is sleepy and feels no need of prayer, or reading, or hearing, or meditating, but thinks itself tolerably well without it; or else grows sick and is against it, and troubled to use it; so that every duty is like eating to a sick stomach, then it is easy to tempt it to neglect or omit many a duty: a little thing will serve to put it off, when men feel no need of it.

**Direction 12.** O keep up a lively sense of your needs: remember always that time is short, and death is near, and you are too unready. Keep acquaintance with your hearts and lives, and every day will tell you of your needs, which are greatest when they are least perceived.

**Temptation 13.** The tempter gains much by ascribing the success of holy means to our own effort, or to chance, or something else, and making us overlook that present benefit, which would greatly encourage us: as when we are delivered from sickness or danger after prayer, he tells you so you might have been delivered if you had never prayed. Was it not by the physician's care and skill, and by such an excellent medicine? If you prosper in any business, Was it not by your own planning and diligence?

**Direction 13.** This separating of God and means, when God works by means, is the folly of atheists. When God hears your prayer in sickness or other danger, he shows it by directing the physician or yourself to the fittest means, and blessing that means; and he is as truly the cause, and prayer the first means, as if he wrought your deliverance by a miracle. Do not many use the same physician, and medicine, and labor, and diligence, who yet fail? Just observation of the answers of prayer might do much to cure this. All our industry may say as Peter and John, "Why do you stare at us as if we had made this man walk by our own power or piety?" (Acts 3:12) when God is glorifying his grace, and owning his appointed means.

**Temptation 14.** Lastly, the devil sets up something else in opposition to holy duty, to make it seem unnecessary. In some he sets up their good desires, and says, God knows your heart without your expressing it; and you may have as good a heart at home as at church. In some he sets up superstitious follies of man's devising, instead of God's institution. In some he claims the Spirit against external duty, and says, The Spirit is all; the flesh profits nothing. Yea, in some he sets up Christ himself against Christ's ordinances, and says, It is not these, but Christ, that profits you.

**Direction 14.** This is mad contradiction: to set Christ against Christ, and the Spirit against the ordinances of the Spirit. Is it not Christ and the Spirit that appointed them? Does he not best know in what way he will give his grace? Can you not preserve the soul and life, without killing the body? Can you not have the water, and value the cistern or spring, without cutting off the pipes that must convey it? O wonderful! that Satan could make men so mad, as this reasoning has shown us that many are in our days. And to set up superstition or claim a good heart against God's worship, is to accuse him that appointed it of doing he knew not what, and to think that we are wiser than he! and to show a good heart by disobedience, pride, contempt of God and of his mercies!

**Temptations to frustrate holy duties, and make them ineffectual.**

The devil is exceedingly diligent in this: 1. That he may make the soul despair, and say, Now I have used all means in vain, there is no hope. 2. To double the sinner's misery by turning the very remedy into a disease. 3. To show his malice against Christ, and say, I have turned your own means to your dishonor.

Consider, therefore, how greatly we are concerned to do the work of God effectually. Means well used are the way to more grace, to communion with God, and to salvation; but badly used, they dishonor and provoke him, and destroy ourselves, like children that cut their fingers with the knife, when they should cut their meat with it.

**Temptation 1.** Duty is frustrated by false aims: as, 1. To procure God to bear with them in their sin (whereas it is the use of duty to destroy sin). 2. To make God satisfaction for sin (which is the work of Christ). 3. To merit grace (when the imperfection merits wrath). 4. To prosper in the world and escape affliction, James 4:3 (and so they are only serving their flesh, and desiring God to serve it). 5. To quiet conscience in a course of sin (by sinning more in offering the sacrifice of fools, Ecclesiastes 5:1, 2). 6. To be approved by men (and truly they have their reward, Matthew 6:5). 7. To be saved when they can keep the world and sin no longer (that is, to obtain that the gospel may all be false and God unjust).

**Direction 1.** First see that the heart is honest, and God, and heaven, and holiness most desired, else all that you do will lack right aims.

**Temptation 2.** When ignorance or error makes men take God for what he is not, thinking blasphemously of him, as if he were like them, and liked their sins, or were no lover of holiness, they frustrate all their worship of him.

**Direction 2.** Study God in his Son, in his word, in his saints, in his works: know him as described before, chapter 3, direction 4. And see that your wicked corrupted hearts, or willful forgetting of him, do not blind your understandings.

**Temptation 3.** To come to God in ourselves and out of Christ, and use his name only by custom, and not in faith and confidence.

## Translator's notes

- **Structure:** the range opens mid-series with Temptation 7 and its Direction. The source then has a centered heading ("Temptations to frustrate holy Duties, and make them ineffectual") and restarts the numbering at 1; we keep Baxter's numbering and render the heading in bold. The file ends after Temptation 3, with its Direction in the next file. Baxter's "Tempt." and "Direct." are rendered "Temptation" and "Direction," as in the neighboring files.
- **Scripture translation:** NET Bible, fetched from labs.bible.org.
- **Jeremiah 20:9 (flag):** Baxter's King James wording, "I will not make mention of him, nor speak any more in his name," becomes in the NET "I will make no mention of his message. I will not speak as his messenger anymore." The NET has "his message" where the King James has "him," and "as his messenger" for "in his name." The sense Baxter uses (a preacher tempted to give up speaking) is unchanged. In the NET the prophet goes on to say that the message becomes like a fire in him and he cannot hold it in; Baxter quotes only the discouraged half.
- **Jeremiah 20:7 (reference corrected; flag):** Baxter prints "Jer. xx. 8." The NET words that match his quotation ("a constant laughingstock. Everyone ridicules me") are in verse 7; NET verse 8 reads differently ("continual insults and derision"). We use 20:7. Baxter's King James-style quotation is "a derision daily, every one mocketh thee," in the tempter's voice; to fit that voice we put "you" outside the quotation marks after "ridicules" in place of the NET's "me." The NET's "laughingstock" carries the same idea of being mocked.
- **Acts 3:12 (flag):** Baxter's "Why look ye so earnestly on us, as if by our own power or holiness we had done this?" The NET reads "Why do you stare at us as if we had made this man walk by our own power or piety?" The NET names what was done (made the man walk) and uses "piety" for the King James "holiness." Neither changes Baxter's point, that the apostles disclaim credit for the means.
- **James 4:3** (reference only; Baxter gives no quotation). **Ecclesiastes 5:1, 2** (reference only; the phrase "sacrifice of fools" is Baxter's allusion; in the NET, 5:1 speaks of drawing near to listen "rather than to offer a sacrifice like fools"). **Matthew 6:5:** Baxter's "verily they have their reward" is an allusion; the NET reads "Truly I say to you, they have their reward!" We kept his wording in modern form.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "If Moses's message to Pharaoh ten times seem lost" (the repeated demands and plagues in Exodus; not marked by Baxter); "patience and perseverance win the crown" (possibly compare James 1:12; our suggestion only, not in Baxter's text); "The Spirit is all; the flesh profits nothing" (compare John 6:63).
- **Word swaps** (Baxter's word, then ours): catched / caught; rear (a house) / raises; birth (verb) / give birth; speed (worthy to speed accordingly) / fare; physic / medicine; aileth / troubles; fopperies / follies; distracted (contradiction) / mad; pretence / pretext, pretenses; pretend (the Spirit) / claim; conceit / notion; ends (of duty) / aims; necessities / needs; abating / reducing; contrivance / planning; usage / treatment; miscarry / fail; eject / drive out; go (to walk) / walk; heart-observation (kept; Baxter's term, "most about" meaning most occupied with); peevish / irritable; given off / given up (abandoned); "cast by" (kept; means cast aside); disadvantage (verb, kept).
- **Direction 8:** "Your life-time is your working time" is Baxter's saying; "ready to sit down" means inclined to give up.
- **Direction 9, "at the devil's finding":** being provided for by the devil, as a master finds (provides) for a servant.
- **Direction 10, "heart-observation":** careful watching of one's own heart. The pronouns "their" and "themselves" follow the source, which shifts from "you" to "them" in this Direction.
- **Temptation 14, "set up ... against":** to put forward as a rival to. "Ordinances" is kept: the appointed means of grace (preaching, sacraments, prayer).
- **Direction 14, "doing he knew not what":** as in the source. "cistern": a water tank or reservoir.
- **atheists** (Direction 13): Baxter's word for those who deny God's working through means.
- **Temptation 1, point 7:** "that the gospel may all be false and God unjust" is Baxter's ironic way of saying that such people want something that would make the gospel false.
- **Direction 2, cross-reference:** "chap. iii. direct. iv." is rendered "chapter 3, direction 4," probably the Grand Direction on knowing God earlier in this chapter (Grand Direction 4 in this edition); we have not checked it.
