# Part 2, Chapter 1: Directions 3-6, preparing for marriage, rational love, deliberation, and a godly spouse

**Direction 3. If God calls you to a married life, expect all these troubles, or most of them; and make particular preparation for each temptation, hardship, and duty which you must expect.**

Do not think that you are entering into a state of mere delight, lest it prove only a fool's paradise to you. See that you are equipped with marriage strength and patience, for the duties and sufferings of a married state, before you venture on it. Especially, 1. Be well provided against temptations to a worldly mind and life: for here you are likely to be most violently and dangerously assaulted. 2. See that you are well provided with conjugal affections: for they are necessary both to the duties and sufferings of a married life. And you should not enter upon the state without the necessary preparations. 3. See that you are well provided with marriage prudence and understanding, that you may be able to instruct and edify your families, and may live with them as men of knowledge, 1 Peter 3:7, and may manage all your business with discretion, Psalm 112:5. 4. See that you are provided with resolve and constancy, that you do not trouble yourself and your relatives by too-late repenting; and do not end up with "had I known," or *non putaram*. Fickleness and changeableness is no fit preparation for a state that only death can change. Let the love and resolutions which brought you into that state continue with you to the last. 5. See that you are provided with a diligence proportionate to the greatness of your undertaken duties. A lazy mind is unfit for one that enters himself voluntarily upon so much business; as a cowardly mind is unfit for him that enlists himself as a soldier for the wars. 6. See that you are well provided with marriage patience; to bear with the infirmities of others, and undergo the daily hardships of your life, which your business and necessities, and your own infirmities, will unavoidably bring on. To marry without all this preparation is as foolish as to go to sea without the necessary preparations for your voyage, or to go to war without armor or ammunition, or to go to work without tools or strength, or to go to buy food in the market when you have no money.

**Direction 4. Take special care that fancy and passion do not overrule reason, and friends' advice, in the choice of your condition, or of the person.**

I know you must have love for those that you marry; but that love must be rational, and such as you can justify in the severest trial, by the evidences of worth and fitness in the person whom you love. To say you love, but you do not know why, is more fitting for children or mad people than for those that are soberly entering upon a change of life of so great importance to them. A blind love which makes you think a person excellent and lovable, who in the eyes of the wisest that are impartial is not so at all, or makes you overvalue the person whom you fancy, and be infatuated with one as some admirable creature, that in the eyes of others is next to contemptible, this is only the indicator and evidence of your folly. And though you please yourselves in it, and honor it with the name of love, there is no one that is acquainted with it that will give it any better name than lust or fancy. And the marriage that is made by lust or fancy will never tend to solid contentment or true happiness; but either it will feed till death on the fuel that kindled it, and then go out in everlasting shame; or else more ordinarily it proves only a blaze, and turns into loathing and weariness of each other. And because this passion of lust (called love) is such a stupefying, blinding thing (like the longing of a woman with child), it is the duty of all that feel any touch of it to kindle upon their hearts, to call it at once to the trial, and to quench it effectively; and till that is done (if they have any remnants of sense or reason) to suspect their own understanding, and much more to trust the judgment and advice of others.

*In the margin: How to cure lustful love.*

The means to quench this lust called love I have explained at length before. I shall now only remind you of these few. 1. Keep apart, and at a sufficient distance from the person that you dote on. The nearness of the fire and fuel causes the combustion. Fancy and lust are inflamed by the senses. Keep out of sight, and in time the fever may abate. 2. Do not overvalue empty things. Do not think highly of a silken coat, or of the great names of ancestors, or of money, or lands, or of a painted or a spotted face, nor of that natural attractiveness called beauty: do not judge of things as children, but as men: do not play the fools in magnifying trifles, and overlooking inward, real worth. Would you fall in love with a flower or picture in this way? Consider what work the pox, or any other withering sickness, will make of that frail beauty which you so admire: think what a spectacle death will make of it; and how many thousands once more beautiful are turned now to common earth! and how many thousand souls are now in hell, that by a beautiful body were drowned in lust, and tempted to neglect themselves! and how few in the world you can name that were ever much the better for it! What a childish thing it is to dote on a book of tales and lies, because it has a beautiful, gilded cover! and to undervalue the writings of the wise, because they have a plain and humble outside! 3. Rule your thoughts, and do not let them run masterless as fancy shall command them. If reason cannot call off your thoughts from following a lustful desire and imagination, no wonder if one that rides on such an unbridled colt is thrown into the dirt. 4. Do not live idly, but let the business of your callings take up your time, and employ your thoughts. An idle, fleshly mind is the carcass where the vermin of lust crawl, and the nest where the devil hatches both this and many other destructive sins. 5. Lastly and chiefly, do not forget the concerns of your souls: remember how near you are to eternity, and what work you have to do for your salvation: do not forget the presence of God, nor the approach of death. Look often by faith into heaven and hell, and keep conscience tender; and then I assure you, you will find something else to mind than lust, and greater matters than a frail carcass to take up your thoughts; and you will feel that heavenly love within you which will extinguish earthly, carnal love.

**Direction 5. Do not be too hasty in your choice or resolution, but deliberate well, and thoroughly know the person on whom so much of the comfort or sorrow of your life will necessarily depend.**

Where repentance has no place, there is the greater care to be used to prevent it. Reason requires you to be well acquainted with those that you trust only with an important secret, much more with all your honor or estates; and most of all, with one whom you must trust with so much of the comfort of your lives, and your advantages for a better life. No care and caution can be too great in a matter of so great importance.

**Direction 6. Let no carnal motives persuade you to join yourself to an ungodly person; but let the holy fear of God be preferred in your choice before all worldly excellence whatever.**

Do not marry a swine for a golden trough; nor an ugly soul for an attractive body. Consider, 1. You will otherwise give cause of great suspicion that you are yourselves ungodly: for they that truly know the misery of an unrenewed soul, and the excellence of the image of God, can never be indifferent whether they are joined to the godly or the ungodly. To prefer things temporal before things spiritual habitually, and in the predominant acts of heart and life, is the certain mark of a graceless soul! And he that in so close a matter deliberately prefers riches or attractiveness in another before the image and fear of God gives a very dangerous sign of such a graceless heart and will. If you set more store by beauty or riches than by godliness, you have the surest mark that you are ungodly. If you do not set more store by them, how do you come deliberately to prefer them? How could you do a thing that exposes your ungodliness, and condemns you more clearly? And do you not show that you either do not believe the word of God, or else that you do not love him, and do not regard his interest? Otherwise you would take his friends as your friends, and his enemies as your enemies. Tell me, would you marry an enemy of your own, before any change and reconciliation? I am confident you would not. And can you so easily marry an enemy of God? If you do not know that all the ungodly and unsanctified are his enemies, you do not know, or do not believe, the word of God; which tells you that "the outlook of the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to the law of God, nor is it able to do so. Those who are in the flesh cannot please God" (Romans 8:7-8). 2. If you fear God yourselves, your chief end in marriage will be to have one that will be a helper to your soul, and further you in the way to heaven: but if you marry a person that is ungodly, either you have no such end, or else you may easily know you have no more wisely chosen the means than if you had chosen water to kindle the fire, or a bed of snow to keep you warm. Will an ignorant or ungodly person assist you in prayer and holy watchfulness, and stir you up to the love of God, and a heavenly mind? And can you so willingly lose all the spiritual benefit which you should principally desire and intend? 3. Indeed, instead of a helper, you will have a continual hinderer: when you should go to prayer, you will have one to pull you back, or to fill your minds with diversions or disturbances! When you should keep close to God in holy meditations, you will have one to cast in worldly thoughts, or trouble your minds with empty things or irritation. When you should talk of God and heavenly things, you will have one to stifle such talk, and fill your ears with idle, irrelevant, or worldly talk. And one such hindrance so near you, in your bosom, will be worse than a thousand further off. As an ungodly heart, which is nearest of all to us, is our greatest hindrance, so an ungodly husband or wife, which is next to that, is worse to us than many ungodly neighbors. And if you think that you can well enough overcome such hindrances, and your heart is so good that no such weights can keep it down, you only show that you have a proud, unhumbled heart, that is prepared for a fall. If you know yourselves, and the badness of your hearts, you will know that you have no need of hindrances in any holy work, and that all the helps in the world are little enough, and too little, to keep your souls in the love of God. 4. And such an ungodly companion will be to you a continual temptation to sin. Instead of stirring you up to good, you will have one to stir you up to evil, to passion, or discontent, or greed, or pride, or revenge, or indulgence in physical pleasure. And can you not sin enough without such a tempter? 5. And what a continual grief will it be to you, if you are believers, to have a child of the devil in your bosom! and to think how far you must be separated at death! and in what torments those must lie forever that are so dear to you now! 6. Indeed, such companions will be incapable of the principal part of your love. You may love them as husbands or wives, but you cannot love them as saints and members of Christ. And how great a lack this will be in your love, those know who know what this holy love is.

## Translator's notes

- **Where this file starts and stops:** it begins at Direction 3, whose "these troubles" refers back to the troubles of married life set out in the preceding Directions (not checked against that file). It stops partway through Direction 6, after point 6 of Baxter's reasons for not marrying an ungodly person; the next file continues the Direction.
- **1 Peter 3:7 (flag):** Baxter does not quote the verse but alludes to its King James wording, "dwell with them according to knowledge," so his phrase "live with them as men of knowledge" is kept. The NET reads "treat your wives with consideration," which loses the word "knowledge" that Baxter leans on (husbands need understanding so as to instruct their families).
- **Psalm 112:5 (corrected reference; flag):** the source prints "Psal. cxii. 15," but Psalm 112 has only 10 verses. The words Baxter alludes to, "he will guide his affairs with discretion," are the King James wording of verse 5, so we give Psalm 112:5. The NET reads "conducts his business honestly," which speaks of honesty, not the discretion (prudence) that Baxter's point rests on.
- **Romans 8:7-8 (flag):** NET wording. The King James text Baxter quotes reads "the carnal mind is enmity against God: for it is not subject to the law of God, neither indeed can be. So then they that are in the flesh cannot please God." Baxter's argument is that the ungodly are God's "enemies," which he draws from "enmity"; the NET's "hostile to God" carries the same sense less directly. The NET also lacks the "so then" that joins the two verses.
- **"had I wist," *non putaram*:** "had I wist" is an old proverbial phrase of regret, "had I known"; we give it as "had I known." *Non putaram* is Latin, kept as printed (English: I had not thought so, or I would not have thought it). Both stand for the excuse of one who regrets a choice too late.
- **"conjugal affections":** affections in Baxter's sense, the emotions and desires (kept; see the glossary); conjugal is the love proper to husband and wife.
- **"callings"** (Direction 4, point 4): kept; Baxter's word for one's occupation or vocation.
- **"carnal," "fleshly," "graceless," "unrenewed," "unsanctified":** kept as theological terms (unspiritual, ruled by the flesh; lacking saving grace; not born again; not made holy).
- **"fancy":** kept throughout. In Baxter it means a fond imagination or infatuation, close to the modern "fancy someone," not "liking" in general.
- **"like the longing of a woman with child":** Baxter's comparison of infatuation to the cravings of pregnancy; it reflects the view of his day and is kept as written.
- **"a painted or a spotted face":** painted means made up with cosmetics; spotted probably refers to the fashion of wearing small decorative patches on the face (we have not confirmed which Baxter meant).
- **"the pox":** kept. In Baxter's day this could mean smallpox, which often scarred the face, or another pox; we have not tried to decide which he meant.
- **"in your bosom":** kept; the image is of the closest intimacy, one who shares your life and bed.
- **"a child of the devil":** Baxter's blunt phrase for the unconverted spouse (compare 1 John 3:10, an unmarked allusion, not in the text), kept as written.
- **Views on marriage:** Baxter's insistence that a believer must not marry an ungodly person, and that such a marriage is evidence of the believer's own ungodliness, is kept exactly as he states it.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "a helper" for the spouse (Genesis 2:18); "a proud ... heart, that is prepared for a fall" (Proverbs 16:18); "take his friends as your friends, and his enemies as your enemies" (compare Psalm 139:21-22). These references are ours, not Baxter's.
- **Grammar kept as printed:** "Fickleness and changeableness is no fit preparation" keeps Baxter's singular verb; in Direction 4 the long sentence beginning "A blind love which makes you ..." restates its subject with "this is only the indicator ...," as in the source.
