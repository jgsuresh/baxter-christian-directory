# Part 2, Chapter 1: Questions about marrying an ungodly person; Directions 7-12, on choosing a spouse and making the marriage covenant

*Question.* But how can I tell who are godly, when there is so much hypocrisy in the world. *Answer.* At least you may know who is ungodly if it is plainly revealed. I do not take a scant knowledge for ungodliness, nor a nimble tongue for godliness: judge them by their love: as a man's love is, so is the man. If they love the word, and servants, and worship of God, and love a holy life, and hate the contrary, you may join with such, though their knowledge is small, and their abilities are weak; but if they have no love for these, but would rather live a common, careless, carnal life, you may well avoid them as ungodly.

*Question.* But if ungodly persons may marry, why may I not marry one that is ungodly? *Answer.* Though dogs and swine may join in breeding, it does not follow that men or women may join with them. Pardon the comparison, (while Christ calls the wicked dogs and swine, Matthew 7:6,) it only shows the badness of your inference. Unbelievers may marry, and yet we may not marry unbelievers. "Do not become partners with those who do not believe, for what partnership is there between righteousness and lawlessness, or what fellowship does light have with darkness? And what agreement does Christ have with Beliar? Or what does a believer share in common with an unbeliever? And what mutual agreement does the temple of God have with idols? For we are the temple of the living God ... Therefore 'come out from their midst, and be separate,' says the Lord, 'and touch no unclean thing,'" etc. (2 Corinthians 6:14-16).

*Question.* But I have no doubt that they may be converted: God can call them when he will: if there is only love, they will easily be won to be of the same mind as those they love are? *Answer.* 1. Then it seems because you love an ungodly person, you will be easily turned to be ungodly. If so, you are not much better already. If love will not draw you to their mind to be ungodly, why should you think love will draw them to your mind to be godly? Are you stronger in grace than they are in sin? 2. If you knew well what grace is, and what a sinful, unrenewed soul is, you would not think it so easy a matter to convert a soul. Why are there so few converted, if it is so easy a thing? You cannot make yourselves better by adding higher degrees to the grace you have: much less can you make another better, by giving them the grace which they do not have. 3. It is true that God is able to convert them when he will; and it is true that for all I know it may be done. But what of that? Will you in so weighty a case settle for a mere possibility? God can make a beggar rich, and for all you know to the contrary, he will do it; and yet you will not therefore marry a beggar: nor will you marry a leper, because God can heal him; why then should you marry an ungodly person, because God can convert him? See it done first, if you love your peace and safety.

*Question.* But what if my parents command me to marry an ungodly person? *Answer.* God having forbidden it, no parent has authority to command you to do so great a harm to yourself, any more than to cut your own throats, or to dismember your bodies.

*Question.* But what if I have a necessity of marrying, and can get no one but an ungodly person? *Answer.* If that is really your case, that your necessity is real, and you can get no other, I think it is lawful.

*Question.* But is it not better to have a good-natured person that is ungodly, than an ill-natured person that is religious, as many such are? And may not a bad man be a good husband? *Answer.* 1. A bad man may be a good tailor, or shoemaker, or carpenter, or seaman, because there is no moral virtue necessary to the well-doing of their work. But a bad man cannot be simply a good magistrate, or minister, or husband, or parent, because there is much moral virtue necessary to their duties. 2. A bad nature unmortified and untamed is inconsistent with true godliness; such persons may talk and profess what they please; but "if someone thinks he is religious yet does not bridle his tongue, and so deceives his heart, his religion is futile" (James 1:26). 3. I did not say that godliness alone is all that you must look for; though this is the first, yet more is necessary.

**Direction 7. Next to the fear of God, choose a nature or temperament that is not too much unsuitable to you.**

A clash of dispositions will be a continual vexation; and you will have a domestic war instead of love. Especially make sure of these following qualities. 1. That there is a loving, and not a selfish nature, that has no regard for another except for their own end. 2. That there is a nature reasonably quiet and patient, and not intolerably perverse and impossible to please. 3. That there is a sufficient degree of good sense; for no one can live lovingly and comfortably with a fool. 4. That there is a sufficient humility; for there is no quietness to be expected with the proud. 5. That there is a power to be silent, as well as to speak; for a babbling tongue is a continual vexation.

**Direction 8. Next to grace and nature, have a due and moderate regard for person, education, and wealth.**

1. So far have regard for the person as that there is no ill health to make your condition overly burdensome; nor any such deformity as may hinder your affections. 2. And so far have regard for parentage and education, as that there is no great unsuitableness of mind, nor any prejudiced opinions in religion, which may make you too unequal. Differing opinions in religion are much more tolerable in persons more distant, than in so near relations. And those that are raised too high in idleness and luxury, must have a thorough work of grace to make them fit for a low condition, and cure the pride and indulgence in physical pleasure which are taken for the honorable badges of their gentility; and it is scarcely worth considering how rich such are; for their pride and luxury will use up all of it, and be always in greater lack than honest, contented, temperate poverty.

**Direction 9. If God calls you to marriage, take notice of the helps and comforts of that condition, as well as of the hindrances and troubles; that you may cheerfully serve God in it, in the expectation of his blessing.**

Though man's corruption has filled that and every state of life with snares and troubles, yet from the beginning it was not so; God appointed it for mutual help, and as such it may be used. As a married life has its temptations and afflictions, so it has its particular benefits, which you are thankfully to accept and acknowledge to God. See Ecclesiastes 4:10-12. 1. It is a mercy with a view to the propagating of a people on earth to love and honor their Creator, and to serve God in the world and enjoy him forever. It is no small mercy to be the parents of a godly offspring; and this is the end of the institution of marriage (Malachi 2:15). And this parents may expect, if they are not lacking on their part; although sometimes their children prove ungodly. 2. It is a mercy to have a faithful friend, that loves you entirely, and is as true to you as yourself, to whom you may open your mind and share your affairs, and who would be ready to strengthen you, and divide the cares of your affairs and family with you, and help you to bear your burdens, and comfort you in your sorrows, and be the daily companion of your lives, and sharer of your joys and sorrows. 3. And it is a mercy to have so near a friend to be a helper to your soul; to join with you in prayer and other holy exercises; to watch over you and tell you of your sins and dangers, and to stir up in you the grace of God, and remind you of the life to come, and cheerfully accompany you in the ways of holiness. Proverbs 19:14, "A prudent wife is from the Lord." Thus it is said, Proverbs 18:22, "The one who has found a good wife has found what goodness is and obtained a delightful gift from the Lord." See Proverbs 31:10-12, etc.

**Direction 10. Let your marriage covenant be made understandingly, deliberately, heartily, in the fear of God, with a fixed resolution faithfully to perform it.**

Understand well all the duties of your relation before you enter into it; and do not rush into it as boys to a play, but with the sense of your duty, as those that engage themselves to a great deal of work of great importance towards God and towards each other. Address yourselves therefore beforehand to God for counsel, and earnestly beg his guidance and his blessing, and do not run without him, or before him. Reckon on the worst, and foresee all temptations which would diminish your affections, or make you unfaithful to each other; and see that you are fortified against them all.

**Direction 11. Be sure that God is the ultimate end of your marriage, and that you principally choose that state of life, that in it you may be most serviceable to him; and that you heartily devote yourselves and your families to God; that so it may be to you a sanctified condition.**

It is nothing but making God our guide and end that can sanctify our state of life. They that sincerely follow God's counsel, and aim at his glory, and do it to please him, will find God acknowledging and blessing their relation. But they that do it principally to please the flesh, to satisfy lust, and to increase their estates, and to have children surviving them to receive the fruits of their pride and greed, can expect to reap no better than they sow; and to have the flesh, the world, and the devil the masters of their family, according to their own desire and choice.

**Direction 12. At your first union (and through the rest of your lives) remember the day of your separation.**

And do not think that you are settling yourselves in a state of rest, or happiness, or permanence, but only taking on a companion in your travels. Whether you live in a married or an unmarried life, remember that you are hastening to the everlasting life, where there is neither "marrying nor giving in marriage" (1 Corinthians 7:29, 30). You are going as fast to another world in one state of life as in the other. You are only to help each other on your way, that your journey may be the easier for you, and that you may happily meet again in the heavenly Jerusalem. When worldlings marry, they take it for a settling of themselves in the world; and as regenerate persons begin the world anew, by beginning to lay up a treasure in heaven, so worldlings call their marriage their beginning the world, because then, as bound servants to the world, they set themselves to seek it with greater diligence than ever before. They only begin in marriage (as seekers) that life of folly, which when he had found what he sought, that rich man ended (Luke 12:19, 20), with an "I will do this: I will tear down my barns and build bigger ones, and there I will store all my grain and my goods. And I will say to myself, 'You have plenty of goods stored up for many years; relax, eat, drink, celebrate!' But God said to him, 'You fool! This very night your life will be demanded back from you, but who will get what you have prepared for yourself?'" If you would not die such fools, do not marry and live such worldlings.

## Translator's notes

- **Where this file starts and stops:** it begins partway through Direction 6 (do not marry an ungodly person), with the run of unnumbered Questions and Answers that close that Direction; the Direction's heading is in the previous file. It ends at the close of Direction 12, the last Direction of this Title. The next heading in the source, "Tit. 2. Cases of Marriage," and its Question 1 belong to the next file.
- **First Question:** the source ends the question "...hypocrisy in the world." with a period, not a question mark; kept as printed.
- **2 Corinthians 6:14-16 (flag):** NET wording. The King James "Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers" is the phrase Baxter's whole Direction leans on (marriage as a yoke shared with an unbeliever); the NET's "Do not become partners with those who do not believe" keeps the sense but loses the yoke image. The NET has "Beliar" for the King James "Belial," "unbeliever" for "infidel," and "we are the temple" for "ye are the temple." Baxter's "--" skip is shown as "..."; the words after it ("come out ... touch no unclean thing") are from verse 17, though Baxter's reference stops at verse 16. His reference is kept as printed.
- **Matthew 7:6:** cited, not quoted. The verse does not call the wicked "dogs and swine" in so many words; it warns against giving what is holy "to dogs" or throwing pearls "before pigs" (NET). Baxter's application is his own reading.
- **James 1:26 (flag, slight):** the King James "seem to be religious" becomes "thinks he is religious," and "vain" becomes "futile." Baxter's point (a professed religion with an untamed nature is worthless) stands in both.
- **Malachi 2:15 (flag):** Baxter leans on the King James "that he might seek a godly seed," taking the verse to name godly offspring as the purpose ("end") of marriage. The NET renders the verse quite differently ("What did our ancestor do when seeking a child from God? Be attentive, then, to your own spirit, for one should not be disloyal to the wife he took in his youth") and has no "godly seed." Baxter does not quote the verse, so the text keeps his wording; the reader should know the NET does not support his reading as directly. "Godly seed" is rendered "godly offspring."
- **Proverbs 19:14:** Baxter quotes only the second half of the verse; NET wording ("a prudent wife is from the Lord") matches the King James.
- **Proverbs 18:22 (flag):** the King James "Whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing, and obtaineth favour of the Lord" says finding a wife is itself a good thing; the NET reads "a good wife" and "has found what goodness is and obtained a delightful gift from the Lord." Baxter cites it for the blessing of marriage as such, which the King James supports more directly than the NET's "good wife."
- **"neither marrying nor giving in marriage" (1 Corinthians 7:29, 30):** these words are not in the cited verses, which say that "those who have wives should be as those who have none" because "the time is short" (NET). The quoted phrase is from the Gospels (for example, Matthew 22:30, NET "they neither marry nor are given in marriage"; reference not in the text). Baxter's wording and reference are kept.
- **Luke 12:19, 20 (flag):** NET wording. The quotation actually begins with verse 18 ("I will do this: I will tear down my barns ..."); Baxter's reference is kept as printed. The King James "I will say to my soul, Soul, thou hast much goods" becomes "I will say to myself, 'You have plenty of goods'," and "this night thy soul shall be required of thee" becomes "your life will be demanded back from you." Baxter's point (the worldling's settled plans, ended by death) is unchanged, but the King James "soul" that frames the fool's folly is lost. The NET's nested quotation marks are kept inside Baxter's.
- **Ecclesiastes 4:10-12; Proverbs 31:10-12:** cited with "See," not quoted.
- **Unmarked allusions** (kept in Baxter's wording): "from the beginning it was not so" (Matthew 19:8); "lay up a treasure in heaven" (Matthew 6:20); "reap no better than they sow" (Galatians 6:7); "the heavenly Jerusalem" (Hebrews 12:22).
- **Dated views:** Baxter's comparison of marrying the ungodly to joining with "dogs and swine" is his own blunt language (he asks pardon for it himself), kept as written. His view that a believer may lawfully marry an ungodly person only under real necessity, his counsel that health and "deformity" are fair considerations, and his remarks on the idle rich ("gentility") are kept as he states them.
- **Word swaps:** "palpably discovered" to "plainly revealed" (discover in its older sense, reveal); "a barren knowledge" to "a scant knowledge" (Baxter means little knowledge, as "though their knowledge be small" shows, not knowledge that bears no fruit); "close with such" to "join with such" (close with, unite or agree with); "parts" to "abilities"; "generating" to "breeding"; "consequence" to "inference" (a logical consequence, the conclusion drawn); "make no doubt but" to "have no doubt that"; "for aught I know" to "for all I know"; "take up with" to "settle for"; "mischief" to "harm"; "no more than" to "any more than"; "look after" to "look for"; "make choice of" to "choose"; "crossness" to "clash"; "competently quiet" to "reasonably quiet"; "froward and unpleasable" to "perverse and impossible to please"; "a competency of wit" to "a sufficient degree of good sense" (wit in its older sense, understanding); "competent humility" to "sufficient humility"; "respect to" to "regard for"; "estate" to "wealth" in Direction 8's heading (but "estates" kept in Direction 11, where "increase their estates" is still clear); "unhealthfulness" to "ill health"; "over-burdensome" to "overly burdensome"; "prejudicate" to "prejudiced"; "bred" to "raised"; "sensuality" to "indulgence in physical pleasure" (see the glossary); "scarce considerable" to "scarcely worth considering"; "make even with all" to "use up all of it"; "still in greater want" to "always in greater lack"; "peculiar" to "particular"; "in order to" to "with a view to"; "seed" to "offspring"; "be not wanting" to "are not lacking"; "however" to "although"; "communicate" to "share"; "partaker" to "sharer"; "remember you of" to "remind you of"; "run not upon it" to "do not rush into it"; "Reckon upon" to "Reckon on"; "unfeignedly" to "sincerely"; "owning" to "acknowledging"; "covetousness" to "greed"; "conjunction" to "union"; "felicity" to "happiness"; "continuance" to "permanence"; "assuming" to "taking on"; "hasting" to "hastening"; "in your way" to "on your way"; - **Kept words:** "affections" (emotions and desires; here the love between spouses); "carnal"; "unmortified" (sin not put to death); "the flesh"; "simply" (absolutely, without qualification: a bad man cannot be a good husband in the full sense); "person" in Direction 8 (one's bodily constitution and appearance, as the first point shows); "end" (purpose); "relation" (the marriage relationship); "gentility" (the rank and manners of gentlefolk); "worldlings" (worldly people); "beginning the world" (setting up in life, starting out in the world's business), kept as Baxter's play on words; "regenerate."
